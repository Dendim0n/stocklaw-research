## Possible explanations for the divergences, and counter-evidence

**Divergence 1: sentiment in the 10.8th percentile while the index is still in the 78.5th.**

The S&P closed at 7,623.68, only **-2.50%** from the sample high of 7,818.93. Explanation: four of the seven inputs (breadth, strength, VIX, put/call) are volatility/participation measures that stay dulled for long stretches in a heavyweight-led tape — the composite never reaching 50 in 39 sessions is the evidence. Counter-evidence: if this were "a few stocks holding the index up while most bleed", equal weight should keep lagging. It does not: over the last ten sessions **RSP +1.64%, QQQ -6.24%, IWM -1.14%**, equal weight clearly ahead. The more accurate description is that **sentiment indicators are desensitised at low levels while the index is being carried by the non-tech part of the market** — the two are not contradictory.

**Divergence 2: CNN's junk-bond sub-score +8.0 (improving) versus FRED high-yield spreads +13bp (worsening).**

Explanation: these are not the same indicator, and not the same day — CNN's raw for this item carries a timestamp of **10-23 20:10 UTC**, while FRED's cash-bond OAS is latest at **10-22**. On CNN's own basis the raw improved (1.2528 → 1.2367), consistent with the score; the option-adjusted spread widened. Counter-evidence: if credit were genuinely deteriorating, high yield should lag duration-matched Treasuries on the day; in fact on 10-23 **HYG -0.06%, IEF -0.16%, LQD -0.41%** — HYG slightly better — but over ten sessions **HYG/IEF -2.04%** with HYG -0.23% against LQD +1.41%, so high yield still lags. **Conclusion: no verdict at daily frequency; mildly worse on a ten-day view. Neither "credit has confirmed deterioration" nor "credit is fine" is asserted.**

**Divergence 3: breadth and strength "improving three sessions running" while the Dow -1.23%, RSP lags SPY and defensives/financials lead the decline.**

Explanation: a multi-day cumulative breadth measure can still rise on a down day (see sub-indicator contradiction 2), and strength is a separate normalisation. Counter-evidence: if participation were truly repairing, RSP should not lag SPY by 25bp on a day when tech steadied. **Conclusion: do not accept the "improvement" as repaired diffusion; it is more likely a low-base denominator effect.**

**Divergence 4: oil +3.1% to +3.8%, yet gold -0.65%.**

GLD fell **-0.65%** while geopolitical risk escalated (Brent at a multi-month high, media tying it to US-Iran/Yemen attacks); the dollar ETF UUP rose **+0.21%** and the 10-year real yield is 2.64% — of the three, only dollar strength supports lower gold. Possible explanations are liquidation or profit-taking on prior gains; **no verifiable flow data is available on this machine for any single explanation, so it is logged as unexplained**. At minimum it shows the day's "haven" bid was specific to bonds and the dollar, not to all asset classes.

**Divergence 5: Dow -1.23% while Nasdaq -0.21%, yet CNN's momentum score kept falling (22.6 → 17.6).**

The momentum input only looks at the S&P level against its 125-day average and does not distinguish sectors. With the index just +0.81% above that average, a lower score is an **arithmetic result**, not evidence that tech kept deteriorating (semis were +0.4% on the day). **Counter-evidence: if the momentum score reflected overall risk appetite, it should not have deteriorated on a day when tech steadied and energy led.**
