## Divergences: plausible explanations and counter-evidence

### Divergence 1: −8.0 of the −9.20 total came from safe-haven demand alone

**What happened:** the composite fell 1.31 on the sub-score measure, of which safe-haven demand contributed −8.0; the other six netted −1.2. **Strip that one item out and sentiment barely moved today.**

**Plausible explanation:** the item measures "stocks versus Treasuries" — essentially a 20–30 day window on relative equity/bond performance. On 11-13, SPX −0.40% and TLT −0.43% were nearly equal, but **the cumulative 20–30 day gap has been narrowing** (the equity leg recovering from a deeper negative), which by construction should push this item toward greed — **yet CNN pushed it toward fear.** That is the opposite of what the mechanism implies; see Divergence 2.

**Counter-evidence and boundaries:** (a) the item's score path over four sessions was **17.4 → 29.2 → 21.2**, an 11.8-point swing in two days that **gave back 68% of the prior improvement**; (b) yesterday's report explicitly said the item "measures relative performance rather than absolute risk appetite, and will reverse as soon as bonds stabilise" — **today is that reversal**; (c) therefore **today's −1.31 should not be read as "risk appetite falling." It looks more like a high-frequency noise term giving back a spike.** What matters is credit (new change 6) and rates (new change 5).

### Divergence 3: yesterday's "reversible" call was validated — but that does not make this a second bottom

**What happened:** the composite fell from 40.34 on 11-12 to 38.60, which looks like a failed breakout.

**Plausible explanation:** reading only the total suggests "the sentiment repair has stalled." But **five of seven items barely moved** (momentum −1.4, put/call −0.6, VIX 0, breadth +1.0, junk bonds +4.4); **the entire move sits in one item I cannot independently verify.**

**Counter-evidence:** (a) excluding the safe-haven item, the composite would be (270.20 + 8.0)/7 = **39.74**, essentially yesterday's level — **the "stalled repair" conclusion depends entirely on an unverified item**; (b) the other direction has evidence too: junk bonds +4.4 and breadth +1.0 both point toward greed; (c) **the accurate statement is "sentiment is treading water, with one item up and one down inside it," not "a second bottom."**

### Divergence 4: CNN's junk-bond spread is at a one-year tight, and FRED HY OAS also tightened — same direction, but the meaning is "a thinner cushion"

**What happened:** CNN's raw junk-bond spread of 1.18777 sits at the **4.8th percentile** of the year (95% of sessions were wider over the past year), score 62.40 (greed); same day FRED HY OAS is **3.20%**, the 70.6th percentile of three years and the 86.8th of 2026 (year low 2.60%).

**Plausible explanation:** the two series have different definitions (universe, rating buckets, CCC inclusion, OAS versus effective yield), **with absolute levels about 2.0 percentage points apart; they are not merged and not substituted for one another**. Both tightened, which says **the credit market has not priced the rise in rates.**

**Counter-evidence:** (a) "the 86.8th percentile of the year" and "the tightest 5% of the past year" can both be true — **one compares with early 2026, the other with the last twelve months; the percentiles do not contradict, they use different bases**; (b) **what should worry you is a thinner cushion, not deteriorating credit**: spreads have already widened 60bp from the 2.60% year low, so if the 10Y keeps rising while spreads begin to follow, the character shifts from "discount-rate shock" to "credit pricing" — which is exactly what escalation condition 1 is for; (c) this run does not conclude "credit is fine": **the 11-13 spread is not published yet**, so everything here is 11-12 data.
