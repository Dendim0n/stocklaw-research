## Divergences: Possible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: VIX −9% and Nasdaq-100 +1.35%, yet the composite rose only 2.09 and stays in extreme fear.**

- Fact: six of the seven sub-scores rose (VIX +7.0, put/call +5.0, momentum +1.6, junk bond +1.2, safe haven +0.4, strength +0.2) with breadth flat, summing to +15.4 points, ÷7 = **+2.20**. On the revised historical series, however, 11-05 was 19.9911 and 11-06 is 22.0793, a gain of only **+2.09**. The 0.11-point gap comes from CNN's small upward revision of the 11-05 sub-scores (about 0.8 points across the seven) — the same fact behind the `previous_close` (19.8776) versus series (19.9911) discrepancy.
- Explanation: the composite is a **banded mapping**, not a continuous measure. VIX at 25.64 and 28.18 are both far above the 50-day average and fall in the same extreme band; put/call at 0.8610 remains at the 92.4th percentile, and a 0.0119 decline does not leave the extreme-fear band. **The bounce was simply not large enough in absolute terms; the data is not stale.**
- Counter-evidence: if Friday were the first day of a trend reversal, it would normally come with VIX breaking below 20 and put/call falling toward 0.75 (one-year median 0.7409). Neither happened. So "peak fear" currently has **no sub-index support**.

**Divergence 2: the index bounced while participation (breadth/strength) kept deteriorating.**

- Fact: SPX +0.31% and NDX +1.35%, yet breadth raw fell from 601.93 to 597.55 (−0.7%) and strength raw went from −3.2979 to −3.3369 (worse). The Dow fell −0.46% over the same session.
- Explanation: the bounce was driven by mega-cap technology and semis (SMH +3.62%, QQQ +1.40%) while old-economy weights fell. **This is a price fact, not an inference from breadth**: the closes were checked instrument by instrument, with IWM +0.99% and DJI −0.46%, i.e. a mixed tape.
- Counter-evidence and limits: breadth and strength are slow variables and the API does not publish their constituents or formulas; a 0.7% breadth move in one session may simply be a band boundary effect (the score not moving at all is evidence of that). So **breadth cannot be used to declare "the bounce is fake"**; it is recorded only as "participation unconfirmed".
- Explicitly not inferred: nothing about large-versus-small caps is derived from breadth or strength — small caps (IWM) actually rose 0.99% on Friday.

**Divergence 3: equities stabilised on Friday while the credit spread hit an 18-month high on 11-05.**

- The timing must be aligned first: the 11-05 HY OAS reading prices **Thursday, the day of the equity crash**; FRED simply publishes it a day late. So this is not "stocks fell first and credit followed the next day" but **the same day priced simultaneously in two markets, which this report only now receives**.
- Fact: HY OAS +16bp, of which the HY effective yield contributed +7bp and the benchmark Treasury −10bp. **Both legs moved toward a wider spread**, meaning high-yield bonds were actively sold, not a denominator effect from a Treasury rally.
- Counter-evidence (weakening the "credit event" reading): at 3.48% the absolute level is only the 89.1st percentile of the three-year sample (range 2.59%–4.61%, median 3.02%) — still more than 150bp away from genuine credit crisis territory (above 5%, the script's "panic" band). HYG, the high-yield ETF, fell only −0.31% on 11-05 and −0.06% on 11-06, so cash prices barely moved.
- Counter-evidence (weakening the "technical noise" reading): +16bp ranks 25th by absolute size among 785 daily changes over three years (top 3.2%), and historically comparable one-day widenings cluster in stress periods (2025-04-03 +59bp, 2025-04-04 +44bp, 2024-08-02 +37bp). The same day saw IG OAS rise to the 97.2nd percentile of its year.
- **Conclusion: it is confirmed that credit gave a deterioration signal simultaneously on Thursday; it is not confirmed that a credit event has begun.** The gap is level (still low) and persistence (one day only).

**Divergence 4: the API's point-level ratings contradict the indicator-level ratings (unverified).**

- Fact: the last point of `market_momentum_sp500` is labelled **extreme greed** while its score of 8.0 corresponds to **extreme fear**; `market_volatility_vix`'s last point is labelled fear while score 9.16 is extreme fear; `stock_price_breadth`'s last point is labelled extreme greed while score 45.4 is neutral.
- Treatment: **ratings bind to the score only**; the point-level `rating` field is treated as an unverified API label, is excluded from every judgement, and is never used to reverse-engineer thresholds.

## Data Limitations

1. **The CNN composite and sub-index snapshot is taken about 1 hour 12 minutes after the close (17:12 EST)** and excludes after-hours trading; CNN revises the most recent day (this run: 11-05 from 19.8776 to 19.9911). Sub-index snapshot timestamps are scattered between 20:59Z and 22:12Z, not the same millisecond.
2. **The `previous_close` field conflicts with the historical series** (19.8776 vs 19.9911); the value equals exactly the 11-05 reading published yesterday, so the field appears to carry an old snapshot. Both conventions are disclosed (+2.09 / +2.20) and neither alone is used to claim a gain.
3. **The units and formulas of the sub-index raw values are unpublished by both the API and the script.** SPX level, 125-day average, VIX, 50-day average and put/call match the official definitions; strength, breadth, junk-bond gap and safe-haven gap are "inferred calibre" and are used only for direction and magnitude. The point-level `rating` fields also contradict the score-level ratings (see Divergence 4) and have been discarded entirely.
4. **Credit data lags one trading day:** HY/IG OAS and the HY effective yield run through **11-05**, with 11-06 not yet published. So "did credit improve while equities stabilised on Friday" **cannot be answered this run** — the largest information gap of the period. FRED `DGS*` likewise stops at 11-05, so all 11-06 rate data here comes from the official Treasury CSV. FRED returns only 786 observations for HY OAS (from 2023-11-07), insufficient for long-run historical percentiles.
5. **Leverage and flow data are missing:** the margin debt, IPO and ETF flow figures in `crisis_signals.py` are script constants with no source or as-of date and are not used; the report contains no leverage conclusion.
6. **The script output mixes periods:** inside `3_yield_curve`, t10y/t2y (11-05), spread_10y2y (11-06) and spread_10y30y (11-05) mix three dates. This report uses the Treasury same-day curve instead; the script itself is unmodified.
7. **Data not obtained:** Russell 2000 index (`.RUT.US` returned empty on three retries), Philadelphia Semiconductor index (`.SOX.US` likewise empty), VIX term structure, intraday HY OAS, and the latest monthly FINRA margin debt. Small-cap and semiconductor magnitudes are proxied by the IWM / SMH ETF prices; **ETFs and indices are not fully equivalent**.
8. **Chart calibre:** the three chart filenames use the run date (2026-11-07) while their in-chart titles show the data cutoff (2026-11-06), consistent with the body text ("market data through 11-06") and not a date error. The trend chart was visually verified (title 2026-11-06, terminal value 22 / extreme fear); the radar and sub-trend charts were generated from the same JSON by the same script, but the vision endpoint returned 502 this run, so **they were not visually verified**.
9. **No external attribution:** no news items or broker commentary were retrieved or cited as causes of the moves; every causal statement is limited to "the data appeared at the same time".
