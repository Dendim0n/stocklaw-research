## Divergences: Plausible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

### Divergence 1: index near its high, participation on the floor

- **Fact:** SPX is **−1.82%** from its 250-day high (7,818.93, 2026-10-06) and +21.01% above the 2026-03-30 low of 6,343.72; over the same span breadth sits at the 8.8th percentile and strength at the 8.0th.
- **Explanation A (supported):** breadth and strength are **multi-day smoothed/cumulative measures**, and October's two step-downs (10-14, 10-22) already drove them to lows; the index rebound is only three sessions old and has not yet propagated to these slow variables. Evidence: both have been rising for several sessions (breadth +129.84 over five observations, strength +2.16), consistent with A.
- **Explanation B (less likely, not excluded):** the index's gain is concentrated in a few heavyweights and breadth is structurally narrow. **This report cannot test that** — no 10-26 sector or single-stock data was obtained, and the breadth/strength conventions are undisclosed, so one cannot back out "small caps are falling."
- **Counter-evidence test:** if breadth stays below 500 and strength stays negative for three more sessions while the index keeps rising, explanation B deserves more weight; if breadth recovers above 700 and strength turns positive, A holds.

### Divergence 2: volatility down, safe-haven demand still extreme

- **Fact:** VIX 18.39 (score 39.55), Put/Call 0.7954 (score 22.80, 73.6th percentile), yet safe-haven demand scores only **11.00** with a raw −1.7428 (equities underperforming bonds over 20 days) at the 14.8th percentile.
- **Explanation A (supported):** the safe-haven input uses a **20-day window** that currently spans the two October drawdowns (10-14 to 10-22), so it lags spot VIX by construction. The VIX decline only reflects the last three sessions; the safe-haven term needs the old data to roll out of the window.
- **Counter-evidence test:** if VIX holds at 18–19 while safe-haven demand stays near 11 for three sessions, explanation A fails — that would mean bond outperformance is not drawdown afterglow but a persistent allocation preference, which is the genuine risk signal.
- **Hard boundary:** **neither Put/Call nor VIX identifies "smart money."** A falling P/C can be put protection being closed out or hedge funds rolling positions; the ratio alone cannot distinguish intent. This report uses it only as a measure of hedging cost, never as evidence about who is positioned how.

### Divergence 3: CNN's junk bond sub-score is mid-range while FRED credit spreads widen

- **Fact:** junk bond sub-score **41.60** (one of the two closest to neutral), raw 1.2367 → 1.2328 (slightly lower, i.e. this component reads credit demand as marginally better); over the same days FRED `BAMLH0A0HYM2` (HY OAS) went **3.23% (10-15) → 3.38% (10-23)**, **+15bp** in six sessions, and IG OAS 0.84% → 0.91% (+7bp).
- **Explanation A (most likely):** these are **not the same series**. CNN's 1.2328 equals neither HY OAS nor the HY-minus-IG difference (3.38 − 0.91 = 2.47), and its construction is undisclosed. With no like-for-like series available, **one cannot calibrate the other**.
- **Explanation B:** timing. CNN is a 10-26 close snapshot while FRED only reaches 10-23; if spreads tightened from 10-24 to 10-26 the two would agree — **but 10-26 spreads were not obtained here, so this cannot be confirmed**.
- **Counter-evidence test:** if the 10-26/10-27 HY OAS prints confirm a retreat inside 3.30%, B holds and credit never responded; if spreads widen past 3.50% while CNN's junk sub-score does not fall, then CNN's component is insensitive to cash bonds (possibly CDX- or ETF-based) and last period's "junk sub-score contradicts the credit market" problem persists.
