## Divergences, Plausible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: the composite fell into Extreme Fear (22.73) while breadth and strength both improved.**

- Facts: breadth raw 527.16 → **557.32 (+30.17)**, score 37.20 → **40.80**; strength −4.5415 → **−4.2201**, score 32.40 → **34.00**; on the same day RSP rose **0.76%** and the Dow **0.41%** while NDX fell **1.39%**.
- Explanation: this is a **cross-sectional reversal** — mega-cap tech fell, the median stock rose, and breadth measures exactly that up/down structure across constituents, so it moves opposite to a cap-weighted index. The two readings describe different cross-sections; this is not a data error.
- Counter-evidence and limits: ① breadth at 557.32 is still at the 12.0th percentile and only 55% of its one-year median, so 18 up days represent a lift off the floor, not healthy breadth; ② strength is still negative (−4.22, 11.2nd percentile) with a very low share of stocks making new highs, so "the median rose" is relative performance, not absolute strength; ③ IWM at −0.07% shows small caps did not take over leadership, so one cannot infer capital rotating from large to small caps.

**Divergence 2: momentum scores only 13.20 (Extreme Fear) while the S&P 500 is just 3.55% off its one-year high.**

- Facts: the S&P 500 closed at 7,541.40, **−3.55%** from the 10-06 high of 7,818.93 and at the **69.6th** percentile of its own one-year range; yet it closed **43.60 points (−0.575%)** below the 125-day MA of 7,585.00 — for a **second consecutive session**.
- Explanation: the momentum sub-indicator measures price relative to its 125-day MA and is **threshold-driven**. Once price crosses the average, the score drops into the low band for that region, so a modest decline produces a rating jump — the same effect recorded last issue (35.4 → 14.60), now extended to 13.20.
- Counter-evidence: an MA cross is the mechanical trigger level for trend-following flows, and two failed sessions above it mean pressure is building. But the condition this report set last issue required **three consecutive** closes below the average to change the characterisation; 11-02 is only day two, so this is marked **pending confirmation**. Note also that the 125-day MA is rising (7,582.74 on 10-30 → 7,585.00 on 11-02), which makes reclaiming it harder — a point that cuts against the bulls.

**Divergence 3: safe-haven demand at a fresh one-year low (−6.1317) while yields fall.**

- Facts: safe-haven raw −6.1317 (0.0th percentile; one-year range −6.1317 to +13.0886), score 1.80; over the same period 10Y 4.94% → **4.91% (−3bp)**, 30Y 5.31% → **5.28% (−3bp)**, 10Y breakeven 2.34% → **2.29%**, and the S&P 500 is **−2.99%** over 20 sessions.
- Explanation: **both legs are working** — stocks fell and bonds rallied — so this is genuine risk aversion rather than a bond-price artefact. A 5bp fall in breakevens shows the bond rally embeds lower growth/inflation expectations, not just lower real yields.
- Counter-evidence: ① the 20-day window also contains the late-September/early-October bond rally, and the interface cannot decompose −6.13 into stock and bond contributions; ② the series jumps around day to day (−0.66 on 10-28 → −2.47 → −5.63 → −6.13), so while four consecutive deteriorations is a strong signal, a single print still cannot be extrapolated to "risk aversion out of control"; ③ risk-aversion-driven bond rallies should coincide with wider credit spreads — which is **partly true this time** (HY +18bp, below), though the credit data only run through 10-30.

**Divergence 4: credit spreads widened 18bp in a single day while equity indices fell only modestly over the week.**

- Facts: HY OAS **+18bp to 3.44%** on 10-30, the largest one-day widening since 2026-03-27; IG OAS **+5bp to 0.94%**. The S&P 500 fell 1.82% on 10-30 and another 0.51% on 11-02.
- Explanation: credit markets typically price "mega-cap capex and cash flow" concerns with a lag versus equities, so a single 18bp day looks more like the **start of a risk-premium repricing** than crisis pricing — the absolute level is still 156bp from the 5% panic threshold.
- Counter-evidence: ① one day is not a trend; from 10-23 to 10-28 the spread actually tightened from 3.38% to 3.14%; ② the 11-02 equity decline **has no credit data covering it yet**, so there is no way to know whether credit followed; ③ the spread sits at the 97.2nd percentile of the last 1.5 years, so part of the "widening" is reversion from an extremely tight level, not proof of rising systemic risk.

**Shared constraint:** all four divergences point the same way — **sentiment is now in Extreme Fear while the internals diverge (mega-cap de-rating, median stock resilient), credit has given its first substantive response but at a lag and at a modest level, and rates are pricing risk aversion.** This remains "mega-cap valuation compression + cold sentiment + early credit response". Nothing here supports "a crash is coming", and nothing supports "a bottom is in": breadth is only 55% of its median, strength is still negative, safe-haven demand is at its worst of the year, and momentum has been below its average for two sessions.

**Basis discipline:**

- **10Y-2Y uses same-source, same-day values only.** The primary figure is the Treasury's 11-02 calculation, 4.91 − 4.59 = **+0.32**, which matches FRED `T10Y2Y` at **0.32** for 11-02. **A script defect must be flagged:** `crisis_signals.py` reports `spread_10y2y` from `T10Y2Y` (11-02 = 0.32) while its `t10y/t2y/t30y` fields are still **10-30** values (4.94/4.56/5.31) — **two dates in one table** (4.94−4.56 = 0.38, differing from 0.32 by exactly one day's move). This report does not use that combination, and derives 10Y-30Y from same-day 11-02 values (−0.37).
- **FRED and the Treasury publish on different clocks:** the Treasury curve for 11-02 is already out, while FRED `DGS10/DGS2/DGS30` still stop at 10-30 (4.94/4.56/5.31), as do `BAMLH0A0HYM2`, `BAMLC0A0CM` and `DFII10`. **The 11-02 credit spreads are not yet published**, so "did Monday's equity decline widen credit" **cannot be answered** until the 11-03 data.
- **CNN junk bond demand (1.2428) and FRED HY OAS (3.44%) are different measures** about 2.2 percentage points apart; CNN does not disclose the raw definition in the data package, so they are neither convertible nor usable to falsify each other. Only same-source self-comparison is done for CNN.
- **Margin debt, ETF flows and IPOs are monthly or year-to-date data, not same-frequency.** Record nominal margin balances and record ETF inflows **do not by themselves signal a crisis**; the leverage ratio (6.77) and YoY growth (+31.4%) are cited only to size how sensitive the market is to liquidity, not as a same-day risk reading. ETF and IPO figures were not re-pulled and are carried from the prior issue.
- **No red-light counts or crisis probabilities are published.** The script's `summary` green/yellow/red tally counts hard-coded constants (margin debt $1.304trn as of 2026-04, IPOs ~73, ETFs $856bn) as if they were live status; **this report does not cite that tally** and gives no calibrated crisis probability.
