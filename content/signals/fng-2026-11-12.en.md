## Divergences: Possible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

### Divergence 1: Raw values improved across the board, yet the normalized composite is flat

**Observation:** all seven raw values moved toward less fear without exception (VIX −10.1%, SPX +1.31%, PCR −0.0157, safe-haven +1.83, breadth +18.08), but the composite is **−0.40** against the revised series and still near 37.

**Possible explanation:** the improvement is not yet large in the one-year distribution. VIX 17.49 is at the 55.8th percentile, SPX sits only +0.37% above its 125-day average (my own percentile of the same-day ratio is **5.0**), and breadth/strength remain in the 15–17th percentile. **"Direction right, position unchanged"** is the most defensible reading.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) half of this flatness is revision noise (11-10 marked up 3.91), so "the composite refused to improve" itself sits inside a ±4-point uncertainty band and cannot be asserted as "sentiment rejecting improvement"; (b) if the same normalization base is carried into 11-12, the same raw inputs will produce a higher score — **drift in the normalization layer makes improvement look late or absent**, so single-day score changes cannot identify a sentiment inflection.

### Divergence 2: Momentum score 17.0 (extreme fear), but SPX is above its 125-day average and its level is at the 75.7th percentile

**Observation:** two facts coexist in one sub-indicator — the SPX closing level is at the **75.7th percentile** of the one-year sample (price near highs), the index is **+0.37%** above its 125-day average (just reclaimed), yet the score is only 17.0.

**Possible explanation:** CNN's momentum measure standardizes the deviation from the 125-day average, and for most of the past year the index ran further above that average, so +0.37% lands near the bottom of the distribution. My own ranking of the same-day `SPX / 125-day average` ratio gives the **5.0th percentile** (140 comparable days) — **same direction as 17.0, lower magnitude**, indicating CNN's window/smoothing differs from my simple percentile.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) I cannot substitute my 5.0 for the official 17.0 — different calibers — it only corroborates that the score is not a data error; (b) as long as momentum parks near the average the score stays low — **a low score measures weakness in relative trend position, not expected future loss**; (c) conversely, if SPX slips from +0.37% back below the average, this component drops quickly into deep extreme fear.

### Divergence 3: Junk bond demand extremely tight (7.6th percentile) while FRED HY OAS sits at the 82.9th percentile of three years

**Observation:** CNN's junk bond demand raw spread is 1.2098%, at the **7.6th percentile** of the one-year sample (the tightest bucket); at the same moment FRED HY OAS is 3.34%, at the **82.9th percentile** of three years (loose but not extremely loose).

**Possible explanation:** the two series are defined differently — CNN's proprietary junk spread differs from ICE BofA HY OAS in bond universe, rating weights and CCC inclusion, and the levels differ by more than two percentage points. **They measure different things; they are not two statements of one fact.**

**Counter-evidence:** (a) even on the FRED calibre alone, HY OAS has widened 23bp from 3.11% on 10-09 — the direction is deterioration, not "credit is fine"; (b) more importantly, **credit data is missing for both 11-10 and 11-11**, so using the 11-09 spread to argue "the AI rebound is confirmed by credit" means explaining today's tape with data from two days ago; (c) CNN's score for this component is 51.0 (neutral) while its raw value is at the 7.6th percentile — **score and raw percentile diverge again**, showing the same normalization base is suppressing the "greed" reading here too.

### Divergence 4: Index up, but participation stuck in the 15–17th percentile

**Observation:** SPX +1.31% and Nasdaq-100 +2.28%, while breadth raw 655.04 is at the **15.5th percentile** and strength −2.6399 at the **16.7th percentile**; their scores are 53.4 and 50.6 (neutral).

**Possible explanation:** the advance came from a handful of heavyweights and the AI complex. The verifiable split that day: **equal-weight RSP +0.80% versus SPY +1.32%, Dow +0.49% versus Nasdaq-100 +2.28%** — a direct price fact of "mega-cap/AI leading, equal weight lagging" (not inferred from breadth indicators). Media tallies put the day's advancers/decliners at 1,497 up / 1,327 down on the NYSE and 2,058 up / 1,392 down on Nasdaq (Investing.com, second-hand).

**Counter-evidence:** (a) **this does not imply small caps were crushed** — IWM rose **+0.89%** that day and the Russell 2000 gained 0.9%; (b) it also does not imply "only the megacaps rose" — within SMH, MU +11.9% and AMD +7.3% outpaced NVDA's +2.9%; (c) real counter-evidence requires 11-12 participation: if breadth stays near the 15th percentile while the index keeps advancing, "narrow advance" is established; if breadth catches up past the 25th percentile, today's narrowness was just a one-day lag.

## Data Limitations

1. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series; the gap is 3.91 this issue.** The field reads 33.4571 against **37.3714** for 11-10 in the revised series. The field exactly equals the **prior published snapshot's** seven-score mean, so it is the "prior published value," not the "prior trading day's close." **This report shows both calibers (+3.51 / −0.40) and never announces a move on either alone.**

2. **Snapshot times are not uniform, and one is taken before the close.** Composite 22:11:07Z (17:11 EST); momentum 21:42:03Z (16:42 EST), strength/breadth 22:11:07Z, put/call 22:08:29Z, VIX 21:15:01Z (16:15 EST), junk bond 21:10:00Z, and **safe-haven demand 20:59:59Z (15:59 EST, one minute before the close)**. That last item may exclude closing-auction prices; its mapping to closing prices is **unverified**. The 71-minute span does not materially affect this issue's conclusions but is disclosed.

3. **CNN revises history, and this issue's revision landed in the normalization layer.** Measured: 11-10 33.4571 → 37.3714 (**+3.91**, zero change in raw inputs), 11-09 −0.20, 11-06 +2.27, 11-04 +6.89. **Revision magnitude (up to +6.89) is the same order as genuine daily moves (this issue −0.40 to +3.51) — the index's biggest methodological weakness: single-day changes cannot be used for directional calls.**

4. **All three `previous_*` fields verified this issue.** `previous_1_week` 34.1714 = series value for 11-04; `previous_1_month` 43.5143 = 10-09; `previous_1_year` 30.4571 = series first point (2025-11-11). Exact matches.

5. **Sub-indicator raw units are inferred from the interface.** Momentum (SPX level) and VIX are independently verified via Longbridge; the absolute definitions of strength, breadth, junk spread and safe-haven spread (denominator, window, annualization) are undocumented, so they are usable **only for direction and magnitude, never for equivalence with external metrics**. Junk bond demand especially — it differs from FRED HY OAS by more than two percentage points.

6. **11-11's bond ETF quotes carry no rate or credit information.** TLT/LQD/HYG volume was just 0.67/0.25/0.26× their prior 20-day averages with price moves inside ±0.22%. The underlying Treasury and credit markets were closed; **those ETFs only show the tickers were tradable, not any change in yields or spreads.**

7. **The credit gap is two trading days and is not "zero change."** A FRED range query on `BAMLH0A0HYM2` / `BAMLC0A0CM` confirms the last observation is 11-09, with no values for 11-10 or 11-11 (11-11 holiday, 11-10 publication delay). **Missing cells are marked "unpublished," never treated as zero and never filled with a prior value disguised as current.**

8. **Rates have only one cutoff date.** The 11-10 curve comes from the Treasury CSV (one day ahead of FRED `DGS*`, which stops at 11-09); there is no 11-11 data. `crisis_signals.py`'s `3_yield_curve` is internally mixed-vintage (see the credit/rates section); this report uses same-source same-day values and **did not modify the script**.

9. **Macro and single-stock narratives come from media, not primary releases.** CPI (October headline +0.4%, core +0.3%), CoreWeave results and guidance, the NVDA board investigation and the Baird downgrade, December hike odds of 90% / 50bp at 11.8% / January at 64%, and the day's advance-decline counts come from AP, Reuters (via Metrobank / Globe and Mail), Yahoo Finance, Kiplinger, Investing.com and Tickmill. **BLS releases, company 10-Qs and the raw CME FedWatch page were not read directly.**

10. **Chart calibers and verification status.** The three chart files use the **run date (2026-11-12)** in their filenames while the in-chart title and data use the **market cutoff (2026-11-11)** — consistent with the text, not a date error. **The trend chart was visually verified**: title "CNN Fear & Greed Index — 2026-11-11", last point labelled "37 fear", matching 36.97 / fear. **The radar chart could not be visually verified** (the image interface returned 502/504 repeatedly); source-code review confirms it reads the seven `score` values and top-level `rating`, matching the table's calibre. **The sub-indicator trend grid** colors its scatter points by the **per-point `rating`** field (5 of 7 conflicts this issue), so its colors disagree with this report's ratings — **the table governs**.

11. **Not obtained.** VIX term structure (front/second month), put/call by market segment (index/single-stock/ETF), HY and IG OAS for 11-10 and 11-11, October FINRA margin debt, ICI weekly ETF flows, futures and after-hours prints, SMH constituent-level tape. These gaps mean: **"who bought this bounce" cannot be answered this issue — only "what price changed, by how much, and on what volume."**
