## Divergences: possible explanations and counter-evidence

### Divergence 1: yields down, stocks down — the "discount-rate shock" framework fails today

**What happened:** on 11-16, 2Y −5bp, 5Y −5bp, 10Y −3bp, 30Y −1bp, and 10Y breakeven fell 4bp to 2.20% (2.3rd percentile of 2026), leaving the 10Y real-yield proxy (5.12 − 2.20) at **2.92%, essentially unchanged from 2.91% on 11-13**. On the same day the S&P fell 1.92%, Nasdaq-100 2.67%, SMH 5.11%, VIX rose 18.7%. **The rate leg eased and stocks still fell.**

**Possible explanation:** the marginal driver rotated from "discount rate" to "AI-chain-specific risk": the Commerce Department's new overseas AI-chip and remote-compute licensing (policy), governance uncertainty after NVDA's CEO resignation (company), and de-risking ahead of the 11-17 after-close earnings (timing). All three point to **one narrow channel** — the AI capex narrative — rather than a market-wide discount rate.

**Counter-evidence and boundaries:** (a) **one day of lower yields does not change the five-day direction**: 10Y is still **+24bp** from 4.88% on 11-09 to 5.12% on 11-16, 2Y is **+27bp** from 4.56% to 4.83%, and the curve flattened 3bp net over five sessions; 10Y sits at the **92.2nd percentile** of 2026, 2Y at 96.3, 30Y at 92.7. **The high-rate backdrop has not been overturned; it simply stopped adding pressure today.** (b) Sector structure supports the narrow reading: staples +0.27%, utilities +0.18%, health care −0.51%, real estate −0.27% — all on the "rate-sensitive but not AI" side and all resilient. Under a pure discount-rate shock, real estate and utilities (the longest duration) should be weakest; the opposite happened. (c) Conversely, an all-AI explanation cannot cover IWM −1.66% (small caps have no AI exposure and still fell), so AI is the primary cause and a broader risk-appetite contraction the secondary one. **Conclusion: this run can declare a rotation in drivers, but not the end of the rate shock.**

### Divergence 3: all seven deteriorated together, but nearly a third of the −53 points is a VIX "re-calibration"

**What happened:** VIX rose from 19.30 to 22.91 (+18.70%) while the score fell from 50.0 to 33.33 (−16.67), 31.4% of the total decline.

**Possible explanation:** in the archive this score sat at exactly 50.0 for three consecutive periods (against VIX 19.46, 17.49 and 19.30) and left that level for the first time this run — implying 50.0 is a **plateau (normalisation floor) in the low-VIX region**, not a judgement that "volatility is neutral". Once VIX steps up, the score jumps into the low band. **Earlier reports treated this field as uniformly "degenerate and uninformative"; that needs correcting: the field does move, it just has steps.**

**Counter-evidence:** (a) the step location is unverifiable, and **the mapping between 22.91 → 33.3 and 19.30 → 50.0 cannot be recomputed**, so it still cannot be treated as an independent signal; (b) there is a starker example in the same table: **junk bond demand fell 10.0 points on a 1.6bp widening** (1.18777 → 1.20400), with the raw value still at the 7.6th percentile of the year (the tightest band). That is a score reacting violently to a trivial raw change at the window edge. **A meaningful part of the composite's −7.58 is "normalisation sensitivity", not new bad news.** The genuine new information is in the raw values: VIX +18.7%, SMH −5.11%, credit +3bp.

### Divergence 4: breadth and strength raw values at the 13–15th percentile, but scores say neutral/fear

**What happened:** market breadth 619.49 sits at the 14.8th percentile and stock price strength −3.6484 at the 13.6th percentile (both within the worst 15% of the year), yet the scores are 49.4 (neutral) and 41.4 (fear).

**Possible explanation:** the score uses CNN's own rolling normalisation and thresholds, **which is not the same statistic as a trailing-one-year percentile**; a low one-year percentile means the absolute level is poor, while a neutral score means it is not extreme relative to its recent distribution.

**Counter-evidence and boundaries:** the two **cannot be converted into one another, and neither supports "mega-caps holding the index up while small caps crater".** The direct price facts on the day are the opposite: **equal-weight RSP −1.01% beat cap-weighted SPX −1.92%, and small-cap IWM −1.66% beat Nasdaq-100's −2.67%.** The real underperformer was semiconductors (SMH −5.11%). **So "weak breadth = small caps crashing" is refuted by prices this run.**

### Divergence 5: the composite has been in fear for 24 days, but its percentile fell from 45% to 25.6%

**What happened:** the rating never changed (fear throughout), yet the position halved.

**Possible explanation:** in this score range percentiles are very sensitive. The sample median is **40.77**, with 110 days between 25 and 45 (34 of them in the 30–35 bucket and 30 in 40–45, so density is not skewed). 38.60 sat at the 45.0th percentile and 31.02 at the 25.6th — **7.58 score points equal 19.4 percentile points, about 2.6 percentile points per score point**. A percentile is a function of the score distribution, not a linear scale.

**Counter-evidence:** this is a reminder that **ratings are coarse labels and no substitute for percentiles**. The same sentence "still in fear" can mean the 45th percentile or the 25th — **every positional statement in this report uses percentiles, not rating names.**

## Data limitations

1. **The 11-13 composite was revised down 1.23 this run (38.60 → 37.3714), and the revised value is identical to 11-10's 37.3714.** The seven sub-indicator raw values are unchanged digit for digit; the revision sits only in the normalisation layer. I cannot distinguish from the API between a coincidence (scores keep one decimal, so seven-item sums land on a 0.1/7 grid and repeats are possible) and a duplicated point. **Left unverified; no conclusion is adjusted on its basis.**

2. **There are two independent daily-change measures, 1.23 apart:** −7.58 (versus `previous_close` 38.60) and −6.35 (versus the revised series at 37.37). The third measure (recomputed from sub-score differences) is **mathematically equivalent** to the `previous_close` one (38.60 is 11-13's sub-score sum ÷7) and is not independent evidence. **The report shows both and does not pick one to declare a decline.**

3. **Snapshot times are not uniform, and one item is taken before the close.** Composite/breadth/strength 22:14:41–45Z (17:14 EST); momentum 21:20:01Z (16:20); Put/Call 21:27:10Z (16:27); VIX 21:15:01Z (16:15); junk bonds 21:10:00Z (16:10); **safe-haven demand 20:59:58Z (15:59:58 EST, two seconds before the close)**. Span 75 minutes. **Safe-haven demand excludes closing-auction prices and its correspondence to closing prices is unverified** — and it accounts for 17.7% of this run's decline.

4. **CNN revises retroactively; this run was a 1.23 downward revision.** Archived evidence: 11-13 −1.23, 11-12 +0.40, 11-11 −1.03, 11-10 +3.91, 11-09 −0.20, 11-06 +2.27, 11-04 +6.89. **Revision size far exceeds most genuine daily moves — the indicator's biggest methodological weakness.**

5. **Two of the three `previous_*` fields can be verified.** `previous_1_week` 31.5143 = the 11-09 series value ✓; `previous_1_month` 18.2143 = the 10-16 series value ✓; `previous_1_year` **22.0571 has no match in the 250-day sample** (which starts 2025-11-17, so the corresponding day a year earlier is outside it) and is **unverifiable**.

6. **Independent verification of safe-haven demand is partly, not wholly, successful.** The 5–60 day SPX/TLT return spreads **all agree in direction with this run** (−1.54 to −4.22), and the 15-day change of −2.17 matches CNN's −2.1713 exactly; **but the same 15-day window gave only +0.05 for 11-13, when CNN showed −0.99** (that day matched the 30-day window). **Two sessions each matched by a different window means this is not a reproducible fixed definition; verification is not claimed and the item is used as evidence for nothing.**

7. **The units of the sub-indicator raw values are "inferred from the interface".** Momentum (SPX level) and VIX have been independently verified against Longbridge, digit for digit. The absolute definitions of strength, breadth, junk bonds and the safe-haven spread (denominator, window, annualisation) are not documented by the API, so **they support direction and magnitude only and cannot be equated with external indicators**.

8. **Credit data lags and this run is missing 11-14 and 11-16.** FRED `BAMLH0A0HYM2` / `BAMLC0A0CM` latest is 11-13. **"What credit did on the day stocks fell 1.92%" is still unknown** — every "credit has not confirmed" conclusion here rests on 11-13, which is strictly not the same day.

9. **Rates have only one channel; FRED lags.** The 11-16 curve comes from the Treasury CSV (FRED `DGS*` stops at 11-13). 10Y-2Y has a second channel via FRED `T10Y2Y` 11-16 = 0.29, **and the two agree**; no other tenor has a second source. **Secondary media report 10Y 5.13% / 2Y 4.83% versus Treasury 5.12% / 4.83%; Treasury is used.**
