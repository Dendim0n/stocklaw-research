## Possible Explanations for the Divergences, and Their Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: index level still high (7,718.94, 91.3rd percentile of sample) versus Breadth and Strength at the bottom of the sample (4.4% / 3.2%).**
- Fact: both breadth and strength deteriorated one-directionally from mid-August, bottomed on 10/05–10/07, and have recovered only modestly; both remain at extremely low percentiles of the past year while the index merely oscillated at high levels.
- One explanation: index-level strength rests on a small number of large-weight names while the median stock is far weaker.
- **Counter-evidence / constraint: neither CNN breadth nor strength distinguishes by market cap, so these two alone cannot support any claim about large-cap versus small-cap moves, nor that index heavyweights are propping up the tape; this report makes no such inference.** Breadth and strength have also improved for seven consecutive sessions since early October, which is inconsistent with an "everything is deteriorating" narrative.
- Falsification conditions: if the index keeps falling and breadth/strength make new lows, internal deterioration is confirmed; if breadth/strength keep recovering from the early-October lows while the composite returns above 45, rotation and repair is the better read.

**Divergence 2: Momentum sub-score falling fast (49.8 → 35.6) versus the index still at the 91.3rd percentile of the sample.**
- The momentum sub-indicator measures the index's deviation from its 125-day moving average (**an inference — CNN does not publish this**), not the absolute level. A −1.11% session that closed near the intraday low (open 7,774.18 / high 7,789.15 / low 7,693.31 / close 7,718.94) is consistent with a fast score decline.
- Counter-evidence: a single-day drawdown is not a trend reversal. The index remains above its mid-September low (7,551.81, 09/16).

**Divergence 3: VIX +10.5% on the day to 16.87, but still the 44.8th percentile of the sample and below the sample median of 17.13.**
- Option-market volatility pricing remains in a neutral band, inconsistent with the composite's Fear reading; it is also one source of the composite's sharp drop (the VIX sub-score is 42.7, down from 50.0 a week ago).
- Counter-evidence: a one-day VIX jump is common on index drawdown days and does not confirm a risk re-pricing until it closes above 20. VIX reflects implied-volatility pricing only and **cannot be used to identify "smart money" direction**.

**Divergence 4: Junk Bond Demand sub-score falling from 84.0 (Extreme Greed) to 59.2 (Greed), with FRED HY OAS also widening from 3.09% (10/07) to 3.16% (10/13).**
- Fact: the CNN-defined spread raw value went 1.1641% → 1.1994% (+3.5bp). The two measures have **incomparable levels** (different measurement targets) and are only compared for direction.
- Counter-evidence: HY OAS at 3.16% is still well below the script's 5% "panic" threshold, and IG OAS is unchanged at 0.83% (0.83% on both 10/12 and 10/13) — the credit market has not confirmed a risk re-pricing. The drop in the sub-score from 84 to 59 is far larger than the 3.5bp spread widening, indicating the sub-score is highly sensitive to recent readings; it should not be read on a linear scale.
- Side evidence (different basis, for reference only): US high-yield bond ETFs saw roughly $1.3B of net outflows in September 2026 (Morningstar), while total US ETF inflows that month were about $140–151B, so the high-yield cooling is small in aggregate terms.

**Divergence 5: Safe Haven Demand raw falling −39.8% in a day alongside the composite's weakening.**
- This measure is stocks-versus-bonds relative performance (**unverified definition**), not a fund flow; it cannot be read as money "rushing into" or "fleeing" safe havens.
- Same-direction corroboration: the 10Y yield eased to 5.21% on 10/13 (from 5.28% on 10/07), consistent in direction with an improved safe-haven reading, though the move is modest (7bp).

## Data Limitations

1. **The latest composite value (32.42) is a live snapshot at 18:13 US Eastern**, still updating after the close, and CNN may revise it; it is not treated here as a finalized daily close print.
2. **`previous_close` (44.74) conflicts with the historical series for 10/13 (44.11)** (0.63-point gap) and is unreconciled; the prior report showed the same conflict. The day change is therefore given as a range (−11.69 / −12.32) without declaring a single confirmed magnitude.
3. **The per-point `rating` field in the sub-indicator series is frozen across the whole sample** (most indicators hold a single value across all 252 points) and cannot be used for daily ratings; the scatter colors in the sub-indicator trend chart derive from that field and **do not represent** daily ratings. All ratings follow the standardized score only.
4. **CNN does not publish the sub-indicator formulas or raw-value units**; the "Unit" column is our reading from official descriptions and magnitudes, flagged as unverified. Precise economic interpretation of the raw values is therefore not warranted.
5. **The two credit measures must not be conflated**: FNG's Junk Bond Demand raw (1.1994%) ≠ FRED HY OAS (3.16%); each is labeled with its own source and date.
6. **Date differences between spread and yield series**: `DGS10`/`DGS2`/`DGS30` are latest for 10/13 while `T10Y2Y` is for 10/14; there is no yield data for 10/12 (bond market closed for Columbus Day). The 10Y-2Y spread is given for both dates under the same-source-same-day rule.
7. **Margin debt (August), IPOs (YTD) and ETF inflows (through 9/30) are all lagging data**; October flows and September margin debt are not yet published. The stale script constants (Apr 2026 / 73 deals / $856B) are discarded and must not be treated as current values.
8. **Two key market values were cross-verified this run**: the S&P 500 close of 7,718.94 / −1.11% and VIX 16.87 / +10.48% (intraday 14.98–17.45) were obtained independently via Longbridge and match the FNG raw values.
9. **Data not obtained** (no inference drawn): margin debt-to-market-cap and growth differentials, October ETF and IPO flows, official construction definitions for breadth and strength, options-side aggressive buying and dealer hedging direction, and median single-stock performance. **PCR and VIX cannot identify "smart money"; breadth and strength cannot be used to infer large-cap versus small-cap moves.**
10. This report is based on public data and separates facts from interpretation; it offers no buy/sell recommendations and is not investment advice.
