## 3. Signal Analysis

**Signal 1: AI compute supply chain shifting from NVIDIA exclusivity to diversification**
OpenAI chose Broadcom over NVIDIA for its first custom chip, while Qualcomm acquires Modular for edge AI — major players are actively reducing single-vendor dependency.
→ **Potential impact:** NVDA faces minimal near-term impact (custom chip mass production is years away), but long-term valuation logic must incorporate "customer defection" risk; AVGO benefits (fab orders secured); VRT and thermal supply chain players benefit from liquid cooling adoption.
→ **Watch:** NVDA, AVGO, QCOM, VRT

**Signal 2: AI agents evolving from "chatbots" to "autonomous operating systems"**
Google gives Gemini 3.5 Flash computer use capability; GLM-5.2 delivers a step-change in open-source agents; GitHub agent infrastructure projects (harness, SkillSpector, codebase-memory-mcp) collectively surge.
→ **Potential impact:** AI agent sector moving from proof-of-concept to scaled deployment; SaaS industry faces substitution risk from agents; cybersecurity demand rises in tandem (agent computer access = expanded attack surface).
→ **Watch:** GOOG/GOOGL (Gemini agents), PANW/CRWD (cybersecurity), CRM (SaaS substitution risk)

**Signal 3: Enterprise AI transformation "growing pains" — Elastic layoff as canary**
Elastic's 7% layoff is not an isolated case. When AI agents can efficiently handle log analysis, search augmentation, and similar tasks, legacy DevOps tool vendors must redefine their value proposition.
→ **Potential impact:** Tech companies entering organizational adjustment phase of "AI replacing internal roles"; some mid-cap tool stocks face valuation compression; but simultaneously, demand for AI-native tooling is created.
→ **Watch:** ESTC (directly exposed), DDOG (similar competitive dynamics), AI/PLTR (benefit from enterprise AI transformation demand)
