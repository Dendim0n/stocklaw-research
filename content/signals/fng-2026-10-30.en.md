## Possible Explanations for the Divergences — and Their Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: twelve sessions of Fear while credit spreads sit near historic tights and keep tightening.**

- Fact: composite 34.60 (Fear), HY OAS 3.14% (a one-year low zone, only 14bp above the 3% threshold), IG OAS 0.86%; 26bp of tightening from 10-22 to 10-28.
- Most plausible explanation: this fear originates in **cross-sectional participation** (strength -4.58, i.e. net new highs negative; breadth 510.76 at the 11.2nd one-year percentile) and in **relative long-bond strength**, not in expectations about corporate solvency. Credit prices default risk; breadth prices the distribution of money. They can diverge by construction.
- Counter-evidence (must be retained): credit spreads are a **lagging** variable and in genuine credit events typically widen 2–6 weeks after equities. A 3.14% spread also has little room left to tighten, so using "it has not widened" to argue "it is safe" mistakes low volatility for low risk. HY OAS also cannot capture equity-duration risk in richly valued AI-capex assets, so this divergence offers limited protection for equity valuations.

**Divergence 2: safe-haven demand at 9.6 (Extreme Fear) alongside an S&P 500 only 1.26% below its high.**

- Fact: safe-haven raw value -2.47% (20-day stock-minus-bond relative return), its lowest in 60 sessions, while the S&P 500's 20-day return was **+0.70%** (7,666.45 on 10-01 to 7,720.39).
- Explanation: the direction of -2.47pp is "stocks underperformed bonds", but the cause is a **bond rally**, not an equity selloff: the 10Y fell from 5.24% (10-01) to 4.98% and the 30Y from 5.61% to 5.32%, a substantial price gain in long duration. The "extreme fear" print is therefore manufactured by safe-asset strength, not by money fleeing equities.
- Counter-evidence: the indicator's one-year range is **-6.00 to +13.09**, so -2.47 is at the 9.6th percentile yet still inside normal variation, and it jumps day to day (+0.08 on 10-20, -2.29 on 10-22, -0.66 on 10-28, -2.47 on 10-29). A single print is noise. A genuine safe-haven signal requires stocks down **and** bonds up, both amplified; this run has only the latter.

**Divergence 3: breadth and strength improved while the Nasdaq-100 fell 1.38%.**

- Fact: breadth raw rose five consecutive sessions (440.14 → 510.76) and strength raw improved five straight days (-6.65 → -4.58); the same day the S&P 500 fell 0.30%, the Nasdaq-100 **-1.38%** and the Dow 0.25%.
- Explanation: this is consistent with money rotating out of a handful of megacap technology names into the rest of the index — the index decline is contributed by a few heavy weights while the median stock improved.
- Counter-evidence and limits: (1) strength raw is still **negative** (net new highs below zero) and breadth at 510.76 remains at the 11.2nd one-year percentile, so "improvement" only means leaving the floor; (2) this report draws **no conclusion about large-cap versus small-cap performance** — no usable Russell 2000 quote was obtained this run, and breadth/strength cannot substitute for index returns; (3) one day of divergence is not a style rotation; breadth would need to approach the one-year median of 1,017.89 before the tape counts as broadly strong.

**Shared constraint:** all three divergences point the same way — **the fear in sentiment is not confirmed by credit, rates or index position**. Equally, none of them establishes a bottom, because the causes of that fear (weak participation, strong long bonds) have not reversed.

## Conditions That Would Escalate or Relieve the Risk

**Escalation (requires cross-confirmation; any single trigger is not enough):**

1. **Credit turns:** HY OAS above **3.40%** for three consecutive sessions (back above the 10-22 level) **and** IG OAS above **1.00%**. Single-day 3–5bp moves are not a signal.
2. **Sentiment breaks:** the composite closes below **16.69** (the 10-23 low) **and** breadth raw simultaneously breaks **440.14** (the 10-23 level). A sentiment break without a breadth break indicates megacap drag rather than broad liquidation.
3. **Volatility confirms:** VIX closes above **20** (the top of the one-year series' last 20 sessions is 19.99) and holds for three sessions.
4. **Curve shape shifts:** 10Y-2Y narrows from +0.37 to **below zero** (same source, same day) while the 30Y-10Y inversion deepens beyond -0.34.
5. **Stock-bond reversal completes:** safe-haven raw falls below **-3.0** while the S&P 500's return over the same window is negative — i.e. equities begin to underperform systematically rather than bonds simply rallying.

**Relief (equally requires cross-confirmation):**

1. **Rating leaves Fear:** the composite returns above **45** (the Fear-to-Neutral boundary), currently 10.4 points away, and holds for two sessions.
2. **Participation genuinely repairs:** strength raw turns positive (now -4.58), or breadth returns to its one-year median of **1,017.89** — merely reaching the 20th percentile is not enough.
3. **Credit and sentiment align:** junk bond demand score recovers from 50.0 above 60 while HY OAS keeps tightening. If they disagree, credit governs and no relief is declared.
4. **Safe-asset demand recedes:** 30Y yields rise while equities rise with them (money leaving long duration into stocks), rather than "stocks down, bonds up".

These are observable conditions, not trade signals. Until they trigger, the characterization stands: **fearful, but unconfirmed by credit**.

1. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series.** The field reads 35.857 against a 10-28 series value of 34.40, a 1.457 gap caused by CNN revising 10-28's components. Both are shown; only the comparable series change of +0.20 is confirmed, and no daily move above 1.5 points is asserted.

The script's green/yellow/red `summary` also counts those constants as states; **that tally is not cited here**.
3. **FRED and Treasury publications are not synchronized** (`DGS*` at 10-28, `T10Y2Y` at 10-29), so the script's default combination spans two dates. All spreads in this report are computed from same-day same-source pairs.
4. **VIX has no second source.** Tencent `usVIX` is frozen at 21.67 from 2026-09-15 and cannot verify CNN's 17.20; breadth, strength, safe-haven demand and junk bond demand are likewise single-source (CNN) and can only be compared with their own prior values.
5. **The normalization window for component scores is undisclosed.** The one-year percentiles quoted here are positions within 251 observations and cannot be mapped to scores (e.g. junk bond demand's raw value is at the 9.2nd one-year percentile while its score is 50.0).
6. **Scope of chart verification.** All three charts were generated from the same JSON written at 06:31 CST (`--input /tmp/fng_data.json`); the script takes the title date from the last data point, so the in-chart title is **2026-10-29** while the filename uses the run date **2026-10-30** — by design, not a data misalignment. The trend chart's end annotation should read `35 fear` (34.6 rounded). The vision interface returned 502 repeatedly this run, so **no visual read-back was possible**; only three programmatic checks were completed: generation time, input file identity, and the script's data-selection logic.
7. **No intraday or after-hours data.** The composite is a post-close 10-29 snapshot; 10-30 Asian-session futures and pre-market moves are excluded.
8. **Monthly and year-to-date data are out of sync with daily signals.** Margin debt (2026-09-30), ETF flows and IPO counts cannot support same-day judgments, nor do they prove money is currently leaving or entering.
9. **Not obtained:** Russell 2000 quotes (Tencent `usRUT` returned nothing), so no conclusion is drawn about small caps; the 10-29 10Y real yield (not yet published by FRED).
