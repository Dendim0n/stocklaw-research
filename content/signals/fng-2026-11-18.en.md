## Divergences and possible explanations

### Divergence 1: composite 25.23 (near extreme fear) vs. S&P only −6.12% from its high

**Explanation:** the score is driven by relative measures, not absolute drawdown. The S&P is down 6.12%, but it has simultaneously broken MA20/50/125 and is sitting right on MA200 — three of the four extreme-fear readings (momentum, safe haven, VIX) measure position versus trend, versus bonds, or the level of volatility, not "how much it fell".

**Counter-evidence:** 25.23 sits at the 16.3rd percentile of a 251-day sample and 41 sessions closed below 25. "Close to extreme fear" is not rare this year, so **it cannot be read as "sentiment is extreme, therefore buy the reversal"**. Symmetrically, if the S&P keeps falling through MA200 the score falls with it — 25 is not a floor.

### Divergence 2: price at a 3.5-month low, VIX did not make a new high

**Fact:** the S&P closed at 7,340.53, breaking the 7,353.73 close of 11-05, but VIX at 25.46 **stayed below the 11-06 close of 25.64 and far below the 11-06 intraday high of 27.76**.

**Explanation (supports "orderly de-risking"):** SPY traded 1.47× its 20-day average volume (the prior session was also 1.47×, two consecutive elevated days), yet volatility did not spike, HYG fell only 0.13%, LQD 0.06% and TLT rose 0.21%. **This is "reduce exposure, buy duration", not "liquidity shock plus forced liquidation".** In the latter case VIX would jump and HYG would underperform LQD clearly; neither happened.

**Counter-evidence (this is not a bullish read):** VIX is already at the **94th percentile** of the past year and 49% above its 125-day average, so its absolute level is not low; and it has now risen two sessions in a row (11-16 +18.70%, 11-17 +11.13%). **"No new high" can equally be read as "panic has not yet entered its acceleration phase"** — the second leg of a volatility expansion typically arrives when the index breaks down a second time, which is exactly what happened today.

### Divergence 3: four extreme-fear readings vs. breadth/strength only at fear vs. junk bonds neutral

**Explanation:** fear is concentrated in "index versus trend" (momentum 5.2) and "equities versus bonds" (safe haven 7.2); single-stock breadth (44.6) and strength (36.6) are not yet extreme, and credit (50.0) sees no systemic event at all. **The internal disagreement says this is not a broad panic but a decline led by index-level positioning and safe-haven flows.**

**Counter-evidence:** the **raw** breadth and strength values already sit at the 12.4th and 11.2nd percentiles of the past year, close to the year's worst; the scores are not in extreme fear because CNN's normalisation window is longer than one year (see limitation 7). **"Score only at fear" does not mean "breadth is healthy".** Both fell 4.8 points today, the second consecutive joint decline, so the direction is unambiguous.

### Divergence 4: CNN junk bond demand (neutral 50) vs. FRED HY OAS (74bp wider)

**Fact:** CNN's junk bond raw value of 1.2091 sits at the **8.8th percentile** of the past year (near the year's low) while its score is a neutral 50; over the same period FRED's HY OAS has widened from 2.60% on 28 August to 3.34% (+74bp), the **83.4th percentile** of the available three-year sample.

**Explanation:** the two cannot be converted into one another — CNN does not publish the formula or window for this sub-indicator, and 1.2091 is neither an OAS percentage nor the HY/IG ratio (3.34/0.90 = 3.71). **CNN's neutral 50 cannot be used as evidence that credit is fine, and FRED's widening cannot be used to argue that CNN is wrong.**

**Treatment:** every credit conclusion in this report uses **FRED OAS plus bond ETFs**; CNN's junk bond reading is treated purely as a sentiment component. They are listed separately, never merged and never used to validate each other (see limitation 5).

### Eleven cross-validation conclusions

1. **Credit has not confirmed the breakdown, and this is the hardest counter-evidence this run.** The S&P made a 3.5-month low while HY OAS stayed inside its 20-day range at 3.34% and **below the 3.48% printed on 11-05** — on 11-05 the S&P fell 4.36% and spreads were 3.48%; today the S&P is lower and spreads are lower. **If this were systemic de-risking, spreads would be making new highs alongside equities. They are not.**

2. **Same-day credit proxies for 11-17 are equally calm.** With no OAS published, the ETFs stand in: **HYG −0.13%, LQD −0.06%, TLT +0.21%**. High-yield fell a fifth as much as equities, and long Treasuries rose. **"Equities down, credit flat, bonds up" points to valuation and positioning adjustment, not default-expectation repricing.**

3. **Rates moved lower, so this was not a discount-rate shock.** 2Y −5bp, 10Y −3bp. If higher rates were the driver, 10Y would have pushed above 5.15%; instead it fell. **Safe-haven demand at −5.53 (2.4th percentile) is consistent: money moved into duration.**

4. **But the 10Y-30Y inversion (−0.34pp) says "into duration" is not "bullish on growth".** 30Y at 5.46% is 34bp above 10Y, a term-premium shape rather than a rate-cut shape. **A 4.83% two-year means the opportunity cost of cash is still high; duration is not free.**

5. **HY OAS widening 74bp from 2.60% on 28 August is the only persistent negative credit trend.** That is not small (28% of the low), yet the absolute level is still oscillating near the 83.4th percentile of the available three years — **neither "credit is fine" (which would require sub-3.0%) nor "credit event" (which would require above 4.5%). It is a partial retracement of the excessive optimism priced in during the first half of 2026, nothing more.**

6. **Breadth, strength and the equal-weight index corroborate that the decline has spread.** CNN breadth raw fell **8.3%** on the day and strength fell 0.65; on the same day **RSP −1.45% underperformed SPX −1.03%**, the Dow fell 1.64% and IWM 1.43%. **This is the exact opposite of 11-16** (when RSP −1.01% beat SPX −1.92% and SMH fell 5.11%). **Note: this uses actual index data, not inferences about large-cap versus small-cap moves from F&G sub-indicators.**

7. **Yet the equal-weight index is still above its 125-day average (RSP +0.48%), so the medium-term trend has not broken.** That is the line between "spreading" and "collapsing": **only if RSP also loses its 125-day average does the decline become a medium-term trend event across the whole market.**

8. **Volume looks like high-level turnover, not capitulation.** SPY 1.47×, Dow 1.31×, RSP 1.15×, IWM 1.21× — **all elevated** — but SMH 1.01× and NDX 0.98× **show no volume expansion**. The volume is in broad and value indices, not semis. **Capitulation requires "market-wide volume plus a volatility spike plus a credit-spread jump"; only the first is present.**

9. **No leverage data this run, so no conclusion.** Margin debt only exists through April 2026 (a script constant that points the opposite way from the official October figure), and **the FINRA October reading archived last run ($1.4484T, −2.14% month on month) was not re-verified this run**. **Without leverage data, "are margin positions being forced out" cannot be answered.**

10. **NVDA earnings provided a short-term counter-signal.** The after-hours print of +3.83% (161.42 vs 155.46) says the AI narrative was not disproved on the day. **But that affects only the 11-18 opening gap, not the medium-term rate and credit constraints** — and 39.31m after-hours shares is thin against roughly 390m of daily volume.

11. **Conclusion: "price breakdown + credit not confirming + volatility rising" coexist this run. It is neither the eve of a crash nor a confirmed bottom.** Applying the cross-confirmation rule: **the escalation side is missing credit, the easing side is missing price** — neither case is made. That is the uncomfortable state in which direction is unresolved but the margin for error is narrowing.

## Data limitations

1. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series by 0.05.** The header `previous_close` of 31.0190 is the value published on 11-16; the historical series now carries 31.0673 for 11-16. **The daily change therefore has two bases (−5.79 / −5.84); the sign and magnitude agree, and this report shows both instead of choosing one.** Note also that yesterday's archive had 11-16's `previous_close` at 38.6000 while the series showed 37.3714 for 11-13 — **so the header `previous_close` and the series were never the same numbers and cannot substitute for each other.**

2. **CNN revises history; the revision was small this run but the mechanism persists.** Comparing today's pull with yesterday's archive across 250 overlapping sessions: the composite changed only on 11-16 (+0.048, +0.16%) and safe-haven raw only on 11-16 (−3.7307 → −3.7197); the other six sub-indicator raw series are **unchanged digit for digit**. Historically single revisions have reached ±6.89 (11-04), **so 25.23 should be treated as a first print with ±1 to ±4 points of uncertainty and must not be used for 0.5-point directional calls.**

3. **The `rating` field inside each sub-indicator's `data[]` array is unusable, and this is a known chart defect this run.** In most series the field is **constant**: momentum is tagged `extreme greed` on all 252 points (score 5.2 = extreme fear), breadth `extreme greed` on all 251 (44.6 = fear), junk bonds `extreme fear` on all 251 (50.0 = neutral), strength `extreme fear` on all 251 (36.6 = fear), safe haven `extreme fear` on all 251 (7.2 = extreme fear, coincidentally correct). **Consequence: in the third chart (sub-indicator trend grid) the end-point rating labels conflict with the text — five wrong, two right. Text ratings always follow the score; ignore the labels in that chart and read only the values and the curves.** The script was not modified.

4. **Snapshot times are not synchronised, and one is taken before the close.** Composite/breadth/strength at 22:14Z (17:14 EST); momentum 21:42Z (16:42); put/call 21:20Z (16:20); VIX 21:15Z (16:15); junk bonds 21:10Z (16:10); **safe-haven demand at 20:59:58Z (15:59:58 EST, two seconds before the close)**. The window spans 75 minutes. **Safe-haven demand excludes closing-auction prices and its mapping to the closing price is unverified** — and it is the second most extreme reading today (7.2), contributing 11.3% of the decline.

5. **CNN's credit sub-indicator and FRED OAS are not interchangeable.** CNN's junk bond raw of 1.2091 is neither HY OAS 3.34% nor the HY/IG ratio of 3.71, and **its formula and window are unpublished**. This report keeps them separate: credit conclusions use FRED OAS plus bond ETFs only, and CNN's reading is treated as a sentiment component. **Neither is used to disprove the other.**

6. **The script's yield-curve block mixes two trading days.** `spread_10y2y 0.30` is FRED `T10Y2Y` for **11-17**, while `t10y 5.12 / t2y 4.83 / t30y 5.46` are **11-16** values. **This report uses only the same-day, same-source calculation for 11-16: 0.29pp, and 10Y-30Y at −0.34pp**; the 11-17 value of 0.30 is reference only. **No second source was obtained for the 11-17 curve itself.**

7. **Percentiles are three-year, not ten-year.** The full locally available FRED `BAMLH0A0HYM2` / `BAMLC0A0CM` series start on **2023-11-20** (784 / 783 observations), so the "83.4 / 62.8 percentile" figures are percentiles of the available sample. Only `T10Y2Y` (2,510 observations) and `VIXCLS` (2,557) approach ten years. No "all-time extreme" language is used.

8. **Sub-indicator raw units are inferred from the interface.** Momentum (SPX level) and VIX have been independently verified against Longbridge and match digit for digit; **the absolute definitions of strength, breadth, junk bonds and safe haven (denominator, window, annualisation) are undocumented**, so they are usable only for direction and magnitude, not for comparison with external indicators.

9. **Leverage and fund flows are entirely missing this run.** Margin debt exists only as a script constant ($1.304T, Apr 2026 — seven months stale, and 11% away from the official October figure of $1.4484T in the opposite direction); IPO counts and ETF net inflows are hard-coded text with no as-of date. **This report does not cite either, does not report a red-light count and gives no crisis probability.** The FINRA October official value archived last run was not re-verified this run.

10. **The 11-17 sell-off is not attributed from primary sources.** The only primary facts confirmed here are the NVDA 8-K timing (2026-11-17 21:20:51Z) and the after-hours price. **No verifiable source was obtained for the day's driver (policy, earnings expectations, flows, index changes), so this report does not speculate.** "Why it fell" has no answer this run — only "how much it fell and which indicators moved with it".

11. **Data not obtained:** HY/IG OAS for 11-17, a second source for the 11-17 yield curve, VIX term structure, put/call by segment (index/equity/ETF), ICI weekly ETF flows, 11-17 NYSE advance/decline counts, futures positioning, and 11-17 trade-level detail beyond ETF volume ratios. **"Who bought and who sold" cannot be answered this run.**
