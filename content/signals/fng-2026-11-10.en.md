## Divergences: plausible explanations and counter-evidence

**Divergence 1: the index rose 2.35% and VIX fell 22.31%, yet the composite only reached 31.71 — still fear.**

- Fact: all seven sub-scores rose (total +67.44), but the composite only moved from the revised 24.35 to 31.71. Three sub-indicators remain at extreme fear (safe haven 5.4, momentum 13.8, put/call 17.4), with safe-haven barely off the floor.
- Explanation: the composite is a **mean of banded mappings**, not a mirror of price change. A 2.35% rally only lifts momentum from "well below the 125-day average" to "slightly below it", worth a score of 13.8. Safe-haven demand measures **stocks versus bonds over 20 days**, and 11-05's −4.36% is still inside that window; one +2.35% day cannot offset it.
- Counter-evidence: if this were day one of a trend reversal, safe-haven demand would leave the 10th percentile and put/call would return to about 0.75 (one-year median 0.7427). Neither happened — **there is no sub-indicator evidence that the panic is over; only that extreme readings are receding.**

**Divergence 2: breadth and strength improved, yet both remain near the 15th percentile of the year; and small caps cannot be inferred from them.**

- Fact: breadth raw 597.55 → 624.57 (+4.52%) and strength raw −3.3369 → −3.1416 (an improvement of 0.20); their scores rose to 48.4 / 44.6, yet their one-year percentiles are only 14.4 / 15.6.
- Explanation: both are **slow variables** and measure position within a one-year distribution. A one-week rebound cannot change the fact that most constituents are still near 52-week lows.
- Counter-evidence and limits: the interface does not publish either component's construction, so a 4.5% one-day breadth move may be a piecewise boundary effect (the score moved only 3.0 points). **It is recorded as "participation unconfirmed" and is not used to judge whether the rebound is real or fake.**
- Explicitly not inferred: **no small-cap versus large-cap conclusion from breadth or strength.** Measured directly, small caps (IWM) rose **+3.23%, outperforming SPX's +2.35%** — the opposite of the "mega-caps holding the index up while small caps lag" intuition, which shows breadth and market-cap tiers are different things.

**Divergence 3: the VIX sub-score jumped from 9.16 to 50.0 (neutral) while VIX itself remains in the 80.4th percentile of the year.**

- Fact: VIX 25.64 → 19.92; deviation from the 50-day average narrowed from +51.7% to +17.1%; the score moved from 9.16 to 50.0 and the rating went **straight from extreme fear to neutral**. 50.0 is exactly a round number.
- Explanation: the VIX component has a **steep piecewise boundary near 20** — the same absolute move (−5.72 points) barely changes the score between 28 and 25 but can cross a whole band between 22 and 20. That is a mapping design, not a data error, but **"score 50" must not be read as "volatility is normal"**: 19.92 is still above the one-year median of 17.19 and in the 80.4th percentile.
- Counter-evidence: genuine normalisation normally also brings put/call back to its median and safe-haven demand back toward 0. Here put/call is 0.8412 (88.0th percentile) and safe haven −5.77 (1.6th percentile), both still at the extreme side; **neither supports "risk removed".**
- Unverified: CNN does not publish the VIX mapping function, so a score of exactly 50.0 cannot be independently reproduced; it is marked **unverified**.

**Divergence 4: credit narrowed 6bp on 11-06, in step with Friday's equity stabilisation; but Monday's rally has no credit data.**

- Fact: HY OAS on 11-06 = 3.42% (−6bp), IG OAS flat at 0.93%; FRED has not published 11-09.
- Explanation: the 11-06 narrowing retraces only 37% of 11-05's 16bp, and a −6bp move ranks only in the 73.6th percentile of absolute daily changes over three years — **ordinary noise, not "credit repair"**.
- Counter-evidence: HY effective yield 8.07% → 8.06% (−1bp) while the comparable Treasury rose 2bp (5Y 4.59→4.64). The two legs moved in opposite directions, so the narrowing was driven mainly by **higher Treasury yields**, i.e. a denominator effect — the reverse of 11-05's active selling.
- Conclusion: **"the 11-05 credit impulse did not extend into 11-06" is confirmed; "credit has turned to improving" is not.** The key gap is 11-09 data.

**Divergence 5: point-level `rating` contradicts score-level rating (unverified).**

- Fact: 5 of 7 sub-indicators show a point-level `rating` inconsistent with their score — `market_momentum_sp500` last point tagged extreme greed (score 13.8 = extreme fear), `market_volatility_vix` extreme fear (score 50.0 = neutral), `stock_price_breadth` extreme greed (score 48.4 = neutral), `stock_price_strength` extreme fear (score 44.6 = fear), `junk_bond_demand` extreme fear (score 42.4 = fear).
- Treatment: **ratings bind to score only**; point-level `rating` fields are treated as unverified labels, excluded from all judgements and never used to back out thresholds.

## Data limitations

1. **The CNN composite and sub-indicator snapshot is taken about 1 hour 13 minutes after the close (17:13 EST)** and excludes post-market trading. CNN revises the most recent reading (this run: 11-06 from 22.0793 to 24.3532). Sub-indicator snapshots are spread between 20:59Z and 22:13Z and are not simultaneous: safe haven 20:59:59Z, junk bond 21:10Z, VIX 21:15Z, put/call 21:17Z, momentum 22:12Z, strength/breadth 22:13Z.
2. **The `previous_close` field conflicts with the historical series** (22.0793 vs 24.3532); it equals exactly the mean of the previous run's seven sub-scores. Both bases are disclosed (+7.36 / +9.63) and neither alone is used to claim a magnitude.
3. **`previous_1_year` = 20.4286 cannot be verified inside the series** (the window starts 2025-11-10); the field is not used this run.
4. **The units and construction of sub-indicator raw values are published by neither the interface nor the script.** SPX level, 125-day MA, VIX, 50-day MA and put/call match official definitions; strength, breadth, junk-bond spread and safe-haven spread are "inferred basis" and used only for direction and magnitude. Point-level `rating` contradicts score-level rating on 5 of 7 items (see Divergence 5) and has been discarded entirely.
5. **Credit data lags one trading day:** HY/IG OAS and effective yields are through **11-06**; 11-09 is not published. So "did credit confirm Monday's joint rally" **cannot be answered this run** — the single largest information gap. FRED `DGS*` likewise stops at 11-06, so all 11-09 rates come from the Treasury official CSV. FRED returns only 784 HY OAS observations from 2023-11-10, insufficient for long-run percentiles.
6. **Leverage data is stale:** FINRA margin debt is as of **2026-09-30**, published in the third week of October, about six weeks behind and not covering October–November; it is nominal and lacks market-cap scaling and structural breakdown. IPO and ETF flow data remain unavailable.
7. **The script output mixes periods:** inside `3_yield_curve`, t10y/t2y are 11-06, spread_10y2y is 11-09, and spread_10y30y is 11-06; `4_margin_debt` is an outdated April 2026 constant. This report substitutes the same-day Treasury curve and the FINRA official table; the script itself was not modified.
8. **Not obtained:** Russell 2000 index and PHLX Semiconductor index (`.RUT.US` / `.SOX.US` have returned empty repeatedly; IWM / SMH ETF prices are used instead, and **ETFs are not equivalent to indices**), VIX term structure, intraday HY OAS, ICI weekly ETF flows.
9. **Chart basis:** the three chart filenames use the run date (2026-11-10), while the in-chart titles use the data cut-off (2026-11-09), consistent with the body's "market data through 11-09" — not a date error. The trend chart was visually verified (title 2026-11-09, last value 32 / fear). The radar and sub-trend charts are generated from the same JSON by the same script, but the vision endpoint returned 502 twice this run, so **their visual check was not completed**; their values can be matched item by item against the tables above (the script reads `score` and raw series directly, with no hard-coded values).
10. **No external attribution:** no news or broker commentary was searched or cited to explain the moves; all causal statements are limited to "these data appeared at the same time".
