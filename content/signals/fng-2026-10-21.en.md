## Possible explanations for the divergences, and counter-evidence

**Divergence one: the composite "left extreme fear", but the VIX sub-score alone did it.**

Facts: composite 20.80 → 27.2 (+6.40 or +4.09 depending on the basis used above), which is by construction the mean of seven items; the VIX sub-score contributes +31.25 / 7 = **+4.46 points**, while the other six contribute **−0.37 points** combined. Excluding VIX, the six-item mean is 23.4, **below** yesterday's 23.83. Meanwhile the raw VIX only fell from 18.44 to 17.31.

Explanation (preferred): the VIX sub-score captured at 18:09 ET yesterday (18.75) was most likely an **incomplete update**, and today's 50.0 is the reading consistent with VIX's own 54.2nd percentile — using the rough rule "raw percentile ≈ standardized score", 17.31 (54.2nd) mapping to 50 is coherent, whereas 18.44 (roughly the 70th percentile yesterday) mapping to 18.75 is not. If that explanation holds, **today's "sentiment improvement" is a data revision, not a market change**.

Counter-evidence and the observation window: if tomorrow's VIX sub-score **stays in the 45–55** range with VIX between 17 and 18, and the composite holds above 25, then 50 is a stable reading and at least part of today's rebound reflects real pricing (the volatility risk premium genuinely easing). Conversely, if the sub-score jumps again tomorrow (say back toward 20) with little VIX movement, the sub-score's instability is confirmed and today's "exit from extreme fear" must be downgraded to noise carrying no information. **Until a second stable observation exists, the rating upgrade should not be treated as evidence of sentiment repair.**

**Divergence two: sub-scores scream extreme fear, but the day's tape was broad.**

Facts: breadth at the 6.4th percentile and strength at the 5.2nd percentile (both Extreme Fear), while on 10-20 IWM +1.66%, RSP +1.15% and DJI +1.02% clearly beat SPY +0.34%, QQQ −0.10% and NDX −0.13%.

Explanation: CNN's breadth and strength sub-items measure **structural levels** (how many stocks are near 52-week highs; the level of the breadth gauge), not the day's move. An index at its own 89.6th percentile alongside internal participation in the 5th–6th percentile means "a high index carried by a handful of stocks while most sit far from their highs". A single day of small-cap and equal-weight outperformance can be the **start of a marginal repair** of that narrow structure, or simply mean reversion.

Counter-evidence: if IWM/RSP keep outperforming for several sessions while the breadth gauge continues to rise (now 357.30, still far below the 1,023.91 median), "participation repair" is confirmed; if the index makes new highs while the breadth gauge stalls or falls, the narrow-leadership character is confirmed. **Note that this report does not infer small- versus large-cap direction from the breadth/strength readings — the size conclusion here comes from actual ETF closes.**

**Divergence three: equities bounced, credit did not follow.**

Facts: SPY +0.34% and DJI +1.02% while HYG was **flat (0.00%)** and JNK **−0.02%**; FRED HY OAS at 3.29% (10-19) is still 18bp above 10-09's 3.11%, and IG OAS at 0.87% is 5bp above 10-08's 0.82%.

Explanation: a single day of equity/credit divergence is too small a sample to characterise; credit spreads are a **level-plus-trend** variable, and high-yield prices also carry duration and risk-free-rate exposure (10Y fell 3bp on the day, a small positive for HYG that it did not capture — consistent with spread widening offsetting the rate move). The fair reading is that credit neither confirmed nor denied the one-day equity bounce.

Counter-evidence: if HY OAS falls below **3.10%** (the 10-02 level) while equities keep rising, the credit worry is resolved; if HY OAS breaks above **3.50%** for two consecutive days while equities remain elevated, the equity/credit gap is widening and the "credit leads" rule should downgrade the risk-asset conclusion.

**Divergence four: volatility pricing eased, hedging cost still being paid.**

Facts: VIX 18.44 → 17.31 (−6.13%), yet the 5-day put/call average went 0.8305 → 0.8361 (+0.67%, 86.9th percentile) and has risen for six consecutive records since 10-12 (0.7462).

Explanation: falling implied volatility and rising option hedging are not contradictory — event risk passing lowers VIX, while hedging demand lags. The combination says only that "volatility is priced easier while protection remains expensive"; it does not say risk has passed. VIX is still 10.3% above its 50-day mean.

Counter-evidence: VIX below 17 **and** put/call back below 0.76 (near the one-year median of 0.7312) would constitute a real retreat in hedging demand; VIX alone is a one-legged improvement.

**What this report explicitly does not infer:** put/call or VIX moves are not used to identify "smart money" direction; breadth/strength readings are not used to infer size rotation (actual ETF data is used this run); record nominal margin debt or ETF inflows are not used to call a crisis or a late-cycle top; no uncalibrated "red-light count" or crisis probability is produced.

## Data limitations

1. **The daily change cannot be stated as a single number.** The `previous_close` basis gives +4.09 and the historical-series basis +6.40; the 2.30 gap comes from CNN revising the 10-19 record (live snapshot 23.1071 → series 20.8044), with no disclosed reason. Both bases agree on direction (up), so only a range is given. The `previous_close` field can be reproduced exactly from the previous report's seven sub-score mean (161.75/7 = 23.1071), showing it is "the mean of the previous snapshot's sub-scores", not a historical series record.
2. **Today's 27.2 is provisional.** It is likewise subject to next-day revision (10-16 was revised +4.13 and 10-19 −2.30 in the past week), so every magnitude in this report is phrased as a range or a snapshot value.
3. **The VIX sub-score cannot be used to argue sentiment improvement.** Three snapshots (3.97 / 18.75 / 50.0) against raw VIX (16.46 / 18.44 / 17.31) show no monotonic relationship, and CNN does not publish its normalization method. The item has been removed from the "improvement evidence" set and listed only as a pending verification.
4. **Sub-indicator sampling times are not uniform:** momentum 16:45 ET, breadth/strength 18:09 ET, put/call 16:14 ET, VIX 16:15 ET, junk 16:10 ET, **safe-haven 15:59 ET (one minute before the close)**. The composite merges items sampled at different times, so its "daily change" mixes observation windows; safe-haven demand is the most exposed to a pre-close print.
5. **Unverified internal contradictions:** the per-point `rating` attached inside three sub-item series conflicts with the sub-item's own rating — momentum per-point extreme greed versus its own fear; breadth per-point extreme greed versus its own extreme fear; junk per-point extreme fear versus its own fear; VIX per-point extreme fear versus its own neutral. CNN explains nothing; none of this is used interpretively.
6. **Unverified units:** the raw units for breadth 357.30, strength −8.1010 and safe-haven 0.1184 cannot be confirmed from the response body or CNN's public documentation; only relative position and percentile are used.
7. **CNN's junk spread and FRED HY OAS are not comparable:** 1.2245% (CNN, 10-20 16:10 ET snapshot) versus 3.29% (FRED `BAMLH0A0HYM2`, 10-19) differ in index composition, convention and date. They are reported separately; **no subtraction and no "divergence" narrative are constructed from them**.
8. **Two defects in `crisis_signals.py` were worked around, not silently accepted:** (a) margin debt, IPO and ETF flows are hard-coded constants ($1.304T / ~73 deals / $856bn) that do not match the sources, so they are **marked unusable** and previously fetched source values (with dates) are carried instead; (b) the script prints no as-of date for its rate figures, and its `spread_10y2y` (0.33) is from a **different day** than the `t10y`/`t2y` printed on the same screen (5.15/4.77, i.e. 10-19 values) — subtracting those directly would produce a wrong 0.38.
