## Divergences: possible explanations and counter-evidence

### Divergence 1: the score rose +2.94 while all three price/participation components deteriorated

**Observation:** the S&P 500 −0.35%, VIX +6.92%, breadth −12.87, strength −0.24, momentum −26.98 points — and the composite rose on the same day.

**Possible explanation:** the weighting structure. The composite is an equal-weighted average of seven components, and three of them — put/call, safe-haven demand, junk bond demand — **do not take that day's stock price as an input**. All three moved toward "greed" on 11-12: PCR 0.836 → 0.800 (calls taking a larger share of options activity), the safe-haven spread −2.20 → −0.57 (20-day stock-versus-bond relative return improving), and the junk spread 1.210 → 1.196 (tightening further).

**Counter-evidence and boundaries:** (a) **a falling PCR on a down day could be either contrarian call buying or put selling for premium; a ratio alone cannot distinguish them** — CNN's put/call carries no directional volume information, and reading it as "smart money buying the dip" is over-interpretation (this report does not make that reading); (b) the safe-haven improvement has a mechanical component (see divergence 4); (c) **if these three components give back their gains within three sessions, the score falls back to around 36** — meaning this +2.94 is **reversible and lacks price confirmation**; (d) the concern is not "the score went up" but "the score went up while participation sits at one-year lows", and those two together mean **the disagreement is widening, not closing**.

### Divergence 2: momentum scores 16.0 (extreme fear) while the S&P 500 level is at the 74.5th percentile and the index sits exactly on its 125-day average

**Observation:** two facts coexist inside one component — the S&P 500 close is at the **74.5th percentile** of the one-year sample (the price level is still high), while its deviation from the 125-day average is only **−0.008%** (7,592.40 versus 7,593.02), and the score is just 16.0.

**Possible explanation:** CNN's momentum is a standardised measure of how far the S&P sits from its 125-day average. Ranking the same-day `S&P 500 / 125-day average` ratio myself puts it at the **12.0th percentile** of 251 comparable days — **directionally consistent with 16.0 and close in level**, so the low score is not a data error: **for most of the past year the index traded further above its average**, so sitting on the line is itself the low end of the distribution.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) a low score **is not a downside risk measure**; it gauges weakness relative to trend position, not future drawdown; (b) on 11-11 the ratio was back to +0.37% and the score was still only 17, showing **the score is far less elastic than price** — using score changes to call turning points lags systematically; (c) if the S&P falls another 3% this component drops straight back into deep extreme fear (on 11-05, with the ratio at −3.10%, the score was also only 17.0), so **the score is close to saturated in the low band and losing discriminating power**.

### Divergence 3: CNN's junk spread is at the 6th percentile (tightest), while FRED HY OAS is at the 81st percentile of three years

**Observation:** CNN's junk bond raw spread is 1.1960% (**6.0th percentile** of the one-year sample, i.e. 94% of days were wider), score 58.0 (greed); at the same moment FRED HY OAS is **3.30%**, at the **81.1st percentile** of three years.

**Possible explanation:** the two series are defined differently — CNN's own measure differs from ICE BofA HY OAS in index composition, rating-bucket weights, CCC inclusion and in whether it is an OAS or an effective yield. **The levels differ by 2.1 percentage points.** They measure different things and are not two descriptions of one fact, so **this report never puts them in the same table**.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) even on the FRED definition, HY OAS has widened from the 2026 low of 2.60% to 3.48% on 11-05, now 3.30% — **the direction is wider within the year, not "credit is fine"**; (b) more importantly, **all of these numbers were formed before the 11-12 bond selloff**, so using 11-11 spreads to underwrite 11-12 equity prices is using yesterday's data to explain today; (c) the junk score of 58.0 (greed) against a raw value in the tightest 6th percentile of the year — **the score and the raw percentile point the same way here (tighter means greedier) but the magnitudes do not match**, which suggests the standardisation baseline is also compressing this component.

### Divergence 4: safe-haven demand jumped from 17.4 to 29.2, and half of that came from the bond leg falling

**Observation:** the safe-haven raw value went −2.2003 → −0.5656, an improvement of 1.63 percentage points and +11.8 score points, the largest single contribution this run.

**Possible explanation and self-computed check:** CNN's component measures **the relative return of stocks versus bonds** (roughly a 20-session window). Using same-source daily data, I computed a 20-day return spread as a proxy:

| Date | S&P 500 20-day return | TLT 20-day return | Spread |
|------|----------------------|-------------------|--------|
| 11-06 | −5.56% | +5.05% | −10.62 |
| 11-10 | −3.65% | +3.20% | −6.85 |
| 11-11 | −1.29% | +2.57% | −3.86 |
| 11-12 | **−1.96%** | **−0.31%** | **−1.65** |

Of the spread's improvement (−3.86 → −1.65), **the equity leg contributed −0.67pp (it got worse) and the bond leg contributed +2.88pp**. In other words: **"safe-haven demand falling" is mostly bonds no longer delivering a positive 20-day return, not stocks getting stronger.** On 11-12 itself TLT fell 1.48%, LQD 0.88% and HYG 0.48%, consistent with the 10Y +12bp.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) my proxy and CNN's raw value point the same way but **my magnitude is about half** (−1.65 versus −0.57), indicating differences in window, bond instrument and total-return treatment, so it is **directional support only, not an equivalent check**; (b) this component's "improvement" reverses as soon as bonds stabilise — **it measures relative performance, not absolute risk appetite**; (c) if the component reverted to yesterday's 17.4, the composite would drop from 39.94 to **38.2** — this one line accounts for about 1.7 points of the net change this run, so **the "sentiment back to the middle" conclusion is highly sensitive to its definition**.

## Data limitations

1. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series; the gap this run is 1.03.** The field reads 36.9714 (= the 11-11 seven-score sum of 258.8 ÷ 7) while the revised 11-11 in the series reads **35.9429** (= 251.6 ÷ 7). The field equals the seven-score average of the **last published snapshot**, making it "last published value" rather than "last trading day recomputed". **This report shows both (+2.97 / +4.00) and uses neither alone to announce a move.**

2. **The composite and the displayed sub-scores differ by 0.2.** The seven displayed values sum to 279.4 (mean 39.91) while the official composite of 39.9429 implies 279.6. **The difference comes from sub-scores being shown to one decimal, not from an addition error**, but it is stated because anyone re-adding the table will see it.

3. **Snapshot times are not aligned, and one is taken before the close.** Composite and strength/breadth 22:13:39Z (17:13 EST); momentum 21:58:23Z (16:58 EST); put/call 21:33:33Z; VIX 21:15:01Z; junk bonds 21:10:00Z; **safe-haven demand 20:59:59Z (15:59:59 EST, one second before the close)**. That component may exclude closing-auction prices and its correspondence to closing prices is **unverified**. The span is 74 minutes.

4. **CNN revises history, and this run's revision sits in the standardisation layer.** Evidence: 11-11 36.9714 → 35.9429 (**−1.03** with zero change across 251 trading days of nine sub-series), 11-10 +3.91, 11-09 −0.20, 11-06 +2.27, 11-04 +6.89. **The revision size (up to +6.89) exceeds this run's true daily change (+2.94), which is the indicator's biggest methodological weakness.**

5. **All three `previous_*` fields verify this run.** `previous_1_week` 19.9911 = the 11-05 series value; `previous_1_month` 45.2286 = the 10-12 value (exactly one month); `previous_1_year` 34.5714 = the first series point (2025-11-12). All three match digit for digit.

6. **The units of sub-indicator raw values are inferred from the interface.** Momentum (the S&P level) and VIX are independently verified via Longbridge; the absolute conventions for strength, breadth, junk spread and the safe-haven spread (denominator, window, annualisation) are not documented, so **they are usable only for direction and magnitude, never for equivalence with external indicators**. For the safe-haven spread I used an S&P-minus-TLT 20-day spread as a proxy; direction agrees but my magnitude is about half (−1.65 versus −0.57), so **this is not an equivalent verification**.

7. **Credit is missing 11-10 and has no 11-12.** A FRED range query confirms `BAMLH0A0HYM2` / `BAMLC0A0CM` have no observation on 11-10 while Treasury yields do have one that day — **so the gap is not caused by the holiday, and the cause is unexplained**. Equally, 11-11 has an OAS observation even though the bond market was closed, and the basis of that value **cannot be confirmed**. Missing cells are marked "not published / no observation"; they are never treated as zero or filled with the prior value.

8. **Rates have a single as-of date and skip 11-11.** The 11-12 curve comes from the Treasury CSV (one day ahead of FRED `DGS*`, which stops at 11-10). `crisis_signals.py`'s `3_yield_curve` still mixes periods (its output pairs 10Y 4.98 = 11-10 with a spread of 0.24 = 11-12), so **this report recomputes from same-day, same-source figures; the script is unmodified**.

9. **CPI is self-computed, not taken from the release page.** `bls.gov` is blocked in this environment, so October CPI was derived by differencing the FRED seasonally adjusted series (`CPIAUCSL` 337.476, `CPILFESL` 339.900): +0.35% and +0.30% m/m, 5.72% and 3.75% three-month annualised. **This matches the official m/m SA convention as I compute it, but it has not been checked against the BLS release text.**

10. **Hike expectations and commodity prices are media or cross-session data.** December hike odds, the 2027 path and Goldman's forecast come from CNBC/Reuters citing CME FedWatch (second-hand; the underlying page was not read); gold, silver and oil come from Sina `hf_*` snapshots (cross-session, not settlement prices). **These explain direction only and are not tradable prices.**

11. **The causal story around NVDA is unconfirmed.** The 8-K / EX-99.1 establishes four facts — resignation effective immediately, Kress as interim CEO, Jones as Chair, and the 11-17 report proceeding as scheduled — and **discloses no reason for the resignation**. Media connect it to earlier reporting on personal matters; **that causal chain is not confirmed by any primary document, and this report does not use it to explain the price.**

12. **Chart conventions and verification status.** The three chart files are named for the **run date (2026-11-13)** while their in-chart titles and data reflect the **market data cutoff (2026-11-12)**, consistent with the text — not a date error. **The trend chart was visually verified:** the title reads "CNN Fear & Greed Index — 2026-11-12" and the final label reads "40 fear", matching 39.94 / fear. **The radar chart was visually verified:** the centre reads 40 / Fear and the seven axes sit where the scores imply (the three low scores on the inner rings, junk bonds furthest out). **The sub-indicator trend panels were visually verified:** the title reads "Fear & Greed Sub-Indicators — Historical Trends" and the seven end-point labels read 7,592.4 / 0.8 / −0.6 / 18.7 / 642.2 / −2.9 / 1.2, matching the table digit for digit; however it colours each point by that point's `rating` (momentum is labelled extreme greed), which conflicts with the score **7 of 7** this run, so **the dot colours disagree with the text ratings — the table is authoritative**.

13. **Data not obtained.** VIX term structure, put/call by market segment (index/single-stock/ETF), 11-12 HY/IG OAS, October FINRA margin debt, ICI weekly ETF flows, 11-12 NYSE advance/decline counts, futures positioning. These gaps mean **"who is buying this resilience" cannot be answered this run; only "what prices changed, by how much, and on what volume" can**.
