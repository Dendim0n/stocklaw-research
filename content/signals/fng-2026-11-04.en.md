## Divergences, Possible Explanations, and Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: the index bounced +0.36% while Put/Call rose for a third session to 0.8278.**

- Facts: 11-03 S&P 500 7,568.92 (**+0.36%**), VIX 21.15 → 20.83 (**−1.5%**), but Put/Call 0.8126 → **0.8278**, the highest since 10-20 and the 85.7th percentile of the year.
- Explanation: one reading is that the bounce was **used to buy protection** — with the index back near its moving average, hedges are relatively cheap to add, so institutions bought puts rather than chased. A second reading is that PCR's smoothing window (CNN does not disclose it) still contains the panic sessions of 10-30 through 11-02 and does not yet reflect the calmer 11-03 tape. The report cannot distinguish between the two.
- Counter-evidence and limits: (1) a +0.0153 move is noise-scale (the 20-period range is 0.7448–0.8344; today sits near the upper end but has not broken out); (2) a rising PCR does **not** prove "smart money is bearish" — it only shows puts are a larger share of volume, which can also come from dealer hedging or structured products; (3) if PCR falls on 11-04 while the index keeps rising, this was event-driven hedging rather than a trend.

**Divergence 2: CNN's junk-bond raw value is the highest since late June while FRED HY OAS remains near one-year lows.**

- Facts: CNN junk raw 1.2428 → **1.3720 (+12.9bp)**, the 91.6th percentile of the year and the highest since 2026-06-30; meanwhile FRED HY OAS is **3.43% on 11-02 (−1bp)** with 11-03 unpublished, and IG OAS is flat at 0.94%.
- Explanation: CNN does not disclose this component's definition and its level runs roughly 2 percentage points below FRED HY OAS (possibly a different index or benchmark), so **the two cannot be used to disprove each other**. If official HY OAS widens in step on 11-03, credit really did reprice; if official data is unchanged, the jump is more likely a data-timing artefact on CNN's side.
- Counter-evidence: (1) official HY actually **narrowed** 1bp on 11-02, out of step with CNN's 11-03 jump; (2) this raw series is noisy day to day (last 10 sessions ranged 1.2045–1.3720), and although +0.1291 ranks fourth in a year, the three larger jumps were all followed by pullbacks; (3) at 1.3720 it is still 14.4bp below the one-year high of 1.5160 (2025-11-04) — not extreme.

**Divergence 3: breadth has risen 19 straight sessions while strength has barely moved.**

- Facts: breadth 557.32 → **571.89**, the 12.7th percentile of the year; strength −4.2201 → **−4.2266**, the 11.6th percentile, still negative.
- Explanation: breadth counts advancing names; strength measures the share of stocks near 52-week highs. Both are in low territory but move in different directions, which means **more stocks are rising, but not more stocks are near highs**. This is a repair off the very low 10-07 base, not a broadening.
- Counter-evidence: (1) breadth at 571.89 is only 57% of the one-year median of 1,002.4 and still 284 points below the 20th percentile (856.28); (2) negative strength means more stocks are making new lows than new highs, which directly contradicts "improving participation"; (3) if breadth keeps rising while strength stays non-positive, the likelier story is a count-based repair from mega-cap stabilisation, not money moving down the cap scale.

**Divergence 4: momentum score is only 14.20 while the S&P 500 is just 3.20% from its one-year high.**

- Facts: close 7,568.92, **−3.20%** from the 10-06 high of 7,818.93, raw at the 72.5th percentile; but **17.71 points (−0.233%)** below the 125-day MA of 7,586.63 for a third consecutive session.
- Explanation: CNN's momentum component is **threshold-based** — it measures price relative to the 125-day MA, and once price crosses below, the score collapses into the low band. The 11-03 bounce narrowed the gap from −0.575% to −0.233% while the score moved only 13.20 → 14.20, showing the score is sensitive to **whether** price is above the line, not to **how far**.
- Counter-evidence: (1) the average itself is rising (7,582.74 on 10-30 → 7,585.00 on 11-02 → **7,586.63** on 11-03), so the level to reclaim climbs about 1.6 points a day; (2) a single close above 7,586.63 would trigger a jump in score (the prior report documented the reverse jump, 35.4 → 14.60), so 14.20 does not mean "it fell a lot", only "it is below the line"; (3) −2.38% over 60 sessions and −3.20% over 20 are not extreme declines.

**Shared constraint:** all four divergences point to the same conclusion — **11-03 was a bounce, not a risk-appetite recovery.** The index up and VIX down are price-level repairs; rising hedging demand, CNN's junk-bond jump and safe-haven demand stuck at the year's 1.6th percentile are position- and credit-level continuations. Nothing supports "a crash is coming" (HY 3.43%, IG 0.94%, a positively sloped curve, the index 3.20% off its high), and nothing supports "a bottom is in" (breadth at the 12.7th percentile, strength negative, 15 consecutive fear sessions, three consecutive closes below the moving average).

**Definition discipline:**

- **10Y-2Y uses same-source, same-day values only.** The primary value is Treasury **11-03**: 4.95 − 4.61 = **+0.34**, which matches FRED `T10Y2Y` at 0.34 on 11-03. **The script defect persists:** `crisis_signals.py` now reports `spread_10y2y` for **11-03** (T10Y2Y updates faster) while `t10y/t2y/t30y` in the same record are still **11-02** values of 4.91/4.59/5.28 — a cross-date mix, and 4.91 − 4.59 = 0.32 does not equal the reported 0.34. That combination is not used here.
- **CNN's high-yield indicator and FRED HY OAS are kept separate.** They differ by about 2.06 percentage points, CNN's raw definition is undisclosed, and neither can be converted into or used to disprove the other; they appear on separate rows and are never merged into one comparison.
- **Margin debt, ETF flows and IPOs are monthly/annual and were not re-scraped.** Record nominal margin debt or record ETF inflows **do not by themselves signal a crisis**; the three are excluded from same-day risk readings and serve only as background on how sensitive the market is to liquidity.
- **No red-light counts or crisis probabilities.** The script's `summary` counts hard-coded constants (margin debt "$1.304T @ 2026-04", IPOs "~73", ETFs "$856bn") into its green/yellow/red tally, so the count carries no calibration and is not cited here.

**Cross-check conclusion:** the three legs **do not agree**. The characterisation is therefore: **a one-day bounce without credit confirmation, sentiment still in extreme fear, risk neither escalated nor eased — in a confirmation window awaiting 11-03 credit data.**

## Data Limitations

1. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series by 3.34, and the source of the conflict is identifiable.** The field reads 22.7286 (the 11-02 close published last period) while the re-fetched series reads 19.3917 for the same date, a gap of 3.3369. All seven sub-indicator raw values for 11-02 are **identical** across the two fetches; only the score side was revised, showing CNN adjusted its normalisation between snapshots. Both −0.30 (series) and −3.64 (field) are shown; only the downward direction is confirmed.
2. **Sub-indicator scores are revised retroactively; the junk-bond day change is unusable.** From the 3.34 composite revision, 11-02's junk-bond score must have fallen from the **36.40** recorded yesterday to about **13.0** on the identical raw value of 1.2428. The table's 36.40 → 2.00 (−34.40) is therefore **not a market move**; only the raw 1.2428 → 1.3720 is verifiable. The same revision shows how sensitive the composite's absolute level is to one component's normalisation: with junk at the implied 13.0, today's composite would be **20.66**; with the current 2.00 it is **19.09**; excluding the component entirely, the other six average **21.94**. Cross-report comparisons of the level should carry this range.
3. **`crisis_signals.py` still has three hard-coded constants and one cross-date mix.** Margin debt is fixed at "$1.304T (Apr 2026)", IPOs at "~73", ETFs at "$856bn" (line 146 only prints "searching" and makes no network request); `spread_10y2y` is 11-03 while `t10y/t2y/t30y` are 11-02. This report substitutes the Treasury 11-03 curve for the rates fields, carries over the prior FINRA re-scrape with an "not updated" label, labels ETF/IPO as carried over, and does not cite the red-light count. **The script was not modified.**
4. **Credit data lags one trading day.** HY/IG OAS and 10Y TIPS are 11-02; 11-03 is unpublished. Whether the 11-03 bounce coincided with credit repair or deterioration **cannot be answered this run**.
5. **Six sub-indicators have no second source.** Breadth, strength, Put/Call, junk bond and safe haven exist only on CNN; VIX and the S&P 500 were checked digit-for-digit against yfinance, and the 125-day MA was checked against both CNN's `sp125` and an independent calculation. Tencent's `usVIX` has been stale for a long time and was not used.
6. **The normalisation window is undisclosed.** The one-year percentiles in the table are positions within the 251 observations and are not invertible into score (momentum sits at the 72.5th percentile of raw values but scores 14.20; junk bond at the 91.6th percentile scores 2.00).
7. **Per-point `rating` values are internally contradictory and were not used.** Momentum at 7,568.92 tagged extreme greed, breadth at 571.89 tagged extreme greed, strength at −4.2266 tagged extreme fear — all conflicting with their scores. Ratings here bind only to score.
8. **Chart verification coverage.** All three charts are generated from the same JSON written at 06:30 CST; the script titles them with the last data point's date, so the **charts are titled 2026-11-03 while the file names carry the run date 2026-11-04** — by design. The trend chart was opened and read: title 2026-11-03, endpoint label **19 Extreme Fear**, consistent with the text. The radar and sub-trend charts were confirmed only as generated from the same JSON, not inspected cell by cell.
9. **No intraday or after-hours data.** The composite is an after-close snapshot for 11-03; 11-03 after-hours and 11-04 Asian-session index futures and headlines are not included.
10. **Monthly/annual data is a different frequency.** Margin debt (2026-09-30), ETF flows (through 2026-09-30) and IPOs (YTD) cannot support same-day judgements; FINRA's October data is normally released in the third week of November.
11. **No causal attribution.** No media narrative was used to explain the 11-03 bounce; the report describes structure using verifiable price, rate and sentiment data only and makes no causal claim about why the market rose or fell.
