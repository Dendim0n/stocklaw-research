## Divergences: plausible explanations and counter-evidence

**Divergence 1: volatility is back to neutral, the other fear components are not.**

- Facts: VIX 16.61 puts that component at **50.00 (neutral)**; on the same day Put/Call is **0.8317 (17.60, extreme fear, 86.1st percentile)**, safe-haven demand **−3.3643 (9.80, 3.2nd percentile)** and junk bond **2.6 (extreme fear)**. The seven scores span 2.60 to 50.00 — a **47.4-point** range, standard deviation **16.89** — so 27.29 is an average of disagreeing signals.
- Explanations: three candidates. (a) VIX is a **forward-looking options price**, whereas Put/Call and relative returns are **lagging flow and performance measures**, so it turns first; (b) the Put/Call and safe-haven windows still contain the 10-28 to 11-02 decline and lag mechanically by one to three weeks; (c) the Put/Call rise reflects tail hedging (skew) rather than at-the-money volatility expectations — we have no skew data and cannot distinguish these.
- Counter-evidence and limits: (i) if (a) holds, Put/Call should fall below 0.78 and safe haven turn positive within two or three sessions, otherwise "neutral VIX" is just technical mean reversion; (ii) the Put/Call move is +0.0039, noise-level, so "Put/Call got worse" is a fragile statement; (iii) neither PCR nor VIX can identify "smart money" direction — a share of flow describes structure, not who is trading.

**Divergence 2: the index reclaimed its average while breadth and strength sit in the year's 13th percentile.**

- Facts: the S&P 500 is 1.31% above its 125-day average and only −1.67% from its one-year high, while breadth is 605.70 (13.1st percentile) and strength −3.5751 (net new-high share negative, 13.1st percentile). Breadth has risen (571.89 → 605.70) but is still only **60.7%** of the one-year median of **997.93**.
- Explanation: the advance is carried by heavyweights and participation has not broadened; breadth is recovering from a very low base set on 10-07, which is "more stocks stopped falling" rather than "money moving down the cap scale". This run breadth rose 5.91% while strength rose 0.65% — an order of magnitude apart, consistent with that reading.
- Counter-evidence: (i) the joint low percentile could be a **composition** artefact (new listings or small-cap weighting), and CNN does not disclose constituents; (ii) one session says nothing about broadening — breadth needs three consecutive sessions above the 20th percentile; (iii) if the index stalls while breadth keeps rising, it is catch-up, not a top.

**Divergence 3: the composite still says "fear" while risk assets rose across the board on the same day.**

- Facts: 11-04 SPY **+1.65%**, IWM **+1.51%**, HYG **+0.70%**, LQD **+0.75%**, TLT **+0.80%**, with the 10Y yield down 5bp to 4.90%.
- Explanation: this is "lower rates plus better risk appetite" — stocks and bonds rising together means the market priced a **lower discount rate**, not a flight to safety. The composite still reads 27.29 because four of its seven components (Put/Call, safe haven, junk bond, strength) use **trailing windows** that have not yet absorbed 11-04.
- Counter-evidence: (i) a joint rally can also be liquidity-expectation driven; if yields rebound and equities hold, the move was multiple expansion, not earnings; (ii) one directionally consistent session is not a trend — HYG is still **−0.09%** and SPY **−0.71%** over five sessions; (iii) same-direction moves cannot establish a bottom, because breadth and strength have not confirmed.

**Divergence 4: CNN's junk-bond component reports extreme fear while HY OAS remains historically tight.**

- Facts: CNN junk bond score **2.6** (raw 1.3650, 87.3rd percentile of its own year); FRED HY OAS **3.40%** (11-03) at the **97.6th percentile** of the year (one-year range 2.60–3.46) but the **85.9th percentile** of five years (5-year range 2.59–4.61), still well below its long-run average.
- Explanation: the two agree in **direction** (both near the top of their one-year ranges) but cannot be converted into each other — CNN's 1.3650 differs from HY OAS 3.40% by about 2.04 percentage points because the reference or the universe differs. Treat them as **two independent pieces of evidence, not one indicator**.
- Counter-evidence: (i) official HY OAS has printed 3.44 → 3.43 → 3.40 over three sessions, i.e. it is **tightening**, which is not fully consistent with "extreme fear" on the CNN component; (ii) 3.40% is only the 85.9th percentile over five years and still tight in absolute terms, so "credit crisis" is unsupported; (iii) a single −3bp day is noise — three consecutive days of widening would be required.

## Data limitations

1. **The historical series stores only the composite, not that day's seven component scores.** The 11-03 revision can be attributed arithmetically to the VIX component (the difference equals the VIX change ÷ 7 to four decimals), but not to which side rewrote it; any finer attribution would be speculation.
2. **`previous_close` conflicts with the end of the historical series by 4.4513.** `previous_close` = 19.0915 (the 11-03 value published last run) while the series shows 23.5429. Both point up, but the magnitudes differ twofold (+8.19 versus +3.74); this report uses the two consecutively published values (19.09 → 27.29) for the daily change and says so explicitly.
3. **11-04 carries two timestamps (00:00Z and 22:09:42Z) with identical values.** Keeping the latest per day does not change any calculation; the chart plots raw points, so the last point is a duplicate.
4. **Official credit and nominal-yield data for 11-04 are not yet published.** FRED `DGS10` `DGS2` `BAMLH0A0HYM2` `BAMLC0A0CM` all end on 11-03, while `T10Y2Y` and `T10YIE` in the same batch already reach 11-04 — identically named "latest" values span different days, so every cut-off is labelled and nothing was mixed.
5. **CNN's raw sub-indicator units are undisclosed.** Junk bond 1.3650 is not HY OAS 3.40%; breadth 605.6993 is dimensionless; the basis for strength −3.5751 (whether it includes a low-price condition, and its universe) is not published. All three can only be compared against themselves and cannot be substituted for official data.
6. **The per-point `rating` field is mechanically mismatched (see above) and unused.** Ratings are taken from the top-level `score` only.
7. **Tencent `usVIX` conflicts with two other sources** (21.67 / 0.00% change versus 16.61 from the CBOE-based sources) and is treated as stale; VIX is confirmed by both yfinance `^VIX` and CNN.
8. **Leverage and flow data are monthly/annual and were not re-pulled** (FINRA through 09-30, ETFGI through 09-30, IPOs YTD); they cannot be treated as same-day readings, and FINRA's October data is normally released in the third week of November.
9. **The charts were not verified at the image level.** All three were generated (trend 1796×732, radar 944×709, sub-trends 2081×1521) from the same `fng_data.json` with no resampling; their in-image title date is the data date **2026-11-04** while the filenames use the run date **2026-11-05** (the script's `date_tag` defaults to today). Visual review was not completed because the image service returned 502 repeatedly, so generation success and identical source data are confirmed, but the rendering of each annotation is not.
10. **No red-light total and no crisis probability is published.** The thresholds are absolute bands set inside the script (HY 3–5% is tagged "watch") and are not calibrated to historical percentiles; the same 3.40% is the 97.6th percentile of one year, the 85.9th of five years, and still tight against the long-run average — any single "red light" label would mislead.
