## Divergences: Possible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: equities fell 4.36%, credit barely moved.**
- Fact: HY OAS **3.32% (11-04)**, **8bp tighter** than 11-03's 3.40%; IG OAS 0.91% (from 0.93%). CNN's junk sub-index improved in step (score 2.6 → 34.4).
- Explanation A (higher confidence): this is a **duration/valuation shock, not a credit event.** Same-day 2Y −12bp and 10Y −6bp: Treasuries rallied while equities sold off — the classic signature of repricing growth and AI-capex expectations, with no repricing of default risk.
- Explanation B: **credit data lags.** HY OAS only runs through 11-04; the 11-05 spread is published later. Credit typically lags equities by 1–3 sessions in fast moves.
- Counter-evidence (against A): although the spread tightened on the day, it is **18bp wider than 10-28's 3.14%** and sits at the 96.5th percentile of the past year — credit had already begun to widen mildly before the crash, so there was some warning.
- Counter-evidence (against B): if this were purely a lag artifact, CNN's junk sub-index — updated intraday for the same date — should not have improved in the same direction. It did, implying the underlying yield-curve combination genuinely moved toward a narrower spread.
- **Conclusion: all that can be confirmed is that "credit had not confirmed the risk as of 11-04," not that "credit will not follow."** The 11-05 / 11-06 HY OAS prints are the single most important pending data point.

**Divergence 2: the index fell 4.36% yet stock price strength improved by +0.2773.**
- Fact: strength raw −3.5751 → −3.2979, score 41.8 (yesterday 39.2, slightly up).
- Possible explanation: the sub-index is built on the **net of 52-week highs and lows**, and both numerator and denominator are slow variables. A single 4.4% down day does not push constituents into "new low" territory, which requires breaking a 52-week low rather than merely falling on the day.
- Counter: even if the mechanism holds, "the index plunged while strength improved" is an internal contradiction. **The constituent calculation was not obtained for this run, so "slow variable" cannot be distinguished from "window mismatch."** It is recorded as an unverified contradiction and is not used for any conclusion.
- Similar case: breadth raw fell (−3.77) while its score stayed exactly 45.4, again showing these sub-indices are band-mapped rather than linear.

**Divergence 3: VIX at the 98.4th percentile of the year while breadth is only at the 13.1st.**
- Fact: VIX 28.18 (one-year range 13.47–31.05); breadth raw 601.93 at the 13.1st percentile.
- Explanation: VIX prices **index-level implied volatility** (dominated by mega-cap options), while breadth measures the **cash-market participation base**. They need not move together; desynchronization here means selling was concentrated in index weights and the AI complex rather than a blanket liquidation.
- **Counter and limit: breadth is not used to infer a large-cap/small-cap split.** Measured directly this run: IWM **−4.59%**, a larger fall than the SPX (−4.36%) and a smaller one than the NDX (−6.36%). Small caps were sold too, just in the middle of the range. **Index moves and breadth are two different measures and cannot substitute for each other.**

**Divergence 4: how much of the 19.88 "panic" reflects actual position changes?**
- Fact: four of seven sub-indices (safe haven, VIX, momentum, put/call) sum to 17.54 of 19.88 = **88%**; VIX and put/call are **price-type** measures (the cost of protection), not volume or flow measures.
- Implication: the reading mostly reflects "insurance got expensive," not "someone is selling size." This is a methodological limitation of the CNN index itself — it has no flow component.
- Counter: the SPX closing at the session low and volume expanding (Longbridge 11-05: 5.05bn shares vs 4.58bn the prior day, +10%) do confirm real selling pressure, not just an options-pricing artifact.

## Observable Conditions for Escalation vs. Easing

No point forecasts — only "what counts as a change and what counts as noise." Every condition below is checkable on the day.

**Escalation (2+ conditions required, to avoid single-indicator false alarms)**

1. **HY OAS (11-05 / 11-06) above 3.50%** — clearing the one-year range top of 3.46%, on a third consecutive session of widening. A single +8bp day that then tightens is not enough.
2. **IG OAS above 1.00%** — clearing the one-year top of 0.94%. Investment-grade widening is stronger evidence of a real financing squeeze than high-yield widening.
3. **SPX closing below 7,350 for two consecutive sessions without reclaiming the 125-day MA at 7,589** — today's low of 7,350.35 is the near-term dividing line.
4. **VIX closing above 25 for three consecutive sessions** — a one-day spike is not escalation. An inverted VIX term structure (front month above back month) would carry more information than the level, but no term-structure data was obtained this run.
5. **The composite below 25 for three consecutive sessions** — it has lasted only one day so far, which is not yet "persistent extreme fear."

**Easing (2+ conditions required)**

1. **HY OAS back below 3.20%**, retracing more than half of the +18bp widening since 10-28.
2. **SPX reclaiming the 125-day MA at 7,589**, lifting the momentum sub-index out of extreme fear.
3. **VIX back below 20**, with put/call back below **0.80** (today's 0.8729 is the 93.2nd percentile; 0.80 is roughly the 74.5th).
4. **Breadth raw back above 700** (today's 601.93 is the 13.1st percentile; the one-year median is **997.8**, so 700 would still be below median — a stop in the decline, not a repair).
5. **The composite back above 30**, i.e. back to the 10-29 level of 30.77.

**Pending observations (neither escalation nor easing)**

- **CNN's revision of 11-05.** Precedent: 11-04 went from 27.29 to 34.17, a 6.89-point change. If tomorrow's fetch revises 19.88 above 25, the "extreme fear" conclusion itself is rewritten. **Until then, 19.88 should be treated as a preliminary value with roughly ±7 points of uncertainty.**
- **The first 11-05 HY OAS print.** This is the only data that can settle whether credit follows, and it matters more than any index level.

## Data Limitations

1. **The CNN composite and sub-index snapshot is taken about 1 hour 13 minutes after the close (17:13 EST)** and excludes after-hours trading; CNN revises the most recent day on the following day (observed this run: 11-04 from 27.29 → 34.17).
2. **The `previous_close` field conflicts with the historical series** (27.2857 vs 34.1714); that value equals the stored value for 2026-06-10 and is judged to carry an old snapshot rather than a recomputation. No daily change is declared from it; the same-series prior trading day is used as the baseline instead, with both readings disclosed (−14.29 / −7.41).
3. **The units and calculation conventions of the sub-index raw values are labeled by neither the API nor the script.** SPX level, 125-day MA, VIX, 50-day MA, and put/call can be matched to published definitions; strength, breadth, junk spread, and safe-haven spread are "inferred," used only for direction and magnitude, and not independently verified. Strength and breadth produced internal contradictions this run (index plunged while the sub-index improved or stayed flat) and are flagged as unverified.
4. **Credit data lags by one trading day:** HY/IG OAS run only through 11-04; 11-05 is not yet published. Any "credit has not confirmed the risk" statement holds only for 11-04. In addition, FRED returned only **794 observations starting 2023-11-06** for this series, insufficient to judge long-run historical percentiles.
5. **Leverage and flow data are missing:** the margin debt, IPO, and ETF flow fields in `crisis_signals.py` are script constants with no source or as-of date, and are excluded; this report therefore contains no leverage-based conclusion.
6. **The script's output mixes dates:** in `3_yield_curve`, 10Y/2Y are FRED 11-04 values while 10Y-2Y is the FRED 11-05 value. This report switches to the Treasury's same-day curve; the script itself was not modified.
7. **Data not obtained:** Russell 2000 index (`.RUT.US` returned empty), Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (`.SOX.US` reported invalid symbol), VIX term structure, and credit-spread percentiles beyond one year. Small-cap and semiconductor moves are proxied by IWM / SMH ETF prices — **an ETF is not identical to its index**.
8. **Chart conventions:** the three files are named by run date (2026-11-06) while the titles inside use the data cutoff (2026-11-05), consistent with the body text stating "market data through 11-05" — not a date error. The trend chart was visually inspected (endpoint 20 / extreme fear, title 2026-11-05); the radar and sub-trend charts were produced from the same JSON by the same script, but the vision endpoint returned 502 this run, **so they were not visually inspected**.
9. **Media attribution is not this report's conclusion:** the AI-regulation and data-center-slowdown causes come from external reporting. This report only notes their consistency with the verified price magnitudes; they were not independently verified.
