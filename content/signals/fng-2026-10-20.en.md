## Divergences: Possible Explanations and Counter-Evidence

**Divergence 1: the composite is in extreme fear for a second session, yet the index is still above its 125-day average and rose on the day.**

Facts: the composite at 23.11 is in the 14th percentile of the year and has spent two sessions in extreme fear; meanwhile CNN's momentum raw value rose +0.41% and the SPX sits +1.93% above its 125-day average.

Explanation: CNN's sub-indicators are standardised against their own historical distributions and are highly sensitive to regime shifts. Momentum measures not the absolute index level but the "cushion" over its 125-day average, and the cushion at its thinnest in a year scores low; VIX at 18.44 is only in the 69.6th percentile of its own year yet standardises to 18.75. "Extreme fear" is therefore pricing a **thinning cushion plus shrinking participation plus rising protection cost**, not a realised drawdown.

**Counter-evidence (against "false alarm"):** breadth at the 6.4th percentile and strength at the 4.4th percentile mean participation itself is very poor, so index-level resilience does not prove internal health; the Put/Call 5-day average has climbed to the 86.8th percentile, so protective demand really is rising. If the index keeps rising while breadth and strength fail to improve, the more reasonable reading is that gains are concentrated in a few heavyweights — not that risk has cleared.

**Divergence 2: CNN's junk spread narrowed while FRED HY OAS hit a 6.5-month high.**

Facts: CNN's junk spread is 1.2083% (10-19 snapshot, 2.23bp narrower than 10-16, 7.2nd percentile of its own sample); FRED HY OAS is 3.31% (10-16, 8bp wider than 10-15, the highest since 2026-03-30, 94th percentile of 397 observations). **The two differ in index composition, calculation convention and as-of date, and are neither interchangeable nor subtractable.**

Explanation: they are one session apart (CNN is a 10-19 16:10 ET snapshot, FRED's latest is 10-16), and CNN's sub-indicator does not use the same ICE BofA HY OAS series. "CNN narrower, FRED wider" may therefore be nothing more than a convention-and-timing gap and does not establish a split within the credit market.

**Counter-evidence:** if FRED HY OAS keeps widening past **3.50%** on 10-19 and 10-20 while CNN's spread stays below 1.25%, the "credit is only mildly repricing" judgement fails and must be replaced by "credit and equities are deteriorating together".

**Divergence 3: volatility fell but option protection did not retreat.**

Facts: VIX 19.99 → 18.44 (−7.75%) while the Put/Call 5-day average went 0.8200 → 0.8305 (+1.28%, 86.8th percentile).

Explanation: a lower spot volatility premium alongside higher hedging is not contradictory — VIX can fall as an event risk passes while hedging demand lags the volatility decline. This combination says only that volatility pricing has eased while protection is still being paid for.

**Counter-evidence:** protective demand has genuinely receded only if VIX returns below 17 **and** Put/Call falls below 0.76 (near the 0.7308 median). A VIX decline without a Put/Call decline is a one-legged improvement.

**Divergence 4 (explicitly not inferred):** no index-level quote data was available this run (all independent sources unreachable), so CNN's breadth or strength readings are **not** used to infer large-cap versus small-cap rotation; Put/Call and VIX movements describe protection cost and volatility pricing only and are **not** used to identify "smart money"; margin debt and ETF flows are monthly-basis figures and are **not** used to infer same-day fund flows.

1. **No specific day-change figure can be declared.** The `previous_close` basis (+5.17) and the historical-series basis (+1.04) conflict, and the 4.13 gap comes from CNN revising its 10-16 series record upward (live 17.9383 previously, 22.07 in this run's series); the reason for the revision is not disclosed. 2. **All independent quote sources failed this run.** Yahoo (DNS failure), moomoo OpenD (login failure), Longbridge (connect failure), CBOE's official VIX CSV (only through 10-16) and the browser (no internet) were all unavailable. **S&P 500 7,690.73 and VIX 18.44 are therefore unverified by a second source**, and are used only as CNN's own series readings; last run's digit-for-digit matches cannot be assumed to persist.
3. **Sub-indicator sampling times are not synchronised:** momentum 16:44 ET, breadth/strength 18:09 ET, Put/Call 16:54 ET, VIX 16:15 ET, junk 16:10 ET, **safe haven 15:59 ET (one minute before the close)**. The composite stitches together different observation windows, so its "day change" mixes them.
4. **CNN's internal sub-indicator contradiction is unverified:** momentum, breadth and junk carry per-point ratings that conflict with their own ratings; not used interpretively here.
5. **CNN's standardisation method is undisclosed:** each sub-indicator's normalisation window is longer than this 250-day sample, so score and raw-value percentile can diverge (most visibly junk at the 7.2nd percentile scoring 52.4, and VIX at the 69.6th percentile scoring 18.75). That divergence is **not** treated as an error and **not** used to reverse-engineer the method.
6. **Units unverified:** the raw-value units for breadth and strength cannot be confirmed from the response or public documentation, so only relative position and percentile are used.
7. **Duplicate historical record:** 2026-10-19 appears twice in the series (00:00 UTC and 22:09 UTC) with the same value of 23.11 — no conflict, and the latest timestamp is kept by rule. After de-duplication this run has 250 daily records (251 raw points).
8. **No timestamp conflicts:** a full scan found no "same timestamp, different value" records.
9. **Credit and leverage data have different lags:** HY/IG OAS and the DGS series run through 10-16; `T10Y2Y` through 10-19; margin debt is September; IPO and ETF figures are dated around 10-16 / 9-30. Each is labelled with its own as-of date and **no cross-period splicing** was performed; margin debt, IPO and ETF items were not refreshed and are carried forward with their dates.
10. **Script constants are unusable:** `crisis_signals.py` hard-codes margin debt ($1.304T, 2026-04), IPO count (~73) and ETF flows ($856B), none of which match the primary sources; the script also omits as-of dates for the yield curve, so rate data here was fetched directly from FRED and dated item by item.
11. **What is not inferred:** record margin debt or ETF inflows do not by themselves prove a crisis; Put/Call or VIX do not reveal "smart money" direction; breadth/strength do not determine large-versus-small-cap moves; and no uncalibrated "red-light count" or crisis probability is produced.
