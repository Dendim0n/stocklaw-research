## Divergences: plausible explanations and counter-evidence

### Divergence 1: sentiment is fearful (33.5) but credit shows no stress at all

**What we see:** CNN junk-bond raw spread 1.2194%, the **11.6th percentile** of the sample (very tight); FRED HY OAS 3.34%, the 83rd percentile of three years but below the 90th (3.49%); IG OAS 0.91%. Yet the composite has been in the Fear band for 20 straight sessions.

**Plausible explanation:** the fear is internal to equities (momentum, put/call, safe haven), not in the funding markets. A "bad equity sentiment, good credit sentiment" combination has historically looked more like valuation/positioning adjustment than recession pricing.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) spreads have widened 23bp since 3.11% on 10-09 — the direction is deterioration, only the level is still low; (b) HY OAS 3.34% is 75bp above the 2.59% low of January 2025, so "very loose" overstates it; (c) **11-10 credit data is not published yet** — if the CPI shock transmits, it shows up at the earliest on 11-11. Today's "credit is fine" describes 11-09, not 11-10.

### Divergence 2: VIX is neutral (50.0, at 19.46) but put/call sits at the 90.8th percentile (16.2)

**What we see:** implied volatility is modest (19.46, 13.8% above its 50-day average of 17.11), while put/call at 0.8517 is the 90.8th percentile of 251 observations.

**Plausible explanation:** hedging demand is concentrated in options rather than in volatility itself — protection bought for specific events (the December FOMC, AI-heavy earnings) rather than a blanket VIX bid. Structured-product and dealer hedging flows are an alternative mechanical explanation.

**Counter-evidence:** a high put/call is not a directional call and **must not be read as "smart money is bearish"** (no institutional positioning inference is made here). Real verification needs the VIX term structure (front/second month spread) and put/call split by market (index vs single stock vs ETF); neither was obtained. VIX falling 0.46 while equities fell modestly also looks more like post-event volatility decay than fear subsiding.

### Divergence 3: momentum is in extreme fear (13.2) yet the composite still holds 33.5

**What we see:** the S&P 500 has closed below its 125-day MA for four sessions (0.92% below); momentum at 13.2 is the weakest of the seven, but four neutral items (47.6–50.2) hold the composite at 33.5.

**Plausible explanation:** with equal weights, no single item can dominate. Trend (momentum) has broken, while participation (strength/breadth), volatility (VIX) and credit (junk demand) remain mid-range.

**Counter-evidence:** the **raw** percentiles for strength and breadth are only 16.0% and 15.2%, so the "neutral" scores are an artefact of CNN's own normalisation, not a recovery in participation. If participation stays at the 15th percentile while momentum keeps sliding, the composite breaks lower rather than being propped up indefinitely by four neutral items.

### Divergence 4: rates rose sharply and equities fell only 0.38%

**What we see:** on the same day the 2Y rose 17bp and the 10Y 10bp, the S&P 500 fell 0.38%, VIX fell 0.46, and HYG lost only 0.43%.

**Plausible explanation:** the market read it as "growth is fine + policy tightens" rather than "recession approaching" — semis (SMH) gave back only 0.78% after a three-day rebound, and high yield fell less than investment grade, both consistent with "not a credit event." The intraday low of 7,470.43 (−1.05%) was bought back, implying absorption.

**Counter-evidence:** (a) volume was **0.90x** the prior-20-day average and 0.98x the prior 60 — **neither capitulation nor accumulation**, so the "bought back" evidence is weak; (b) 2s10s at the 0.5th percentile of the year is the classic shape of tightening expectations, and the discount-rate pressure from higher real yields typically shows up with a **lag**, not on the day; (c) futures and post-market data were not obtained, so anything beyond the closing print is missing.

### Divergence 5: safe-haven demand improved (5.4→10.6) but its absolute level is still the 3.6th percentile

**What we see:** this item gained 5.2 points on the day, the largest improvement of the seven, yet it remains rated extreme fear.

**Explanation and counter-evidence:** the raw −4.0351 means stocks still lagged bonds by about 4 percentage points over CNN's window; "improvement" means the lag narrowed, not that stocks lead. On 11-10, with bonds selling off hard and stocks only slightly lower, the direction is internally consistent — but if yields keep rising and equities catch down, this item reverses quickly. It depends on relative stock/bond performance and **cannot stand alone as evidence of returning risk appetite**.

### Divergence 6: CNN's per-point `rating` field is systematically inconsistent with the score (a data defect, not a market signal)

**What we see (measured this issue):** every point in each sub-indicator's `data[]` array carries a `rating`, and it conflicts with the rating implied by that sub-indicator's current score on **4 of 7 items**:

## Data limitations

1. **Snapshot times are not uniform.** The composite timestamp is 22:12:12Z (17:12 EST); the seven sub-indicators are staggered between 21:13Z and 22:12Z (safe haven near 21:19:59Z, junk bond 21:10Z, VIX 21:15Z, put/call 21:14:59Z, momentum 22:00:31Z, strength/breadth 22:12:12Z). Strictly this is not a single-instant cross-section; the differences are minutes and immaterial here, but must be disclosed.

2. **`previous_close` conflicts with the historical series.** The field reads **31.714286** against the series' 11-09 value of **31.514286**, a **0.20** gap. The field equals yesterday's published seven-item average exactly, so it is "the prior publication," not "the prior close." Both bases are shown (+1.74 / +1.94); neither alone is used to announce a move.

3. **CNN revises backwards, and this issue produced a case of "same inputs, different score."** 11-09 moved from 31.7143 to 31.5143 (−0.20) while all six comparable raw values are **identical** — so the revision sits in normalisation, not in the data. Historical revision magnitudes for reference: 11-04 from 27.29 to 34.17 (+6.89); 11-06 from 22.0793 to 24.3532 (+2.27). **33.46 is therefore provisional; daily moves within ±0.2 should not be interpreted.**

4. **All `previous_*` fields verified for the first time this issue.** `previous_1_week` 23.5429 = the series' 11-03 value; `previous_1_month` 43.5143 = the 10-09 value; `previous_1_year` 29.9429 = the first series point (2025-11-10). All three match digit for digit — the check yesterday's report could not complete because the window began exactly one year back.

5. **Sub-indicator units are "interface-inferred."** Momentum (S&P 500 level) and VIX are independently verified via Longbridge; the absolute definitions of strength, breadth, junk spread and safe-haven spread (denominator, lookback window, annualisation) are not documented by the interface and **may only be used for direction and magnitude, never for equivalence with external metrics**. Junk bond demand is the clearest case — it differs from FRED HY OAS by more than 2 percentage points.

6. **CNN's per-point `rating` field is unusable (4 of 7 items conflict with the score, and it is internally inconsistent).** See Divergence 6. The defect directly affects the end-value labels in the third chart (sub-indicator trends): those labels come from this field and may disagree with the body's score-based ratings — **the scores govern**.

7. **Credit and rates have different cut-offs and cannot be combined arithmetically.** HY/IG OAS and `DGS*` are through **11-09**; 11-10 yields come only from the Treasury CSV (one day ahead of FRED); 11-10 credit spreads are **not published**. `crisis_signals.py`'s `3_yield_curve` block mixes dates internally (t10y/t2y 11-09, spread 11-10, 10Y-30Y 11-09); this report substitutes the Treasury same-day curve and **does not modify the script**.

8. **Macro attribution is second-hand, not primary-source.** The CPI month-over-month figures were independently recomputed from FRED index levels (+0.35% / +0.30%, consistent with the published +0.4% / +0.3%). However, Waller's remarks, the $42bn 10-year auction result and its ~5.02% stop, and CME's 99% December-hike / 11% 50bp odds are all taken from CNBC, Bloomberg (via swissinfo), Reuters (via Globe and Mail) and Investing.com — **the Fed speech transcript and the Treasury auction announcement were not read directly**. **October CPI year-over-year does not exist this month**: October 2025 survey data were never collected during the shutdown, so BLS has no YoY base; any "x% YoY" claim this issue is unsourced.

9. **Chart conventions.** The three PNG filenames use the **run date** (2026-11-11) while the in-chart titles and data use the **market cut-off** (2026-11-10), matching the body's "data through 11-10" — not a date error. The trend chart is drawn directly from the same JSON (title takes the series' last date; end label 33 / Fear); the radar chart reads the seven **scores** and **top-level ratings**, matching the body; the sub-indicator trend chart's end labels read the per-point `rating` field and are **inconsistent with this report's rating convention** (Limitation 6). The vision interface returned 502 twice this issue, so **no visual review was completed**; the statements above rest on source-code review plus item-by-item comparison with the JSON values.

10. **Not obtained.** VIX term structure (front/second month), put/call split by market (index/single stock/ETF), Russell 2000 and PHLX Semiconductor indices (proxied by IWM / SMH ETF prices, **which are not identical to the indices**), ICI weekly ETF flows, October FINRA margin debt, intraday HY OAS, futures and post-market prints. The consequence: **"who sold and how much" cannot be answered this issue — only "what price changed and by how much."**

11. **Scope of the self-checks.** All computations this issue (de-duplication, consecutive-day counting, percentiles, same-day spreads, moving averages and volume ratios) were recomputed by script and archived under `analyses/fng/2026-11-11/` (raw JSON, `crisis_signals` output, FRED and Treasury verification results). **A successful archive is not proof that a conclusion is right**: definitional judgments (e.g. which fields to discard) remain my judgment, not a programmatic fact gate.
