## 2. Key incremental items

**① Morgan Stanley: global equity long/short de-risking the largest in over a decade, Asia leverage back at the April 2025 low｜11:55, ID 234908**
Since late June, aggregate gross exposure at global equity long/short funds has shrunk **10.7%**. Asia long/short gross leverage fell **3pp in a week to 124%**, close to the 123% record low set during the April 2025 tariff shock. US long/short **net leverage dropped 5pp in one trading day to 42%**. In the first week of November every region's average hedge fund return was negative; Asia −1.5% (MSCI Asia-Pacific fell more than 5% over the same stretch), taking the year-to-date to +2.6%, about 6% behind US peers. **China long/short ran +1.1% on the week and +6.7% year-to-date**, with the mainland and Hong Kong among the few net-buying destinations — flows concentrated in domestic-consumption, semiconductor and SOE leaders, and Morgan Stanley states explicitly that this "mainly reflects passive uplift from short covering rather than the establishment of new aggressive longs."
Impact: the most important item this hour — it turns "seller exhaustion" from a feeling into measurable positioning, and explains why Korea, Taiwan and Nasdaq futures could extend. **It also caps what the bounce is**: covering from an extreme low can support one to two weeks, not a trend reversal; 42% net leverage means part of the ammunition is already spent.

**② Goldman extends the hiking path from one meeting to two: 25bp each in December and March 2027｜11:44, Jin10 flash**
Goldman now expects the Fed to hike **25bp each** in December this year and March 2027, versus a prior call of "a December hike" only. Jin10's CME citation puts the probability of a **50bp** move in December at **11.5%** — the market still treats a single 25bp as the base case.
Impact: sell-side is converting "one event" into "a cycle". The valuation hit is not the extra 25bp of discount rate but a **systematically higher terminal rate and a deferred easing path** — a persistent headwind for long-duration growth, unprofitable AI and gold. Counter-evidence: if the 2027 hike expectation gets absorbed by subsequent data (PCE in particular), this gets withdrawn.

**③ Japan's 10-year adds 9.0bp to 2.780%, the front end moves more, and the yen keeps weakening｜11:38 (plus 09:10 and 09:30 reads)**
10-year **+9.0bp to 2.780%**, 5-year **+8.5bp to 2.185%**, 2-year **+7.0bp to 1.845%**; over the same stretch USD/JPY was 158.81, up just 0.05% on the day, **failing to strengthen despite higher JGB yields**.
Impact: front-end yields rising faster than the long end (bear flattening) plus a weaker yen says this is **not Bank of Japan hike pricing** — it looks more like fiscal supply and spillover from US yields. That is a chronic drag on global duration and carry-trade high-beta equity, but not today's dominant variable. **Confirmation**: USD/JPY below 157 while the 10-year keeps rising would reclassify this as hike pricing, and risk-asset volatility would step up a level.

**④ CoreWeave puts "strong orders, tight credit" on the table at the same time｜11:09, ID 234904**
Q3 revenue **$3.66bn (+170% y/y, above the $3.55bn consensus)**; loss per share $0.95 (LSEG basis, better than the $1.16 expected). More important are both sides of the balance sheet: backlog **$132.5bn**, up **$28.5bn** in the quarter from $104bn at end-June; Q3 capex **$14.5bn** (versus $9.4bn in Q2), with the full-year guide raised to **$37–39bn**; total debt around **$46.9bn**, and its 2031 notes with a 9.75% coupon yield roughly **15.5%** (from 16.7% at the peak). Impact: demand keeps refuting the "AI peak" story, but **the 15.5% cost of debt is the line that constrains expansion** — the thicker the backlog, the more it depends on the credit market cooperating. Watch its bond yield, not its revenue growth.

**B｜Rates are the only real opponent of this bounce, and today they are getting stronger, not weaker.** Judgement: keep a **structurally bearish** view on long-duration growth and gold, but do not treat it as this hour's trading driver. Basis: Goldman pushed its hiking path to March 2027 (previously December only), and the CME 50bp probability is still 11.5% — the "how fast" disagreement is unresolved while the "how far" expectation shifts higher; meanwhile Japan's 10-year is up 9.0bp to 2.780% with the front end leading and the yen not strengthening, another leak in the global term-premium hull. But the clash between API's +8.968m barrel build and the EIA consensus is unresolved, so **before the official weekly report, any directional bet around 86 is a bet on a data source — chase neither side**.
