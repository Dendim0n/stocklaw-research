## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**1. 🚨 Strait of Hormuz Situation Escalates Sharply**

Vessel traffic through the Oman channel of the Strait of Hormuz dropped sharply on Sunday. A day earlier, multiple ships transiting the strait abruptly reversed course, underscoring Iran's continued tightening of control over this strategic waterway. HFI Research notes that in the past 24 hours, many vessels originally planning to transit the Oman channel have switched to the Iranian side. Following attendance at Khamenei's funeral, Medvedev went further, stating that while Iran may not truly possess nuclear weapons, it has discovered a weapon no less powerful — the Strait of Hormuz — and called the Bab el-Mandeb Strait Iran's other trump card.

**2. 💾 Nomura: Global Memory Industry Still in Severe Shortage**

Nomura's latest report argues that the core dynamic in the global memory industry remains severe supply shortage, with AI-driven structural demand growth yet to peak. Recent investor concerns about oversupply are significantly overblown. Samsung and SK Hynix have both previously increased investment.

**3. 🇺🇸 Trump Emphasizes "American Exceptionalism" at Independence Day**

Washington DC held its 250th Independence Day celebration amid record heat and thunderstorms. Trump delivered a speech emphasizing "American Exceptionalism," stating he would never let America decline.

**4. 🇷🇺 Russia-Ukraine: Russia Claims 71 Ukrainian Drones Destroyed Overnight**

Russia's Defense Ministry reported intercepting and destroying 71 Ukrainian drones across multiple regions overnight. China-Russia "Joint Sea-2026" exercises are set to begin in Qingdao, with Russian naval vessels already arrived.

**5. 🚗 Uber Suspends Most European Food Delivery Expansion**

According to the Financial Times, Uber has scrapped plans to launch services in 5 of the 7 European countries it originally targeted this year, as it continues efforts to acquire its largest European competitor.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The Strait of Hormuz is becoming the single largest tail risk for global markets. Iran is using waterway control to push geopolitical brinkmanship to a new level, with Medvedev now extending the threat to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — putting both energy chokepoints under simultaneous pressure.

Gold breaking above 4170 is no accident; it reflects the resonance of geopolitical premiums and rate-cut expectations. In the current environment, gold pullbacks are buying opportunities, not selling signals.

WTI crude at $68 appears to underprice the risk. If the Hormuz situation deteriorates further, oil could gap higher. Watch Iran-related developments closely this week.

The memory chip supply shortage narrative forms a positive feedback loop with AI infrastructure investment. Samsung and SK Hynix increasing capex actually confirms robust demand rather than oversupply — the sector merits attention after recent pullbacks.

This week's FOMC minutes will reveal internal details of Warsh's debut meeting, with markets intensely searching for year-end rate hike clues. After the weak payrolls report, rate hike expectations have already receded — dovish minutes would support risk assets.
