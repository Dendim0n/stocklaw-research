🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

Gold's breakout above $4,066 to a new all-time high signals that safe-haven demand has shifted from "precautionary" to "panic" pricing. The core driver is not inflation expectations but the repricing of tail risk from Middle East escalation. If the Strait of Hormuz faces a substantive blockade, gold could quickly test $4,200 in the near term.

Crude oil's gains notably lag gold, reflecting the market's ongoing debate over supply disruption probability. A clear signal of tanker sinking or strait closure could rapidly push WTI above $90. Current levels around $83 represent a "half-priced" state — the risk-reward skews to the upside.

The turning point for the US-Iran conflict may come at the Congressional hearings. If Congress rejects or substantially cuts the $87.6 billion war funding request, it would force the White House to reassess the scale and duration of military operations — a potential bearish signal for both oil and gold.

The AI sector faces policy uncertainty heading into the midterms. HSBC's "golden pit" call deserves attention — if AI regulation becomes a campaign focal point, combined with current elevated valuations, the tech sector could present a buying opportunity by late Q3.
