## 2. Key increments

**① Nvidia's overnight loss widened from 3% to nearly 6% as the board opened an independent investigation into "foreign intelligence operations" | 09:03, 14:51 Jin10 flashes**
Jensen Huang admitted an extramarital affair in a letter to employees on 8 November, which Nvidia published on its website (per China News Service). The board's statement said that in light of recent reports that Huang "may be the target of an intelligence operation by a foreign intelligence service," it has launched an independent investigation overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel, reviewing all available information. The board said it has "no reason to believe" US national security or Nvidia confidential information has been compromised and expressed confidence in Huang and his team. The company says Huang remains CEO; Trump said on the 8th that he should stay. **Read-through**: this is a governance and national-security investigation, not an order-book, margin or supply event — but for the single stock, CEO risk has to be repriced, and index futures are already paying for it.

## 3. Assessment

**A. US equities and AI hardware: bearish for Nvidia at tonight's open, but this does not support upgrading the story into an "AI narrative repricing."**
Three pieces of evidence: the overnight loss went 3% → 6% (09:03 → 14:51) and Nasdaq futures flipped from +0.16% to −0.37% this hour, while S&P futures (+0.23%) and Dow futures (+0.49%) stayed green — a systemic risk-appetite turn would have dragged all three down together; the board statement and company line both point to "operations unaffected, CEO stays," so the event sits in governance and national-security review, not the order book; and Asian cash markets bought chips all day. **Impact**: the most Nvidia-levered links (HBM, CoWoS, Taiwanese foundry, optical modules) see amplified volatility tonight, with SOX more volatile than the S&P; the AI hardware complex in China and Taiwan will trade tomorrow off tonight's Nvidia close, not today's Asian gains. **Falsification/confirmation**: if the loss narrows inside −2% after the open and Nasdaq futures recover above +0.3%, treat it as an event discount; if the investigation produces a specific allegation touching confidential information or supply, that is a fundamental markdown, not something to wait out. **Evidence gap**: VIX was not obtainable on this machine, so volatility cannot be used to cross-check the degree of stress.

**B. Precious metals and Treasuries: gold downgraded from bullish to neutral-bullish pending absorption; the bond call has firmer support than the gold call.**
Gold fell 0.30% this hour and sits 0.81% below its high, silver −2.2%, while the dollar was softer and the 10-year official yield kept falling (4.86%, −45bp from the high) — rates remain a support for gold, not a headwind, so this looks like risk-appetite recovery plus long liquidation rather than a higher cost of carry. The bond side has four official data points behind it: the entire curve down 33–45bp, the 10-year falling most, and a $2trn deficit (about 6% of GDP) where divided government narrows the expected supply expansion. **Impact**: gold miners have more torque than bullion; long-duration assets remain sensitive at 4.86%, and only another leg lower in yields is a real tailwind for growth stocks. **Falsification/confirmation**: if gold loses 4,070.7 (today's base, near Friday's close), the entire +0.74% is given back and the repair thesis is void; the bond rally must pass two gates — tonight's 02:00 3-year auction (prior stop 4.93%, bid-to-cover 2.62) and tomorrow's CPI month-on-month. Fail either and the decline stalls near 4.86%.

**C. Crude: bearish stance unchanged, but today's evidence is "alerts do not lift prices," not "supply has been restored."**
Brent at 96.88 (−1.46%) and WTI at 89.94 (−0.98%) fell only 0.14% this hour — slowing, not bouncing. Riyadh issued a danger alert at 14:18 and lifted it at 14:19; yesterday a Saudi Aramco office building in the King Abdullah Financial District was damaged by missiles and the Houthis claimed it. The threat is real yet crude has priced none of it back. Together with yesterday's Goldman positioning data (about $6.6bn of Brent exposure sold in a week, $4.9bn of it long liquidation) and tonight's 01:00 Trump–Qatar PM meeting, pricing power still sits with liquidators and diplomatic expectations. **Impact**: upstream energy and oil services earnings expectations stay under pressure; refiners, airlines and chemicals get cost relief; no effect on tomorrow's CPI energy component, since the drop is a November price. **Falsification/confirmation**: an actual loading or production interruption at an export terminal or through Hormuz (not verbal threats), or Brent recovering above 98.32, voids the bearish call; downside confirmation requires consecutive closes below 96.31 (today's low) with this week's EIA weekly inventory building.
