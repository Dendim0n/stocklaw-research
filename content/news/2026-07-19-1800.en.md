The Strait of Hormuz situation today is the most significant geopolitical event of the weekend — Iran directly intercepting four vessels, combined with explosions in Bahrain and the Aqaba evacuation, indicates both intensifying conflict and expanding geography.

WTI crude already gained 3.6% Friday to $82.33, but these weekend events are not yet priced in. A gap-up open Monday is highly likely, with the $85 level set to be tested.

Gold above $4000 reflects more than geopolitical hedging — the combination of hawkish Fed signals before the blackout period and an oversold bounce creates a dual catalyst for the precious metal.

The Strait of Hormuz handles roughly 20% of global oil transit daily. If shipping disruption escalates from "interception" to "blockade," the oil price shock would far exceed current pricing.

The Bahrain explosions are the most dangerous signal — this suggests the conflict may no longer be a bilateral US-Iran confrontation but is spilling into the broader Gulf region. If Gulf states are forced to pick sides, the complexity of the situation increases exponentially.

Crude oil and silver LOF premium warnings should not be ignored — elevated premiums indicate irrational inflows. If sentiment reverses, premium convergence losses could exceed underlying asset volatility.

A-share earnings season continues with copper foil and chip distribution sectors showing explosive profit growth, but beware of post-earnings profit-taking.
