## 2. Key Increments

**① Anthropic launches its Nasdaq IPO roadshow today: up to $69.3 billion at $70–80 per share | 09:06–09:46, copy of filing seen by Reuters**

Terms: **866,666,664 Class A shares** at **$70–80**, implying a raise of **$60.7–69.3 billion** — which would be the largest IPO on record (Saudi Aramco raised $29.4 billion). Measured against the $70–80 range and the $1.1–1.3 trillion valuation buyers have been discussing, the offering sells roughly **5%** of the company; the three figures are mutually consistent (own calculation: 866,666,664 × 70 / 80 = $60.67bn / $69.33bn). Ticker **ANT**, Nasdaq Global Select Market, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading, a 22-bank syndicate (per Jin10 citing IFR around 09:15).

Three companion items in the same window: **Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft and TSMC** will subscribe **$26 billion** via private placement at the IPO price (09:16, sources; equal to 37.5% of the maximum raise); **cornerstone investors will sell 23,333,331 shares** in the offering (09:17, sources); and **Anthropic told bankers that the IPO filing previously sent to them contained errors and that it will update the data** (09:46, sources).

**Why this is more than "another IPO"**: as recently as 11-06 the reporting was that the roadshow might be postponed because of the AI selloff; proceeding on schedule is itself a risk-appetite signal, and $26 billion of industrial capital entering at the offer price binds "the shovel sellers" and "the miners" into the same equity register. **But every figure and term comes from sources, and at 09:46 the company itself flagged that the filing needs correction — this issue does not treat the $69.3 billion or the $26 billion as final numbers**, only as the starting point of a roadshow. The valuation anchor's slide is a hard fact: from roughly $2 trillion discussed months ago → $1.4–1.5 trillion among buyers → $1.2–1.3 trillion per Bloomberg on 11-06, with some as low as $1.1 trillion.

**Fundamentals (11-02 amended prospectus, as reported)**: Q3 revenue **$16.956 billion** (up ~47% q/q, +1,127% y/y), compute and infrastructure spend **$9.863 billion** (up 77% q/q, equal to **58%** of quarterly revenue), and a GAAP swing from Q2 operating profit of $974 million to a **$567 million operating loss**; committed purchase and lease obligations total about **$895.4 billion**, of which roughly **$69.8 billion** falls due within 12 months, with payments running to 2045. Annualising Q3 revenue gives about $67.8 billion, i.e. roughly **18x sales** against a $1.2 trillion valuation (own calculation) — what buyers are haggling over is the bill, not the revenue.

**② Barkin says the direction of hikes is clear; Goolsbee says AI data-centre buildout shows classic overheating | 08:27 (article 234760)**

Richmond Fed President Barkin, speaking in South Carolina: demand is healthy, inflation is stubborn (**nearly six years above the 2% target**), the risks to the dual mandate sit mainly on the inflation side, and "the direction policy should take is relatively clear" while the pace requires care; he becomes an FOMC voter in 2027. Chicago Fed President Goolsbee said the same day that inflation pressure is spreading into services prices not tightly linked to energy or tariffs, and stated explicitly that **AI data-centre construction is beginning to show the hallmarks of traditional demand overheating, which central banks usually need to answer forcefully**. Policy facts: a unanimous 25bp hike in September took the target range to **3.75–4.00%**, and October held at 10 votes to 2. Market pricing: Bloomberg reported Monday that traders assign about **80%** odds to a hike at the 8–9 December meeting. **Implication**: this is not a new direction but a shift from probability to near-default for December, and it names AI capex itself as an inflation source — a double hit for long-duration assets (higher discount rate plus policy attention on the capex).

**⑤ Russia and Ukraine restart three-way closed talks in Abu Dhabi today at 14:30 Beijing time, for two days | 09:49, 09:52 (article 234771)**

The first time the three parties have sat at the same table since shuttle diplomacy broke down in February, with **ceasefire monitoring, long-term post-war security guarantees and territorial division** on the agenda; Kyiv says talks begin around **14:30 Beijing time**. A US official described the talks as "cautiously optimistic" (09:49). Ukraine's most practical ask is a staged ceasefire covering **Black Sea grain corridor security and a halt to mutual strikes on energy infrastructure**. **Implication**: this is the single biggest variable for crude over the next few hours — if only "no strikes on energy facilities" is agreed, that is further bearish for oil; if talks collapse, some of the war premium removed on Monday can return. Most geopolitical specialists judge the room for substantive breakthrough to be "very limited".

## 3. Situation Assessment

**A | AI and US equities: financing-side pricing matters more than CPI this week — near-term bullish for the hardware chain, neutral for the index.** Three pieces of evidence: first, $26 billion of industrial capital entering at the offer price effectively binds the $895.4 billion of future compute purchase commitments to suppliers' equity returns, which is a credit enhancement on the demand side for NVDA, AMD, AVGO, MU and TSM; second, the valuation being cut from $2 trillion to $1.1–1.3 trillion while only about 5% of equity is sold means this is a "small-size, high-price probe" rather than a clearance sale, so the liquidity drain is smaller than the $80–100 billion previously expected; third, index futures were essentially flat this hour (NQ −0.08%, ES +0.11%, YM +0.28%), so the market is not treating it as a negative. **Impact**: the real pressure point is the 18–20 November pricing window — at that point AI assets will have a public, shortable comparison anchor, and private-market valuations (OpenAI and peers) will be forced toward it. **Trade-off**: do not chase AI hardware during the roadshow, and do not short it either; once the price range is set, judge on the gap between primary-market pricing and secondary-market peer multiples rather than on sentiment. **Confirmation / invalidation**: pricing in the upper half of $70–80 with the $26 billion list intact → bullish case holds; if the range slides toward $60 or the strategic tranche is downgraded to "indications of interest", then the financing side of AI capex is a genuine problem, and more than one stock will reprice.

**B | Gold: downgraded from "event-driven rebound" to near-term bearish, because this hour's price action lines up with rates.** Three pieces of evidence hold at once: gold fell from a 09:28 high of 4,139.80 to 4,113, with a session low of 4,112.72, giving back about two-fifths of Monday's +2.11%; silver fell 0.73% in parallel and the gold/silver ratio edged up to 77.56, so this is a **broad precious-metals pullback** rather than gold-specific weakness; and over the same window the 2-year Treasury yield rose 5bp to 4.56% with 2s10s narrowing to 32bp, the short end pricing a December hike. Add two Fed officials naming AI capex itself as an inflation source, and gold's "AI bubble hedge" narrative weakens — if AI investment is inflationary, that is bearish for gold, not bullish. **Impact**: gold miners have more beta than the metal and typically fall first during a grind lower; into tonight's CPI, gold's current level means it carries no premium for an inflation surprise. **Trade-off**: do not add on the long side before CPI; 4,113 is the session's low zone, and a break would take price back toward the 6 November close (the two sources available here disagree on that close — 4,056 versus 4,071, about $15 apart — so it is a directional reference only, not a precise support level). **Invalidation / confirmation**: core CPI at or below +0.2% m/m with the 2-year back below 4.50% → the bearish call is void; core at or above +0.3% → hike pricing moves up and gold has room for another leg lower. **Evidence gap**: real yields (TIPS) and the dollar index were not available intraday here, so the move cannot be split between "real rates rising" and "risk premium compressing" — two very different things for gold.

**C | Crude: bearish stance maintained, but do not short at the bottom of the range — today's two bearish items are not new supply-demand facts.** SC breaking 600 and fuel oil −7% is Asia catching down to Monday's US session; the INE limit adjustment is a risk control. On price, WTI sat between 85.68 and 86.93 for three hours without a second leg down, suggesting Monday's liquidation is largely done. **Impact**: upstream energy and oil services stay under pressure, while refiners, airlines and chemicals get cost relief; tonight's CPI energy component is unaffected (November prices are falling, but CPI measures October). **Trade-off**: with Brent already down more than 17% from roughly $118 on 30 October, shorting here offers worse odds than waiting for a bounce toward $92. **Confirmation / invalidation**: WTI closing above **86.85** (the 11-09 settlement) voids the bearish call; a break below **85.68** (today's low) combined with a further EIA inventory build opens the next leg. If the Abu Dhabi talks starting at 14:30 produce a halt to strikes on energy infrastructure, that is the first real news that could undercut a bounce — note the direction: a deal is bearish for oil, a collapse is bullish.
