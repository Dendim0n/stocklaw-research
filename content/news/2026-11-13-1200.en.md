## 2. Key incremental items

**① Asia's semiconductor chain turns negative: KOSPI from +3.03% to −0.56% | 09:00–12:13**
KOSPI printed a session high of **5,504.44 (+3.03%)** and now sits at **5,312.64 (−0.56%)**; at 11:05 it was still +1.16%. The Nikkei's morning high was 61,902.50 (+1.78%) and it closed the morning at 61,270.17 (+0.74%). Nasdaq futures went from +0.46% to −0.12%. A-share midday: Shanghai +0.22%, Shenzhen −0.56%, ChiNext −1.47%, half-day turnover CNY 979.4bn with **more than 2,900 stocks down**. Inside the tape: CPO kept sliding, with Lianxun Instruments and Kehua down over 10% and TFC Optical and Zhongshi Technology down over 9% ([11:10](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113111020649800)); compute chips opened high and closed low (Cambricon, Enflame, Moore Threads all faded); **banks went the other way and printed record highs** (Bank of Jiangsu, Bank of Nanjing, Bank of Qingdao, Bank of Chengdu), alongside a wave of limit-ups in film exhibition ([11:41](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113114116168800)).
Impact: this is no longer "opening gap given back" but a trend reversal. The tape only gives price and sector dispersion, though — there is no main-force flow data for this hour, so **one cannot assert the same money rotated out of AI hardware into banks and film exhibitors**.

**② JGBs higher again: 10-year +8bp to 2.87%, 2-year 1.890% | 11:31–11:50**
The 10-year JGB yield rose **8bp to 2.87%**, the 30-year +7bp to 3.99%, and the 2-year added another 4.0bp to **1.890%** at 11:50 ([11:50](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113115030196800)). A Tokai Tokyo strategist said JGBs and Treasuries fell together and the move "may be an overreaction" but that there is "no clear catalyst" for a bounce; the same report said overnight index swaps imply roughly an 87% probability of a BOJ hike before December ([11:31](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113113150779800)).
Impact: this is the same chain as gold's −2.76% yesterday — global duration is being repriced, not a collapse in haven demand. Higher JGB yields also raise yen carry costs, a reasonable backdrop for Asia's risk-asset pullback, but two things moving together is not causation.

**③ Bloomberg: SpaceX–Tesla merger talks advance, announcement as early as December | first seen 04:10, long-form 11:41**
Bloomberg, citing people familiar: both boards and advisers are negotiating terms, share arrangements and post-merger governance, with an announcement **as early as December**, though a delay into early 2027 was also discussed; at current valuations SpaceX is above $1.9trn and Tesla about $1.5trn, **over $3.4trn combined**; obstacles include how to ring-fence parts of Tesla's overseas business and national-security issues around SpaceX's US Defense Department contracts ([235131](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235131)). **Attribution matters here**: the headline first appeared at 04:10 via Jin10's "market chatter" feed, neither company has responded and the deal is unconfirmed; TSLA traded up as much as +2.5% intraday on 11-12 and closed **397.45 (+1.69%)**, while SPCX's gain narrowed to about +0.5% — the market has already priced part of it.
Impact: for TSLA this is event premium (the 17:00 pre-market is the first continuous pricing session); for SPCX and the related supply chain it is a governance and exchange-ratio question, not a fundamental improvement.

**④ Cerebras raises full-year guidance, adds Microsoft as a customer, but the 2027 guide is light | 11:58**
Q3 core revenue **$230.9m (+70%, consensus $214.7m)**, of which cloud and other services **$144.9m (+272%, 63% of the total)**, while the hardware business including direct chip sales fell to **$86m** (from $96.8m a year earlier); net loss widened to **$161.2m** (from $22.2m) and loss per share was $0.67. Full-year core revenue guidance was raised to **$940–960m** from $880–890m (consensus $887.5m), Q4 guidance is **$310–330m** (consensus about $271–275m) at a 47–49% core gross margin; Microsoft becomes a customer after OpenAI and Oracle, and the CEO said the company has sufficient wafer supply commitments from TSMC ([235134](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235134)). The stock rose about 15% after hours. **But "triple 2026" for 2027 works out to roughly $2.85bn, below the LSEG consensus of $2.92bn.**
Impact: a major customer is sourcing a second supplier away from Nvidia — a negative read for Nvidia's ecosystem lock-in, a positive for TSMC foundry and the ASIC route; it is not a positive for A-share CPO, since inference capacity expansion does not equal optical-module orders.

**⑤ SocGen: January 2027 index rebalancing implies about $3.3bn of gold buying | 11:29**
SocGen estimates that funds tracking BCOM and the S&P GSCI will sell about $17.2bn of February gold futures and buy about $20.5bn of the April contract in the **2027-01-08 to 01-14** annual rebalance, a net purchase of about **$3.3bn**, equal to roughly 56% of the April contract's average daily volume; silver would see about **$2.5bn** of inflows ([235125](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235125)).

## 3. Assessment

**A | Asia's semiconductor chain: the rebound is reversed; bearish on chasing, but this is not a crash.**
Evidence: KOSPI from +3.03% to −0.56%, Nikkei from +1.78% to +0.74%, Nasdaq futures turning negative — three markets in the same direction. ChiNext −1.47% against Shanghai +0.22% is a "high-volatility down, low-volatility dividend up" structure, not broad de-risking; banks at record highs and film exhibitors limit-up show the bid is still in the market. Impact: CPO / optical modules, AI server assembly and Korean/Taiwanese memory bearish; banks, dividend names and domestic-demand themes are the relative destination. Invalidation: if at the 15:00 A-share close ChiNext reclaims 2,500 (psychological level) and KOSPI finishes above yesterday's 5,342.73, this section is void; the harder test is **Nvidia's Q3 FY27 report on 11-17** — if guidance is cut, the pullback goes from "sentiment" to "demand".

**B | Gold: the rebound stopped at 3,920 and is already rolling over; neutral-to-bearish, chase neither way.**
Evidence: up only 0.05% hour-on-hour, but back to 3,914.03 by sampling, below where the hour started; silver −0.24% on the hour and 50.32 by sampling versus 50.55 at 11:00, having flipped from leader to laggard; JGB 10-year +8bp, 30-year 3.99%, 2-year 1.890%, and USD/JPY at 159.93 still below 160 — nothing on the duration side has loosened. The SPDR holdings figure checked last issue showed a cut of only 0.38 tonnes, i.e. the selling is leveraged positioning; that explains both how fast last night's bounce came and how fast it stopped today. Impact: gold, silver, gold miners, gold ETFs. Invalidation: a PPI monthly print above the 0.4% consensus at 21:30 adds another layer of pressure; if 2-year Treasuries fall below 4.70% (16bp or more off the 11-12 close of 4.86%) and gold is still near today's low of 3,874.93, the driver has shifted from duration to positioning, and WGC quarterly supply/demand plus monthly ETF flows would be needed — not obtained this issue. For any trend repair, 3,987.93 (the 11-11 close) has to be reclaimed first.

**C | Two company events in opposite directions: TSLA is event premium, Cerebras is a competitive discount for Nvidia.**
Evidence: the merger report already took TSLA to a +1.69% close on 11-12 (+2.5% intraday), but the deal is unconfirmed and the obstacle list (ring-fencing overseas operations, national-security review of Defense Department contracts) says this cannot land quickly. The most notable Cerebras number is not +70% but **hardware revenue falling year-on-year while cloud rose 272%** — inference demand is expanding, but customers would rather buy compute as a service than buy chips, and a hyperscaler like Microsoft is deliberately bringing in a second supplier. Impact: TSLA / SPCX event-driven bullish but needs confirmation; for Nvidia this is a marginal negative for the valuation narrative, and a positive for TSMC and the ASIC chain. Invalidation: if Nvidia does not cut guidance on 11-17 and hyperscaler capex is revised higher again, this is incremental rather than substitution, and the section downgrades from "share shift" to "competitive diversion".
