## 🔥 Key Developments This Hour

**Iran Issues Evacuation Warning for Dubai and Abu Dhabi Airports**

An Iranian security official warned via Fars News that if the US strikes Iranian infrastructure, airports in Dubai and Abu Dhabi, as well as the ports of Fujairah and Jebel Ali, should prepare for evacuation. This marks the first time Iran has directly threatened core civilian infrastructure in Gulf Arab states, signaling the conflict may be spreading from military targets to civilian facilities.

**Iraq Reroutes Oil Exports Through Syria to Bypass Strait of Hormuz**

Iraq is reportedly using large numbers of trucks to transport fuel through Syria, rapidly transforming the neighboring country into a key Middle Eastern export hub. The move aims to circumvent potential blockade risks at the Strait of Hormuz. Within months, Syria has gone from shipping zero fuel to becoming a regional transit center.

**Russia-UAE Foreign Ministers Hold Call; Multi-Nation Diplomatic Push**

Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov spoke with UAE Deputy PM and Foreign Minister Abdullah, jointly emphasizing that the US and Iran must immediately cease fire, resume negotiations, and ensure freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz. The same day, Qatar's Foreign Ministry called for an immediate and comprehensive halt to all military actions threatening regional security. The GCC Secretary-General condemned Iran's attacks on infrastructure in Bahrain, Kuwait, and Jordan as war crimes.

**Turkey Plans to Sell Russian Air Defense Systems in Exchange for F-35s**

According to the Wall Street Journal, Turkey plans to sell its Russian S-400 air defense systems while seeking to purchase US F-35 fighter jets, potentially reshaping US-Turkey defense relations.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The 3.6% surge in crude oil is not a technical bounce — it's a repricing of geopolitical risk premium. Iran directly threatening Dubai and Abu Dhabi airports signals the next phase of escalation may no longer be confined to bilateral US-Iran conflict but could spill over to the entire Gulf region.

Iraq's detour through Syria to export oil is rational risk hedging, but it inversely confirms genuine market concern about Strait of Hormuz disruption. If this rerouting becomes the norm, global crude trade routes will undergo structural change.

Gold reclaiming $4,000 serves both as a geopolitical hedge and a technical recovery from oversold conditions. The current pattern of gold and oil rising in tandem suggests the market is pricing a dual scenario of escalating conflict plus inflation expectations.

Diplomatic efforts are intensive but lack substantive progress. The Russia-UAE call, Qatar's statement, Pakistan's mediation — all parties are vocal, but none has presented an actionable ceasefire plan. The diplomatic window is narrowing.

This week's focus: if the US-Iran conflict continues to spread to civilian infrastructure, Monday's Asian market open could see panic-driven gaps, with gold and crude oil at the forefront.
