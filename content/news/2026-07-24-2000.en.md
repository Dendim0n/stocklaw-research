🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

Iran's missile strike on the US base in Kuwait is the most significant geopolitical escalation today, directly fueling safe-haven demand — gold bounced sharply from the 4022 low to 4063.

Yet oil's price action is completely divorced from the risk-off narrative — WTI's 3% plunge suggests the market is far more concerned about demand-side fears than Middle East supply disruption.

One possible interpretation: if the US responds with restraint after Iran's Kuwait strike, the probability of all-out war actually decreases, and the market reads it as a "sell the fact" moment.

Another possibility: global economic slowdown expectations are weighing on oil, with reports of US refineries running at full capacity and SPR near depletion reinforcing a narrative of ample supply.

The extreme divergence in gold/oil ratios — gold's safe-haven premium alongside crude's risk discount — is typically unsustainable.

Next week's FOMC rate decision and Core PCE data will determine near-term direction; expect elevated volatility until then.
