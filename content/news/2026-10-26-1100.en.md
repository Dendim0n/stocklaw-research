## 4. Judgement

**Crude: the downside momentum is clearly fading; chase neither direction.** Three same-source data points: USOIL fell only 0.42% this hour versus -2.03% last hour; the intraday low of **100.224** held above last hour's 100.615; and the platform-to-December-WTI spread held at 0.72%, with no expiry or roll jump. Last issue I wrote that "upstream energy equities have give-back pressure"; that call is **disproven** in Hong Kong — the three oil majors are up 2.7–4.2% today, and Sinopec (+4.15%) rose almost exactly as much as PetroChina (+4.22%), which suggests the market is not simply buying "higher upstream prices" and that refining and dividend characteristics may be doing work too — a layer the current data cannot prove. Impact: no short chase on upstream; refining and diesel chains stay tight. Invalidation: USOIL below 100.2 with product cracks falling in tandem upgrades this to a trend; verifiable physical disruption to Hormuz loadings puts crude back above 103 and voids the call.

**Gold: neutral-to-bullish, but this is a weak signal.** Spot gold is back at **4,000.88**, +0.30% on the hour, **0.58%** off the 3,978 intraday low, with the day now +0.08% and silver +0.20% (COMEX silver +0.39%). The point is the **directional divergence**: if the market truly believed "no strike today, it ends soon", gold should have fallen with crude, and it did not. The 10:29 memo and the 10:25 Houthi claim provide narrative support, but gold moved only 0.3% and no minute-level data is available, so **the rally cannot be attributed to those two items**. What is genuinely missing is the 10-year real yield and gold ETF flows, neither of which was obtained in this window. Impact: do not trade miners off one hour of gold price (last issue's Chifeng -9% was half a Q3 earnings story, net profit -41.84%). Invalidation: gold back below 3,978 while crude keeps sliding would mean today is a broad unwind of risk premium.

**Asian equities: a style rotation, not broad risk aversion.** Shanghai Composite -0.38%, Hang Seng flat, 3,700+ decliners (10:16), yet index losses are far smaller than breadth would imply and banks plus the three oil majors rose against the tape — the classic shape of money moving from high-multiple hardware into cash-flow assets. Two implications: first, the pressure on AI hardware comes from the discount rate and crowding, not from collapsing demand (last issue noted Cambricon's Q3 net profit +147.74%); second, the strength in dividend/energy only becomes a global rotation if the US replicates it tonight (SOX falling less than the Nasdaq, energy equities rising). Strategy: no bounce-chasing in AI hardware; wait for the relative performance of SOX versus the Nasdaq after the 21:30 open. Invalidation: if SOX falls clearly less than the Nasdaq while bank stocks roll over, this was local repositioning only; if the Nasdaq and SOX fall together and energy stocks follow crude lower, we are back to "risk assets under broad pressure".
