## 3. Incremental Items

**① [published 10:40 / event 10-20 US after-hours] Supermicro raises Q1 revenue guidance 27% — verified against the 8-K**

- 8-K (SEC, filed 2026-10-20, EX-99.1): Q1 FY2027 revenue of **$18.5–19.5bn** versus prior guidance of $14.5–15.5bn; gross margin of **12.0–12.5%** versus prior 10.4–10.8%. The company attributes this to increased AI platform demand and customer/product mix. The filing states the figures are **unaudited**; final results and the call are on 11-03.
- After-hours prices (Sina, 19:59 EDT): **SMCI 37.55 (+15.65%)** versus a regular close of 32.47 | **DELL 489.03 (+5.95%)** | AMD 550.65 (+2.98%) | MU 915.17 (+2.13%) | NVDA 221.06 (+2.12%).
- Why it matters: this is the first-hand evidence that AI server purchasing did not slow in the September quarter, directly offsetting the "capex has peaked" trade reflected in semis being down 14% cumulatively since 10-06 and the semiconductor ETF's nine straight down days last week. But it proves **volume**, not customer **returns** — the 8-K has no customer breakdown, and revenue could reflect pulled-forward deliveries or orders taken from competitors.

**② [10:15 / 10:20] JPMorgan downgrades the battery chain; CATL A-shares fall more than 10%**

- Research note (as relayed by Jin10 at 10:15): CATL H-share target cut to **HK$500 from HK$725**, CALB to HK$17 from HK$40, Lead Intelligent to HK$22 from HK$45, all cut to **Neutral** from Overweight. The rationale is that chain-wide profitability is deteriorating faster than expected, even while acknowledging 2027 global EV and storage demand growth of 20–25% is above consensus — i.e. volumes are fine, margins are not.
- Prices: CATL A-shares down more than **10%**, H-shares -9.3%, and more than 50% below the May high (Jin10 10:20).
- **The incremental detail is on cash flow** (10:36): CATL said the Q3 operating cash flow decline came from **proactively shortening supplier payment terms, paying prepayments and funding supplier R&D** from Q3 onward — deliberate cash deployment, not deteriorating collections. It also said the inventory build is mostly goods shipped but not yet recognised, with turnover days stable. This does not change the profit decline, but it separates "cash burn" from "deliberately funding the supply chain".

## 4. Assessment

**Call one: Asia's move is a "sidecar-grade" pulse, not a trend. Price has already proved it this hour, and chasing carries more risk than missing out.**

Evidence: KOSPI fell from 5,800.55 (+4.46% at 10:02) to 5,756.67 (+3.67% at 11:00), 1.03% below the intraday high. In the same hour the Shanghai Composite moved from -0.09% to -0.42% and sits on its intraday low (3,719.58 versus 3,719.68 now); CSI 300 -0.66%; ChiNext turned from up to down at -0.80%; the Hang Seng went from +0.54% at 09:48 to -0.36%, just 3 points off its low. The 09:51 buy-side sidecar shows buying was briefly one-sided enough for the exchange to halt it. Korea's own driver is an improvement in the stock, not the flow (verified last issue: working-day-adjusted export growth of 65.9% is below September's 89.8%), which cannot support a second day of acceleration.

Affected assets: Korean equities and the memory chain see wider short-term swings; A-share semis are riding sentiment spillover, not fundamental transmission. **Confirmation**: KOSPI holds above +3% at the 14:30 close and European tech picks up after 15:00. **Invalidation**: KOSPI gives back to within +1.5% by the close, or SMH spikes and fades tonight — in which case this was short covering.

**Call two: Supermicro's guidance punches a hole in the "AI hardware demand has peaked" narrative, but it only fixes "volume", not "returns". Upstream is bullish; hyperscaler cash flow is still under pressure — trade the two separately.**

Evidence: the 8-K shows the revenue guidance midpoint rising from $15.0bn to $19.0bn (+27%) and gross margin from 10.4–10.8% to 12.0–12.5%, attributed to AI platform demand and product mix. After hours: SMCI +15.65%, DELL +5.95%, AMD +2.98%, MU +2.13%, NVDA +2.12% (Sina, 19:59 EDT). Gross margin moving up alongside revenue is the more important detail — this is not volume bought with price cuts.

Counter-evidence and limits: the figures are unaudited and not final until 11-03; the 8-K discloses no customer mix, so traditional cloud buyers cannot be separated from CoreWeave/SpaceX-type new buyers; a single-quarter revenue surge can also come from delivery timing. It is therefore not enough to infer that semiconductor demand across the board is recovering, and certainly not that returns on AI investment have improved — that depends on depreciation and financing costs, and DayOne's shrunken IPO shows the equity side of that funding is tightening.

Affected assets: server ODMs, GPUs and memory are short-term bullish; debt-funded neoclouds and data-centre operators remain the pressured side. **Confirmation**: SMH closes higher tonight on volume above its trailing 20-day average, with DELL/HPE holding gains. **Invalidation**: SMH gaps up and fades on below-average volume — the news was worth one session.

**Call three: the marginal price-setter for crude is negotiation headlines and diesel, not crude inventories. Most of today's 1% is catch-up rather than lost supply; do not chase it as a trend.**

Evidence: platform USOIL at 98.950 (+1.09%) is only +0.24% versus 98.717 at 10:00, and Sina NY crude at 99.258 is only +0.13% versus 99.130 at 10:02, with December Brent at 110.487 (+1.56%) — the gain flattened during the Asian session rather than accelerating. Tuesday's large across-the-board API draws (crude -4.931m bbl, gasoline -1.954m, distillates -2.577m, all beating expectations) went unpriced that day, so today looks like catch-up. Supply-side hard data remains loose: Middle East oil flows are above the pre-war 23.3 mb/d at 24.0 mb/d, while Hormuz itself is only back to 82% of pre-war (verified last issue). What is genuinely tight is refined product: a Jin10 column at 10:45 said the IEA has released 20m barrels of diesel without suppressing prices, with StoneX's Saggese arguing uncertainty matters more than fundamentals — that is an analyst view, not data.

Affected assets: upstream oil and gas cash flow benefits but is capped by policy; diesel cracks and refiners are stronger than crude itself; cost pressure on airlines and chemicals is unresolved. **Confirmation**: the 22:30 EIA report confirms a draw with Cushing turning from +936k to a decline while Hormuz flows stay impaired. **Invalidation**: Witkoff's meeting with Putin today yields real progress (lifting the Russian diesel sanctions or an energy ceasefire), or the IEA announces specific release volumes — either would erase today's dollar first.
