🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The Middle East conflict is expanding from military targets to civilian infrastructure, with Iran's direct strikes on US bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan marking a qualitative escalation — these Gulf states were not previously direct targets.

Iran's claim of Patriot defense penetration, if verified, would undermine market confidence in US regional air defense capabilities and further inflate risk premiums.

WTI's 3.6% single-day move remains within rational bounds, but if the conflict widens to threaten Strait of Hormuz shipping security, oil's upside potential would expand dramatically.

Gold reclaiming $4,000 suggests safe-haven logic will continue to dominate near-term price action, though rapid pullback risk exists should tensions de-escalate.

Sustained massive inflows into A-share ETFs indicate the "national team" remains firmly engaged in market stabilization, with broad-market ETFs now serving as a core policy tool.

The flurry of WAIC announcements shows China's AI compute investment is still accelerating, but the ongoing correction in US semiconductor stocks signals that global chip valuation pressures cannot be ignored.
