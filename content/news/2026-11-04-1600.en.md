## 4. Situation assessment

**Equities: bullish, but this is a continuation of the repair, not a new trend - do not chase the index.** The basis is continuous slope rather than a gap: futures are 0.63% above the 11-03 close yet still 0.54% below the 28,512 high, meaning the morning drop has been recovered but no new high set. Institution views point the same way: Julius Baer at 16:05 argued divided government caps fiscal expansion and supports Treasuries, while Standard Chartered at 15:13 stressed that even a landslide cannot overcome the veto, so little policy actually lands. That matches a "repair, not re-rating" price structure. **Affected assets**: longest-duration growth and AI hardware have the most beta; energy and metals lag; equal-weight and small caps underperform. **Confirmation/invalidation**: taking out **28,512** completes the repair; a drop back below **27,615.75** would make this hour's climb short covering. Tonight's 21:15 ADP and 23:00 ISM are the tests - the services prices-paid component was 74, and if it rises again hike expectations will press back in.

**Crude: turned tactically bearish, but what is being sold is the "normalisation expectation", not demand.** All three quote bases fell 0.4-0.6% together, and for the first time a named house has written strait normalisation into a one-year baseline; Saudi Arabia's ~$22bn Q3 deficit shows producers have no incentive to cut. The counter-case is that the foreign ministry still treats "reopening Hormuz" as a goal to be achieved, so transit is not normalised - any **actual loading or discharge disruption** (not another attack report) would put the premium back in one move. **Affected assets**: energy equities move from event premium back to supply-demand pricing, airline and transport costs improve, and lower inflation expectations modestly help the long end. **Confirmation/invalidation**: tonight's 23:30 EIA is expected to swing from **-1.892m barrels last week to a +877k build**; if inventories draw hard and crude still will not rally, "pricing is not in inventories" gets a second confirmation and the bearish tilt can stay.

**Gold and credit: gold neutral-bearish short term; private credit is a slow variable, not a trading signal.** The gold case rests on price itself - flat, high untaken, a domestic premium of only 0.38%, no fresh physical or ETF buying evidence - and the "rate-driven" leg is missing its two-year yield check, hence neutral-bearish rather than outright bearish. The two credit warnings land on **software and the 2027-2028 maturity wall**, but Blackstone's successful raise the same day shows the primary market is still open, so this is a valuation-discount issue that shows up first in BDC and SaaS multiples rather than immediate defaults. **Affected assets**: gold miners lose beta, silver is more fragile than gold because of its industrial leg, and highly levered software plus private-credit BDCs should re-rate before fundamentals move. **Confirmation/invalidation**: spot breaking and holding above **4,029.08** restarts the gold uptrend; a single large private-credit fund gating redemptions would be the start of a credit event.
