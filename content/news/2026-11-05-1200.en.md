**3) Treasuries fall 3bp or more across the curve, 10Y the lowest since 10 September - but discount the attribution | 11:26-11:52**
Yields fell at least 3bp at every tenor, with the 10-year down to **4.84%**, the lowest since 10 September; the dollar index fell another 0.2% intraday (Jin10 11:26, attributed to the Democratic sweep limiting fiscal expansion). The 11:52 feature gives a finer sequence: after the Treasury's quarterly refunding statement, **the curve initially steepened with the long end underperforming**, and only then did yields across maturities fall as oil dropped. The statement removed the word "at least" used for nearly three years, opening room to issue more short/intermediate paper and less at the long end, but JPMorgan expects the actual shift only around **August 2027**; next week is still the eleventh consecutive $125bn refunding ($58bn 3-year on 11-09, $42bn 10-year on 11-10, $25bn 30-year on 11-12). Japan's 10-year auction the same day had a bid-to-cover of 3.21 (3.76 last time, 3.24 twelve-month average) and a tail of 0.25 (0.02 last month); the 2-year yield fell 3bp to 1.855%, and the September minutes showed some members arguing for faster hikes. **Implication:** the near-term driver of lower long yields looks more like oil and positioning than the issuance structure - treating "election to fiscal restraint to Treasury rally" as a causal chain overstates its durability. Global duration supply pressure is not a US-only problem either.

**4) Memory: contract prices up, equities down | 11:07**
Per TrendForce: server DRAM contract prices continue to rise and supply is shifting faster to long-term agreements; enterprise SSD leads the NAND contract market while consumer bargaining stays stuck; spot is diverging, with DDR4 slightly lower and 512Gb TLC wafers down 4.33% on the week. Citi expects the memory market to remain undersupplied through 2027; Micron's buyback restriction lifts in December; Samsung's Taylor fab 2 is reported to target HBM base dies with AMD as the main potential customer (Jin10 11:07 roundup - single-source flashes, not confirmed by company filings). **Implication:** rising contract prices alongside a 4% fall in Korean memory stocks means the market is pricing regulatory and financing discounts, not a demand break. But spot DDR4 and TLC have already turned, and whether that reaches contract prices is the line between "valuation discount" and "cycle top".

**5) Houthis demand $51bn, Saudi Arabia boxed in | 11:15-11:55**
According to the Wall Street Journal, after Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers in the Red Sea and their grip on the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint, Saudi Arabia opened talks and was quoted **$51bn** in compensation plus a demand that Riyadh formally recognise Houthi rule over Yemen; neither side commented. Per ACLED, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia were near-daily in October, with 26 reported incidents inside the kingdom in recent days, including civilian airports and Aramco facilities; Houthi forces have advanced to about 50 miles from Aden (Jin10 11:55). **Implication:** this is the real source of the "Saudi energy infrastructure" risk premium, yet oil still fell 0.3% in the same hour - the market is pricing war ending, not attacks escalating. Only one of those can be right.

## 4. Assessment

**A. China assets: from "index rebound" to "structural rotation" - do not chase the index.** The evidence is three consistent readings: over 4,700 A-share advancers against +0.10% on the SSE Composite, IC/IM up nearly 2% against +0.12% on IH, and banks/coal leading declines; in Hong Kong the index is up 3% while optical modules, PCB and oil lead the downside. Money is not entering; it is changing direction inside the same pool of positions. **Implication:** CSI500/1000, power equipment and batteries, and precious metals are relatively favoured; SSE50, banks and sentiment-driven optical names are relatively exposed. **Invalidation:** if the SSE Composite closes above today's high of 3,701.59 with banks turning positive at 15:00, that is fresh money rather than rotation and the index call should be raised; if Hang Seng Tech breaks 4,000 in the afternoon (-3% from the midday close), Hong Kong's move is only an extended gap-fill.

**B. AI hardware: fundamentals improving, equities falling - this divergence buys time, not direction.** Two opposing sets of evidence. On contract prices: server DRAM still rising, enterprise SSD leading, Citi seeing undersupply through 2027. On equities: KOSPI back down to -4.36%, Nikkei -1.24%, Zhongji Innolight and YOFC leading Hong Kong's declines, and A-share money leaving CXMT, Innolight and Eoptolink. Both are true, which says the discount comes from regulatory scrutiny and financing cost rather than orders. **Trade-off:** do not short hardware that has already corrected hard, and do not bottom-fish second-tier names just because contract prices are still rising. To express a view, prefer links whose cash flow covers capex and that already own capacity - equipment and domestic substitution actually attracted inflows this hour. **Invalidation:** if spot weakness (DDR4 slightly lower, TLC -4.33% on the week) spreads into contract prices, or a hyperscaler formally cuts its 2027 capex guidance, the divergence resolves in favour of the equities and hardware exposure should be cut across the board.

**C. Gold versus oil: this hour these are not two ends of one trade - do not write it as money migrating.** Gold is +1.90% on the day and +0.17% on the hour with an intraday high of 4,146.79; crude is -1.28% on the day and -0.33% on the hour with an intraday low of 86.650. Each has its own reason: gold tracks the fall in the 10-year to 4.84% and a weaker dollar, oil tracks expectations of a US-Iran deal. The counter-evidence should be stated too: Iran's Revolutionary Guard again claimed overnight that tankers struck mines and caught fire in Hormuz and that two US drones were captured (Jin10 12:01, relaying an IRGC statement), but this repeats claims from 11-03 and 11-04, and neither Reuters nor BBC Verify found evidence for "four tankers destroyed"; UKMTO confirmed only that one tanker was hit by two unknown projectiles 14 nautical miles north-east of Limah with no casualties. **A unilateral statement like that cannot support a supply-disruption call**, and the same hour's price action shows the market is not pricing one. **Trade-off:** gold is biased higher but sits near its intraday high - do not chase above 4,130, wait for a pullback; crude is biased lower but do not short it, since 86.65 coincides with the prior session's low and is a double-bottom test rather than a break. **Invalidation:** gold below 4,100 with the 10-year back above 4.90% ends this rebound; WTI back above 88.12 (prior settle) together with a genuine drop in Hormuz transit volumes would overturn the "war premium to zero" pricing.
