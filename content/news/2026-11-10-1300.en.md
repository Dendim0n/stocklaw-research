## 2. Key incremental items

**① US pulls refuelling aircraft from a second UK base over an Iran threat; both bases raised to higher alert | 12:43 flash, 12:50 detail (Jin10 citing the Wall Street Journal)**

On Monday two KC-135 tankers based at RAF Mildenhall were rerouted to Germany, and later that day several more tankers from the same base were also moved to Germany and other locations. US officials are still assessing whether the threat is "imminent" or "general"; an Air Force spokesperson said aircraft rotate between installations for many reasons and declined to give details. The Pentagon had already withdrawn B-1 bombers from RAF Fairford earlier. **Independent cross-check**: TWZ and the BBC both confirm both bases were raised to Charlie alert status with unusual departures, but state plainly that this "could fit preparations for a large-scale air operation, but it can also be a defensive dispersal or a planned exercise" — no official link to strike preparations.

**Impact**: the direction is opposite to last night's "Trump is in no hurry to resume strikes on Iran". This is **option value on the risk premium, not a supply disruption**. Oil did not rise but drifted slightly lower this hour, which is itself information: a real supply shock requires Hormuz transit volumes to fall, not base alert levels to rise.

**② October LNG departures through Hormuz hit 31 laden carriers, the most since the war began in February | 12:59 (Jin10 citing S&P Global Energy and ship-tracking data)**

**23** loaded at Qatar's Ras Laffan and **8** at Das Island in the UAE, above September's 23. S&P Global Energy's analyst said UAE transits are back to pre-conflict levels while Qatari transits have recovered to more than **30%** of pre-conflict levels. Context matters: the US naval blockade of Iran is still in force, and these are outbound cargoes.

**Impact**: the counter-evidence inside the same hour — threats escalating while actual flows recover. It lowers the weight on "the strait gets blocked", which is fundamentally bearish for oil and gas, and it weakens the geopolitical premium narrative in item ①. Only by holding both items together can one explain heavy geopolitical headlines against flat crude.

**③ JGB 30-year yield rises 2.5bp to 3.865% after a weak auction; December hike priced near 77% | 12:03, 12:20**

The bid-to-cover was **3.36** against 3.88 at the prior auction and a **3.6** twelve-month average, with the yield rising afterwards; USD/JPY was 157.976 at the same time. Jin10's 12:20 detail adds two points: last week's 10-year auction was also soft, and **swaps price roughly 77% odds of a BOJ hike at the December meeting**.

**Impact**: another leg of global long-end yield pressure, driven by fiscal sustainability and central-bank normalisation rather than US inflation — a discount-rate headwind for gold and high-multiple growth, and support for the yen. **This is the only item this hour that changed the pricing logic**, yet gold did not fall, which says part of the pressure is already in the price.

**④ Anthropic IPO: formal marketing as early as Tuesday, Nasdaq listing before Thanksgiving, Dimon livestream Friday | published 11:59:59 (Bloomberg, via Jin10)**

Dario Amodei will hold a livestreamed conversation with JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon around lunchtime Friday in New York as part of the IPO marketing effort; Bloomberg reports formal marketing could begin as early as Tuesday, with pricing and a Nasdaq debut before Thanksgiving (11/26). **No price range has been disclosed** — the "70–80 dollars, $69.3bn" figures retracted in the previous window cannot be reused. **Impact**: the previous brief's "the valuation anchor has been pushed back" is partly repaired — the timetable is clear again, the price is still blank. Positive for private-market AI sentiment; no direct cash-flow effect on listed hardware leaders, so do not read it as demand evidence.

**⑤ Japan's Rapidus falls behind on 2nm mass production, no working SRAM cell yet | 12:37 (Jin10 citing people familiar)**

The SRAM milestone that was due this summer has not been met, and mass production of cutting-edge chips by March 2028 is "unlikely to be met on schedule", putting the company at least several months behind.

**Impact**: the timetable for diversifying compute supply slips further, reinforcing scarcity for existing foundry and GPU suppliers near term. But it is a non-event for current orders and revenue — it only changes supply assumptions for 2027 and beyond. On the same timeline, ④ and ⑤ both point to "existing suppliers are scarcer", not "demand is larger".

**B | Gold: cut from bearish to neutral — rates still press, but the price has stopped falling.**

Evidence: +0.08% this hour (4,110.28→4,113.41), the first positive hour since 08:00 in Asia; silver +0.24% in parallel with the gold/silver ratio nearly unchanged at 77.45, so this is precious metals stabilising rather than gold alone bouncing; the intraday low of 4,108.13 has not been retested, and the 14:05 check shows 4,124.06 with the day loss narrowed to 0.41%. The pressure is real and simultaneous: JGB 30-year at 3.865%, ~77% odds of a December BOJ hike, USD/JPY at 158.02 — real rates are still heading up. **Price diverging from rates means the market is paying an insurance premium for some risk**, and that is precisely the wrong setup to short on a rates argument. **Trade-off**: neither short nor long; let the 21:30 CPI decide. **Confirmation / invalidation**: core month-on-month at or above 0.3% with gold losing 4,108 returns the call to bearish, with only a directional reference near 4,070 below (the two sources' closing bases differ by about $15); core at or below 0.2% with gold back above 4,140 turns neutral into bullish. **Evidence gap**: no real-time TIPS real yield or dollar index on this machine, so "real rates rising" cannot be separated from "risk premium expanding" — the two have opposite implications for persistence.

Evidence: Nasdaq futures repaired from -0.38% at 12:04 to -0.19% at 13:09, while S&P futures were +0.12% and Dow futures +0.33% — megacaps still lag; items ④ and ⑤ cut both ways and both point to scarcer existing supply rather than a larger demand pool; the equal-weight-over-cap-weight record from the previous brief is intact, so the rotation structure has not broken. **Trade-off**: do not chase the AI hardware bounce and do not short the index; the real test is the 22:30 open — whether NVDA reclaims 180 and the SOX index gets back above 10,033. **Invalidation**: core CPI at or above 0.3%, another leg up in the discount rate, hardware de-rates first and even equal-weight cannot hold.
