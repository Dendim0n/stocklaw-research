## Assessment

**A | Precious metals: this is deleveraging, not a new narrative — short-term bearish, and 3,800 is not support.**
Evidence: gold and silver fell together but silver's decline was 2.2x gold's, with palladium, copper and tin weaker in parallel; all three dollar crosses moved less than 0.1% (EUR -0.08%, JPY +0.03%, CNH +0.02%), so a "strong dollar" explanation does not hold, and no single news item explains the acceleration between 10:05 and 10:06. Goldman's 10:08 note supplies the mechanism: CTA and macro funds face drawdown limits and are forced to cut when price moves against them, while COMEX speculative positioning sits only at the 45th percentile — extreme crowding is not required for trend-following stop-losses to amplify a move.
View: **short-term bearish; do not buy the dip.** 3,800 is a psychological level only; the one reference with an actual sample is today's low of **3,792.12**, and a reclaim of **3,832.20** (the 11-16 low) would be needed to call it repaired. **Invalidation/confirmation**: dovish minutes at 03:00 on 11-19 plus a softer dollar and a move back above 3,832 = liquidation over; hawkish minutes and silver below 45.93 = the liquidity squeeze moves down a notch.

**B | Rates and Asian equities: the BOJ adds a hawkish leg, and the weak-index/strong-structure mix should persist.**
Evidence: Ueda's explicit 10:49 call to keep hiking points the same way as the 96.8% CME probability of a December Fed hike and the 5.10% 10-year yield (11-17 Treasury curve); Asian indices turned from gains to losses (Hang Seng -1.01%, Shanghai -0.30%), yet STAR chips, PCB and computing-power names rose, with Zijin Mining's RMB 795m outflow contrasting with Huagong Tech's RMB 564m inflow.
View: **neutral-to-bearish on the indices, relatively positive on AI hardware** — but this is intra-market rotation, not fresh money, so it is not a signal to buy the index; the Japanese hiking bias weighs more on Japanese equities than on A-shares. **Invalidation/confirmation**: Hang Seng breaking today's low of 23,248.50 while A-share computing names weaken = rotation turns into a broad selloff; turnover keeps expanding and STAR chips hold their 2% gain = rotation continues.

**C | Crude: do not bet on direction before the EIA; the 5.278m API build is only half the evidence.**
Evidence: WTI broke 83.80 and recovered, Brent was unchanged on the hour and the same-month spread held at 8.76; the API build is large while the calendar consensus is only +58k — expectations are already near zero growth, so unless the EIA shows a large draw, the build thesis stands. On the geopolitical side (Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia's east-west pipeline, tankers hitting mines east of Hormuz) nothing has produced verifiable lost output or lower loadings.
View: **neutral; no directional bet.** If forced to choose, inventory evidence outweighs the geopolitical premium — mildly bearish, but with limited room. **Invalidation/confirmation**: an EIA build in the same direction → the Brent premium gives back within a week; Cushing continues to draw (API -1.691m) → range-bound rather than bearish; evidence of lower loadings or damaged facilities → the view is overturned.
