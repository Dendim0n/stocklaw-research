## 🔥 Key Overnight Developments

**🛢️ Red Sea Situation Escalates Sharply**

The Saudi-led coalition conducted airstrikes on Houthi military targets in Hodeidah, Yemen, calling it a "proportionate military response." A Saudi commercial vessel, NCC MASA, was attacked in the Red Sea sustaining minor damage. Iran's Tasnim News reported that the US Fifth Fleet base in Bahrain was targeted by missiles and drones. Strait of Hormuz transit volume dropped 60% on July 24, while Bab el-Mandeb traffic partially recovered. Shipping is entering a phase of "cautious but selective resumption."

**⚔️ Trump Convenes Advisors on Iran Military Escalation**

Trump met with senior advisors and cabinet officials Friday to decide whether to intensify military strikes on Iran. Trump stated he is "ready for higher-level military action against Iran" while also noting "Iran is becoming more serious in negotiations." The NYT reported US intelligence assesses Iran's new leadership has greater nuclear ambitions. The fragile ceasefire and memorandum of understanding have both collapsed.

**📉 Oil Plunges Despite Tensions: WTI -3.12%**

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The Middle East is entering a dangerous new phase with both the Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz under simultaneous stress.

Yet oil's counterintuitive selloff Friday suggests markets are pricing in "contained conflict" — the Saudi strikes deliberately avoided port facilities, and Trump is signaling openness to negotiations.

The semiconductor rout deserves close attention. The across-the-board collapse in memory chip stocks hints at a market reassessment of the AI hardware cycle thesis.

The core tension in markets right now is not direction but the tug-of-war between geopolitical risk premiums and tech valuation corrections.

The VIX remains in neutral territory, but the Fear & Greed Index at 39.4 indicates broadly cautious sentiment.

Next week's FOMC decision is the single largest certainty event — markets are likely to remain rangebound until then.

Gold stabilizing near $4,050 with safe-haven demand in greed territory suggests capital is still seeking safety.
