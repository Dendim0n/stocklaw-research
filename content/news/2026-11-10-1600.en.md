## 2. Key incremental items

**① Saudi Bahri runs four tankers through Hormuz under US coordination, Middle East loadings near pre-war; Abu Dhabi talks open 15:35 | 15:00, 15:35–15:39**

Khaled Al-Hammad, head of the ship management arm at Saudi National Shipping (Bahri), said in Dubai that the company is working with the US government to guarantee tanker passage and **sent four tankers through the Strait of Hormuz today**, aiming to bring Saudi export volumes close to pre-war levels; he added that tanker transits have risen markedly over recent months and **total Middle East oil loadings are now close to pre-conflict levels** (Jin10 15:00). Half an hour later the trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia talks formally opened in Abu Dhabi; Ukraine's Umerov said the first session was held with the UK, France, Germany, Italy, NATO and the European Commission, with Witkoff and Kushner attending, followed by the trilateral session, with the agenda covering a halt to hostilities and post-war security guarantees (15:35–15:39). A Russian delegation source said Moscow sees "a new European security framework" as the main focus.

**Impact**: Supply-side and geopolitical bearish news landed together — not new barrels, but **the basis for pricing strait-disruption risk has been weakened**. Four tankers are marginal to global daily supply, though: crude only printed its low eight minutes after the talks opened (15:43) and recovered **more than $0.50** within the hour (85.47 at 15:55), which says the market accepts that "the premium should come down", not that "supply is in surplus". Bearish crude; for tanker rates this is a mix of bearish and bullish, so do not push it one way.

**② SoftBank posts a ¥1.12tn net loss in Q2, raises $5bn against Arm stock | 14:43, 15:07, 15:52**

SoftBank booked a **¥1.12tn (about $7.1bn) loss** in its fiscal second quarter (Jul–Sep), its first net loss in nearly two years; the Vision Fund segment lost **¥81.6bn**, with declines in Arm, Intel and Coupang dragging results (Jin10 14:43). Another flash in the same window put the **half-year loss through September at ¥597.2bn** — the two figures do not simply add up, and with no access to SoftBank's original filing this desk does not extrapolate. The company disclosed at 15:07 that in **September 2026 it raised $5bn by increasing margin loans secured on Arm shares**; CFO Yoshimitsu Goto said at 15:52 that "the scramble for data-centre materials has already begun" and that "power-generation equipment has long lead times, so it is better to buy early". SoftBank stock is down more than **50%** from its June record high and is treated by the market as a bellwether for OpenAI and AI sentiment.

**Impact**: The information here is not the loss but **the financing method** — borrowing against Arm stock to fund data centres puts the leverage of AI capex on top of its most important listed asset. That is a second category of risk beyond "is AI demand real": **even if demand is delivered, the financing structure can amplify the drawdown**. Bearish SoftBank and Arm; for AI hardware this is sentiment pressure, not an order-book event.

**③ Infineon lifts FY2027 data-centre revenue to about €4.0bn | 14:47**

Infineon expects data-centre revenue in the fiscal year ending next September to reach **about €4.0bn**; the prior guidance was about **€2.5bn** for AI data-centre power solutions plus roughly **€0.5bn** a year from traditional data centres — i.e. an upgrade of about €1.0bn over the previously implied €3.0bn. The CEO said revenue and profitability should grow significantly in FY2027, with power chips among the main drivers.

**Impact**: This directly conflicts with ② — **financing is tightening downstream while order guidance is being raised upstream**. Power semiconductors are one of the tightest links in the data-centre chain, and guidance like this usually reflects actual pull-in earlier than cloud capex disclosures do. It is a plus for INTC and the power chain and a counterpoint to the "AI demand has peaked" narrative; but an FY2027 guideline cannot be verified against current cash flow and is not a quarterly result.

## 3. Assessment

**A | Crude: bearish stands, but the odds have clearly worsened — do not chase short near 85.**

Evidence: WTI settled the hour at 85.254, down 1.29% on the day, having broken the 85.52 level the prior issue identified as the trigger, down to 84.8–84.9, with the range widening from 1.6% to 2.42% — the prior issue's condition ("break 85.52 and see API builds") **was half triggered**: the price broke, but the inventory data is not due until 05:30 tomorrow, and the market recovered more than $0.50 within an hour of the break. Supply has genuinely progressed (Bahri transits, Middle East loadings near pre-war), while demand remains a counterpoint (China's October crude imports +13% month on month, as reported earlier). **Trade-off**: the bearish direction is unchanged, but shorting here is worse odds than waiting for a bounce into 86.3–86.9 (today's open 86.43, NYMEX prior settle 86.85); until 84.85 is decisively broken this is the bottom of a range, not the start of a trend. **Confirmation / invalidation**: a WTI close below 84.85 plus a smaller-than-expected draw at 05:30 (consensus −327k barrels) → the next leg opens; real progress in the talks (a ceasefire framework) → the war premium compresses further and you can follow without chasing; if the talks collapse or new evidence of obstructed transits appears → this call is void and the bounce targets back above 87.

**B | Gold: no hourly direction at all — do not trade direction inside 4,108–4,146.**

Evidence: −0.25% this hour (4,125.10→4,114.65), a second consecutive hourly decline; at 15:46 the dollar index was 101.55, down 0.12 points, so **the dollar weakened while gold fell, meaning this hour was not dollar-driven**, and there is no new rate data to explain it either. More troubling, the call itself has flipped twice in four hours: the 13:00 issue upgraded from bearish to neutral on "the Asian session stopped the decline", and the afternoon's grind lower has now voided that basis. Price has spent nearly nine hours inside the 4,108–4,146 day range without choosing a direction (high at 07:12, low at 11:49), and **continuing to oscillate between bearish and neutral is not honest** — the correct conclusion is that, until the range breaks, hourly directional calls have no edge. **Trade-off**: no chasing either way; leave direction to the 21:30 CPI. If a view must be expressed, express it as a range rather than a direction (below 4,108 is the bear confirmation zone, above 4,146 the bull zone). **Confirmation / invalidation**: core month-on-month ≥0.3% with gold losing 4,108 → back to bearish, with 4,070 as a directional reference only (the two sources' closing bases differ by about $15); core ≤0.2% with gold above 4,146 → turn bullish. **Evidence gap**: this host still has no TIPS real yields or positioning data, so "risk-premium expansion" cannot be separated from "real rates rising" — and the two have opposite implications for gold's durability.

**C | AI: buy the link, not the whole — the relatively bearish hardware call just got new evidence.**

Evidence: Dow futures +0.48%, S&P +0.22%, Nasdaq just +0.01%, with heavyweights still a drag, in the same direction as the prior issue's "equal-weight outperformance versus cap-weight the widest since 2009"; the three AI items this hour split — SoftBank's large loss and $5bn Arm-backed borrowing (financing tightening), Infineon raising data-centre guidance (orders improving), and Samsung/SK Hynix surging (memory singled out for buying). **Trade-off**: do not chase a broad AI hardware bounce and do not short the index; if you want exposure on this theme, order visibility (power, memory) beats valuation elasticity (compute leaders). The real test is the 22:30 open: whether NVDA reclaims 180 and whether the SOX index gets back above 10,033. **Invalidation**: core CPI ≥0.3% pushes discount rates up again, hardware de-rates first, and equal-weight cannot insulate itself either; conversely core ≤0.2% and the rotation may converge back toward the leaders.
