## 3. Judgements and counter-evidence

**A. US equities: this is war-premium unwind plus short covering, not a fundamental turn — do not chase.**

Three supporting points. **First**, the driver was a single second-hand report at 03:39, not any economic release, and it was partly contradicted by Trump's "blockade continues" remarks at 05:36 — yet price did not give the gains back, which points to positioning rather than new information. **Second**, the volume ratio was **0.90x** (3.555bn vs a 3.936bn five-day average); a 2.34% gain with no volume confirmation, against 5.040bn shares (1.28x) on the 11-05 crash day. **Third**, rates moved the other way (2y +5bp, December-hike odds 84.2%); if this were a fundamental improvement, the front end would not be turning hawkish at the same time.

Counter-evidence against this judgement, which must be stated: the S&P **opened at the session low**, ground higher all day and closed near the high (7,549.94 vs a 7,577.39 high) — not a typical one-day short-covering shape; VIX fell 22.31% to 19.92, back below 20, so the fear premium is largely gone; and from the 11-05 low the index is already **+2.67%**, having retraced **61.3%** of the crash.

**Confirmation / invalidation (no trade instructions, only observable tests)**: if the S&P holds above **7,422.24** (today's open, which was also the low) after CPI and the 2-year stays below **4.60%**, the "event repair" reading holds and 7,577 becomes near-term resistance rather than a top. If a hot CPI pushes the 2-year through 4.60%, today's entire gain becomes suspect — because the rally had no rates support and rates will move first on the way down.

**B. Semis: SOX +6.85% against NVDA −4.27% is the most important structural signal of the night.**

In one session the SOX rose 6.85% with AMD +13.26%, AVGO +8.78%, MU +6.25% and TSM +3.51%, while the sector's leader fell 4.27%. That is not a broad sector rally; it is **rotation inside the sector** — out of the single name carrying concentrated governance risk and into the rest of the AI hardware beta. **Supporting**: Taiwan's October exports +58.9% and imports +75.1% (reported in the previous issue, not repeated here) say physical orders are intact and what is broken is Nvidia's governance discount. **Counter**: if it were pure rotation, NVDA's decline should be contained, but it fell as much as 8.79% intraday and sits 17.03% below its 10-29 high, and a congressional subpoena is a new procedural risk that could run for weeks rather than days.

**Tiered view**: AI hardware beta (foundry, memory, analog, optics) is investable in this cycle, because demand evidence (exports, imports, SK Hynix +5.5%) and price now point the same way; Nvidia itself does not yet qualify as "down enough to be an opportunity" until the investigation and subpoena resolve, because price has already discounted part of the governance risk in that 17% drawdown, but **the timeline of a congressional process is not modelable** — it is a variable no valuation framework can price. **Caveat**: this describes relative strength only, not a view on the semiconductor cycle — one 6.85% session proves no industry trend.

**C. Gold: the reason it rose is not haven demand, which determines that it now trades with equities rather than against them.**

Facts: spot gold 4,136 (+1.61% vs Friday) on a night when VIX fell 22.31%, the S&P rose 2.34%, silver rose 2.12% (more than gold), the gold/silver ratio fell from 78.20 to 77.45, and the dollar index fell **0.63%** to 101.657 (04:22, New York close). **Of those four variables, the haven and fear measures (VIX, gold/silver ratio) both point the other way**; the only aligned drivers are dollar weakness and inflation expectations stuck at 3.9%.

**Counter-evidence against this judgement**: if gold were trading inflation, then on a night when crude fell 5.85% and petrol is about to break $4/gallon, inflation expectations should be falling rather than holding; yet the NY Fed 1-year expectation came in at 3.9% against a 3.78% consensus, October PPI ran at 6.3% with Friday's consensus at 6.6% — **the stickiness comes from services and AI capex, not from energy**, which is exactly what Goolsbee described. So gold's bull case and the equity bull case are not contradictory; they are two sides of the same bet on demand overheating.

**An honest correction of the previous call**: the 11-09 16:00 issue said "neutral, do not buy above 4,100, the repair thesis is void below 4,070.7". Gold never broke 4,070.7; it went from 4,089 to 4,136 (+1.14%). **The "wait for a bid" stance missed a 1.1% move.** The three divergence signals cited then (silver not falling, dollar weak, thin domestic premium) were individually correct but the conclusion was drawn the wrong way — the divergence could be read as "gold is weak" or as "silver is catching up", and ex post it was the latter.

**D. Crude: bearish maintained, but the risk/reward on shorts has deteriorated because price has priced "peace" more fully than official statements have.**

The facts are clean: settlements −4.38% (WTI) / −5.85% (Brent), post-settlement low 90.842 at 03:50; all three supply-side items point to looser balances (OPEC +1.38 mb/d to 90% of pre-war, SPR at a 1982 low meaning no buffer, pipeline attack with no operational impact). **But** Trump said at 05:36–05:51 that the blockade continues and no deal is done, and crude only recovered from 90.84 to 92.07 — **it did not retrace**. The market's pricing of a deal now runs ahead of the official line.

**That implies asymmetry**: if a deal does land there is another leg down (Hormuz reopening plus $6–9bn of unfrozen assets is real new supply/macro relief); if talks collapse, the upside from 90.84 is larger than the downside that remains. **Confirmation / invalidation**: a further leg down needs Brent to close consecutively below **90.84** (today's post-settlement low) with rising API/EIA inventories on Thursday; the bearish case is void on consecutive closes above **98.32** (Friday's settle). **Why no directional bet**: both paths hinge on a single political event and sit outside what data can resolve.

## 5. Data limitations
