## 4. Reading the situation

**Call one: crude is short-term bearish, but do not chase it below $99.** The condition and the execution have to be separated: Iran said at 15:43 the strait cannot reopen without sanctions relief and at 15:54 it could be implemented within two days — and between those two statements no corresponding US action was announced. The physical side runs the other way: ADNOC's near-1bn-barrel inventory draw, 35-year-low diesel stocks and logistics at 30% of per-barrel cost, while OPEC+ kept December quotas unchanged even as Kuwait raised October output to **2.10m b/d** (the highest since the war began) and Kazakhstan lifted October output **8% month on month to 2.08m b/d**. The price has already discounted a deal being reached; if an Israeli strike lands after 11-3 (Netanyahu pressing, Trump saying on 11-1 that "at some point we'll have to use the hammer"), the premium has to be added back. Impact: lower crude helps airlines, chemicals and importers such as India (whose 10-year yield fell 10bp on the move at 19:24) and hurts tankers and upstream. **Invalidation: a specific US sanctions-relief list, or an independently measured recovery in Hormuz transits, opens the downside toward ANZ's earlier 90–95 range; the opposite confirmation is Brent back above 112.**

**Call two: AI hardware is neutral-to-bearish — do not chase the bounce.** Two opposing signals coexist in the same hour: MRVL is paying $115bn in stock for capacity, which says orders are still being placed, while Melius cut NVDA and AVGO targets by a quarter, which says the market will no longer pay a premium. More telling is that ECB Vice-President Vujčić said at 18:19 that "AI valuations" are among the risks highlighted in the financial-stability assessment — an official view, not a sell-side one. Friday's Intel guidance gap says the PC side is deteriorating while AI demand has not been disproven, which makes this divergence rather than liquidation. Impact: do not chase the bounce; wait for AMD's report tomorrow (consensus EPS 1.95, revenue $13.09bn) and Apple's guidance tonight. **Confirmation: both beat on revenue and guidance and the repair continues; invalidation: guidance disappoints, the capex-to-revenue lag gets repriced and hardware retests.**

**Call three: gold is neutral — chase neither direction.** The driver has changed: today's +0.63% tracks the weaker dollar (DXY at a one-month low of 101.43), not haven buying — silver's +1.85% outperformance and the narrowing gold/silver ratio also point to a liquidity/dollar driver. But the carrying cost has not eased: 10-year 4.94%, 2-year 4.56%, Logan still saying "at least two more", and an ECB governing council member saying at 19:22 that action is possible at every meeting. Impact: use today's high of **3,916.90** and the 10-30 settlement of **3,876.89** as the observation levels and do nothing in between; **a close above the former means the repair is upgrading, a break of the latter confirms the downtrend and requires reassessing the next psychological round number near 3,800.**
