## 1. What happened in this window

**The Fed held rates at 3.75-4.00%, but both dissents wanted a hike; Warsh gave no forward guidance and said the 2% target has no "soft version". The market's answer was not buy or sell — it was to steepen the curve: 2Y 4.67% (-3bp), 10Y 5.05% (+6bp), 30Y 5.37% (+7bp), 2s10s from 29bp to 38bp; DXY fell from 102.4153 at 16:00 yesterday to 102.1458 (-0.26%); spot gold printed $4,054.36 (-0.35%).**

The information that actually matters came from earnings, not the statement. **Alphabet reported revenue of $111.2bn (ex-partner payments) and EPS of $2.12, both below expectations, kept 2026 capex at $195-205bn with a sharp 2027 increase, disclosed more than $1trn of future commitments, leases and purchases, and management itself said TPU expansion would pressure free cash flow and that Q4 margins would take a hit from using third-party cloud capacity; the stock fell 7.9% after hours and at one point more than 10%. Microsoft supplied the mirror image the same night: Azure +48% to $33.17bn (vs $32.84bn expected), capex $45.2bn against its own $50bn guide, free cash flow $18.6bn against $8.54bn expected; the stock rose nearly 6% after hours.**

Stance: **you cannot conclude today that the AI bubble has burst; you can only conclude that the chain is being re-ranked by cash-flow quality.** What the market rejected was not AI capex but AI capex with no visible cash return. At the index level, Nasdaq futures -0.99% versus S&P futures -0.39%, a gap driven mainly by Alphabet's weight (jin10 at 06:09 explicitly attributed the move to Alphabet's after-hours slide) — that is not a broad sell-off. On rates, 10Y 5.05% plus 30Y 5.37% leaves no room for a discount-rate reprieve for long-duration growth. Gold is consolidating in a narrow band near $4,050; with core PCE due tonight, neither chasing the short nor bottom-fishing is justified. Crude's war premium has given back 0.87% from the 102.780 settlement; no chasing longs.

## 4. Evidence-based views and their counter-arguments

**View 1: this is "the front end accepting the pause while the long end adds an inflation premium", not a recovery in risk appetite.**
The evidence is curve shape, not price direction: 2Y -3bp, 30Y +7bp, 2s10s +9bp. A pure risk-off move would have taken the long end down. **Implication: at 10Y 5.05% there is no discount-rate escape hatch for long-duration growth; any "rate-cut trade" assumption fails on this curve.**
Counter: 5.05% embeds a war-premium inflation expectation (World Bank +23% commodities, oil at 101-102). If tonight's core PCE prints below 0.3% m/m, the long end can give back those 6bp quickly and the "higher long end = tightening" inference weakens on the spot.

**View 2: the AI capex split has moved from narrative into line items, but the direction is divergence, not collapse.**
Alphabet's penalty is threefold: revenue/EPS miss, capex rising rather than falling, and management **voluntarily** conceding that TPU expansion pressures FCF and Q4 margins. Microsoft is the exact opposite on the same statement: Azure beat, capex below its own guide, FCF more than double consensus. **Same night, same rate environment, a 16-percentage-point after-hours pricing gap (-10% vs +6%).**
Counter: Microsoft's capex miss is slippage of third-party capacity, and the company says capex accelerates in the second half to about $170bn this calendar year — **part of that $18.6bn FCF is timing**, so it cannot be used to argue a structurally healthier cash profile. Likewise, Oracle's $30bn is an **FY2029 target**, not realised cash.

**View 3: an index decline is not a broad market decline, and there is no evidence today for an "AI bubble burst".**
NQ -0.99% vs ES -0.39%, a 0.6pp gap directly tied to Alphabet's weight (jin10's 06:09 attribution). In the same window SK Hynix +13%, Micron +6%, Oracle +10.5%, Microsoft +6% after hours — money is rotating **inside** the AI chain. **The defensible conclusion is re-ranking by cash-flow quality, not a broken chain.**
Counter (the one thing to watch today): if tonight's cash session spreads Alphabet's decline to Microsoft and Oracle — indiscriminate selling rather than divergence — views 2 and 3 are refuted the same day.

**View 4: gold's -0.35% runs against the safe-haven story, and the only consistent explanation is real rates.**
10Y 5.05% and 30Y 5.37% raise the cost of holding; at the same time SPDR added 1.71t. **Rates pressing down while ETFs stay put means this is rate pricing outweighing haven pricing, not capital leaving.** Today's range is only 4,050.89-4,069.20 ($18.3, 0.45%) — narrow: $4,050 is not a "bottom" and -0.35% is not a "breakdown".
Counter: if gold closes higher while the 10Y keeps rising, haven/de-dollarisation buying is back in charge and the real-rate framework should be down-weighted.

**View 5: crude's "-0.125%" and "-0.87%" must be read separately.**
WTI's 10-28 settlement area is **102.780** (source field); this morning it is 101.883, a **0.87%** give-back. jin10's platform shows only -0.125% because its basis is the 06:00 rollover print of 101.243. **Reading -0.125% as "oil did not move today" is wrong; reading -0.87% as "trend reversal" is overreach.** The facts: yesterday's settlement was the highest close of this war-premium leg, and overnight it gave back modestly on "contact confirmed, proposal denied". The OPEC+ Sunday meeting (yesterday's 15:33 source: output targets unchanged) has not happened yet.
Counter: a positive Iranian answer to the Qatari proposal on Thursday could accelerate the premium unwind; a rejection, or new trouble in Hormuz (US Central Command said at 03:21 that "all channels are free of mines", i.e. it was answering rumours), would refill the gap below 101 quickly.
