## 🔥 Key News This Hour

### 1. Fed's Bostic Strikes Hawkish Tone: Inflation Too High, No Rate Guidance

Fed's Bostic made several important statements:

- **Inflation stance hawkish**: "Economy is strong, inflation is too high"; "want to see more progress on core inflation"
- **Rate guidance**: "Cannot provide rate guidance at this time, keeping an open mind"
- **Policy assessment**: "Data suggests Fed policy is moderately restrictive"; "If no progress on inflation, may need to recalibrate policy"
- **Energy impact**: "Energy prices are volatile, data suggests their impact can be temporarily ignored"
- **Meeting frequency**: Wants to weigh pros and cons of 6 vs 8 FOMC meetings per year

Bostic's overall tone is hawkish, emphasizing inflation remains high and policy restrictiveness is only "moderate," implying current rate levels may be insufficient to quickly bring down inflation.

### 2. US June Trade Deficit Narrows to $73.3B, Imports Fall for First Time This Year

US Commerce Department released June trade data:

- **Trade deficit**: $73.3B (expected $73.0B, prior $77.6B), narrowing 5.6%
- **Imports**: $3,880B (prior $3,953B), first decline since start of year, across multiple sectors
- **Exports**: $3,147B (prior $3,177B)

The import decline may reflect the fading effect of front-loaded inventory buildup by businesses. The narrower trade deficit is a positive contribution to Q3 GDP.

### 3. Anthropic Signs $10 Billion Compute Deal

Anthropic reached a $10 billion compute agreement with Volta Infra Holdings, an infrastructure startup founded just months ago, to meet growing product demand. The AI compute arms race continues to escalate.

### 4. US June Oil Import Prices Up 37.6% Year-over-Year

US June oil import price was $86.06/barrel, up 37.6% from $62.53 in June 2025 (May was $87.44). Despite the recent crash in international oil prices, the lag effect of import prices means US consumers and businesses still face high energy costs in the near term.

### 5. Bessent's Additional Comments: Stabilizing Yen Crucial

Treasury Secretary Bessent continued his CNBC interview with new incremental comments:

- **Yen**: "Stabilizing the yen is crucial"; "Japan currently faces inflation issues" (due to weak yen)
- **Japan policy**: Optimistic about Japan's policy path
- **Inflation**: Core inflation is easing (Wash's flexibility angle already reported prior hour, not repeated)
