**③ Korea's semiconductor exports for the first ten days of November rose +331.2%; full-year exports will top $1trn early｜10:44, Korea Customs Service preliminary data**
Exports for November 1–10 were **$33.5bn, +111.9% y/y** — the highest for that period on record and the second-highest of any ten-day window ever (behind $35.1bn in early September); with 7.5 working days versus 7.0 a year ago, the daily average rose **+97.7% to $4.46bn**. Semiconductors were **$16.6bn, +331.2%**, a record for the period and the largest gain among major categories. Year-to-date exports reached **$963.7bn**, on pace to cross $1trn **around November 20**. Same-day Q3 data: exports of $318.6bn (+72.3%, the highest quarter since records began in 2015), semiconductors $148.0bn (+219%) at **46.5%** of the total versus 25.1% a year earlier; the top ten exporters **exceeded 60%** of exports for the first time, with large firms +103.5% and SMEs +12.8% and +10.7%.
Impact: the hardest demand evidence of the hour, and a direct rebuttal of "AI capex has peaked". But the same dataset states the risk: **one category at 46.5% of exports and ten companies above 60%** makes concentration an amplifier in both directions.

**④ Trump–Putin diesel deal delivers nothing after a month; tankers keep diverting｜10:52, Jin10 ID 234900**

The Agioi Fanendes, carrying about 300,000 barrels, had New York as its destination and has now re-declared for South America; three other Russian diesel tankers diverted last week (two to Brazil, one to Egypt). The US has not imported Russian diesel since April 2022; domestic refineries are self-sufficient and still export more than 1.5m bpd of diesel. Diesel is around **$6.10/gal** this week, having topped $6.50 in September.
Impact: a concrete source of core-inflation stickiness — Timiraos also notes crude has fallen more than 20% in recent weeks while transport costs have not followed. It is hawkish for the rate path and supportive for refining margins; it does not constitute a crude supply gap.
