## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**🛢️ Oil Crash: US-Iran Ceasefire Sends Crude Down 8.6%**

After 13 days of intense mutual strikes, the US and Iran announced a pause in air operations, triggering an immediate oil selloff. WTI tumbled from 84.40 to 81.26, while Brent lost the $85 level. This is the largest single-day drop since the Middle East crisis began. The key question: is this a genuine turning point, or just a breather before the next leg up?

**⛽ Supply Double Hit: Kazakhstan Output Halved**

CPC export terminal closure has slashed Kazakhstan's Sunday oil output to roughly 1 million b/d — more than halved from June averages. Supply contraction would normally support prices, but it's being completely overwhelmed by ceasefire sentiment.

**🥇 Gold Rises Defiantly: Safe-Haven Logic Shifts to Fed Anxiety**

Despite the Middle East ceasefire theoretically reducing gold's safe-haven appeal, prices rose 1% to $4,096. The market is rotating focus to this week's Fed decision — Warsh's refusal to provide forward guidance, dubbed a "policy black box" by Wall Street, is driving uncertainty-based gold allocation.

**🏛️ Fed Week: Warsh "Black Box" Strategy Rattles Wall Street**

Fed Chair Warsh has abandoned traditional forward guidance, refusing to telegraph policy direction. This week's rate decision is a complete blind box, with markets struggling to price appropriately. Commodity traders are closely watching historical patterns of Fed-day asset moves.

**🇯🇵 Japan Politics: Takaichi Approval Hits Record Low**

Plagued by surging living costs and yen weakness, PM Takaichi's approval rating has fallen to its lowest since taking office. Analysts warn that to regain public support, the government may be forced into aggressive measures, putting Japanese assets under pressure.

**🇬🇧 UK's New PM: Will Publicly Confront Trump If National Interest at Stake**

Newly installed PM Burnham has drawn a hard line on transatlantic relations, stating he will not hesitate to push back against Trump when core national interests are involved. The BoE is expected to hold rates at 7-2 this Thursday.
