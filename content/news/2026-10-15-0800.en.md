## Situation Assessment (evidence, counter-evidence, affected assets, invalidation)

**View 1: The CPI outcome is not "inflation out of control" but "the Fed is forced to hike sooner, which makes long-run inflation more controllable" — hence richly valued growth and AI hardware are the main casualties while long-end Treasuries rallied.**

Evidence: headline 3.7%, core 2.6% and core m/m 0.3% all beat consensus, pushing the October hike probability from 22% to above 50% intraday; yet by the close the 2Y was up 12.72bp while the 10Y and 30Y closed 2.7bp and 3.8bp lower, VIX rose only to 16.87, and the S&P closed at -1.10% rather than in panic territory. Several independent statements support one reading: Hirtle argued Warsh would judge that hiking "can stabilise long-end Treasury yields"; TD Securities called a 50/50 October probability "fairly reasonable"; and official language (Daly, Bowman, Barkin, Goolsbee) consistently landed on "further adjustment may be needed, but not at the October meeting".

Counter-evidence and invalidation: (i) if tonight's PPI (20:30) and retail sales stay strong, the market will shift from "wait" to "October has to move", at which point the long end could sell off in tandem, the curve could bear-steepen and growth pressure would escalate; (ii) the probability data itself conflicts — Jin10's 06:08 flash says 33.2% while its 07:06 futures breakfast cites 52.9%, two values from one platform; this brief uses the 06:08 figure and flags it as unverified; (iii) Warsh speaks publicly in Bangkok at 10-16 11:30, the last window before the blackout, and if he explicitly rules out October the "faster hikes" premise weakens. Affected assets: long-duration growth, AI hardware and pre-profit assets relatively bearish; short-end Treasuries bearish; long duration and gold's relative allocation value higher.

**View 2: The AI hardware selloff is an event in rates and positioning, not a deterioration in fundamentals — but my evidence only supports "neither chase the drop nor buy it", not a strong call in either direction.**

Evidence: in-window on one side, CoreWeave -15.46%, Oracle -8.83%, Dell -11.27%, the memory chain -4% to -6% and Korea pre-market Samsung -6% / SK hynix -7%; on the other side, Anthropic's Q3 revenue of $16.8bn (nearly +50% q/q) with positive cash flow, GlobalFoundries' $21bn fab plus a US government stake and a +4.80% close, and analysts expecting TSMC to deliver a record quarter with +65% profit growth. Ranked as cash quality > growth story > valuation elasticity, this looks more like a re-rating on the discount rate than a deterioration in orders or cash flow.

Counter-evidence and gaps: (i) no verifiable company-level negative news exists in-window for Oracle or CoreWeave, and "no explanation found" is not "no explanation exists" — I record it as unknown; (ii) CoreWeave traded 111.2m shares, about 3.2x the prior day, so the selling was real, but volume cannot prove institutional net outflow, short covering or hedging; (iii) the CoreWeave CEO blaming "market irrationality" is a self-interested management statement, not evidence; (iv) TSMC has not yet reported, so every "record" figure before 14:00 is an analyst estimate, not a fact. Affected assets: near-term avoid highly valued, highly levered compute and memory names; relatively favoured are software/platform assets with recurring cash flow and buyback capacity (in the same window Adobe rose 1.86% and Microsoft fell only 1.80%, a clear split from Oracle's -8.83%).

**View 3: The bullish evidence for crude (the large API draw, IEA diesel tightness, the Beige Book's economy-wide energy costs) has not been confirmed by price — do not chase longs.**

Evidence: API crude fell 11.123m barrels against a consensus build of 1.9m; the IEA put Q3 global diesel supply at -1.7m b/d and said middle-distillate tightness will persist into 2027; the Beige Book said all 12 districts felt higher oil prices. But price has not confirmed: platform USOIL was -0.53% on its platform day while the NYMEX settlement was +1.01%, and the two are not comparable, so **no** "big API draw implies higher crude" inference is made. Two counterweights also appeared on the same day: the US proposal to unfreeze some Russian assets to enable talks, and the possibility of Russian diesel reaching the US before 3 November.

Confirmation: if the official EIA data at 16 Oct 00:00 confirms a comparable draw and the diesel crack widens, the bullish case holds. Invalidation: if EIA diverges sharply from API, or Russian diesel inflows materialise, the diesel-tightness narrative and crude weaken together. Affected assets: relatively bullish tankers, diesel cracks and refined products; refinery feedstock costs and airlines pressured.

**View 4: Precious metals are being re-rated by rates rather than moving in a one-way trend, and the previous edition's short-term copper call is withdrawn.**

Evidence: gold fell as much as 2% to $4,077.23 intraday (00:02, in-window) before recovering to $4,113.48; domestic night sessions saw SHFE gold -1.61%, SHFE silver -6.29% and SGE silver T+D -5.95%, i.e. simultaneous deleveraging in both metals. Holdings diverged: SPDR Gold Trust stayed flat at 1,054.196 tonnes while iShares Silver Trust added 12.64 tonnes (06:25, 06:47). A single day of holdings data does not establish a flow trend, so I draw no "capital leaving precious metals" or "buy the dip" conclusion. On copper, the averted Escondida strike voids the previous edition's supply-premium logic; LME copper closed -0.97% and platform spot copper fell 1.97% across samples. Affected assets: gold's short-term reference range is $4,077–$4,114 (this window's measured high and low, an observation reference only, not a breakout threshold); copper returns to demand- and inventory-driven pricing with no supply-side push.
