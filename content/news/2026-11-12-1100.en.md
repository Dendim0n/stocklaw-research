## 2. Key increments

**① Nvidia succession gets its first named candidate: CFO Kress | 10:11**
According to US financial outlet Semafor, some Nvidia directors favour appointing Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO (relayed by Jin10 at 10:11:28, **not confirmed by the company**). This is not a brand-new direction — at 07:47 Jin10 already reported the company was "considering an interim CEO before a broader search" — what is new is that **the candidate has a name**; and Kress is precisely the person designated in the company's 10-28 announcement to write the Q3 results commentary (company IR text), i.e. a "business as usual" arrangement rather than a strategic pivot.
No price update this hour: 11-11 close 173.73 (+2.87%), post session closed at 09:00 at **158.78** (−8.61% versus the close), post-session low 156.20; the next tradable print is the 17:00 pre-market. **Q3 FY27 results are due after the close on 11-17** (company IR notice of 10-28; call at 17:00 ET), with guidance of $108.0bn revenue ±2%, gross margin 74.0% ±50bp, and **no China data-centre compute revenue assumed** — that is the next point at which narrative and numbers can be separated.

**② Congress starts keeping a record: Warren and Coons ask for Nvidia documents | 10:49**
Senators Warren and Coons asked Commerce Secretary Lutnick, Secretary of State Rubio and the Pentagon to preserve Nvidia-related records (Jin10 10:49:51, per Warren's office). **A preservation request is usually a precursor to an investigation**, but there is no filing, subpoena or hearing notice this hour, so it cannot be called "an investigation has been opened".
Implication: within one week Nvidia's risk now appears on both the political and the financing side at once — per its Q2 FY27 release the company has set up compute financing platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR aiming to mobilise over **$500bn** of third-party capital (the release says "subject to definitive agreements", i.e. no final contracts); and the IMF's October Global Financial Stability Report notes hyperscaler bond issuance rose from 1% to 10% of the $15trn investment-grade market in two years (Jin10 10:40).

**③ Dollar spillover: the rupiah hits a record low of 18,204 | 10:04–10:16**
USD/IDR fell as much as 0.3% to **18,204**, breaking the early-June low of 18,190; MSCI proposed in October to reclassify Indonesia as a standalone market on investability grounds, and Goldman estimates about **$5.6bn** of passive outflows if that happens; the 10-year Indonesian yield is 7.62% and the rupiah is down more than 8% this year, Asia's worst performer (Jin10 10:16:55).
Implication: this is the first crack from "US hikes plus a strong dollar" in a peripheral market, and it is not a positive for gold — this hour gold and EM currencies were pressed in the same direction.

**④ The BoJ is still split: a dovish member argues for a cautious pace | 10:31–10:41**
Board member Toichiro Asada made four remarks this hour: the BoJ must watch for rate hikes damaging the economy (10:34), the effect of hikes may take a year or longer to appear (10:35), the pace must be cautious even if rates rise further (10:38), and further hikes require a virtuous wage-price loop (10:41). The backdrop — October corporate goods prices at **+7.1%** year on year (expected 6.5%) and yen-denominated import prices at +24.8% — was already reported earlier. **The only new element is Asada's remarks; the inflation data is not repeated.**
Implication: USD/JPY at 159.77; **160 is a psychological level, not a technical one**. Ahead of the BoJ's 12-17/18 meeting, yen fragility is a source of carry-trade unwind pressure, but nothing triggered this hour. Asada's dovishness does not change the BoJ's stated hiking bias; it only shows the split will reappear.

## 3. Read on the situation

**A | Nvidia: from "will they" to "who", and pricing has not reached fundamentals.**
View: bearish short term, but no directional bet; wait for two verifiable nodes. Basis: the only new items this hour are the candidate and the congressional paper trail, while the price is completely frozen (158.78 post session, next trade at 17:00); sell-side targets have already fanned out — Baird cut to 200 from 500 (a drop of over 60%), Mizuho 250, Citi 285 (still a buy), Wells Fargo 300, and Morgan Stanley removed it from its "top picks" list. **The dispersion itself is information**: pricing key-man risk as a permanent discount (200) versus a one-off shock (285–300) is a gap of more than 40%.
Assets affected: Nvidia, Nasdaq 100 weights, and the neocloud and compute-leasing chain that depends on Nvidia chips and financing.
Falsification: if the board announces he stays, or appoints an outside CEO with an unchanged strategy, the discount closes and this section is void; if the stock still fails to repair around the 11-17 results, pricing has moved from "key man" to "the AI capex cycle", and what needs revising is the whole chain, not just Nvidia.
