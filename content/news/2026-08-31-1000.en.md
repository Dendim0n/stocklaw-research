🧭 **Assessment**

Geopolitical risk is the dominant theme at Monday's open: the US military's action against Iranian mining forces in the Strait of Hormuz, combined with a standstill in channel transit, has brought a sharp return of oil risk premium, with WTI up over 2% and SC crude leading futures.

Trump's 65-billion-barrel Venezuela oil deal — with the Pentagon itself taking a stake — is theoretically a medium-term bearish supply increase, but short-term pricing is fully focused on the risk of a Hormuz supply disruption.

Gold's safe-haven logic failed this hour: despite the escalation, gold pulled back 0.3% from highs, Chinese gold stocks fell over 5% collectively and multiple gold-stock ETFs gapped sharply lower — warning of profit-taking pressure after crowded longs.

China's August manufacturing PMI beat expectations at 49.8, but both manufacturing (49.8) and non-manufacturing (49) remain below the 50 line; the real-estate credit reform and 40-year mortgage terms ignited a pulse rally in property chains — policy is aggressive, but fundamental verification will take time.

The yen's renewed break below 160, with Bessent publicly ruling out intervention and setting the tone for the BoJ, adds suspense to the Bank of Japan's September policy meeting.
