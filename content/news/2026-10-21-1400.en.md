## 3. Key developments

**1) [14:00 | UK September CPI beats across the board; ONS names fuel as the main driver]**

- Headline **3.5%** y/y (expected 3.4%, prior 3.1%), **+0.4%** m/m (expected 0.3%); core **2.8%** y/y (expected 2.7%, prior 2.6%); services CPI rose from 3.4% to **3.7%** and goods from 2.7% to 3.2%; CPIH 3.6%. ONS wording: transport, particularly motor fuels, made the largest upward contribution to the monthly change, partially offset by restaurants and hotels.
- Released alongside: RPI 3.9% y/y (in line); public sector net borrowing **£24.52bn** (expected £23.85bn); on the Jin10 calendar basis, input PPI **8.3%** y/y (expected 7.2%, prior 6.1%) and output PPI 4.6% (expected 4.3%) — **the PPI figures were not checked against the ONS original**.
- Impact: this is energy pass-through inflation, not domestically driven overheating; it links oil-to-inflation-to-rates into a single chain and makes BoE Governor Bailey's remarks tonight the first test. Source: ONS, "Consumer price inflation, UK: September 2026" <https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/bulletins/consumerpriceinflation/september2026>

**2) [13:15–13:47 | Peace track advances, crude refuses to fall: premium compression shows up only in agriculture and shipping]**

- Russia's defence ministry said it **hit a cargo ship** in the Black Sea (13:15) and struck a Ukrainian **power substation** (13:18). At the same time, US envoy **Witkoff arrived in St Petersburg** (13:29 TASS; 13:47 detail), and Rubio called the 20 October Oslo meeting "positive" with "no surprises" (13:32).
- Price divergence: crude did not fall (platform +0.94%, futures +1.40%); the most-active wheat contract fell as much as 0.5% intraday, its fifth straight daily decline and the lowest since **5 August** (13:12, on Trump pressing Ukraine toward a peace deal); the Europe-route container index main contract fell **7.54% to 2,453** (13:48).
- Impact: crude assigns very little weight to "paper progress → actual supply restoration"; pricing is still about physical barrels and inventories. The falls in wheat and freight are pure risk-premium giveback. **A turn in crude requires a deliverable clause such as lifting the Russian diesel sanctions, not the meeting itself.**

**3) [13:38–13:40 | Trump: new large data centres must self-generate power and pay communities a "dividend"]**

- Two points: new large and in some cases "massive" data centres must **generate their own power**, feeding surplus back to the local community or grid, which he said means prices "won't go up, they may come down substantially"; and companies that want to be welcome "should pay a dividend" to the community and/or individuals in it.
- Nature: a social-media statement with **no rule text, no effective date and no enforcing agency**. Impact: directionally supportive for self-generation equipment (gas turbines, storage, grid connection) and for wholesale power price discovery; an added cost and siting constraint for park-style data-centre operators that simply rent power. It is not a tradeable certainty until formal rules appear.

**4) [13:43 energy roundup | France locks in Gulf diesel as Europe sources alternatives to its product gap]**

- French presidency: **Bahrain and the UAE each committed 100,000 tonnes of diesel per month, Saudi Arabia 95,000 tonnes per month**, starting in coming weeks (about 295,000 t/month in total, roughly 70,000 b/d assuming 7.45 bbl/tonne — an estimate); Norwegian Offshore Directorate: September oil, condensate and NGL output was **1.9 mb/d**.
- Impact: this is the other end of last edition's "US Energy Department turning to India and Europe for help" — the gap is being filled with Gulf barrels, easing Atlantic Basin product tightness at the margin and compressing the extreme pricing in diesel cracks; but nearly 300,000 t/month is marginal relief, not a reversal of the shortfall.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Call 1: Inflation is the lead again; the counterparty to higher crude is rates, not demand.**

Evidence: the marginal driver of UK CPI was motor fuel, services CPI rising from 3.4% to 3.7% shows the pressure is not energy alone, and input PPI at 8.3% is far above expectations (Jin10 calendar basis). The 16–20 October Reuters poll already had all 100 economists expecting a December hike and 94 seeing 4.00–4.25% by year-end (one 25bp move on top of 3.75–4.00%); this hour brought independent data supporting it rather than a new expectation.

Impact: the discount rate for long-duration growth stocks and utilities keeps rising; gold's two forces (structural central-bank buying versus higher real rates) are in balance, producing the 4,106–4,132 range; silver's decline (futures -0.94%, platform -1.20%) is clearly larger than gold's, consistent with an asset that has no central-bank bid and leans more on industrial demand and liquidity.

**Confirmation**: the 20-year Treasury auction at 01:00 tomorrow clears above the prior 5.42%, or Bailey explicitly signals more tightening this year at 21:00. **Invalidation**: next month's UK services CPI shows this was a one-off base effect, or long-end Treasuries rally after the auction while gold breaks above 4,131.72.

**Call 2: Crude's divergence from the peace talks cannot last, but betting either way right now is guessing at timing.**

Evidence: in the same hour the peace track advanced and the attacks continued (Black Sea cargo ship, Ukrainian substation). Crude did not give back gains, meaning traders are waiting for physical evidence; this morning's large API draws (crude -4.931m bbl, distillates -2.577m bbl) had not been priced and are being caught up today, which also sits on the "physically tight" side. After the session high of 99.570 crude could not push further, so the longs are not accelerating either.

Impact: constructive on crude but not a chase; 99.5 is today's actual resistance. Certainty in diesel cracks and refining margins still exceeds that in the outright crude price. **Confirmation**: EIA refinery utilisation stays at or above 93.5% at 22:30 with another distillate draw (expected -1.162m bbl). **Invalidation**: utilisation falls, or the Witkoff–Putin talks produce a deliverable clause lifting Russian diesel sanctions — crude would fall first and cracks would compress with it.

**Call 3: A-share weakness is structural; judging today by the index will mislead.**

Evidence: Shanghai -0.53%, CSI 300 -0.89%, ChiNext -1.34%, but the selling is concentrated almost entirely in the battery chain — CATL's A-share is **-11.65%** (after UBS flagged Q3 unit profit of RMB 82/kWh as a record low); meanwhile TAIEX is +1.28% and KOSPI +2.6%, and A-share semiconductors and memory names were still being marked up in the afternoon (13:27, 13:32 flashes). This is not a liquidity event but a re-rating of one supply chain.

Impact: China and Hong Kong indices are tactically bearish but downside is limited by index composition; the real risk is the re-rating of batteries, lithium and storage-system integrators, while Korean and Taiwanese hardware is still strong but already at intraday highs with poor risk-reward. **Confirmation**: the Shanghai Composite holds today's low of 3,710 into the 15:00 close. **Invalidation**: CATL narrows to within -7% late in the session and ChiNext turns positive, which would make this a single-stock event rather than sector pricing.
