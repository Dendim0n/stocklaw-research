🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**1. US-Iran Talks "Rashomon" Continues**
Trump claimed negotiations with Iran have started and the Strait of Hormuz could reopen "by tomorrow at the latest," warning this is the "last chance" for a deal. However, Tehran publicly denied any talks are underway. Gulf analysts note Iran is pursuing a "gradual escalation" strategy, expanding threats to Middle East trade routes and energy infrastructure.

**2. 25 States Sue Trump's New Tariffs**
25 Democrat-led states filed a lawsuit challenging Trump's new round of global tariffs covering 60 trading partners as "unauthorized and illegal," seeking to suspend enforcement and refund collected duties. This is the largest-scale legal challenge to the tariff policy to date.

**3. Iranian Drones Strike Kuwaiti Military Base**
According to Israel's i24NEWS, several Iranian one-way attack drones struck a military base in northern Kuwait on Monday night. Risk of Middle East conflict spillover continues to rise.

**4. WuXi AppTec Hits Daily Limit, H1 Profit Exceeds 10 Billion Yuan**
WuXi AppTec's A-shares hit the daily limit with nearly 5 billion yuan in turnover. The company's H1 net profit surpassed 10 billion yuan, with a comprehensive upward revision of full-year guidance. HK-listed WuXi AppTec surged over 11%, driving the CRO sector higher — Pharmaron +7%, Asymchem +6%.

**5. South Korea Pledges to Ease Market Volatility**
South Korean Finance Minister Koo Yoon-chul stated he will work to ease stock market volatility, with the ministry moving quickly to implement measures limiting the use of leveraged single-stock ETFs. South Korea is also reforming property taxes, with higher-value and non-owner-occupied properties facing higher tax burdens.

**6. Fed's FIMA Tool Becomes Key Yen Stabilizer**
Japan gained US support to stem the yen's decline, with the Fed's FIMA repo facility serving as a key buffer. However, institutions warn the tool's capacity is limited ($60 billion ceiling), and may be insufficient if markets test the resolve of both governments again.
