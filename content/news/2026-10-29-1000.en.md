## 1. The hour's main line

**The AI-hardware selloff has broadened from US after-hours trading into a cross-market repricing: Samsung reported the first KRW 100tn-plus quarterly operating profit by any tech company, but was sold anyway because net income missed and 2026 capex was raised about 60% (shares down nearly 6% in Seoul, KOSPI −3.0%). A-share memory and CPO names gapped down, ChiNext −3.11%, STAR50 −3.39%, more than 4,400 decliners; the hedge was banks and precious metals — SSE50 flat, spot gold up 0.6% off 4,036, silver stronger still.**

What changed versus the previous edition is the character of the move: last night it was still stock-specific dispersion ("two earnings reports the same evening, a 16-point after-hours gap"), this hour it is **coordinated de-risking in Asian cash markets**, and the target has widened from "companies that overspent on capex" to "the suppliers being paid by that capex." Stance: **do not catch the falling knife in high-capex, high-multiple compute hardware with no pricing power; the memory cycle itself is not disproven, so do not turn bearish on the industry; gold is tactically long but only valid above 4,036; crude at 101 is neither a chase-long nor a short.**

## 4. Assessment

**Judgment 1: this is a double hit from "expectation gap + capex", not a falsification of memory demand; bearish on high-capex links, neutral-to-bullish on those holding capacity and long-term contracts.**
The evidence comes out of Samsung's own mouth: record DS operating profit of KRW 106.5tn, memory revenue +427% YoY, 2027 HBM sold out, LTAs reaching 60–70% of capacity by 2030, Q4 DRAM growth of 15–20%, tightness through 2028 — **not one volume-side data point weakened**. What was sold was the KRW 108.5tn expectation gap, the KRW 89.06tn net-income gap, and +60% capex. SK Hynix falling just 0.4% in the same hour is the most direct counter-evidence: the market is picking companies, not rejecting the cycle.
Impact: A-share memory modules, CPO and equipment — down 8–12% — carry the most valuation beta; do not catch them short-term. Links with locked-in pricing and long-term contracts should hold up better.
Invalidation: if Micron, SanDisk and SK Hynix ADRs fall more than 5% after the 21:30 US open, this is a global de-rating and the "picking companies, not the cycle" call is void; if Samsung recovers half its Seoul loss, today's Asia-Pacific selling was mostly local sentiment plus profit-taking.

**Judgment 2: gold is tactically long, but only above 4,036 — do not chase.**
Three independent pieces of evidence: +0.60% on the hour and $32.7 off the low; silver +0.94% outpacing gold while copper does nothing (an inflation trade would not leave copper out); the dollar failing to extend (102.11, high 102.30 versus yesterday's 102.49). The suppressing variables are unchanged: 10Y at 5.05% (10-28 close) and a 78.4% probability of a 25bp December hike (CME via Jin10, 06:10 timestamp; CME's page was not checked directly).
Impact: a short-term repair in miners and precious metals is tradeable, but this is "deep dip bounce plus hedging," not a trend reversal; 4,050–4,070 is today's churn zone and chasing there is poor value.
Invalidation: if tonight's 20:30 core PCE comes in at ≥0.3% m/m (consensus 0.30%) with Q3 GDP near 3.2%, real rates push back up; **if 4,036 breaks decisively (hourly close basis), this bounce call is over**.

**Judgment 3: crude is a two-way swing between "spot falling, structure tightening" — neither chase long nor short at 101.**
Evidence: NYMEX −0.99% and Brent −0.98% today, yet the structure is uniformly tight — Gulf exports down from 24.0 to 15.7 mb/d in two weeks, freight at five times pre-war, drawable inventories the lowest since 2016, and Goldman's $27 premium. **That divergence means the market is oscillating between "diplomatic progress" and "medium-term supply disruption" rather than capitulating in either direction.**
Impact: fuel-cost-sensitive industries such as airlines and chemicals stay under pressure; energy equities have earnings support above $100 but no fresh catalyst today (the Saudi siren report is single-source and did not sustain a move).
Invalidation: if Iran formally rejects the Qatari proposal (Israeli media say a reply could come "as early as Thursday"), the premium returns immediately and the gap below 101 fills fast; if the reply is positive, JPM's $94 fair value becomes the next target and the spot premium unwinds faster.
