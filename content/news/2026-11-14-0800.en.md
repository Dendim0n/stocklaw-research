## 3. Judgements and counter-evidence

**A | Semis: a double hit to the multiple (denominator plus ratings), not to earnings - so neither chase the sell-off nor catch the knife.**
Evidence: SOX bottomed at -6.45% and closed -3.43%, **recovering 3.23 percentage points off the low**; over the same session the Dow was +0.10%, Meta +2.90% and Microsoft +0.17%, and VIX only reached 19.30; AMD -8.75% has no company-level news in the retrieved feeds, leaving target-price cuts and a 5.15% 10-year as the verifiable drivers. **Judgement**: this is concentrated de-leveraging in AI hardware, not evidence of a cycle top. **Two pieces of counter-evidence**: the close sits 3.2% above the 9,828.98 low, so selling was absorbed into the bell; but the Nasdaq 100 fell 1.22% versus 0.40% for the S&P, so the pressure was concentrated in tech weights rather than broad. **Falsifier**: if Nvidia guides down next week and SOX breaks 9,828.98, the story shifts from "multiple" to "capex cycle" and the whole chain (memory, optics, neoclouds) has to be re-rated.

**B | Nvidia: key-person risk has entered a "candidate leaks, board denies" phase, and the information vacuum runs to 11-18.**
Evidence: the FT and CNBC reports are three hours apart, and the company chose to deny a "formal approach" rather than deny having considered Haas; Musk's public backing of a reinstatement petition politicises a governance question; the stock closed 162.85 (-2.01%), **-6.26% from the 11-11 close** and far short of the 11-12 panic open at -13%. **Judgement**: the market is pricing a CEO change as the base case but has not yet priced damage to strategic continuity. **Counter-evidence**: the board also says it is **hiring a headhunter**, which means the process could run for months - and the longer it runs, the more likely interim CEO Kress's retreat on financing commitments (about $164.5bn per the last filing) actually lands. That is where the cash-flow risk sits. **Checkpoints**: the 11-17 earnings report (the previous brief cited Jin10's "next Tuesday"; **not re-verified against the company's IR this time**) and the 11-18 all-hands.

**C | Precious metals: silver -2.87% against a flat gold is a leverage gap under the same rate shock, not the disappearance of haven demand.**
Evidence: gold ranged 1.43% (3,867.55-3,922.99) but closed 3,879.92, essentially unchanged from 3,877.43; silver broke 49 to 48.63 and SLV closed 44.13 (-2.19%), **-58.21% from its 2026-01-28 high**. **Key counter-evidence**: GLD closed 355.45 (+0.16%) on 11-13 and **did not revisit the 354.89 of 11-12**, which was the lowest close since 2025-10-02; SLV also held above its 2025-11-04 low of 42.74. **Judgement**: silver falling 2.87% while gold is flat (+0.06%) points to high-beta, high-leverage positions being liquidated first under a rate shock, not to industrial demand collapsing - there is no silver-specific supply or demand news in the window, so reading -2.87% as "recession pricing" is over-reach. **Falsifier**: if gold follows through below 3,867 and GLD makes a new low, this shifts from "leverage flush" to "allocators leaving".

**D | Crude: the geopolitical premium has gone numb; fresh attacks no longer set the marginal price.**
Evidence: two new attack claims (Riyadh airport, two tankers mined in Hormuz) produced a Brent settlement of **+0.02%** and WTI **-0.20%**, with Brent touching 97.13 before fading; ICE Brent speculative length fell 48.2k lots in a week; US oil rigs were unchanged at 463. **Judgement**: Middle East risk is already in the base price, so incremental events create intraday noise rather than a new centre of gravity - in contrast with the 11-12 round, when the same class of event paid +3.3% on Brent. Price response to this category is decaying. **Counter-evidence**: none of the attack details has been confirmed by Saudi Arabia or a third party, and the tanker status is a Revolutionary Guard claim only; actual loading disruptions or official Saudi confirmation of pump-station damage would kill this conclusion immediately. **Caveat**: volume and positioning cannot prove "money leaving energy", only that long positioning is less crowded.

**E | Policy mix: the White House and the Fed are publicly out of step, while an official report names AI - the least friendly combination for high-multiple assets.**
Evidence: Treasury Secretary Bessent said (21:17-21:21, outside the window) that "over-tightening just as an energy supply shock fades would be inappropriate" and that core inflation remains contained, while Daly and Collins said more hikes are likely the same night. Meanwhile the Fed's financial stability report named AI the top risk and put advanced-computing debt at $680bn, 31% of investment-grade issuance, with hedge-fund leverage the highest since 2013. **Judgement**: a rising denominator, an official risk warning and a split between fiscal and monetary messaging all compress the multiple on long-duration growth. **But the counter-evidence is live**: VIX at 19.30 is not panic, the Dow closed higher and Meta rose 2.9% - the market is discounting AI hardware, not re-pricing total risk. **Falsifier**: no December hike (LSEG currently 91%) or a 10-year back below 5.00% reverses the denominator argument immediately.

## 5. Data limits

1. **Run date vs data date**: this runs on 11-14 (Saturday) with US equities and commodities closed. Every change quoted is the **11-13 (Friday) session**, and none of it is 11-14 trading. A-shares and Hong Kong closes for 11-13 were covered by the previous 16:00 brief and are not repeated.
2. **Incomplete flash coverage**: `flash` returned 420 items, of which **122** fall inside 11-14 00:00-08:00; the latest is 06:02:30 and **nothing arrived between 06:02 and 08:00**, so post-06:00 developments - especially whether the FAA ground stop was lifted - are not verified here.
3. **Three WTI numbers kept separate**: official settlement 88.56 (-0.20%), post-settlement last 88.985 (+0.28%), Jin10 platform 89.066 (+0.22%, base 88.871). Different times and bases; any "change in the change" across them is spurious.
4. **Brent bases differ by source**: Jin10 UKOIL base 95.74 versus Sina `hf_OIL` prior settle 95.820, a $0.08 gap. The $7.38 spread uses the same-source Sina series and must not be mixed with the $7.28 settlement-basis figure.
5. **SOX close** is from Sina `gb_$sox` (10,146.32, -3.43%); the -5% intraday figure is a Jin10 flash and the -6.45% is Sina's intraday low. **No SOX intraday series or constituent contribution was obtained**, so no attribution to specific heavyweights is possible.
6. **Hike odds come from a single venue**: LSEG only (21:54 flash, for the 8-9 December meeting). CME FedWatch was not obtained this run; the previous brief's FedWatch numbers use a different methodology and are not comparable.
7. **13F nature**: the Soros Fund position changes are a **2026-09-30** snapshot filed on 11-13; at the 11-13 close the 858,822 NVDA shares are worth about **$139.9m**, too small to infer institutional flow, and no conclusion is drawn from them.
8. **Not obtained**: CME FedWatch, SOX intraday data, Saudi official response to the attacks, third-party confirmation of the two tankers, Microsoft's root-cause report, the FAA lifting time, and the original Fed financial stability report (the definition of "advanced-computing debt" is not given in the flashes).
9. **Unattributed moves are flagged as such**: AMD -8.75%, Dell -7.18% and silver -2.87% have no matching company or commodity-specific news; no cause is invented, and only contemporaneous verifiable variables are listed.
