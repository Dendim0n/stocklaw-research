## 4. Judgement

**Call one: gold moves from "bearish, do not chase" to neutral; silver stays bearish — the same decline, for two different reasons.**

Evidence: after a day low of 3,987.40, gold bounced to 4,000.84 at 13:02 and trades near 3,998; the dollar index moved only 0.05% this hour and sits at 101.80, offering no new reason to sell; the COMEX-over-spot premium narrowed from 10.59 to 6.67, so futures-driven short pressure is easing. Silver instead has hard physical evidence: London vault stocks at a near four-year high, tradeable inventory close to double the October 2025 low, ETPs down 60m oz this year, managed-money net length at the lowest since February 2024, and a projected swing to surplus in 2027.

Implication: **precious metals should not be traded as one block.** Gold is a monetary, liquid asset and in a "strong bonds, weak gold" tape looks more like churn; silver is an industrial-plus-leverage asset whose high price is destroying its own demand (PV thrifting, collapsing coin sales). If one wants to be short precious metals, **the fundamental case is stronger in silver than in gold** — but 51 is today's low, not a value anchor, and the payoff on chasing is just as poor.

Falsifiers: if gold closes below 3,987.40 the bounce has failed and neutral goes back to bearish; if CME net length recovers above 30m oz, or the London inventory figure is revised down (this consultancy has revised hard before), the silver call is downgraded.

**Call two: the BOJ's hawkishness has not exported to the long end — a plus for global duration, a persistent valuation drag for Japanese equities.**

Evidence: 5y at 1.32% (−3bp), 10y at 2.780% (−5.5bp), 30y at 3.840% (−5.5bp) — the central bank declared underlying inflation at target and doubled annual ETF sales to ¥860bn, yet the long end fell the most. That does not look like Japanese money repatriating into JGBs; it looks like a market concluding the terminal rate is constrained by growth — the BOJ itself wrote an "AI profits not keeping up with investment" drawdown risk into its statement. Deutsche's "focus has moved to the terminal rate" and Meiji Yasuda's "intervals may shorten to three months" are opposite readings, and the bond market chose the former.

Implication: **stay long US intermediate/long duration**; a narrower US-Japan rate gap is mild support for the yen (157.45, day low 157.10); **Japanese equities stay under pressure** (Nikkei −2.5%), with ETF sales a long-run supply from 2027. This is a "hawkish policy, dovish price" combination — the easiest way to get the direction wrong is to trade the statement instead of the tape.

Falsifiers: if Ueda's 14:30 press conference names a high neutral-rate ceiling or sketches a continuous hiking path, the curve should steepen and this section is downgraded; if the long end keeps falling while the Nikkei stabilises, the "equity drag" half also weakens.

**Call three: both legs of the AI trade got tested separately — real cash on the demand side, its own earnings on the supply side.**

Evidence: on one side Anthropic pays SpaceX about $3.1bn a month from December under a contract worth up to roughly $120.5bn, backed by $132bn of cash and +$3.9bn of operating cash flow in H1; on the other, Shenghong's revenue rose 34.3% while net profit fell 52.17% and 66% sequentially, and a third broker cut Intel's target today.

Implication: **"AI has no demand" was weakened today; "AI supply-chain profit is being redistributed" was strengthened** — the money is going to compute and infrastructure rather than to component manufacturing. For neoclouds and compute lessors: the orders are real, the risks are customer concentration and 2027 termination clauses. For A-share optical modules and PCBs: keep treating them as a quarterly sequential-verification story; today's limit-up wave in components is not evidence.

Falsifiers: if Nvidia's 17 November report shows sequential acceleration, or hyperscaler capex guidance is raised, the supply-side downgrade must be withdrawn; conversely, if customers like Anthropic start pressing compute unit prices, the demand-side logic needs re-underwriting.
