- **Russia-Ukraine tensions rising**: peace talks described as "back to square one"; Russia reportedly considering expanding conventional ballistic missile strikes, downtown Kyiv could be a target
- **Fed Governor Cook pushes back**: vows to fight Trump's attempt to remove her from the Fed; lawyer says White House letter rests on unproven allegations
- **Jackson Hole underway**: global central banks converge on guarding against a second inflation wave; Fed Chair Warsh's speech is the key risk for Treasuries

🧭 **Outlook**

Nvidia delivered earnings that "beat but failed to thrill": all numbers topped, yet the after-hours swing from -3% to +4.5% shows the market remains split on valuation and supply constraints.

A 70% growth guide coexisting with "persistent supply limits" keeps the AI compute thesis intact, but the declining margin path (Q4 trough at 71%-72%) warrants caution.

The Iran-Oman Hormuz toll-sharing deal is a landmark easing signal overnight, trimming oil and cooling gold's advance.

But Iran's deputy FM saying a preemptive strike "remains an option" shows tensions are far from resolved — energy risk premium can re-ignite anytime.

Jackson Hole is the week's biggest focus; hawkish or vague remarks from Warsh would pressure Treasuries and growth valuations.
