## 4. Read on the situation

**Judgement 1: the A-share afternoon is large caps holding the index while small caps bleed — a two-tier split, not a bottom. Today's pullback is not a buying point, but it is also not a map of a global AI unwind.**

Evidence: from 13:02 to 13:08 SSE50 and CSI300 narrowed their losses back toward the midday close, while CSI500, STAR50 and ChiNext widened theirs and made new intraday lows, with CSI500 breaking the 10-22 close of 6,655.25; index futures at midday had IC at −0.97% and IM at −0.76%, weaker than the cash indices, so the futures market is more bearish. The counter-evidence is just as clear: on the same day **KOSPI +3.91% (up from +3.65% at midday), Nikkei +1.89%, and PHLX Semiconductor +2.82% on 10-26** — the rest of Asia and US semis are rising. Calling the STAR weakness "global AI deleveraging" is wrong; it looks more like a domestic funding problem (half-session turnover down RMB 136.1bn, futures at a discount, and the QDII premium buying offshore tech exposure we documented last edition). Implication: large-cap value and banks are relatively favoured, small-cap growth and STAR names are under pressure, and index losses understate the pain at the position level. Confirmation and invalidation: if CSI500 recovers 6,655 after 14:30 with turnover expanding versus yesterday, today was a false break and this call weakens; if it closes below the 10-23 intraday low around 6,440.29, this upgrades from "split" to a second leg down. The observation levels are 6,655 (10-22 close) and 6,440 (10-23 low), both real price levels.

**Judgement 2: the US problem is positioning, not index level — this is deleveraging driven by longs selling to themselves, so the tilt is quality over momentum.**

The evidence is Goldman's three sets of numbers rather than a narrative: the 10-22 deleveraging was entirely long-selling driven and the largest since September 2025, meaning the pressure comes from people who already owned the stocks choosing to exit, not from shorts adding; the momentum pair basket is −43% with all excess return surrendered; and 2026's 30 sessions of ±5% moves exceed all three crisis years while average VIX is only 18.1. Meanwhile the quality factor is +6% since mid-August, equal-weight S&P −4%, and 61% of members are above their 50-day — together these say money is **rotating**, not leaving. Implication: the index can keep looking calm, but thematic ETFs, high beta and AI names dependent on external financing will keep realising volatility above what the index implies; in this structure, index diversification is illusory and factor and thematic exposure is the real risk. Invalidation: if VIX breaks 25 while quality's relative performance turns down, this is broad de-risking rather than rotation and the bearish tilt should be raised; conversely, VIX back below 16 with the momentum basket stabilising means liquidation is close to done (Molavi himself says we are closer to the end than the beginning), and the bearish tilt should be lowered.

**Judgement 3: gold failing at 4,025.49 while silver makes a new intraday low says precious metals lack buyers, not catalysts; "crude below 100" holds only on the platform quote.**

Evidence: gold 4,009.75 (−0.17%), intraday 4,003.83–4,025.49, having traded above last edition's 4,021.49 high this hour without holding it; silver 53.354 (−1.13%) with its intraday low of 53.350 right at the last print, a decline 6.7 times gold's, gold/silver ratio 75.2; crude USOIL 99.933 (−0.62%, intraday low 99.851) while NYMEX December 100.700 (−1.04%, intraday low 100.610) still holds 100, Brent 109.637 (−1.00%). Gold and crude falling together with silver weaker still is not the signature of safe-haven buying; it looks more like forced selling in a tighter-liquidity environment — two ends of the same thing Goldman describes as "longs selling". Implication: gold is neutral-to-weak short term and should be treated as a range, with no case for directional adding; crude is bearish within a range but not a short to chase, and the real supply signal has to come from actual API and EIA inventories. Confirmation and invalidation: gold above 4,025.49 with silver back above its 53.965 prior close means the repair is real; gold below 4,003.83 means the bounce failed, and 4,000 below that is only a psychological round number, not technical support. Crude: NYMEX December breaking 100 with a large API build confirms the bearish case; if API shows a large draw and crude still does not fall, the supply-gap thesis has to be taken back.
