## 2. Key incremental items

**① Goldman: $6.6bn of Brent exposure sold in a week, $4.9bn of it long liquidation | 12:35, Jin10 ID 234680**
Between 27 October and 3 November managed money sold roughly **$6.6bn** of Brent exposure while crude fell 5.3%; about **$4.9bn (close to three quarters)** came from existing longs being actively closed, not from new shorts being established. Net length fell from about $28bn to **$19bn** (it exceeded $45bn in April). January Brent touched about **$118** on 30 October and closed last week below 98. More important is the CTA signal: short-term momentum turned **negative for the first time since August**, with medium- and long-term trends still positive but deteriorating. **Read**: escalation headlines such as today's "navigation aids destroyed" cannot lift crude, consistent with liquidation flows temporarily setting the price; the data ends 3 November and does not cover the further $2.7bn drop in open interest on 4 November.

**② China October passenger-vehicle retail sales 1.65m, −27.3% y/y | 13:01, CPCA**
NEVs **1.109m**, **−13.7%** y/y, **+2.3%** m/m. A cross-check on the basis: NEVs are 67.2% of the total, matching the 67.8% retail penetration in the 4 November preliminary release, confirming these are retail (terminal) rather than wholesale figures (wholesale was 2.635m, −10%). Backing out from the total and NEV numbers, internal-combustion vehicles were about 541,000, roughly **−45%** y/y (own calculation). **Read**: ICE is the main drag while NEVs held up better; the common explanation is a high base, since last October was a buying rush ahead of the purchase-tax incentive expiry. The more worrying number is the month-on-month one — October is peak season, yet sales rose only 2.3%, clearly weaker than in prior years. This is the weakest monthly demand data of the domestic cycle.

Its significance for oil is as a reminder that infrastructure is now being targeted; only a similar event at an export terminal would change the order of magnitude.

## 3. Assessment

**A. Crude: bearish; do not chase the rally, and do not chase the sell-off at this level either.**
The case rests on three aligned pieces of evidence: positioning ($6.6bn sold, $4.9bn long liquidation, CTA short-term momentum negative), diplomacy (Washington explicitly unwilling to escalate, plus tonight's meeting), and supply (JPMorgan estimates a surplus of 1.4m b/d for two consecutive months, cited in the previous edition). Price structure supports it: Brent has fallen from about 118 on 30 October to 97.30, **−17.5%**, and made a session low on an escalation headline, so the war premium has largely been removed. Implications: upstream energy earnings expectations face downgrades; oil services and refiners benefit more through volatility than direction; cost pressure on airlines and chemicals eases near term. It does not affect the October CPI due 10 November — today's decline only feeds into the November energy component. **Invalidation**: talks collapse and actual shipping or capacity disruption follows (not verbal threats), or Brent closes back above 98.32 (Friday's settlement). **Downside confirmation** requires consecutive closes below 96.96 together with further builds in this week's EIA inventory data. The reason not to chase the sell-off: the move is already 17.5% and the shorts are "new" rather than "covering", so the risk-reward is worse than waiting for a bounce.

**B. Precious metals: bullish, but do not chase above 4,130.**
Gold +1.33%, silver +1.93% and copper +1.44% rose together while the dollar only softened modestly (EUR/USD +0.21%, USD/JPY −0.16%), which points more to broad commodity allocation than to an FX move; this is the same structure as last edition's "flat dollar, broad metals rally", and two consecutive editions make it look more like allocation behaviour. Short-term evidence is still thin, though: gold peaked at 4,134.14 at 10:06 and has not regained it, the latest price is 0.12% below the early sampling, the gold/silver ratio actually rose from 77.5 to **77.90** (gold outperformed silver today), and ETF holdings and CFTC data are not updated until Friday, so new money cannot be proven. **Trade-off**: existing positions can be held; do not add above 4,130; watch whether 4,100 — touched at 08:42 this morning — attracts bids on a pullback. **Invalidation**: a drop back below 4,070 (6 November close) would make this rally just weekend short covering.

**C. China's auto chain: bearish, but separate the base effect from the trend.**
Within the −27.3% y/y print, ICE at −45% is the main drag while NEVs at −13.7% held up better and rose 2.3% m/m, which looks more like a high base plus an accelerating ICE exit than a sudden collapse in monthly consumption. What deserves attention is the month-on-month number: October is peak season, yet sales rose only 2.3%, clearly weaker than in prior years. Implications: valuations for automakers and dealers come under pressure, and demand growth assumptions for lithium (lithium carbonate futures −3% today), auto parts, and copper/aluminium auto demand all need marking down. The afternoon fade in A-shares (Shenzhen Component turned negative at 13:02, ChiNext gave back its intraday gain) occurred in the same window as this data, but autos are only one sector among many — no causal claim is made. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if weekly retail sales turn positive m/m for two consecutive weeks in mid-November, the "demand destruction" call fails; if November m/m growth stalls again, domestic-demand pricing has to move lower.
