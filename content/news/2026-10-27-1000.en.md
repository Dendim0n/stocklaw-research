## 1. This hour's main thread

**The credit terms behind AI compute financing have been put on the table for the first time: Broadcom is providing up to $43bn of residual value support on Anthropic's $63bn lease financing — equal to the entire first-lien tranche.** Last issue this was only "an off-balance-sheet SPV, tranching undisclosed"; the structure is now clear — all $63bn is debt, there is no equity layer, and the risk is absorbed by Broadcom's own credit.

At the same time China's September single-month industrial profit came in at **+14.4%** (prior +4.2%), but the increment came almost entirely from electronics (+103.8%), non-ferrous smelting (+79.7%), coal mining (+57.0%) and chemicals (+49.2%); autos −22.8%, ferrous smelting −73.3% and power/heat production and supply −17.9% are still falling hard. Stance: **overweight AI hardware and upstream resources, underweight highly leveraged AI infrastructure and neoclouds, neutral-to-bearish on broad A-share indices.** What changed versus the last issue is not the direction but the grade of evidence — the line "can committed spending be covered by cash flow" has moved from Anthropic's accounts onto Broadcom's contingent liabilities.

## 3. Key incremental items

**① [09:13 / 09:24 | All $63bn is debt, and Broadcom itself backstops $43bn]**

- Wall Street Journal (relayed by Jin10 at 09:13): Broadcom's CEO met bank executives over the weekend, **agreed to raise the rate** and provided a financial guarantee so banks would be more comfortable with more than $40bn of exposure; some banks had grown increasingly anxious in recent weeks about the deal codenamed "Project Polaris."
- Company announcement (Broadcom's 10-26 press release and investor materials, read directly from the source): the $63bn is executed through a newly formed SPV; **the first-lien tranche is arranged by BofA, Citi and Morgan Stanley, with Blackstone the sole committed provider of the second lien**. **Broadcom provides "residual value support" (RVS) to the SPV equal to the first-lien portion, with peak exposure expected at $43bn, amortising straight-line to zero over five years**; the RVS payout is the gap between **72.5%** of outstanding lease amounts and the value of the recovered AI racks, and the company stresses it is not a direct cash outlay.
- **The tranching numbers come from Reuters, not the release**: Reuters, citing two people familiar, puts the first lien at about **$43.5bn (69% of the $63bn)**, with roughly $19.5bn of subordinated paper. **Broadcom's release does not disclose this split**, so $43.5bn can only be labelled a media citation.
- **This is genuine new information**: last issue explicitly said "how much of the $63bn is equity versus debt was undisclosed." What is now disclosed is **two debt tranches (first plus second lien) with no equity layer visible**, and Broadcom putting its own credit behind the lease of a company that is not yet profitable. Reuters also reports that in the prior $35bn Apollo/Blackstone deal Broadcom already supplied a backstop of up to **$29bn**; the two together imply roughly **$72bn of contingent exposure ≈ 4.7% of Broadcom's $1.54trn market cap** (RVS alone 2.8%).
- Implication: financing costs for AI capacity expansion are rising, and the exposure has shifted from "the startup" to "the chip supplier." That is a valuation discount for Broadcom and a tighter constraint for neoclouds that still expand off external funding.

## 4. Assessment

**View 1: This deal moves AI from "demand validation" to "credit pricing." Short term it is a discount item for Broadcom, and a tighter constraint for neoclouds that expand off external funding.**

Evidence: the RVS covers the entire first lien (peak $43bn), triggers on the gap between 72.5% of outstanding lease balances and equipment residual value, and amortises to zero over five years; two deals together imply about $72bn of contingent exposure = 4.7% of market cap; the WSJ says banks were anxious and Broadcom only closed it by raising the rate. **Note two wording differences**: Jin10 calls this "the largest AI debt deal ever" while Reuters' own text says "one of the largest debt deals tied to the AI buildout"; and the $43.5bn first lien comes from Reuters' "two people familiar," while the company release does not disclose the split — neither is written up here as confirmed.

Implication: Broadcom should no longer be valued as a "pure chip supplier"; financing costs rise for AI infrastructure operators that need external capital; whereas sellers of physical equipment (Nvidia, the memory makers) bear no lease residual risk and are not constrained by this item near term. Invalidation or confirmation: if the actual coupon disclosed later is close to high-grade levels, the banks were not demanding meaningful risk compensation and this view should be downgraded; if a second SPV requiring supplier guarantees appears from November with Blackstone's subordinated spread widening, "credit pricing" is established.

**View 2: The right way to read China's profit data is "structure," not "total." A-share pricing already agrees — a premium only for electronics/compute, selling in the broad index. Do not chase a broad rebound.**

Evidence: within the +14.4% single month, electronics alone contributed 9.6pp of the growth in all industrial profit (NBS commentary); non-ferrous +79.7%, coal +57.0% and chemicals +49.2% are the second leg; meanwhile autos −22.8%, ferrous smelting −73.3% and power −17.9% show domestic demand and the property chain still contracting. Three price-side data points corroborate each other: red within 30 minutes of the open, a RMB 5.563bn drop in margin balances, and a 20.2% fall in broker Q3 profit. On the premium side, Nasdaq-tracking ETFs at 11.7%–33.65% premiums and halted show risk appetite has not disappeared — there is simply nothing domestic to buy.

Implication: electronics/memory/optical modules and upstream non-ferrous are relatively favoured; banks and brokers, mainland property, autos and power operators are relatively exposed. The power point is especially clean — **capacity +11.2% (Jan–Sep) against industry profit −17.9%, so the volume-up/price-down scissors are widening**. Invalidation: if premiums converge below 5% quickly after the 10:30 reopen while A-share tech strengthens in parallel, the "money only flows through the offshore channel" claim needs revision; if this week's September retail sales and fixed-asset investment show real domestic improvement, the "structural" conclusion needs rethinking.

**View 3: Crude is bearish but not a chase; gold keeps going sideways with no direction.**

Oil: all three quotes fell together, SC −2.0%, container freight −5.76%, and neither the producing-region warning nor the airport suspension bought any upside, which says the geopolitical premium is being squeezed out. But the counter-evidence is hard — US SPR has fallen to **282.6m barrels, the lowest since October 1982** (last issue), so the buffer is thin, and Houthi attacks continue (two intercepted in this section). This is therefore "bearish within a range," not a trend short. **Invalidation: a verifiable loading disruption** (Saudi officials confirming damage to eastern facilities, or revised-down strait transit volumes) kills it immediately; if API inventories build sharply for two consecutive weeks while crude holds above 100, the supply side really is short and the short thesis must retreat.

Gold: spot 4,010.84, +0.12% over two hours, intraday range only 0.40%; the 10-24 high of 4,034.03 is still unrecovered. Spot silver −0.18% and futures −1.03% fell harder than gold, so this is precious metals broadly soft rather than gold alone. Observation levels: 4,003.83 (today's intraday low) below as the first marker, 4,034 above as the repair confirmation; both are observations, not trading thresholds. Invalidation: two consecutive days of ETF outflows above 1 tonne → weaker; reclaiming 4,034 with the dollar weakening in parallel → stronger. **Nothing here is a buy or sell recommendation.**
