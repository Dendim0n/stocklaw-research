## 4. Situation assessment

**Judgment one: the memory "repair" is local and technical while global pricing is still compressing multiples — bearish for the Philadelphia Semiconductor index and the memory chain, and rebounds are better treated as de-risking windows than as right-side entries.**

Evidence: KOSPI's -4.76% intraday low came after the Philadelphia Semiconductor index rose 1.61% and SK hynix ADR rose 4.6%; foreign and institutional investors sold a combined KRW 1.39 trillion; SK hynix's -6.4% also carries a new company-specific negative (the Solidigm duplicate-listing controversy); since 23 September the two leaders have fallen 23.6% and 31.4% while forward EPS was revised up, so what is being cut is the multiple. **Counter-evidence and invalidation**: if KOSPI closes above 5,600 today, the Philadelphia Semiconductor index holds yesterday's 11,316.34 close tonight, and Micron's 21 October technology event upgrades HBM pricing and volumes, then "multiple compression" should be downgraded to an oversold rebound; if KOSPI closes below today's low of 5,493 and KOSDAQ loses 780, this is confirmed as global deleveraging rather than sector rotation. What is missing: Korean pension and mutual fund subscription/redemption data, and foreign futures positioning, neither available today.

**Judgment two: this gold decline is a real-rate-driven pullback, not a collapse of the hedging case — tactically neutral-to-bearish; do not treat the dip as a buy point, and do not chase it short.**

Evidence: gold fell with the 10-year at 5.15%, COMEX gold -0.12% and the platform -0.28% pointing the same way; SPDR holdings fell another 0.57 tonnes to 1,058.472 tonnes yesterday (single source); Hong Kong gold miners (Zijin Gold International -8%) fell far more than the metal, which is equity-side deleveraging. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if COMEX gold reclaims its prior settlement of 4,160.10, the pullback is over; if the dollar index holds near 101.98 while gold climbs back above 4,150, the buying is liquidity-driven rather than rate-driven and this judgment should be softened. Missing today: intraday real yields (TIPS) and ETF flows, which need the US session.

**Judgment three: crude is range-bound at USD 98–99 because "Iranian exports at zero" and "Trump wants lower prices" cancel out — tactically neutral-to-bullish, but do not chase.**

Evidence: December WTI +0.04% and Brent +0.09% against prior settlements, with exchange contracts barely moving; part of platform USOIL's +0.35% is the basis narrowing from -0.62 to -0.175; Iranian exports at zero (single source) supports physical tightening; Trump's 09:25 remarks are an explicit push for lower prices, and the USD 8 billion diesel subsidy is itself an admission that diesel costs have become a political problem. **Invalidation**: if December breaks today's intraday high of 99.00 and the 04:30 API stock draw continues (previous -11.123 million barrels) on 21 October, the bullish case can be upgraded; if API shows a build and December loses today's low of 98.04, price returns to the lower end of the range. Gold down and oil up describe relative performance only — **there is no flow data to prove money moved from gold into crude**.
