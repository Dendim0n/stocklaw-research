## 4. Assessment and counter-evidence

**View 1: what sold off today was the contractualised liability of AI capex, not the discount rate.**
Evidence: in one session and one market, the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.60%, Nasdaq-100 2.90% and SOX 6.7%, while the Dow was **+0.02%** - a split, not a uniform de-risking. Over the same day the Treasury curve fell 13-17bp, so the discount rate clearly improved and the Nasdaq still lost 2.6%; the denominator is not the driver. The marginal information came from Meta's **off-balance-sheet commitments**: $825bn of contracts (from $700bn in July), $355.7bn of leases not on the balance sheet, $121.4bn due in 2027, alongside a CDS near 119.3bp at a record since inception. Capex has moved from a discretionary annual budget to an irrevocable contract - future free cash flow has been pre-pledged. That is what is being repriced.
Counter-evidence: Meta revenue grew 26% with a Q4 guide above consensus (verified in the 22 Oct 08:00 edition); operations did not deteriorate. If Microsoft, Amazon and Google **cut** capex guidance next week and the stocks rally, the market is afraid of "spending too much" rather than "committing too much" and this view must change.
Falsifier: the Nasdaq recovering **26,702.86** (the 21 Oct close), SOX retracing half of its 6.7% loss and the 10-year still below 5%, all three together, would downgrade this to one-day repositioning.

**View 2: the Treasury move is a haven bid, not rate-cut pricing, and the supply problem is unresolved.**
Evidence: 2Y **-16bp**, 3/5/7/10Y each **-17bp**, 20Y -15bp, 30Y **-13bp**. Genuine rate-cut pricing would show the 2-year leading; instead the 2-year and 10-year fell almost identically (16 vs 17bp) and 30Y-10Y widened from 29bp to **33bp**. Corroboration: Treasury at 23:00 explicitly tied the 26 Oct 2-year auction stop range to an unscheduled 5-year reopening.
Implication: duration has tactical defensive support; **"yields have peaked" cannot be used to justify buying the dip** - the 16:00 edition already established that this sell-off is about growth and multiples, and today adds the AI-credit channel.
Counter-evidence: bill demand improved - the 4-week and 8-week auctions stopped at **3.945% / 4.015%** (prior 4.00 / 4.05) with bid-to-cover **2.82 / 2.57** (prior 2.56 / 2.26). If that is a trend rather than a one-off, front-end demand has a fundamental basis and this view needs softening.
Falsifier: 30Y back above **5.42%** and 10Y back above **5.13%** would make yesterday a one-day squeeze; a further 10bp+ one-day fall in 2Y with 30Y unchanged would reclassify it as rate-cut pricing.

**View 3: gold's fall cannot be explained by the dollar or by haven demand failing - only by ETF/position outflows; but "who sold" cannot be established.**
Evidence: the dollar index at **101.5398**, **down 0.07%** from the NY close (not stronger); the 10-year nominal yield 17bp lower; geopolitics escalating (Trump 04:28: "we have hit them hard, we may go further"). With all three supports in place, gold still fell from the 22 Oct high of **4,145.93** to a low of **4,021.38 (-2.98%)** and trades at 4,022.34-4,023.13. Verifiable fact: SPDR Gold Trust holdings **fell 11.973t to 1,045.644t (-1.13%, 07:25)**; silver was down as much as **6.0%** intraday to 54.49, losing 55 for the first time since 17 July, now 54.536.
**Not established**: Jin10 at 22:30 cited Goldman as saying managed funds sold about **$565m** of silver in the week to 13 October, roughly **$2.2bn** over the prior three weeks - a second-hand aggregate that does not identify today's sellers and cannot be used to name an institutional actor.
Implication: gold has reverted to "real yields plus holdings" pricing; until ETF holdings turn positive, rallies are repairs. It is only **-0.56%** from **4,000**.
Counter-evidence: a stronger dollar with gold still falling would restore the dollar-pricing explanation; reclaiming **4,050** (the level first lost since 4 August) with SPDR holdings turning positive would break the ETF-led reading.
Falsifier: a daily close below **4,000** shifts the range down; reclaiming and holding 4,050 downgrades this to neutral.

**View 4: crude has moved into a phase where official statements contradict each other; the odds do not support new longs.**
Evidence: 22 Oct settlements Brent December **113.17 (+4.0%)** and WTI December **99.47 (+2.39%)**; Brent-WTI **$13.70 = 13.8% of WTI**, a seaborne physical-tightness reading (WTI does not transit Hormuz). This morning both are essentially flat versus settlement: CL 99.403 (-0.07%), Brent 113.103 (-0.06%). Three new official data points point in different directions: (a) Trump 04:47-04:55 invoked the Defense Production Act to support domestic expansion (medium-term bearish for price); (b) Trump 23:42 said oil flows through Hormuz are **above pre-war levels**; (c) CENTCOM 01:58 said day 100 of the blockade has blocked at least **$25bn** of Iranian crude exports, and Bessent 22:03 said Kharg Island has no tankers left; Goldman 21:40 said Hormuz LNG flows are down by up to **300 mcm/day**. (b) and (c) are mutually inconsistent and (d) conflicts with (b); **with no independent transit data I do not use them to set direction**.
Implication: the price is now set by the relative credibility of political statements versus physical data. A 4.0% single-day move shows how thin liquidity is; reversals will be as fast as the rally.
Counter-evidence: hard loading or production data (not statements) would move the premium onto a fundamental footing; verifiable evidence of restored Hormuz transit would quickly unwind the area above $113.
Falsifier: Brent December below the 22 Oct low of **109.81** and WTI below **97.72** marks a failed breakout; upside needs shipment or production data, not new statements.

**Cash-quality lens**: today's ordering is unchanged - Treasuries and money funds (ICI $7.94tn) are absorbing flows, gold is being bypassed and AI multiples are compressing. Cash at 4.22% (3-month) and 4.41% (1-year) is a real hurdle for heavily capitalised AI names whose free cash flow is now **pre-committed by contract**. The problem with those names is not that multiples are high; it is that future cash flow has already been spoken for, so the test is delivery against commitments, not a lower P/E.
