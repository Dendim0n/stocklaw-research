## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**1. Trump Calls Off New Iran Strikes, Oil Crashes**

Trump called off a new round of military strikes against Iran, saying Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and others requested he hold off. He claimed a Hormuz Strait deal is "imminent" and that US-Iran negotiations will begin Monday local time. WTI crude plunged over 8% at open, now at $78.85, down 7.27%. Geopolitical risk premium rapidly unwinding.

**2. China July Manufacturing PMI Slows**

China's July RatingDog Manufacturing PMI came in at 50.9, down from 51.7 prior — still in expansion territory but at a noticeably slower pace. A-shares reacted negatively: STAR 50 index down 4%, ChiNext down 1.59%, Shanghai Composite down 0.6%.

**3. Trump Acknowledges Yen Intervention, Shorts Squeezed**

Trump publicly backed US-Japan joint market intervention, admitting the US participated in last week's yen intervention, calling it a "signal of friendship." Treasury Secretary Bessent's meeting notes were accidentally leaked, suggesting more coordinated intervention may follow. Yen shorts squeezed; Nikkei 225 down 1.65% today.

**4. Wash Proposes Streamlining Fed Operations**

Fed Chair Wash proposed reducing the number of FOMC meetings from 8 to 6 per year, having already streamlined statements and shortened press conferences. Analysts warn this could increase market volatility during periods of policy uncertainty.

**5. HKEX Launches 5-Year CNH Bond Futures**

Hong Kong Exchange officially launched 5-year RMB bond futures today — the only treasury futures contract in the offshore market, designed to meet growing demand from international investors for interest rate risk management.

**6. Asia Manufacturing PMIs Released**

Japan July Manufacturing PMI final at 54.5 (slightly below 54.7 prior), South Korea at 53.1 (above 52.1 prior), Vietnam at 52.9, Australia at 52.0. Asian manufacturing broadly remains in expansion.

## 🧭 Assessment

Trump's decision to call off Iran strikes is the biggest variable this hour — geopolitical risk premium compressed rapidly, with WTI's 7% single-day plunge reflecting a one-time unwinding of prior escalation pricing.

However, Hormuz Strait transit remains disrupted, and the supply "chokepoint" issue is not fundamentally resolved — a short-term oil price rebound cannot be ruled out.

China's manufacturing PMI slowing to 50.9, combined with STAR 50's 4% drop, signals market doubts about the pace of economic recovery.

The yen intervention reflects deep US-Japan exchange rate dynamics — forced short covering doesn't necessarily reverse the trend; BoJ's subsequent policy moves warrant close attention.

Tonight's US ISM Manufacturing PMI (expected 54) is the first major data point this week — a beat would reinforce the US economic resilience narrative.
