## 3. Key increments

**① [11:02 | OpenAI cuts its own valuation 11% to a $1.25T pre-money as the round closes]**

- Jin10 news [233240](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233240), citing foreign media: OpenAI plans to raise roughly **$30B** at a **$1.25T pre-money valuation**, down $150B (**about -11%**) from the $1.4T previously negotiated; Coatue has entered co-lead talks alongside UAE money, with commitments targeted for signing around **October 30**.
- Dilution math: $30B / $1,250B = **2.4%**, a minority stake. The company says annualised revenue is about **$50B** after GPT-6 Astra with $70B seen by year-end. The round is positioned as an "opportunistic" bridge to an IPO; the CFO has previously said a listing could come in 2027.
- Implication: this is the first time in this AI cycle that the **issuer itself** cut the price, rather than the secondary market doing it. It sets the reference for Anthropic's public raise in November — the price buyers offered last month is now what the seller accepts at a discount.

**② [11:02 | Global corporate spreads at 129bp; Asia high-grade dollar spreads widest since July 2025]**

- Jin10 flash 11:02: spreads on Asia high-grade corporate dollar bonds widened to the highest since last July; institutional data put the global corporate bond yield premium at **129 basis points**, the widest since May 2025.
- The disagreement is now public: Citadel Securities CEO Griffin warns of a bubble, while Blackstone President Gray sees no cause for concern, citing demand growing "almost parabolically."
- Implication: credit is starting to price debt-funded AI capex, and that channel constrains behaviour earlier than equity multiples do — **wider spreads → higher issuance cost → slower capex**.

**③ [11:22 | Three AI infrastructure offerings blocked within a month]**

- Jin10 news 11:22: beyond SK Hynix pulling Solidigm's US IPO (covered in the prior brief), Australian data-centre operator **Firmus Grid cancelled a near-$5B IPO** citing market volatility, and Blackstone-backed **Airtrunk postponed its Singapore listing** to the first half of next year.
- Implication: three independent cases point the same way — the equity window for AI infrastructure narrowed this month. For listed peers the effect is mixed (slower competitor expansion is good for concentration, a lower valuation reference is bad for multiples) but net negative, because the discount hits existing assets first.

## 4. Assessment

**View 1: The AI repricing has entered a third stage — from multiple compression to credit, and now to issuers cutting their own price. That is bullish Treasuries and bearish AI infrastructure.**

Three same-direction pieces of evidence landed in this hour: OpenAI cut its pre-money by 11% and rushed to lock money before Anthropic; Firmus Grid and Airtrunk pulled or pushed their deals; corporate spreads hit 129bp with Asia high-grade dollar spreads the widest since July 2025. Add the Jin10 11:48 note that the inverse correlation between Treasuries and AI stocks has returned to year-ago levels, with Nomura's McElligott saying bonds are hedging again as the market re-rates the odds of a "growth scare" in real time.

For assets: AI infrastructure, memory, high-yield credit and the private valuation chain are bearish; duration is bullish; the index level can still "kill AI without killing the market." The trade-off is **not to catch AI infrastructure on the way down, and not to short the index here** — the repricing is happening in financing, and the index is only bearing the load.

Confirmation and invalidation: if Anthropic's November raise prices at the original level, or the next AI-related bond issue tightens again, the window was never shut and this view is downgraded. If 10-year yields keep falling on haven bids while semis keep sliding, stage three is confirmed — and cash plus duration beats any AI exposure.

**View 2: Oil and gold going flat together is an information vacuum, not a reversal, and I am not calling direction in this window.**

The evidence is plain: over the past hour CL moved from 100.169 to 100.151 and spot gold from 4,017.22 to 4,014.64, both inside 0.1%; none of the day's extremes (100.970/98.840, 4,032.70/3,986.56) was retested; there was no new production, loading or attack fact. The only fresh item is 11:43 — a Korean owner, Sinokor, carried 105.6 million barrels through Hormuz in September, about **40%** of the channel's volume. That is a concentration of insurance and sanctions risk in one owner, not a supply disruption.

For assets: tanker-rate elasticity remains, upstream already embeds the premium, downstream cost pressure is unresolved; within metals, silver carries the higher beta (-1.24% intraday, with Shanghai silver closing the morning session -5%, a figure that includes the night session and cannot be subtracted directly). The stance is **hold existing positions, add on neither side**.

Confirmation and invalidation: CL below today's low of **98.840** → second leg of premium unwind, bearish case holds; above **100.970** → the range resolves higher. Spot gold below **3,986.56** → retest of the low; back above **4,050** with SPDR holdings turning up → the repair upgrades.

**View 3: A-shares are a faster deleveraging, not a re-rating of Chinese assets — the evidence is the small-cap versus large-cap split, not the index decline.**

The case: IM -3.88% against IH -0.09% is a 3.8-point gap, meaning the selling is concentrated in levered, high-turnover positions like CSI 1000 constituents; turnover up RMB 140B with 4,800 stocks lower is churn, not a low-volume grind; Hang Seng turned positive at +0.58% and the RMB fix earlier hit its strongest since February 2023, so external pricing has not flipped. The counter-evidence is also real: southbound sold a net RMB 7B, so domestic money is trimming too and this cannot all be laid at foreign investors' door.

For assets: small-cap growth and high-premium QDII are most fragile, large-cap blue chips and insurers relatively resilient; Hang Seng Tech has more upside beta than A-share growth but needs southbound to turn net buyers. The trade-off is **not to chase a small-cap bounce this afternoon**, but to wait for the 15:00 close against 3,617.60 — above it is a stop, below it this remains the weakest week since August 2025.

Confirmation and invalidation: a close above **3,617.60** with IM's discount narrowing → deleveraging near its end; if afternoon turnover keeps expanding while the index breaks 3,590 again, this upgrades from deleveraging to trend selling, and every A-share exposure gets re-rated by beta.
