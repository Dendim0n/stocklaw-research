🔥 **Key Headlines This Hour**

**Iran Drone Strikes on US Airbase in Kuwait**
Iran's military announced the launch of "Operation Lightning" Phase 27, using suicide drones to strike fighter jet hangars, satellite communication systems, and equipment warehouses at the Ahmed Al-Jaber Air Base in Kuwait. Iran stated the attack was retaliation for recent US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island and other locations.

**Israel Conducts Massive Explosions in Southern Lebanon**
According to Lebanon's National News Agency, Israel carried out large-scale explosion operations in the Yohmor al-Chqif, Arnoun, and Kfar Tebnit areas of southern Lebanon around midnight local time. Strong shockwaves shook the entire region, accompanied by heavy gunpowder odor.

**WTI Crude Plunges, Nearing $80**
WTI crude dropped 2.76% intraday to $80.37, despite sharply escalating Middle East tensions. Goldman Sachs' latest model shows global refining throughput down ~6.5 million b/d year-over-year, with diesel at the "epicenter of the supply crisis." Yet markets appear more focused on demand-side concerns.

**Asia-Pacific Stocks Surge as AI Fears Ease**
Strong tech earnings reignited optimism about AI demand, sending the MSCI Asia Pacific Index up 4.3% — its biggest gain in nearly four months. Hong Kong storage and AI IPO stocks soared, though the Invesco Great Wall Global Chip LOF was suspended by the SSE due to a significant premium to NAV.

**Musk Denies Tesla China Business Sale**

**BOJ Holds Steady, Hawkish Undercurrent**

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The Middle East situation is escalating sharply — Iran is directly striking US military assets in Kuwait, while Israel simultaneously conducts large-scale military operations in southern Lebanon. Two fronts are igniting at once, and geopolitical risk premiums should theoretically surge.

Yet crude oil is falling, not rising, down 2.76%, and gold is also weakening. This "safe havens not acting safe" divergence demands serious attention — the market may be pricing in demand-side deterioration, or betting that escalating conflict paradoxically accelerates diplomatic resolution.

Asia-Pacific stocks are rallying strongly on AI earnings optimism, with the MSCI Asia Pacific up 4.3%. But the scars of the recent tech deleveraging remain fresh — Korean retail margin balances have collapsed and a prominent AI fund has blown up. The sustainability of this rebound is questionable.

While the BOJ stood pat, a hawkish member's call for consecutive rate hikes signals that the tightening direction is unchanged. The yen's trajectory and carry trade unwinding risks warrant continued monitoring.

Goldman's warning about a diesel supply crisis contradicts the oil sell-off — if refining capacity is indeed facing structural shortages, crude at $80 may be a mispricing. But confirmation of demand-side deterioration is needed before drawing conclusions.
