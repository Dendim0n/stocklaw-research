## 🧭 Market Assessment

The Middle East is the dominant driver this hour — the Houthi "blockade for blockade" declaration elevates Red Sea-Arabian Sea chokepoint risk to a systemic threat level.

WTI's intraday V-shaped recovery (88.11 → 90.89) signals the market is rapidly pricing in a Middle East supply disruption premium, consistent with JPMorgan's earlier assessment that each month of conflict adds $7-8 to oil prices.

The Tyumen refinery attack combined with the UK nuclear shutdown means non-Middle Eastern supply is also tightening, exposing multi-front energy infrastructure vulnerability.

Gold's narrow consolidation near 4050 suggests safe-haven flows have not yet flooded in — the market is awaiting further confirmation of the Middle East situation rather than immediately pricing the worst-case scenario.

Typhoon Hongxia's landfall may temporarily disrupt logistics and port operations in southern China, though impact on global commodity markets should be limited.

Global markets are closed for the weekend; current pricing primarily reflects electronic trading and geopolitical sentiment. Gap risk at Monday's open warrants close attention.
