## Assessment

**A | Gold: the sell-off is not about the dollar, it is capitulation to a 5% risk-free rate — do not bottom-fish before the minutes.**
Evidence: -1.25% on the day, silver -2.42%, palladium -2.00%, while all three dollar crosses moved less than 0.1% — a "strong dollar" story is not supported by the data. Nor is there a single headline: 08:00–08:45 was three evenly-paced 15-minute legs lower, the 09:00 rebound to 3,818 failed, price broke 3,800 again after 10:00 (low 3,797.81) and recovered to 3,801 at 10:10 — steady selling, not an event gap. What the media offers is the standing narrative, not a fresh catalyst: Investing.com's 08:48 snapshot cites Musalem's "several more hikes before spring" from yesterday and notes the 10-year is approaching its longest stretch above 5% in nearly two decades.
**Call: short-term bearish, do not bottom-fish** — the failed rebounds show selling is not absorbed, and there is no bullish catalyst before the minutes at 03:00 on 11-19. Observable levels use only prices that actually printed today: **3,797.81** below (today's low) and **3,832.20** above (Monday's low; reclaiming it would count as repair). 3,800 is a psychological round number, not a technical level. **Invalidation/confirmation**: dovish minutes + a softer dollar + a move back above 3,832 = de-risking over; hawkish minutes and silver below 46.183 = liquidity squeeze accelerating.

**B | Crude: geopolitics escalating while price falls — the risk premium has been voted down again.**
Evidence: Bahrain attacked, Hormuz risk unresolved, yet Brent -0.46% and WTI Jan -0.83%; the spread widening from 8.49 to 8.76 shows selling concentrated in WTI while Brent's freight and insurance premium is intact but not expanding. Last night's API showed crude +5.278m barrels and gasoline +5.122m — products are building.
**Call: neutral-to-bearish.** The reason is not "de-escalation" (attacks are still happening) but that "attacks have not turned into lost output or lost loadings" — the market is voting with price. Trade-off: no chasing longs, no shorting volatility via options; wait for EIA to set direction. **Invalidation/confirmation**: a large build in EIA at 23:30 → Brent's premium keeps bleeding within a week; Cushing drawing again (API -1.691m) → range trading rather than a bear turn; verifiable falls in loadings, an actual Strait closure, or damage to producing facilities would overturn all of the above.

**C | Asian equities: Nvidia's "relief" did not carry into the indices, but this is rotation inside AI, not an exit from AI.**
Evidence: Nikkei from +1.31% to -0.67%, KOSPI from +3.31% to +0.47%, Hang Seng -0.49% — all three opened strong and faded; A-shares show the split most clearly: Henkun New Materials -10% at 09:33 and innovative drugs sliding (Tonghua Jinma limit-down), while compute names rallied at 09:58 (STAR 50 +2%, Moore Threads-U and Shenghe Jingwei +9%, Cambricon +7%) and CPO with photoresist were strong (Wuhan Fangu limit-up, Lianhe Chemical +15%). This mirrors last night's US tape (software crushed, SOX +0.34%): money is moving inside AI from narrative to compute; the 09:59 WSJ report (Anthropic pricing above range) shows primary-market demand for AI has not been marked down with secondary software, so the cash drain gets larger.
**Call: neutral at the index level, structurally long AI compute and interconnect, short semiconductor materials and innovative drugs**; do not read NVDA's +3.5% after-hours as a trend repair — after-hours volume was only 11.7% of the regular session, and thin liquidity amplifies price elasticity. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if SOX breaks Monday's low of **9,295.01** tonight with CPO leaders following, rotation becomes broad retreat; a 10-year back below 5.05% relieves rate pressure, lifting both precious metals and growth, and invalidates the bearish calls above.
