**② Crude turns from down to up; the first officially announced tanker explosion in Hormuz | 14:59 / 15:08**
WTI front month rose from 85.72 at 14:00 to 86.60 at 15:00 (+1.02%), flipping its day change from −0.93% to +0.08%; Brent went from 91.98 to 92.89 (+0.99%), also turning positive. On the news side: at 14:59 Iranian state media, citing the Revolutionary Guard navy, said two tankers exploded and halted on an "unauthorised route" in Hormuz on Wednesday night ([14:59:07](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261112145907941800)); at 15:08 the detail was expanded to two supertankers carrying more than 4 million barrels combined ([15:08:10](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261112150810075800)). This is a one-sided claim by a party to the conflict — owner, cause of the explosion and cargo destination are all unconfirmed by third parties.
One thing must be said plainly: the price low was at 12:47 (85.218), nearly two hours before that headline, so **the entire rebound cannot be attributed to it**; meanwhile Kpler data at 15:01 showed crude arriving in India via Hormuz up to 2.1 million b/d, above the 1.8 million b/d of Russian crude, with October exports from the four Gulf producers at 15.53 million b/d, close to early-year levels ([15:01:33](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261112150133661800)) — actual flows are recovering.
Impact: this is "transit risk" (insurance, rerouting, halted voyages), not "lost production"; the elasticity is far greater for front-month contracts, freight and war-risk premiums than for the forward supply-demand balance.

## 3. Assessment

**A | Gold's drop is a denominator story, not the collapse of haven demand; tactically bearish, but do not chase it down.**
Evidence: the break came at 14:46, with 3,964.52 at 15:00 and 3,960.70 by 15:26 (session low 3,959.07); over the same window USD/JPY at 159.81 is pressing 160 and the rupiah hit a record low of 18,250 — the pressure is coming from the dollar side, not from bad news specific to gold. The case for "higher for longer" actually strengthened this hour: UK Q3 GDP rose 0.7% q/q (the fastest in two and a half years) and September was +0.2% m/m versus zero expected, with Jin10's own 15:14 headline reading "investors bet on a December hike" ([15:14:14](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261112151414552800)); Macquarie's base case at 14:28 is a 25bp Fed hike in December, even though it also lists three factors that could derail it ([14:28:59](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261112142859954800)). The official 11-10 curve shows 2-year at 4.73% and 10-year at 4.98%; official values for 11-11 and 11-12 are not yet published, and I am not citing a new hike probability this hour — the last verifiable CME sample was 11-10, at 59.6% for a 25bp December move, taken before CPI.
Affected assets: gold, silver (51.27, −0.77%), A-share and Hong Kong gold miners, gold ETFs, long-duration growth stocks.
Falsification and confirmation: if the 2-year falls back below 4.70% while gold recovers 4,000 (today's high of 4,000.40 is a reference level, not a "holds for N minutes" threshold), this section is void; if gold keeps falling while the dollar and real rates stop rising, the driver is position reduction rather than the denominator — that requires ETF and central-bank purchase data, and no quantitative update was available this hour.

**B | This hour's crude rally is "transit risk" pricing, not a supply shortfall; do not chase it long.**
Evidence: WTI front month +1.02% and Brent +0.99% between 14:00 and 15:00 — the two moving together points to regional risk rather than a single-contract anomaly; the trigger is the 14:59 tanker headline, but that is a one-sided claim by a party to the conflict, and the price low (85.218 at 12:47) preceded it by nearly two hours. Counter-evidence is present at the same time: Kpler shows Hormuz flows back at 2.1 million b/d with Gulf exports near early-year levels, while the API report published on 11-11 for the week to 11-06 showed a +8.968 million barrel crude build against −0.327 million expected — inventories are still accumulating. Escalation is not the same as a lasting supply disruption; two halted tankers are a freight and insurance issue, not a production issue.
Affected assets: front-month crude, product cracks, tanker and war-risk costs, energy equities; transmission to inflation expectations is lagged, so this cannot be used as evidence that inflation has peaked or bottomed.
Falsification and confirmation: if the 01:00 EIA report confirms a large draw (expected −1.109 million barrels), the "loose inventories" line has to change; only owner- or third-party-confirmed sustained disruption would upgrade "transit risk" to "supply risk"; if crude falls back below 85.2 without new attacks, this hour's move was a one-off pulse.

**C | Nvidia: political risk is escalating, but the demand side is pushing back, and the price has not yet chosen between the two explanations.**
Judgement: do not chase the decline and do not bottom-fish — both are bets right now, not investments. Evidence: the 13:34 subpoena statement (single-sourced), the 11:42 Oppenheimer downgrade to "market perform", and Tradegate −7.4% at 14:43 all point to a widening discount; but in the same window Foxconn posted NT$77.5bn of net income and guided 2027 AI rack shipments to double-digit growth, so the order side has not weakened, and SK Hynix's HBM4 yield above 70% corroborates demand from the supply side. On price structure, Nasdaq 100 futures were +0.13% this hour, −0.43% over four hours and −0.99% on the day — the decline is widening but not disorderly; S&P futures −0.47% and Dow futures −0.03% on the day make this a single-sector tech event, not systemic selling.
Affected assets: Nvidia, Nasdaq 100 weights, GPU rental and neocloud names, A-share CPO/optical modules (Zhongji Innolight with 2.299bn yuan of net main-force outflow).
Falsification and confirmation: which side of 158.78 (11-11 post-market) and 173.73 (11-11 close) the stock opens on at 17:00 is the first verifiable signal; only if the 11-17 guidance is cut and Foxconn's AI server language weakens in tandem does this shift from key-man risk to a capex-cycle risk — and then the whole chain has to be repriced, not just Nvidia.
