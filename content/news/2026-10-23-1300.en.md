## 3. Key incremental items

**① [12:04–12:52 | Six optical-module firms file "operations normal" at midday; Eoptolink announces RMB 0.8–1.0bn buyback]**

- All six filed within 48 minutes (12:04:19–12:52:01): Accelink 12:04, Eoptolink 12:20 and 12:21, Yuanjie 12:27, Zhongji Innolight 12:32, Tianfu 12:36 (plus a 12:42 digest summarising five of them), Lianxun Instruments 12:52.
- Zhongji Innolight disclosed that major customer orders "already extend into 2027", with some key customers giving **2028 demand guidance**, and said it expects 1.6T/800G high-end module demand to keep growing fast.
- Eoptolink: buyback of **RMB 0.8–1.0bn**, price cap RMB 500/share, 12-month term; at the cap that is roughly 1.6–2.0m shares (**0.11%–0.14%** of shares outstanding).
- **The scale needs correcting**: the company's share count is calculated at the RMB 500 cap. At the current RMB 257.53, the same money actually buys 3.11–3.88m shares, about **0.25%–0.31%** of shares outstanding, or **0.31%** of the RMB 323.1bn market cap — a gesture, not real buying.
- Read: the filings contain no new orders; their function is to break the "de-rating → stop-loss → more de-rating" chain. The real information is that management put "orders into 2027, some into 2028" on the record, which matters more than the buyback amount.

**② [12:49 | Counterpoint: Q3 global PC shipments -21%, driven by memory prices]**

- Preliminary data: Q3 global PC shipments **57m units, -21% YoY, -13.8% QoQ**, attributed to "persistently high memory and other component costs pushing PC prices up". Q4 is forecast at **-27%** YoY, with full-year 2027 roughly flat versus 2026.
- Vendors: Lenovo 15.0m (-23%, 26.3% share), HP 10.3m (-31%, 18.1%), Dell 7.6m (-25%, 13.3%), Apple 5.6m (-11%, share 8.7%→9.8%), Asus 5.2m (-7%).
- Read: this is the first hard demand-side data point on "memory inflation". It does not refute the price increases, but it cuts 2027 **bit demand** — price up, volume down, so revenue growth gets trimmed. Short-term positive, long-term negative for memory makers; a direct negative for PC brands and ODMs.

**③ [12:51 / 12:57 | FT: US prepares for a long standoff with Iran while letting Russian diesel flow]**

- 12:51: Waltz, Trump's nominee for UN ambassador, conceded that a year from now the US may still be stuck in stalemate with Iran — no nuclear deal, and Hormuz shipping not back to pre-war levels. He said the president and the Navy can sustain the blockade of Iranian ports "for the foreseeable future" and that the administration is "intensifying" economic pressure.
- 12:57: the same source said the Iran war has produced record fuel prices plus fertiliser and commodity shortages, and that Trump struck a deal with Putin this month to **allow Russian diesel sales** to bring fuel costs down.
- Read: "long standoff" and "let Russian diesel flow" appear in the same story, which says Washington's goal is to cap prices, not escalate. After the single most bullish headline available for crude, CL only moved from 100.151 to 100.372 (+0.22%), still 0.6% below the 10:05 high of 100.970 — the marginal pricing power of the war premium is fading.

**④ [12:19–12:22 | Samsung adds tooling at Taylor Fab 1; 2nm capacity to reach 20k wpm]**

- Korean media (ZDNet Korea and others): Samsung has ordered equipment to add **6,000 wafers per month** at its Taylor, Texas Fab 1, reaching **20,000 wpm** of 2nm capacity by H2 2027, filling previously reserved empty space.
- Contrast with last hour's SK Hynix withdrawal of the Solidigm US IPO: **the equity financing window is closing, but industrial capital has not stopped**. At 20k wpm this fills out an existing shell rather than adding a giant new fab, so it does not change the foundry landscape.
- Read: modestly positive for semicap equipment (especially US toolmakers); marginally negative, but limited, for 2nm foundry pricing and share.

**⑤ [12:11 | FT: CalPERS exits large-cap tech]**

- Read: directionally consistent with "the AI trade is cooling", but one pension fund rebalancing several times a year does not prove a trend; treat it as circumstantial evidence that institutions are trimming AI weight, not as a standalone conclusion.

## 4. Judgement

**View 1: The afternoon move in A-shares is an oversold bounce after leverage was flushed out, plus company support buying. Short-term bullish, but only through today's close — not enough to call a trend reversal.**

Evidence: Shanghai Composite 3,614.12→3,642.58 (+0.79%), ChiNext 2,521.77→2,609.33 (+3.47%), CSI 300 +1.10%; five optical-module names filed within 48 minutes and Zhongji Innolight is up **8.62%** off its intraday low of 553.48 and now +1.88% on the day; KOSPI narrowed from -2.01% to -0.52% and the Hang Seng is +0.72%, so regional risk appetite is repairing alongside. The counter-evidence is just as solid: after 13:00 the only Jin10 item through 13:04 was a sell-side comment from Industrial Securities' Wang Han (attributing the drop to overseas volatility and AI return expectations) — **no policy or flow headline**, so the bounce lacks a verifiable external catalyst; morning turnover was already up more than RMB 140bn on the prior day, and if the afternoon cannot keep that pace the move is short covering, not new money; and a buyback worth 0.25%–0.31% of shares cannot support a RMB 667.3bn market cap like Zhongji Innolight.

Assets: small-cap growth has the most beta and the most fragility; optical modules are the most direct de-rating target; large-cap blue chips (insurers, banks) were already defensive and therefore have less upside in the bounce. The trade-off is **do not chase the first afternoon spike, wait for the close** — touching a level intraday is not holding it.

Confirmation / invalidation: a 15:00 close above **3,617.60** with afternoon turnover holding the morning pace → the bounce is real and the flush is near done; a close back below **3,590** → one-day wonder, and the read reverts to "leverage flush incomplete".

**View 2: The most bullish geopolitical headline available produced no new high in crude — the marginal pricing power of the war premium is decaying. No chasing crude either way; relative preference for tankers.**

Evidence: Waltz's "still stalemated a year from now, blockade sustainable" is the first time an official US voice has conceded long duration, which by any normal logic should have taken crude to a new high. Instead CL rose just 0.22% this hour and Brent 0.33%, and neither touched the 10:05 peaks of 100.970 / 114.720. The same story's "let Russian diesel flow to cap prices" shows Washington is offsetting with supply-side tools rather than escalating. Demand is also self-limiting: last hour's report of US crude export orders to Asia nearly halving and the MEH premium collapsing from $3 to 25 cents is high prices pushing out marginal buyers.

Assets: upstream already embeds the premium; downstream (airlines, chemicals) still faces cost pressure — FT at 12:08 quoted American Airlines saying fares will not fall even if oil does, which is an airline defending margin, not evidence of strong demand; tankers benefit from rerouting and freight, independent of the direction of crude. The stance is **hold existing positions, add on neither side**.

Confirmation / invalidation: CL above **100.970** with freight still rising → the premium is re-expanding and the "premium give-back" bearish call is void; below **98.840** with MEH premium and freight falling together → second leg of the give-back confirmed. A verifiable loss of production or loadings → the logic flips from "premium" to "supply gap".

**View 3: Demand destruction from memory inflation now has hard data, and that constrains 2027 earnings forecasts more than the AI valuation debate does. Downgrade the memory chain from "price and volume both up" to "price up, volume cut".**

Evidence: Counterpoint's Q3 PC shipments -21% and Q4 forecast -27%, with the firm itself pointing to memory and component costs pushing up system prices; every top-five vendor except Apple and Asus fell 23%–31%. Meanwhile Samsung is still adding 6,000 wpm of tooling at Taylor, so supply has not stopped because financing tightened. Put together: prices are still rising (TrendForce reported DDR5 16Gb spot +0.16% yesterday) while volumes are being marked down.

Assets: PC brands and ODMs (Lenovo, HP, Dell) are directly hurt; memory makers are short-term positive, long-term negative; the A-share memory theme is rallying against the tape today (Dagang Holdings limit-up) on the "price hike" trade, which points the other way from this demand data — a timing mismatch. One supply chain, two markets, two pricing logics; that does not persist.

Confirmation / invalidation: Q4 shipments better than **-27%** (Black Friday / Christmas pull) or memory contract prices flattening QoQ → demand destruction is refuted; if November contract prices keep rising while PC orders are cut again → this view strengthens, and 2027 revenue assumptions for the memory chain should be cut across the board.
