## 4. Situation Assessment

**Judgement 1: Today's A-share decline was institutions selling into retail bids — a change of hands, not panic — so the quality of any rebound depends on whether main-force money returns, not on the index level.**
Evidence: main force −RMB69.836bn and large orders −RMB31.778bn against small orders +RMB50.563bn; ChiNext −4.45% versus SSE50 +0.36%, a 4.8pp spread; Treasury futures higher across the curve, meaning domestic money is de-risking rather than leaving; Hong Kong tech closing within 0.05% of its low, i.e. the same move across markets.
Impact: stay overweight large-cap/high-dividend/banks; do not catch the knife in compute hardware or thematic growth. **China bonds rose while the US long end sold off, so the A-share link to global rates is currently broken — a falling Treasury yield is not a precondition for an A-share growth rebound.**
What would invalidate it: main-force flow turning net positive tomorrow with ChiNext outperforming CSI300 by more than 2pp; or turnover back above RMB1.9tn while main force is still net selling — that would mean the incremental buyer is retail sentiment, and the rally would be even more fragile.

**Judgement 2: This hour, precious metals are trading against long-end term premium — neither the dollar nor equities — so "hike odds up, Warsh read dovish, gold still down" is internally consistent and needs no fresh bearish catalyst.**
Evidence: gold −1.05% (4,026.12), silver −2.27%, the gold/silver ratio 76.34 → **76.36**, with silver's decline consistently more than double gold's; meanwhile the dollar index is 102.1644 (+0.03% on the day), Nasdaq futures −1.32%, copper −0.92%. Hike odds are above 83% on all three platforms, so the front end is saturated; the official 10-28 curve was a bear steepener (10Y +6bp, 30Y +7bp, 2Y −3bp).
Impact: precious metals are **bearish but not shortable here** — gold is 0.10% above the session low of 4,022.11 with no historical reference below, so the risk/reward on chasing is poor. Gold as an equity hedge has now failed for a fourth consecutive hour; the substitute is cash and short-dated bills. Silver has more beta and will bounce harder, but only once the long end loosens first.
What would invalidate it: gold reclaiming today's basis of **4,068.76** and silver reclaiming **53.95** would turn this neutral; if tomorrow's 20:30 core PCE prints ≥0.4% m/m, this section strengthens rather than weakens.

**Judgement 3: Energy has split in two — gas is breaking out while crude recovers — so "energy is tight" cannot be traded as a single long.**
Evidence: TTF broke €100/MWh on Wednesday for the first time since December 2022, German front-year baseload is €159.30 and Dutch day-ahead power €311.53 today, with the constraint being **LNG shipping plus Qatar's force majeure, Norwegian outages and storage at 74.9%**. In the same window USOIL recovered from 100.706 to 101.069 (−0.17% on the day) and Brent's day change is essentially zero (−0.01%), with the 100.286 session low untouched; Jin10 at 15:42 attributed crude's softness to "US–Iran diplomatic contact", matching reports of Vance's letter to Iran and a Qatari proposal awaiting Tehran's answer (media reporting, not confirmed by Iran).
Impact: **long LNG and European gas, short European energy-intensive manufacturing; crude stays neutral — no chase either way around $100.** Gas is constrained physically (cargoes, force majeure) while crude is constrained politically (talks), so the two should not be expressed with the same position. If the ECB tonight characterises energy inflation as not yet feeding wages, the European curve may flatten rather than steepen.
What would invalidate it: TTF back below €90 with day-ahead power under €200 weakens this section; USOIL above today's high of **101.528** together with hard evidence — suspended loadings, an insurance-rate spike, a day of zero transits — is what would move crude from political to supply pricing.
