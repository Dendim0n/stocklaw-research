🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**CPI Turns Negative for First Time in Four Years — Inflation Inflection Point?**
US June CPI fell 0.4% MoM, the largest monthly decline since April 2020, with the annual rate easing to 3.5%. Core CPI also slowed, and housing cost increases hit their lowest since 2021. White House NEC Director Hassett called it 「the best inflation report in six years,」 with the energy price collapse as the primary driver.

**Warsh's Congressional Debut Imminent**
Fed Chair Warsh will appear before the House Financial Services Committee in minutes. His prepared testimony vows to end the era of high inflation with 「zero tolerance」 for persistent price pressures, and pledges to assess AI's impact on productivity and employment.

**Chip Stocks Surge Across the Board**
Philadelphia Semiconductor Index +3.1% in early trading; AMD +3.6%, Micron +3.5%, Western Digital +4.2%, Marvell +4.2%. Goldman Sachs CEO Solomon stated the AI buildout remains in its early stages.

- Lebanon and Israel in Rome negotiations to finalize a ceasefire agreement

- GOP Senator: Iran 「decades away」 from nuclear weapons, Gulf states should deploy ground troops if necessary

Microsoft (MSFT) fell over 2%, one of the few weakening names among tech mega-caps.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

A cooler-than-expected CPI is near-term positive for risk assets, but energy prices remain the biggest wildcard — if the US-Iran conflict escalates further, oil could reverse the disinflation trend.

Warsh's testimony tone is the key variable. The 「zero tolerance」 language in his prepared remarks leans hawkish, but the CPI print may soften his stance — watch the Q&A session for signals.

Gold spiked and retreated, with clear resistance at the 4100 level. Bulls need more explicit rate-cut expectations to break through.

Chip stocks roared back as the AI narrative regained favor against a cooling inflation backdrop, but the BofA Bull/Bear Indicator has flashed 「extreme bullishness」 — a contrarian caution flag.

Oil prices are supported by geopolitical risk premium in the near term, but if the Lebanon-Israel ceasefire makes material progress and Iran tensions ease, crude could retreat rapidly.
