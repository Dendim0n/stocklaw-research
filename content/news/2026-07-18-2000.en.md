**⚠️ Middle East Escalation — Iran Strikes Kuwaiti Oil Facilities**

Kuwait's Defense Ministry confirmed detecting and intercepting Iranian ballistic missiles and drones in its airspace since early morning. Germany's Interior Minister disclosed that, given increasing reports and intelligence, Germany is raising its security alert from "abstract threat" to "high threat level."

Nearly 19,000 documents—including engineering blueprints, supplier information, and employee records—were leaked from India's Kudankulam Nuclear Power Plant, the country's largest. Experts worry this may pose security risks to the facility.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

Gold's break above $4,000 is not technically driven—it is the concentrated pricing of geopolitical panic. With Iran expanding its strike range from military targets to Gulf states' civilian oil infrastructure, markets are pricing in the tail risk of full-scale conflict escalation.

WTI's +3.6% single-day move is a direct reaction to supply-side shock. Kuwait is a key OPEC producer; its oil facilities coming under attack turns supply disruption risk from "concern" into "reality."

Germany raising its security alert level signals that Europe is also preparing for conflict spillover. This is not an isolated Middle East event—it is reshaping the global security architecture.

If Iran continues attacking Gulf oil-producing infrastructure, crude could push further into the $85–90 range in the near term. Whether gold can hold above $4,000 depends on whether the conflict expands over the weekend.

Markets are not pricing known facts—they are pricing unknown escalation paths. Uncertainty itself has become the single largest risk premium.
