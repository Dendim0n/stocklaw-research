**② [10-25 15:29 (weekend catch-up) | Diesel: the first Russian cargo has sailed, but it covers only 7% of one US day]**

- Reuters, citing LSEG ship data: a tanker carrying **40,000 tonnes** (about 300,000 barrels) of Russian diesel is heading to New York, expected in the **first week of November**; the Kremlin confirmed the first cargo sailed. 300,000 barrels is about **7.5%** of US daily diesel consumption (roughly 4 million bpd).
- Scale of the gap: Kpler estimates the market needs an **extra 400,000 bpd** to meaningfully ease prices; the IEA says Russian and Middle Eastern diesel export disruptions reached **1.3 million bpd** in September, about 4.5% of global diesel demand, and warned the shortage could last into 2027. The US average diesel price rose nearly 10 cents last week to **$6.28/gallon**, still near record ([article 233316](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233316)).
- Impact: this is the floor under crude and direct evidence that "crude falling ≠ products loosening"; refining cracks are unlikely to ease soon.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Crude: the geopolitical premium is starting to unwind, but products are holding the floor — near-term weakness, not a trend reversal.** Three facts at the same timestamp: December WTI fell from Friday's 103.27 settlement to 101.28 (-1.93%); Chinese chemical futures are still limit-up (methanol +6% at RMB 3,389/t, asphalt +6% at RMB 5,311/t, PX/styrene/polypropylene up more than 5%, fuel oil up more than 4%, [flash 09:00](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261026090002616800), [09:40](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261026094007228800), [09:48](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261026094815569800)); US diesel at $6.28/gal is near record, with IEA-measured September diesel export disruption of 1.3 million bpd. What was removed is the "expectation premium", not the "physical shortage". Impact: Friday's gains in CVX +2.88% and XOM +2.79% face give-back pressure; refiners and airlines get marginal cost relief, but diesel cracks remain high. Invalidation/confirmation: a verifiable physical interruption to Hormuz loadings or Saudi export facilities puts crude back above 103 and voids this call; a verifiable mediation outcome plus two consecutive weeks of rising Russian diesel arrivals would let the pullback continue.

**AI hardware: numerator and denominator are being cut at once; Asia sold first and US futures only gave back gains, which says the US has not priced it — do not chase a bounce tonight.** Goldman cut its KOSPI target 25%, forward P/E is about 4x, and semis imply a 65% drop in 2027 earnings — that is earnings expectations being cut, not "valuations being cheap". The KOSPI is 35% below its June high, Hong Kong's Hua Hong -7%, Chinese CPO -6~7%, STAR50 -2%: an entire hardware chain is being sold. The counter-evidence is just as hard: Cambricon's Q3 net profit was **RMB 1.404bn, +147.74% YoY**, so compute demand is not broken — what is broken is financing cost and the timing of return delivery. Impact: memory and equipment names (MU, AMAT, ASML-type) come under pressure, and the hyperscaler capex narrative faces repricing. Invalidation: if SOX holds Friday's close tonight and the 10-year falls back below 5.0%, this is just valuation noise; if the long end pushes back above 5.1% after this week's Fed meeting, the pressure spreads from AI to the whole market's multiple.

**Precious metals: mildly bearish, but this is a positioning problem, not a broken thesis.** Spot gold fell only 0.22% today and traded up to 4,004 intraday, which says Friday's concentrated selling is fading; what actually presses on it is the cost of carry — 10-year 5.007%, 2-year 4.699% (Friday closes), and high real rates mean paying a higher premium to hold gold. The miner drop (Chifeng -9%) is not a gold signal; half of it is a Q3 net profit decline of 41.84%. Impact: do not express a gold view through miners, since earnings amplify the error; 4,000 is only a psychological line, and the real references are Friday's intraday high of 4,034 and low of 3,975.58. Invalidation: gold back above 4,034 with gold ETF flows turning positive would shift the call to mildly bullish.
