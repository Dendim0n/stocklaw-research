🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The US-Iran conflict has escalated from military strikes to a full-scale information war over control of the Strait of Hormuz — Iran says closed, US says open, the truth lies somewhere in between.

Yet crude is down just 0.35% and gold down 0.11% — markets have priced in little from weekend headlines. The real safe-haven reaction won't be visible until Monday's Asian session open.

If the Strait of Hormuz is truly blocked, roughly 20 million barrels per day of oil transit would be disrupted, triggering a sharp price spike. But for now, Iran appears to be flexing muscle rather than imposing an actual blockade.

The WSJ survey's "stickier inflation" conclusion deserves attention — the war's economic impact isn't GDP contraction but rising stagflation risk. This poses headwinds for the Fed's rate-cut path.

Next week's CPI + Warsh congressional debut will be a double test — if CPI beats expectations combined with hawkish Warsh rhetoric, gold could face renewed pressure.
