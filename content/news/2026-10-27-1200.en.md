## 4. What it means

**Judgement one: the pricing axis for US equities this week is rates, not earnings — 10Y at 5.00% is now official data, not anyone's opinion.**

Evidence: the Treasury's 10-26 curve shows 10Y 5.00% and 20Y 5.35%, with the long end above the short end (2Y 4.68%) — not a recession-style flattening; Zandi's above-7% mortgage rate and widening credit spreads are the household and corporate mirrors of the same fact; Asia is moving in step (Korea's GDP beat hardening a November hike, Japan's 2Y at 1.885%). And with the FOMC meeting today, there is room for the argument that the market has already done the tightening and no further hike is needed. Impact: the assets with the most expensive denominator — long-duration growth and unprofitable AI infrastructure — carry the most pressure, while 3-month bills near 4.2% remain a real opportunity cost for holding cash. Falsifier: if the 10Y falls below 4.9% after the 10-29 decision with the 2Y following, the tightening expectation has cleared and this judgement should be downgraded.

**Judgement two: the bullish case for AI memory has to be delivered by tonight's report; KOSPI's +3.65% is front-running, not confirmation.**

Evidence: the three rumoured return-plan figures differ by 3-4x (KRW 16trn versus 50-60trn) and the company itself has not confirmed any of them; the share price has already reacted and has come off an intraday +4.90%, which indicates some money is reducing into the print; and on media figures KOSPI is still more than 35% below its June high, with foreign investors net sellers of over $150bn of Korean equities this year — this is a rebound from a deep drawdown, not a new trend. Impact: memory and equipment names should not be chased before the report. Confirmation: HBM pricing holding, next year's capex kept restrained, and the long-term contract coverage confirmed on the company's own numbers would establish supply discipline and support the bullish case; if instead the company announces aggressive expansion, the pricing logic weakens and exposure should shift from volume growth toward price stability. The counter-case also stands: if KOSPI spikes and fades after the print while foreign selling continues, this was only an oversold bounce.

**Judgement three: gold erasing its intraday loss is a technical repair, not a restarted thesis; crude "below 100" holds only for the platform quote.**

Evidence: gold at 4,017.72, range 4,003.83-4,021.49, up 0.20% this hour, with no matching catalyst anywhere in the window — no Fed speaker, no geopolitical escalation, no ETF holdings data; silver gained only 0.13% and is still −0.57% on the day, the gold/silver ratio at 74.9, so precious metals are not being bought as a group. A Jin10 column (Stephen Innes, 11:15) attributes the recent decline to deleveraging and cash demand, citing 10-22, when the Nasdaq fell 2.9% and gold fell 2.8% while real yields dropped — **that is the author's framework, not a verified conclusion**; I cite it because it offers a testable alternative explanation, not because I endorse its directional call. On crude: platform USOIL printed an intraday low of 99.899, NYMEX December is 100.796 (−0.95%, low 100.670), Brent 109.728 (−0.91%); a broker-promotional flash also circulated this hour saying senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan visited Iran and the four agreed to consult on regional security (10:56, **unconfirmed officially, and its "diplomatic warming pushed crude lower" is the broker's own causal claim**). Impact and falsifiers: gold reclaiming today's high of 4,021.49 with silver turning positive would confirm the repair; losing 4,003.83 would mean the bounce failed. For crude, only NYMEX December breaking 100 (now 100.796) would be a trend signal — a single platform print below 100 is not.
