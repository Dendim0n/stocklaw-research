## 1. The hour's main thread

The most important change this hour was the spread of the Asian tech selloff from Korea to the whole region: KOSPI fell nearly 8% intraday and the Korea Exchange triggered its program-trading halt, the Nikkei dropped more than 4%, and mainland Chinese and Hong Kong equities opened broadly lower, with memory and compute names hit hardest. At the same time long-dated yields fell as equities dropped — US 10Y -7bp, JGB 30Y below 4% — and gold largely recovered its post-CPI losses. The directional call is "tech is biased lower on valuation and positioning, while duration and gold are relatively favoured", but the bond move is a risk-off cushion, not a pivot to easing. Relative to the last issue, the main thread has shifted from "pricing the CPI" to "deleveraging the AI hardware chain".

## 3. Key incremental items

**① Chain selloff in Asian tech, memory hit hardest (the hour's dominant event)**

- Korea: KOSPI opened -5.59% at 5,673.01, then widened to nearly -8%; KOSDAQ fell 7.2%–7.5%; the Korea Exchange triggered its Sidecar mechanism and halted program selling on KOSPI; SK Hynix fell more than 10% intraday and Samsung Electronics nearly 8%. Per Jin10 news 232517 (09:56), KOSPI marked a seventh straight down session and its lowest level since late July.
- Taiwan, Japan, mainland China and Hong Kong moved together: TSMC -2.0% and MediaTek -4.7% (09:49); the Nikkei was -4.00% at 65,029.47 as of 09:19; mainland ChiNext opened -3.13% and STAR 50 -3.91% with the components sector retreating; Hong Kong AI and memory names led the declines (09:21–09:48).
- Background check: the US semiconductor index (^SOX) has fallen for six consecutive sessions since 10-07, closing 10-14 at 11,684.38, down **-11.6%** from 13,217.82 on 10-06 (independently verified via yfinance). The "$1.6trn of market value erased" figure is Jin10 news 232517's own framing and I could not verify it independently.
- Competitive variables reported by media: CXMT (ChangXin Memory) plans to launch a server-memory product using a new chip structure before year-end; CoreWeave's bond prices have fallen to levels usually associated with issuer financial distress. Both come only from Jin10 news 232517 and were not confirmed by an official or independent source.
- Implication: memory, compute hardware and debt-funded AI infrastructure names are biased lower near term.
- Published just before my sample (10:01–10:04, marginally outside this window): the IMF's Asia-Pacific outlook raised its 2026 regional growth forecast to 4.8% (from 4.4% in April) while warning that "given Asia's exposure to the AI supply chain, any adjustment in AI demand would hit Asia particularly hard", and said US monetary tightening plus fiscal stress in advanced economies will keep pushing Asian bond yields higher.

**② Rates: long ends rallied while equities fell, and the market cut its expected peak rate**

- US 10Y yield -7bp to 5.1183%, which Jin10 called a three-week low (flash 09:32). I cannot independently verify that timestamped quote; all I can confirm is that ^TNX closed 10-14 at 5.190% (intraday low 5.177%).
- Japan's 30Y yield -4.5bp to 3.995%, breaking below 4% for the first time since 9-10; 20Y -5bp to 3.815%; German bund futures +0.65% and French futures +0.76% (09:50).
- US fed funds futures extended gains, with the December 2027 contract up 9bp in price (i.e. the implied rate fell), as the market lowered its expected peak rate (09:54).
- Counterpoint: Jin10 news 232508 (09:11) said swaps fully price a 25bp Fed hike in December, while Prestige Economics' Jason Schenker (the first economist in Bloomberg's survey to predict the September hike) said December could bring 50bp instead — a personal forecast, not a policy signal.
- Implication: the discount-rate pressure on expensive growth stocks eased a little this hour; but "stocks down, bonds up" is a risk-off structure, meaning the market is worried about growth and funding sustainability, not about inflation running away.

- Implication: the Australian dollar and Australian rate-sensitive assets; this is also the first hard evidence of a cooling labour market since the CPI, and it offsets the "must hike faster" narrative.

Buying far above net asset value while offshore investors sell is a sentiment signal rather than a valuation one, and the premium itself is the risk.
- Implication: domestic liquidity is on the easy side, supporting domestic long bonds and heavyweight stocks; for A-shares this is a structural earnings-and-policy-driven market, not a broad revival of risk appetite.

**⑤ Crude: price falling as policy starts actively pushing oil down; the bullish evidence still lacks price confirmation**

- Platform USOIL at 92.762 (platform intraday -1.371%) and UKOIL at 102.79 (-1.61%); at 09:00 both WTI and Brent were down 1% on the day and at 09:16 Brent lost the $103/bbl handle (-1.42%).
- Policy side: US Energy Secretary Wright said the government is discussing relaxing sanctions on "shadow fleet" vessels to reduce record shipping costs. According to Reuters data cited by Jin10 news 232511, in early October a VLCC carrying 2mn barrels of US crude from the US Gulf to China saw an 11-month voyage charter cost of about $80mn, equivalent to roughly **$40/bbl of freight**, versus about $8.60/bbl before the Iran war began in February. Energy consultancy Energy Aspects judged the effect would be limited: sanctioned tankers are old, most have kept operating over the past three months, and insurers remain bound by EU and UK rules; Washington has not yet announced formal changes or a vessel list.
- Counterpoint: API crude stocks fell 11.123mn barrels in the week (published 04:30) and the IEA's diesel-tightness framing are bullish, but price did not confirm either this hour.
- Implication: crude and tanker equities are biased lower near term; but this is "expected" capacity rather than capacity already delivered, so it is not enough to call a supply-demand reversal.

## 4. Read on the situation

**View 1: Asia's selloff this hour is a re-pricing driven by rates, positioning and valuation — not a deterioration in already-reported fundamentals — so my trade-off is "don't chase the drop, and don't buy the bounce ahead of TSMC's earnings".**

Basis: the size of the falls tracks semiconductor weight (KOSPI -7.7%, KOSDAQ -7.5%, versus Hang Seng at only -1.1%), with memory worst (Hynix -10% intraday, Samsung nearly -8%); at the same time mainland copper-clad-laminate/PCB names went limit-up on triple-digit nine-month profit pre-announcements, so supply-chain profits have not deteriorated in step; ^SOX is down six sessions running and -11.6% from its 10-06 high, i.e. serial re-rating on the valuation side. Affected assets: memory, compute hardware and highly leveraged AI infrastructure are biased lower; software/platform names with recurring cash flow and low leverage are relatively favoured (on 10-14 ADBE +1.86% and MSFT -1.80% versus ORCL -8.83%).

Confirmation and invalidation: **TSMC's Q3 results and earnings call at 14:00 today** are the most direct test for this chain. If both the numbers and the guidance are strong and the stock still falls, that confirms a liquidity/valuation-driven re-pricing; if guidance weakens, the "fundamentals are deteriorating" case holds and the whole chain needs to be marked down, and my call shifts from trading to trend. On competition, a single headline about CXMT's year-end product does not prove the landscape has reversed, but it will keep weighing on Korean memory valuations.

**View 2: The genuine macro change this hour is falling long-end yields plus a lower expected peak rate — a risk-off cushion, not an easing pivot.**

Basis: US 10Y -7bp to 5.1183% (Jin10 09:32, described as a three-week low), JGB 30Y below 4%, 20Y -5bp, German and French bond futures +0.65%/+0.76%, while the far-dated fed funds contract rose in price as the market cut its expected peak rate — combined with "stocks down, bonds up" and gold recovering its post-CPI losses (now 4,145.47, platform intraday +1.00%), this points to concern about growth and funding sustainability rather than runaway inflation. A caveat on sourcing: Jin10 itself is not consistent on hike pricing (its news piece says swaps fully price a 25bp December hike; its flash says the peak expectation was cut), so I read it as "one December hike is close to consensus, the debate is the terminal level" and do not quote specific probabilities.

Invalidation: if tonight's **20:30 US September PPI (expected 5.3% y/y) and retail sales (expected +0.3%) both beat**, the long end could turn bear-steepener and the "lower peak" pricing would be reversed, pressuring growth stocks and gold together. Implication: front-end Treasuries and floating-rate assets are biased lower, long duration is relatively favoured.

**View 3: Crude's marginal price-setter this hour is the policy side pushing oil down, so do not chase longs; treat gold as range-bound.**

Basis: platform USOIL and UKOIL fell together and Brent lost 103, while news emerged that the White House may relax shadow-fleet sanctions — same direction of pressure. But Energy Aspects' assessment and the fact that Washington has published nothing formal show this is still expectation. The bullish evidence (API -11.123mn barrels, IEA diesel tightness) has no price confirmation, so it is not a reason to go long. Gold's observed range this issue is 4,113.48–4,151.42 (sampled 08:03→10:23); 4,151.42 was touched only once, so it is not a breakout. Silver outperformed gold at +0.90% platform intraday and the gold/silver ratio is narrowing, but with no updated positioning or ETF data a move in the same direction of prices does not establish that "funds moved out of crude into gold".

Confirmation and invalidation: **the official EIA inventories at 00:00 on 10-16 (crude expected +1.9mn barrels, previous -3.186mn) and the diesel crack spread** are the key tests for crude's direction; if gold loses today's low of $4,099.95 in the US session, the post-CPI rate shock has regained the upper hand.
