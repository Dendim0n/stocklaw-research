🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**Russia's Central Bank Sharply Downgrades Economic Outlook**

The Bank of Russia cut its 2026 GDP growth forecast from 0.5-1.5% to 0-1.0%, while slashing its baseline oil price assumption from $65/bbl to $60/bbl. The central bank acknowledged the direct impact of temporary production capacity declines in certain sectors and noted that fiscal policy expansion over the next three years will exceed April's expectations. The central bank explicitly referenced "the Iran war" — the World Bank similarly flagged that Morocco's 2026 inflation will rise to 2.4% due to the Iran conflict. Crude oil plunged nearly 3% intraday, as the central bank's pessimistic outlook and market selling reinforced each other.

**Israel Suspends Leave, Reinforces West Bank Ahead of Major Operation**

According to Israel's i24News, the IDF has suspended all soldier leave and deployed reinforcements in preparation for a large-scale military operation in the West Bank, following a deadly attack in the region. Combined with the emergency meeting between Russian and Iranian foreign ministers on Persian Gulf tensions, Middle East geopolitical risk is spreading from the "Strait of Hormuz – Red Sea" axis to multiple fronts.

**Bank of America Raises Dividend by 14% to $0.32/Share**

Bank of America (BAC) announced a 14% increase in its quarterly dividend to $0.32 per share. Amid an uncertain rate environment, the major bank is putting real cash back in shareholders' pockets, signaling confidence in its capital adequacy and earnings outlook.

**Intel Delivers Strong Earnings Beat**

Intel reported impressive results: both revenue and profit significantly exceeded Wall Street expectations, with data center chip demand growing rapidly. Q3 guidance was raised again. The stock surged nearly double digits in pre-market trading Friday. This marks Intel's second consecutive quarter of positive signals — AI compute demand is now spilling over from GPUs into the broader chip ecosystem.

**Russia Strikes Three Ukrainian Ports, EU Expands Maritime Enforcement**

Russian airstrikes hit three major Ukrainian ports, severely damaging supply chains, while the EU authorized searches of Russian "shadow fleet" tankers in the Indian Ocean. The maritime conflict is extending from near-shore strikes to global shipping lanes, creating multiple disruptions to energy supply chains.

**Trump Sets Condition: US-Saudi Nuclear Deal Tied to Israel-Saudi Normalization**

Trump has directly linked the US-Saudi nuclear cooperation agreement to Israel-Saudi normalization, adding uncertainty to what was already a closely watched negotiation.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

WTI crude plunged 2.63% and lost the $91 level — yet paradoxically, Middle East geopolitical risk is intensifying rather than easing.

Israel reinforcing the West Bank, Russia-Iran emergency foreign minister talks, Russian strikes on three Ukrainian ports, EU searching ships in the Indian Ocean — four fronts heating up simultaneously.

The oil sell-off is driven more by the demand-side panic triggered by Russia's own pessimistic outlook (GDP slashed to 0-1%, oil price forecast to $60) rather than any genuine geopolitical cooling.

Gold edged up 0.25% to 4059, confirming that safe-haven flows have not exited — they have simply rotated into purer haven assets.

This "rising geopolitics + falling oil" combination is unsustainable — either oil prices will subsequently recover, or the market's pricing of geopolitical risk needs a reassessment.

Next week's FOMC meeting and core PCE data are the key variables determining near-term direction.

Global energy buffer capacity is near depletion (SPR drained + refineries at full capacity) — any actual disruption in the Strait of Hormuz could trigger a violent price reversal.
