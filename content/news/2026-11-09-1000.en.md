## 3. Read on the situation

**A. The structure of inflation matters more than its level: constructive on upstream resources, do not chase the index.**
The evidence is the CPI/PPI divergence plus a 78% hike probability, which together describe "cost-push plus weak demand"; six energy industries supplied 80% of the monthly PPI gain while food and gold jewellery dragged on CPI. The implication is that upstream resources and precious metals are relatively favoured, mid-chain manufacturing and consumer discretionary are pressured, and for US equities this is **a valuation problem more than an earnings problem**. Confirmation/invalidation comes with US October CPI at 21:30 on 11-10: a month-on-month print below 0.3% would loosen the hike narrative and growth would rebound faster than resources; at 0.4% or above, the 2-year (4.51% as of Friday) keeps rising and duration assets come under broad pressure.

**B. Crude: the absence of a geopolitical rally is itself information — neutral, neither long nor chasing the short.**
The escalation events are specific and yet price did not respond; CFTC net length had already fallen to 86,469 lots last week, the supply side carries a 1.4 mb/d surplus estimate, and the demand side has winter support from a halving of European wind output and gas storage switching from injection to withdrawal. Implication: oil services and refiners benefit more from volatility than from direction; cost pressure on airlines and chemicals eases near term. **Invalidation**: Brent reclaiming 98.32 (Friday's settle) would show the market starting to price shipment risk; **downside confirmation** requires a break of 97.06 (today's low, Sina January contract) plus another inventory build in the 11-12 EIA report. The loss volume from the three tanker fires has not been published and recovery time is unknown, so this cannot be treated as a supply disruption.

**C. Precious metals: last edition's confirmation condition has triggered, but it is only price confirmation.**
The condition set last time was "reclaim 4,090 with silver turning positive in tandem": today gold is **0.96%** above it and silver is +2.62%, so both legs are met — and they were met with **the dollar flat**, meaning the bid does not depend on dollar weakness. The counter-evidence is equally clear: no ETF holdings or CFTC data yet shows new money entering (positioning reports update Friday), and the shape is a step-by-step grind rather than an event gap, which reads more like continuous allocation. The gold/silver ratio has fallen to around **77.5**, so silver has more beta than gold. **Invalidation**: a drop back below 4,070 (11-06 close) would mean this repair was only weekend short-covering.
