## 4. Situation Assessment

**Crude: bearish within the range, but don't chase the short; 100 is a psychological level, not technical support.** Three sets of facts support this: the intraday low of **100.050** held and the hour gained **+0.32%**; BPCL at 14:03 explicitly attributed its Russian crude cut to "cheaper Middle East grades", buyers voting on spreads; and against that, the Houthis claimed strikes on Saudi **east-west pipeline pumping stations**, Riyadh airport, and the Yanbu and Rabigh facilities (first published at **00:01 and 10:25** today; the 14:48 daily commodities roundup merely repeated it, and Saudi Arabia has **not confirmed** it) — the market clearly discounts single-source claims, with crude still down 2.23% on the day. Implications: upstream beta is bearish, refiners benefit from cheaper feedstock; today's +4.94% in PetroChina is a dividend and cash-flow bid, not a crude call. Invalidation and confirmation: if Saudi officials confirm damage to pumping stations or Yanbu, or verifiable loading disruptions appear, crude jumps back above today's high of **101.736** toward CL's intraday high of **102.63**, and this call is void; conversely, if US-session USOIL cannot reclaim 101.7 and Middle East official selling prices and freight stop deteriorating, keep using 100 as the range floor.

**Gold: mildly bearish short term, don't chase; today's pullback is sector-wide, not gold-specific deterioration.** Evidence: the day gain has shrunk to **+0.22%** (4,006.44), **0.17%** below 14:00's 4,013.07; both pushes to 4,019.3 failed and the intraday high was not retested; silver fell **0.66%** this hour, so precious metals moved together; and Morgan Stanley's 13:42 note on Thursday's pattern (real yields down about 14bp while gold fell 2.8%, a combination last seen in December 2008) shows gold gets sold as an ATM during deleveraging. Implications: don't chase, keep avoiding gold miners — Hong Kong miners were already falling today, diverging from high spot prices. Invalidation and confirmation: a reclaim of **4,019.3** with Nasdaq futures turning up together would mean allocation buying rather than liquidity selling, and this call gets revised; a break of the intraday low **3,978** confirms downside, requiring a fresh look at the validity of the 10-24 high of 4,034. What is missing: gold ETF tonnage was unavailable for a second consecutive edition, so **redemptions cannot be separated from paper selling** — the single largest source of uncertainty in this call.

**Asia AI and A-shares: this is "weights defended, growth bled", not a risk-appetite recovery.** Evidence: KOSPI **-1.45%** and Nikkei **-0.80%**, the two most semiconductor-heavy Asian markets, led the region lower; in A-shares Shanghai rose +0.19% and CSI300 fell -0.04% while ChiNext dropped -1.71%; afternoon turnover was only RMB 597.9bn of a RMB 1.61tn day, and broad-index ETF volume failed to lift growth, pointing to passive buying; and 8-layer HBM4 only ramps in Q2 2027 with 12-layer still this year's focus, so 2026 memory revenue mix barely changes. Positioning: don't chase an AI compute bounce, wait for SK Hynix's Q3 after Tuesday's close and for semis to set direction; the robotics theme (Unitree up over 56% this month) is crowded, don't chase it; in A-shares, dividends and weights are the defensive spot, but today's oil-stock gains came from dividend pricing, and if crude keeps sliding and product spreads narrow, that support weakens. Confirmation and invalidation: if SK Hynix guides HBM above expectations and semis then outperform the Nasdaq, deleveraging has run its course and AI compute can be re-assessed; if semis keep lagging the Nasdaq while A-share weights rally alone, this is a local dividend rotation unrelated to global risk appetite.
