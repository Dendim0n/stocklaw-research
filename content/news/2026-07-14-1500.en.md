🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**Fed Rate Hike Expectations Surge**
Fed Governor Waller stated that if this week's core CPI comes in hot again, the FOMC will have to consider a near-term rate hike, and suggested delaying the dot plot release schedule. Markets are now nearly fully pricing in a September rate hike, with two hikes expected by end of Q1 2027. The "Fed whisperer" also emphasized that core CPI matters more for the Fed. Tonight's 20:30 CPI print will be decisive.

**BOE Rate Hike Bets Surge**
Traders have fully priced in a 25bp hike by September, with rate futures now implying ~50bp of tightening by December (up from ~40bp on Monday), as UK inflation pressures continue to exceed expectations.

**WTI Crude Surges 2.6% Past $80**
Oil prices surged intraday, briefly breaking above $80. The Strait of Hormuz crisis combined with Kazakhstan's H1 production drop of 8.4% is tightening supply expectations. Goldman Sachs noted that Trump's proposed 20% transit fee on passing vessels would make oil structurally more expensive.

**BHP Labor Talks Collapse**
The Australian Combined Ports Union held 5-hour wage negotiations with BHP without reaching a deal. Workers plan to continue striking on July 16, with the next negotiation set for July 21.

**India June WPI Far Above Expectations**
India's June WPI rose 9.87% YoY (vs. 9.15% expected), with the fuel price index surging 27.41% YoY, highlighting significant imported inflation pressures.

**South Korea Launches Real Estate Policy Review**
South Korea's government kicked off a three-day real estate policy seminar focusing on appropriate holding tax levels and ultra-luxury housing regulation. Meanwhile, the anti-stock manipulation task force reported ~10 major cases over the past year, deploying AI to combat market manipulation.

**Hong Kong PCB Stocks Surge**
Kingboard Laminates surged over 14%, Kingboard Holdings and Guanghe Technology rose over 9%, as semiconductor supply chain momentum continues.

**Goldman: Leveraged ETFs Amplify Volatility, Semi Cycle Not Peaked**
Goldman Sachs noted that leveraged-position crowding has been the primary driver of recent tech stock turbulence, but industry fundamentals still provide support.
