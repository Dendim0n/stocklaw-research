## 2. Key incremental items

**1) CoreWeave priced chip collateral at 7%-7.5%, half the cost of its unsecured debt | 15:45, Jin10 citing the FT**
The company is preparing a **multi-billion-dollar loan secured on its chips**, expected to price at **7%-7.5%**, to test market appetite for AI financing. Against the figure already reported at 12:00: the yield on its **unsecured** 2031 notes, 9.75% coupon, is about **15.5%**. Same company, same asset pool, roughly an **8pp** gap between secured and unsecured.
Impact: this is the most direct positive evidence this week on whether AI capex can keep getting funded — **the secured credit market is open; only the unsecured door is shut**. But be precise about what it is: **a structural improvement, not a credit improvement**, with risk shifted from shareholders to creditors with priority. It is a modest positive for the revenue side of AI infrastructure and power equipment, and **not** a reason to turn positive on the equity of highly levered compute operators. Amount and tenor are media reports and unconfirmed by the company.

**2) Goldman moves its first cut from September 2027 to December 2027; the terminal math is 4.25%-4.5% | 13:48, ID 234915**
The substance is more than "one more hike in March": Goldman expects a December hike, another 25bp in March 2027, and now puts the **first cut in December 2027 instead of September 2027**, followed by March, June and September 2028. The current target range is **3.75%-4%**, rising to **4.25%-4.5%** after two hikes. March rather than January is chosen because the energy shock "may feel largely over" by then, though most FOMC members are still expected to signal a third hike in the December dot plot.
Impact: for valuations this is not about 25bp of extra discount rate — it is the **cut starting point moving back a full quarter and the terminal rate being marked higher**, a persistent headwind for long-duration growth, unprofitable AI and gold. The prior issue only carried "extended to March"; this is the full version of that call.

**3) The Middle East settles into "half war, half peace": volumes restored, costs are not | 15:51, ID 234952**
Three usable numbers from this Jin10 round-up: Kpler data show Hormuz transits averaging **10.5 million b/d** since early October versus **13.5 million b/d** before the war; including bypass routes, total Gulf crude exports are back to **16.7 million b/d** (pre-war 17.0m); and per Reuters analysis, **freight and insurance still run about 10x pre-war levels**. IMO data: **131** confirmed maritime security incidents near the strait from the 28 February outbreak of war through 9 November, with **35 in October** alone, the highest single month. The piece also says Brent briefly broke $90 on Tuesday, the first time since end-August, down more than 20% from $118 on 30 October.
Impact: **volumes have largely been repaired; the cost line has not** — which explains why crude can trend lower while diesel and freight stay elevated. Neutral for crude itself, positive for shipping and insurance, negative for refining margins. ## 3. Assessment

**A | AI financing chain: a modest upgrade to bullish, but watch the pricing, not the share price.** Judgement: modestly raise the revenue-side view on AI infrastructure and power equipment from "neutral-to-bullish"; keep the **equity of highly levered compute operators** at neutral with no adds. Basis: CoreWeave secured 7%-7.5% on chip collateral against a 15.5% unsecured bond yield, an 8pp gap that says collateral is accepted and the credit market has not shut on AI assets, while its backlog already stands at $132.5bn and Q3 capex at $14.5bn — the order side is not the problem. Affected: compute leasing, liquid cooling, transformers and grid connection. **Confirmation**: final pricing at the low end, with size and tenor disclosed, plus a second similar structure from another issuer. **Invalidation**: pricing above 8%, or size materially below the "multi-billion" description — that would mean deep collateral haircuts and merely deferred risk.

**B | Rates: the path now runs to end-2027, but there is nothing to verify today.** Judgement: keep a **structural bearish** stance on long-duration growth and gold, with no new directional trade today. Basis: after October CPI (core m/m 0.3%, three-month annualised 3.75%) Treasuries bear-flattened — 10y **+9.03bp to 4.952%**, 2y **+16.54bp to 4.715%** (11-10 21:31 print) — and Goldman pushed the first cut to December 2027 with a terminal of 4.25%-4.5%. **Hard constraint**: cash Treasuries were closed for Veterans Day on 11-11 while equities and Treasury futures traded normally (confirmed by a 16:26 flash), so any claim today about "the market starting to price a higher terminal" can only be inferred from equities and credit spreads. **Invalidation**: after the 11-12 reopen, a 10y back below 4.85% while the 2y holds would mean this is inflation-expectation pricing rather than policy-path pricing, and the bearish stance should be cut.

**C | Gold and oil: both directionless, so the trade is not to trade.** Judgement: gold **neutral**, oil **neutral-to-bearish but not a short here**. Basis: gold fell 0.05% this hour, its session high of 4,066.90 never reached 4,070, the 4,026.55 low held all day, and the +0.61% day is only a recovery off the low. Oil fell this hour despite two supply-side negatives (Russian refining -30%, 131 Hormuz incidents), showing the geopolitical premium is already compressed. Affected: for gold, physical and central-bank demand is cooling (an 11:38 commentator view, not independently verified by me) and rallies remain capped by the downtrend; for oil, cracks and freight benefit while crude itself lacks direction. **4,070 and 4,026 are range boundaries and psychological levels; a touch is not a hold**. Oil needs the official EIA weekly to resolve the standoff between API's +8.968m barrels and expectations of -1.109m.
