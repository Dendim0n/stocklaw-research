## Key incremental items

### 1. BlackRock upgrades Treasuries, ends a near six-year underweight, cuts US equities to neutral

- **Time**: 14 Oct, 13:41 CST | **Source**: Jin10 flash (BlackRock Investment Institute report)
- **Content**: BlackRock raised its allocation rating on US Treasuries, favouring the short end, saying the sharp rise in yields makes prospective returns more attractive and that "higher yields, and a market that may be overpricing Fed tightening, create room" to end its underweight — which had been one of its main allocation stances for nearly six years. Funding came partly from its US equity allocation; US equities were cut to neutral.
- **Impact**: **The most substantial item this hour — after long-end yields hit multi-decade highs, this is the first sizeable institutional "buyer stepping in" language, bullish for short-dated Treasuries and a dent to the narrative of multiple expansion for long-duration growth stocks.** To be clear: this is BlackRock's view on its own portfolio, not market-wide flow data, so it does not support a claim that "money is moving from stocks into bonds".

### 2. The world's largest copper mine, Escondida, is set to strike tonight; LME copper approaches $14,700/t

- **Time**: 14 Oct, 13:24 CST (strike set for 8:00am local Chilean time, i.e. 19:00 Beijing) | **Source**: Jin10 flash; Jin10 Futures citing Reuters
- **Content**: The supervisors' union at BHP's Escondida mine in Chile failed to reach agreement in mediation and workers will strike from 8:00am local Wednesday; the union represents about a quarter of direct employees. Jin10 reported London copper approaching $14,700/t, near a record high. Background: Chilean Copper Commission data showed Escondida output fell 27.6% year on year in August to 76,100 tonnes (10-10). A separate 11:22 report said the union had restarted talks to avoid a strike, so the situation is still evolving.
- **Impact**: **A supply-side event, near-term bullish for copper and copper miners, with little chance of contrary evidence before 19:00; but it is an output disruption rather than a demand signal and cannot be extrapolated into "accelerating global copper demand".** Confirmation: a stoppage lasting more than 24–48 hours, or other Chilean mines joining. Counter-evidence: a last-minute deal would quickly unwind the supply premium.

### 3. ASML's Q3 and guidance came in above market expectations

- **Time**: 14 Oct, 13:02–13:09 CST | **Source**: Jin10 flash (ASML Q3 results release)
- **Content**: Q3 net sales of €11.66bn, gross margin 57.5%, net income €5.0bn; Q4 guidance of €14.2–15.2bn in net sales (market estimate €14.189bn) and gross margin about 57%; full-year guidance of €44–45bn (market estimate €43.647bn) with full-year gross margin raised to about 56% (previously 54%–56%); an interim dividend of €1.88 per share payable 4 November. The company said planned 2027 and 2028 volumes will not fully meet customer demand and that it is studying a 2028 capacity increase of more than the previously announced 30%; the CEO said order momentum kept growing versus the prior quarter and that some customers have expressed interest in 2029 and even 2030 orders.
- **Impact**: **The increment versus the prior edition is in comparable terms — guidance above consensus, an upward revision to the gross-margin range, and a 2027–2028 supply shortfall. Fundamentally bullish for semiconductor equipment, leading-edge foundry and memory.** A counterpoint: Jin10's 13:48 wrap notes growing industry scepticism about how long the AI boom can last; implied Q3 net margin of about 43% is well above roughly 31% in Q2, and tax rate and one-off items must be checked on the call rather than read as a step change in recurring profitability.

### 4. Japanese institutions are repatriating funds into JGBs; overseas bonds see net selling

- **Time**: 14 Oct, 13:42 CST | **Source**: Jin10 article ID 232409 (citing the Financial Times)
- **Content**: Based on Japan Securities Dealers Association data, large Japanese banks and insurers net bought roughly ¥5.4tn (about $34bn) of JGBs with maturities over two years in the five months to August, the highest for that period since early 2023. Ministry of Finance data show net selling of overseas bonds by large Japanese investors rose to ¥2.5tn in September, among the highest since 2005, and Japanese banks' net selling of overseas bonds in the first nine months of 2026 exceeded any prior year's same-period level. Pension funds, however, have not notably shifted; Japanese pensions still net bought about ¥11.4tn of overseas long-term bonds this year.
- **Impact**: **Structurally bearish for marginal demand for US and European bonds and bullish for yen assets — an independent evidence chain for the "rising term premium" narrative at the long end.** The report itself states a full capital repatriation cannot yet be confirmed — banks and insurers are moving, pensions are not — so it does not support a conclusion that "overseas money is fleeing Treasuries".

### 5. Options market is pricing late-month volatility; implied average daily S&P 500 move up about 50%

- **Time**: 14 Oct, 13:27 CST | **Source**: Jin10 article ID 232408 (citing Dow Jones Market Data, ORATS, BTIG, Bespoke)

## Situation assessment

**Judgement one: the "yields rise forever" narrative met positive language from the allocation side for the first time this hour, but that is not yet a reversal.** Three aligned signals support this: BlackRock upgrading Treasuries and ending a near six-year underweight in favour of the short end; UOB saying dollar upside momentum is fading and that a pullback could test the 21-day EMA at 101.50; and the WSJ survey consensus for tonight's CPI of +0.6% m/m headline, +3.6% y/y, with core at just +0.2% m/m and +2.5% y/y (Jin10, 11:58), which if delivered would cool tightening expectations at the margin. The counter-evidence is just as hard: the 10Y Treasury yield closed at 5.22% and the 30Y at 5.56% on 10-13, still multi-decade highs, and BondBloxx's strategist argues above 5% is not abnormal — deficits and issuance are what push the term premium higher. Asset implications: in the base case short-dated Treasuries and gold are relatively favoured, valuation pressure on long-duration growth stocks eases at the margin, and a softer dollar is mildly positive for the yuan and emerging-market assets. Confirmation: core CPI at or below +0.2% m/m tonight with the 10Y no longer making new highs. Invalidation: core m/m at 0.3% or above — the threshold Jin10 article 232274 flagged — which would immediately overturn the "allocation money stepping in" logic, hit the long end first, bounce the dollar and simultaneously remove gold's near-term support.

**Judgement two: copper's supply premium is tradeable, but it is not demand evidence, and gold's pullback does not mean "money is rotating into industrial metals".** Basis: the Escondida supervisors' union failed in mediation with the strike set for 8:00am local Chilean time (19:00 Beijing), and the mine's August output was already down 27.6% y/y; the platform copper quote is $6.6554/lb (about $14,670/t, +0.36% on the day), consistent in magnitude with Jin10's "LME copper approaching $14,700". But gold -0.38%, silver -1.26% and copper +0.36% this hour are relative performance only; there is no flow or positioning data to support a conclusion that money moved from precious to industrial metals. A better fit is that precious metals are consolidating at record highs while copper is driven by a single supply event. Asset implications: copper miners and base metals favoured, cost-sensitive smelting and processing under pressure; precious metals near-term neutral pending CPI. Counter-evidence: an 11:22 report said the union restarted talks to avoid a strike, so a deal before 19:00 would quickly unwind the copper premium; a one-day stoppage would be immaterial to the annual supply-demand balance.

**Judgement three: the internal split in Asian equities shows index-level signals carry little information, and tonight through 28 October is the test for the low-volatility regime.** Basis: inside this window the ChiNext index fell more than 2% (wind equipment, aerospace equipment and semiconductors leading the decline, 13:37) and the FTSE China A50 futures fell more than 1% (13:44), while the Hang Seng Tech index rose more than 2% (13:24), Taiwan's weighted index closed up 0.59% at 48,625.83 and Korea's KOSPI erased an early 2.6% gain to turn negative (13:42) — same region, same period, opposite directions, implying the drivers are sector and flow structure rather than macro direction. Adding options pricing that shows the S&P 500's expected average daily move rising about 50% from 28 October, with the index already 52 sessions without a 1% daily drop, my lean is: **do not chase risk assets before CPI lands, and do not treat "low volatility" as a reason to add leverage; watch first and let the 20:30 print and the 21:30 US open show the structural reaction before setting direction.** What is missing this edition: A-share closing turnover and foreign-flow data (only available after 15:00) and whether Hong Kong's tech strength has southbound support — neither is obtainable, so this third judgement is weaker than the first two. Counter-evidence: if CPI meets consensus and AI heavyweights keep underpinning the index, the low-volatility regime can persist to the 28 October FOMC.
