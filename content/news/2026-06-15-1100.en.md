🧭 **Analysis**

Russia's Kyiv strikes serve as a reminder that the Iran-US deal only resolves one geopolitical variable — the Russia-Ukraine conflict and other flashpoints remain active, sustaining the case for safe-haven assets.

Gold's continued surge despite Iran-US positives suggests the market has shifted from "geopolitical risk premium" to "monetary system restructuring" pricing — driven by Singapore's gold clearing infrastructure, persistent central bank buying, and Fed policy uncertainty.

Silver's 4% jump signals the precious metals rally may be broadening beyond gold. Silver typically delivers greater elasticity in the latter stages of a bull market.

Crude breaking below $80.5 enters a critical support zone. If the Iran deal is formalized this week and Hormuz reopening proceeds smoothly, WTI could test $78-79. However, the Kyiv strikes suggest geopolitical risk isn't retreating in a straight line, potentially limiting oil's downside.

The NDRC's nine-sector energy-saving initiative is a medium-to-long-term positive for related sectors, though near-term market reaction may be limited. Aluminum smelting and coal power stocks warrant continued monitoring.
