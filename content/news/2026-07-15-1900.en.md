## 🧭 Analysis

The Middle East is the dominant theme this hour — the US airstrike on civilian facilities marks a qualitative escalation from military engagement to infrastructure targeting.

Trump's insistence on the Hormuz toll has far exceeded market expectations, invalidating earlier optimistic readings that the plan had been abandoned.

If the transit fee is implemented, global crude trade cost structures will fundamentally change, and the Brent-WTI spread could widen sharply.

The ECB's "ready to act" rhetoric deserves attention — if sustained oil price gains fuel inflation expectations, the ECB may be forced to delay rate cuts or even pivot toward tightening.

Gold's retreat from the 4060 resistance zone is healthy. Buying support exists at the 4000 round number, but a breakout above 4100 requires a fresh catalyst.

Crude oil is locked in intense battle around 80 — geopolitical risk premium is partially priced in, but any material deterioration in Hormuz could push prices through the 82-85 range.

With the Black Sea oil route under attack and Hormuz tensions simultaneously elevated, two critical global energy chokepoints are under pressure for the first time in years.

Wall Street's top five banks posting 39% profit growth tells one story: geopolitical turmoil ≠ financial crisis. High-volatility environments actually benefit market-making and trading operations.

China's decade-low pork prices warrant continued attention — deflationary pressure may be spreading from industrial goods to consumer staples.
