## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**1. Iran Announces Hormuz Strait Passage Suspension**
Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Affairs Authority announced that due to US "aggressive actions," the strait is no longer safe for navigation and will only resume once "order and calm are restored." This is the most serious strait closure threat in recent years, directly igniting crude prices.

**2. Japan Suspected of Spending $53B on FX Intervention, US to Follow**
Insources reveal Japan likely spent over $50 billion intervening in FX markets on Thursday, potentially a historic record. The US Treasury has notified banks it may also intervene in the JPY market on Friday, asking banks to prepare. Strong signal of coordinated US-Japan intervention.

**3. Russia Launches Massive Strikes on Kyiv Military Facilities**
Russia's Defense Ministry reported large-scale strikes on multiple military and logistics targets in Kyiv overnight July 31–August 1. Ukrainian President Zelensky confirmed 9 killed, dozens injured; 18 buildings and the Lithuanian Embassy damaged. Zelensky urged urgent resolution of Patriot interceptor missile shortage.

**4. Ukrainian Drones Sink Russian Civilian Vessel in Black Sea**
Rosatom reported one of its civilian vessels was sunk overnight by Ukrainian drones in the Black Sea; all crew survived. Russia condemned the attack as "piracy."

**5. Kuwait Affected by Iranian Attacks**
Kuwait's military reported destroying hostile drones in Kuwaiti airspace since Saturday dawn. Iranian attacks hit civilian facilities on Bubiyan Island and a government building in the north. Regional conflict spillover risk rising.

**6. Hungary's Paks Nuclear Plant May Fully Shut Down This Weekend**
Hungarian PM Magyar warned that low Danube water levels may force the Paks nuclear plant to fully shut down this weekend. New decrees seek voluntary power demand cuts from large users, with mandatory sanctions if insufficient. New variable for European energy supply.

**7. Fed Internal Dissent Intensifies**
Wash's streamlined communication strategy faces investor criticism as "counterproductive," with 30-year Treasury yields surging. Hawks Hamack and Kashkari loudly warn against entrenched inflation. Wash caught between White House preferences and internal pressure.

## 🧭 Situation Assessment

Iran's Hormuz Strait closure declaration pushes geopolitical risk to new heights, but the market is still digesting the gap between "threat" and "actual execution."

The crude surge reflects risk premium rather than actual supply disruption — truly blocking the strait requires military capability, and Iran's move is more political pressure than anything.

Gold's pullback suggests the market hasn't entered full safe-haven mode, with some capital taking profits from short-term highs.

Japan's $53 billion intervention is unprecedented in scale, and US follow-through is a rare coordinated G7 action, signaling tolerance for yen depreciation has reached its limit.

But intervention buys time, not trend reversal — yen trajectory ultimately depends on the US-Japan rate differential. If Wash's Fed continues refusing clear forward guidance, yen pressure won't disappear.

Russia-Ukraine battlefield intensity is escalating again, with massive Kyiv airstrikes and a civilian vessel sunk in the Black Sea — no signs of de-escalation.

Hungary's nuclear plant shutdown risk is a new gray rhino for European energy markets; Danube water levels need close monitoring.
