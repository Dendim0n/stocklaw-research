## 4. Situation assessment

**Judgement 1: what is falling in Asia is AI-hardware crowding, not Asian risk assets - so broad indices should not be read as bearish, but hardware should not be bought on the dip.**

The evidence is the simultaneous divergence: STAR 50 -3.51%, ChiNext -1.74%, CSI 500 -1.04% and KOSPI -4%, against just -0.52% for CSI 300, +0.78% for Hang Seng Tech and +1.43% for Tencent; MSCI Asia-Pacific overall lost only 1%, far less than the KOSPI's 4%. Breadth in the morning points the same way: at 11:35 more than 3,300 A-share stocks were still higher, with the bleeding confined to the compute chain. Morning and afternoon turnover cannot be compared (only three minutes of the afternoon had traded), so volume is not used as evidence here. **Implication**: neutral A-share broad indices, mildly bullish Hong Kong platforms, bearish AI hardware. **Invalidation**: if at the 15:00 close CSI 300 breaks its session low of 3,969.55 while the Hang Seng turns negative and Hang Seng Tech gives back all of its gain, the selling has spread from hardware to the broad market and the "rotation" call is void.

**Judgement 2: crude's bounce has no attributable news behind it and is not a reversal of the supply story - do not chase it up, and do not chase it down either.**

Three supports: no fresh crude supply news accompanied the rally, the only energy headline being a five-year LNG term deal; WTI December has recovered 1.04% from its session low and last hour's invalidation triggers (USOIL below 95.173, WTI December below 96.120) were not hit; and the 8% morning drop in Shanghai SC crude lines up exactly with the time-zone gap, filling in Monday's -7.4% overseas move rather than reflecting a new event. **Implication**: neutral crude. The cause of the bounce cannot be identified this hour; the working view is pre-election position adjustment, but that is an argument by elimination with no positioning or flow data behind it, so it is not a basis for trading. Establishing exposure here means using a fundamental rationale to make an event bet. **Invalidation**: pricing of supply restoration only begins if Washington publishes a concrete list of concessions or WTI December falls back below 96.120; conversely, if Brent's spread over WTI widens further from 8.23, the market is re-pricing Middle East risk and the crude call should shift to mildly bullish.

**Judgement 3: the RBA's "hike plus pause hint" is a peak-tightening signal, but gold did not take it - what is pressing gold is the level of real yields, not the expectation of more hikes.**

The evidence: a 6-3 split vote, Bullock's "may pause / no guidance", and State Street's "dovish, AUD faces downside" together form a fairly clean peak-tightening combination; yet gold fell only 0.06% on the hour, silver 0.41%, and the gold/silver ratio rose from 78.95 to 79.22 - none of the bullish impulse transmitted. The comparator is the 10-year TIPS real yield: **2.62%** on 11-02, up **2bp** on the day (2.60% on 10-30), with no easing at the nominal end. **Implication**: bearish-to-neutral precious metals; buying the dip requires real yields to turn, not a dovish central bank; bearish AUD. **Invalidation**: if real yields fall this week and gold still does not rise, the selling is coming from somewhere else (ETF redemptions or fading central-bank demand) and new evidence is needed; if gold reclaims Monday's close of 3,946.47 and silver returns above 50, this call is void.
