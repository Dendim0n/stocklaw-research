🔥 **Key Headlines This Hour**

1. **Israel resumes heavy bombardment of southern Lebanon** — Israeli forces shelled and conducted airstrikes on Habush town in the Nabatieh area of southern Lebanon. Hezbollah and the IDF remain locked in fierce fighting across southern Lebanon; Israeli military casualties have risen to 932. The ceasefire brokered just two days ago has effectively collapsed.

2. **Iran takes hardline stance: distrusts US, preparing dual scenarios** — Iranian parliamentary officials declared Lebanon ceasefire a "red line" that will not be conceded. A senior Iranian advisor warned the US to act "with extreme caution" to avoid the peace deal becoming a casualty of Israeli actions.

3. **Iran issues new Strait of Hormuz transit rules** — Iran now requires all transiting vessels to submit applications 48 hours in advance and hold insurance recognized by the Persian Gulf Strait Administration. Compliant vessels can obtain insurance free of charge. Transit threshold tightened.

4. **World Bank warns: Asian LNG prices surged 94%** — The World Bank's March natural gas price index rose 24%, with Asian LNG benchmark skyrocketing ~94% and European benchmark up 59%. Surging energy costs may exacerbate global inflationary pressures.

5. **Slovakia confirms continued Russian gas imports** — Slovakia states it will meet most natural gas demand through Russian supply, further entrenching Europe's energy divergence.

6. **Australia confirms first H5N1 avian influenza case** — A migratory bird in Western Australia tested positive for H5N1, marking the first confirmed case on the Australian continent, raising public health concerns.

7. **Analysts warn: gold's path next week hinges on data** — SPI Asset Management partner said gold likely to remain range-bound next week; markets awaiting US core PCE data for Fed rate path clues, with downside risk from sell-offs.

🧭 **Market Assessment**

Israel-Lebanon conflict has reignited just two days after a ceasefire agreement, with the intensity of Israeli bombardment of southern Lebanon far exceeding expectations; Hezbollah is vowed not to back down and the ceasefire framework is effectively dead.

Iran's new Strait of Hormuz transit rules combined with its hawkish posture on the US are repricing Middle East risk premiums, providing sustained support for crude oil prices.

Gold has broken below $4,160, Goldman Sachs has cut its year-end target to $4,900, and the market narrative is shifting from safe-haven demand toward Fed tightening expectations — the dollar index breaking above 101, its highest in over a year, is providing strong overhead resistance.

World Bank data showing Asian LNG prices surging 94% risks driving up industrial costs across the Asia-Pacific if sustained, with medium-term implications for global supply chains and inflation dynamics.

Next week's core PCE data will be the critical inflection point — if inflation comes in above expectations, Fed rate cut expectations will be further erased, creating a double headwind for gold and risk assets.
