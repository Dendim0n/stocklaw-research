🔥 **Overnight Key News**

**1. US-Iran conflict escalates in the Strait of Hormuz**
US forces sank the Suezmax tanker "Kylo" (empty) and crippled "Stark I" and "Downy"; Iran struck 3 tankers plus US-linked vessels, a US drone and an unmanned surface vessel. Iran's top security official announced a new "restricted zone" outside the Strait, with parliament speaker warning retaliation will be "faster, heavier, more painful." Kpler: only 1 commercial ship crossed the Strait on Saturday; 10-day average of 10 ships/day is the lowest since May; US has diverted 92 commercial vessels.

**2. Oil prices jump**
WTI opened +0.9% back above $90, Brent above $95 (+0.7%). US diesel retail average hit a record $5.85/gallon.

**3. Russia-Ukraine talks restart: US shuttle diplomacy yields "three-day ceasefire"**
Witkoff and Kushner visited Moscow then Kyiv (Witkoff's 8th trip to Russia; their first to Ukraine), holding 3-hour talks with Putin. Zelensky called the talks "very substantive," but territorial disputes (Donbas) remain unresolved and Ukraine is prepared for another winter of war; the US hopes to announce a new round of talks soon.

**4. Fed September hike odds hit 58.3% while Trump pressures for cuts**
August NFP smashed expectations, reviving hike pricing — but Trump, while praising the data, again demanded sharp rate cuts, creating a rare divergence between market pricing and political pressure; Fed's Hammack says inflation is still too high and action is needed.

**5. China headlines**
8 central state-owned financial firms plan a 360bn yuan capital injection, backed by special treasury bonds; PBoC conducts 500bn yuan outright reverse repo Monday.

**6. Asset allocation moves**
Norway's sovereign wealth fund plans to cut US Treasury holdings by ~$80bn; France completed repatriation of its entire national gold reserve.

**7. AI momentum intact**

🧭 **Assessment**

The Strait of Hormuz is the single biggest risk variable for global markets — escalation from tanker strikes to a "restricted zone" threat makes supply-disruption risk materially higher.

Oil has already reacted (WTI back above $90, Brent above $95), but actual transit volume is at its lowest since May; if the restricted zone materializes, oil has room for a second leg higher.

Gold fell over 2% intraday Friday on the strong NFP, but safe-haven demand and Trump's pressure for cuts provide dual support — the 4400 level is the near-term line in the sand.

The 58.3% hike odds versus open presidential pressure for cuts is a rare divergence; this week's CPI will be the decisive catalyst.

The "three-day ceasefire" and shuttle diplomacy signal a thaw, but territorial disputes remain unresolved, so the geopolitical risk premium won't fully dissipate soon.

US markets are closed today (Labor Day); volatility will concentrate in European bonds, FX and index futures — thin liquidity could amplify moves.
