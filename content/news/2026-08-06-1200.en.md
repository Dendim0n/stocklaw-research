## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**1. Fed Internal Divergence Intensifies: Daly vs. Warsh**

San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly warned that inflation pressures from tariffs, energy prices, and AI investment could evolve from short-term shocks into persistent problems, potentially requiring faster rate hikes. Meanwhile, Chair Kevin Warsh is betting that AI-driven productivity gains will lower inflation and create room for future cuts. The 30-year Treasury yield hit a 19-year high.

**2. A-Shares Midday: ChiNext Down 0.67%, Hang Seng Tech Falls 2%**

A-share indices were mixed: Shanghai Composite flat (+0.01%), Shenzhen -0.52%, ChiNext -0.67%. CPO and PCB concepts outperformed. Hong Kong AI application stocks sold off broadly, with Quantgo Tech down ~8%, Alibaba and Xiaomi each down over 3%.

**3. Indonesia Nickel Quota Surge Pressures SHFE Nickel**

Indonesia's largest nickel miner WBN received approval for 25 million tons of new H2 quota, and Indonesian officials signaled "measured relaxation" of RKAB approvals. The previously supportive "tight ore supply" narrative collapsed, sending SHFE nickel down over 2%.

**4. China Responds to US Blocking China-Argentina Enterprise Cooperation**

**6. China Futures Morning: Precious Metals Surge, Chemicals Slide**

SHFE gold and silver rose over 3%, red dates and iron ore up over 2%. On the downside: methanol fell over 3%, propylene down nearly 3%, SHFE nickel, PP and others down over 2%.

## 🧭 Market Assessment

The Fed's hawk-dove duel is becoming the market's core narrative — Daly's camp fears inflation diffusion while Warsh's camp bets on AI-driven disinflation, with the outcome directly determining H2 rate trajectory.

Gold's spike to 4300 followed by retreat signals intense market gaming between inflation fears and rate expectations, with elevated short-term volatility ahead.

The sharp pullback in Hong Kong tech stocks reflects growing concerns about AI valuation bubbles, as previously over-optimistic expectations face correction.

Indonesia's nickel quota relaxation breaks the tight-supply thesis, potentially opening a downward channel for nickel prices — watch subsequent quota execution.

The Middle East remains an uncertainty factor; Israeli casualties in Lebanon could trigger a new round of retaliatory actions.
