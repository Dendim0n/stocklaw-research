🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**🚨 US-Iran Conflict Escalates Sharply — Multiple Gulf States Attacked**

The situation deteriorated significantly this hour. Iran's IRGC issued multiple statements confirming strikes on US aircraft carrier logistics support center and refueling platform at Duqm Port in Oman, and announced strikes on a second "violating vessel" in the Strait of Hormuz. More critically, Al Arabiya reported that Iran launched fresh attacks on **Bahrain, Qatar, UAE, and Kuwait**. Kuwait Armed Forces confirmed they are intercepting hostile aerial targets in Kuwaiti airspace. Qatar's Defense Ministry reported being hit by multiple ballistic missiles.

**Former Emir of Qatar Passes Away**

The Qatari Royal Court announced the passing of former Emir Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani. This development adds further uncertainty amid extreme regional tensions.

**Iran's Speaker: Era of Unilateral Agreements Is Over**

Iranian Parliament Speaker Qalibaf posted on social media, declaring "the era of unilateral agreements is over," stating Iran had previously demanded commitments be honored or consequences would follow, and that "reality is now issuing its warning." Iran's military spokesperson also stated that US attempts to establish "illegal shipping lanes" in the Strait of Hormuz have exacerbated regional insecurity, and Iran's armed forces target list is being continuously updated.

**Iran-Oman Talks Address Strait Shipping Management**

Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson said Saturday's talks with Oman primarily discussed traffic and shipping management arrangements in the Strait of Hormuz, with a Qatari delegation present for some discussions. UKMTO confirmed that crew from a vessel involved in an incident 9 nautical miles east of Oman have been rescued by local authorities.

**Analyst Warns: Hormuz Could Become a "Long-Term Powder Keg"**

Ian Ralby, Senior Fellow at the Maritime Strategy Center and President of Auxilium Worldwide, warned that the US-Iran war over the Strait of Hormuz cannot be resolved militarily, and ongoing conflict could plunge the region into prolonged instability.

**Japan to Push GPIF Toward More Alternative Assets**

Nikkei reported that Japan aims to increase the allocation of unlisted stocks, real estate, and other alternative investments in the portfolio of GPIF, the world's largest pension fund. Finance Minister Kato has been trying to boost Japan's sluggish capital markets.

**Beijing Airports Lift Flight Delay Yellow Alert**

Both Beijing Capital Airport and Daxing Airport have lifted their large-scale flight delay yellow response mechanisms.

🧭 **Situation Analysis**

Middle East geopolitical risk has escalated from a "war of words" to multi-point military conflict, with the actual navigational safety of the Strait of Hormuz under direct threat.

Iran's decision to launch simultaneous strikes on multiple Gulf states while Qatar was mediating indicates Tehran has no intention of returning to the negotiating table in the near term, preferring military action to gain leverage.

Kuwait and Bahrain have been directly drawn into the conflict for the first time — spillover effects are expanding, with the entire western Persian Gulf now on high alert.

If the conflict persists, the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 20% of global oil passes, faces a real risk of disruption. Oil prices could see a sharp gap at Monday's open.

Gold at $4,119 currently does not reflect the weekend's geopolitical escalation. A significant gap-up is likely at Monday's Asian open — watch the $4,150-$4,200 resistance zone.

Japan's GPIF move toward alternative assets is worth monitoring, as it may prompt other sovereign funds to follow suit, creating long-term demand support for global private equity and real estate assets.
