## 3. Assessment

**A｜Gold: direction right, reference level wrong by $54 — that one is on me.**

Yesterday I wrote: core m/m ≥0.3% plus gold losing 4,108 means back to bearish, with 4,070 as the downside reference. Core came in at 0.3% and gold did not stop at 4,070 — it closed **4,032.94 (−2.61%)**, traded near **4,016**, i.e. **$54 (1.3%) below the reference**.

Where the reasoning failed: 4,070 was a **path marker** derived from the width of the intraday range, and I treated it as a target. This decline was set by the discount rate and the dollar together (2-year +17bp, 10-year 4.98%, DXY 102.23) — **the size of the move is a function of rates, not of a technical level**. In rate-driven moves a technical level can only be written as "a possible resting point", never as "the downside reference".

Call for today: **bearish, but do not chase**. Reasons: after −2.61% gold stabilised in Asia (4,037.46, +0.11%); SPDR Gold Trust holdings fell only **0.285t to 1,042.223t** (−0.03% at 07:26), so there is no ETF-level selling evidence; the gold/silver ratio barely moved at 78.30, so this is broad bullion weakness rather than gold-specific liquidation. **4,070–4,080 has flipped from support to resistance.** Invalidations: a close back above 4,080 makes this a false break. **Evidence gap**: no TIPS real yields and no CFTC positioning locally, so "real rates up" and "risk premium down" cannot be separated — if the latter dominates, the decline is young; if the former, December is already 88.5% + 11.5% priced and the room is limited. Until that is resolved, no target price should be quoted.

**B｜Crude: the data confirmed, the price did not — do not chase either way.**

Yesterday's trigger was "below 84.85 plus a soft API draw opens the next leg". API was not soft, it was **+8.968m versus −327k expected**, a 9.295m miss; yet WTI December settled 86.04 (−0.93%), still **1.4% above** 84.85, and Asia drifted up to 86.17. **Half the condition triggered: the data did, the price did not.**

Assessment: bearish holds, but this is a **inventory-and-normalisation short, not a geopolitical short**. The three Iranian headlines failed to lift the price, which is part of the short thesis rather than a rebuttal — the war premium is already thin. Two reasons not to chase: API weekly swings are extreme (prior −5.267m), so the official EIA report is needed; and 86 sits in the upper part of the 84.85–86.93 range, which is poor risk-reward. **Operational reading (conditional, not an instruction)**: if EIA confirms a large build and price holds above 86, that is "bearish data that will not fall" and short exposure should be reduced; if price breaks 84.85 with inventory confirmation, that is the trend leg. **Counter-evidence**: a verified Houthi strike that actually removes barrels would reverse this pricing quickly.

**C｜AI hardware: both tests failed, but the nature is multiple compression, not demand destruction.**

Yesterday's tests were "can NVDA reclaim 180 and SOX reclaim 10,033". Both failed (168.89, 9,998.33). Yet the order side did not deteriorate: CoreWeave Q3 revenue **$3.66bn (+167%, expected $3.55bn)**, EPS loss 1.27 versus 1.46 expected, full-year guidance raised from $12.4–13.2bn to **$13.0–13.3bn**, and the stock was up as much as 8.7% after hours. Management said 2027 renewals will add **close to $4bn of annual incremental revenue**, that 70% of this year's deals were prepaid, and that effective demand through end-2027 is **10x** available capacity.

Call: **valuation compression holds; the demand side is not being cut**. The dividing line is concrete — **orders and prepayments** (CoreWeave has them) versus **multiples and the discount rate** (which is what is being sold). Risks and counter-evidence: the CFTC extended its review of compute futures by at least **60 days** (04:02–04:10, FT), leaving the nascent market without public forward pricing and with higher volatility; Fitch said a Democratic sweep could tighten data-centre permitting and **supports the view that AI capex peaks in 2027** (04:06); Nscale is considering delaying a **$35bn** listing because of the AI selloff (03:10). All three are early signals of a capex peak, but all are "considering" or "could", not events.

**D｜Primary market: Anthropic has written the capital loop into its prospectus.**

Facts: 825m shares × $70–78 = $57.75–64.35bn; plus $26bn of cornerstone private placement, up to $90.4bn; target valuation $1.4trn. **The cornerstone investors are AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC Partners — its own chip and cloud suppliers.**

Inference (this is judgement, not fact): suppliers inject equity into the customer, the customer spends it on the suppliers' chips, and that spend becomes the suppliers' revenue — a loop that lifts both sides' reported revenue and mark-to-market valuations without generating external cash flow. It **does not prove** a bubble, but it lowers the comparability of "AI revenue" as a number. **The test**: first-day trading on 11-20 and the subsequent lock-up rhythm will say more about whether $1.4trn is accepted by outside capital than any sell-side note.
