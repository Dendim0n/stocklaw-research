## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The Strait of Hormuz traffic hitting zero is the biggest black swan event of this Monday open — crude supply-side risk premiums are being priced in non-linearly.

Iran's ballistic missile strike on US military targets in Jordan signals the conflict is no longer confined to Persian Gulf waters; the front line has now extended toward the Red Sea, directly challenging Jordan's strategic role as a US logistics hub.

A rare divergence between gold and oil — crude surging 2%+ while gold actually edged lower — suggests the market is currently prioritizing physical supply disruption over safe-haven demand, a warning sign for gold bulls.

Today's Chinese asset rally stands in stark contrast to the Middle East turmoil; the A50's 3% surge likely reflects domestic policy expectations and improved liquidity, but risk appetite could reverse rapidly if the Middle East situation spirals further.

The combination of hawkish Fed signals and Middle East geopolitical risk creates a classic "rising inflation expectations + rising tightening expectations" mix — extremely unfavorable for tech valuations, making this week's earnings season a tough battle ahead.
