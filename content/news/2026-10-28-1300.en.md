## 4. Read on the situation

**Judgement 1 (a correction to the previous edition): silver's catch-down makes this look like position-level de-risking across the complex rather than a gold-specific physical-demand problem — no chasing the short before the FOMC, but no case for buying the dip either.**
Evidence: (1) gold -0.59% and silver -1.00% left the gold/silver ratio almost unchanged at 74.90→74.95, i.e. both were cut roughly in proportion, not silver alone breaking; (2) the dollar index at 102.412 is exactly flat versus 12:00, so this hour is not dollar-driven; (3) the long end is still above 5% (10-27 official: 10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.30%) and Japan's 5Y rose 3.5bp to 2.265%, so the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset has not improved; (4) the physical gap remains (Citi sees Indian Q4 imports at 106t, half the historic average — listed in the previous edition); (5) but gold is already about 24% below its late-February level, and 4,000 is a psychological level rather than a valuation anchor, so a further break needs a new catalyst.
Impact: gold miners' relative strength stays weak; the "relatively clean hedge" description of silver fails this hour — when both fall together it provides no diversification.
What would invalidate it: after the **02:00 decision**, gold reclaiming today's base of **4,060.75**, or silver back above **54.404**. Confirmation of the bear case: two consecutive closes for gold below 4,000–4,040 with the gold/silver ratio still rising (silver falling faster), which would reclassify this from rate pricing to risk-asset liquidation.

**Judgement 2: the Treasury question is shifting from "how high are rates" to "who still buys automatically" — that matters more than today's gold price, and it explains why equities can absorb supply while the long end sits near 5%.**
Evidence: (1) the Bloomberg study's collapse in allied buying (USD 174.3bn, -60%+) with Japan at USD 389m and Korea at -USD 23.8bn looks structural rather than a single-month wobble; (2) immediate auction demand is fine: the 10-27 5-year stopped at 4.845%, below the prior 5.03%, with a 2.33 bid-to-cover versus 2.21 — **price-sensitive buyers came back while price-insensitive buyers left**, which is precisely a higher term premium; (3) Japan's 5Y at 2.265% and 2Y at 1.895% both rose, giving Japanese money more reason to stay home; (4) but this is a second-hand media compilation with custodian-attribution error, so it is not a precise official flow.
Impact: bearish for long-duration assets that lean on the discount rate (unprofitable growth, long-dated cash flows); limited for bank net interest margins and short-end business; the dollar's direction depends on the FOMC language, not on this holdings data.
What would invalidate it: 10Y back below 4.80% after the decision with the next 5-year auction still above a 2.3 cover — that would make this custodian noise; a 10Y above 5.05% (5.00% on both 10-26 and 10-23) would strengthen the structural case.

**Judgement 3: the morning's A-share rally has not converted into incremental money, and style divergence is widening instead — broad indices can wait for a pullback, and chasing today's strongest sleeve is the worst risk/reward.**
Evidence: (1) within five minutes of the reopen the SSE Composite was 3,672.19, below both the midday close and the 3,676.92 open, SSE 50 went from +0.08% to -0.08% and CSI 300 from +0.68% to +0.57%; (2) the 2.30pp style gap is the widest of the day (1.94pp at 11:00, 2.20pp at midday); (3) Hong Kong gave back in parallel (Hang Seng +0.28% versus +0.43% at midday, Hang Seng Tech +1.52% versus 1.80%), so this is a regional risk-appetite fade rather than an A-share-specific move; (4) structural evidence: the chip LOF suspension for premium and Dongshan Precision's 0.52x public tranche — money is chasing on-exchange premiums rather than absorbing new supply, a funding mix that does not support persistence; (5) but turnover is still expanding (RMB 1.01trn in the morning session, up RMB 143.7bn), so this is high-level churn rather than a low-volume fade.
Impact: broad indices (CSI 300 / CSI 500) still offer better risk/reward than any single theme; memory and semi equipment remain the source of relative strength but crowding is rising; the limit-up proxies in humanoid robots and PEEK are the worst place to chase.
What would invalidate it: the Composite closing above the **3,676.92** open with SSE 50 turning positive would kill "no incremental money"; full-day turnover below 10-27's would downgrade "volume-backed rally" to "a pulse".
