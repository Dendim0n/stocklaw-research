**① WSJ: US forces struck Qeshm Island on Monday, and Gulf states have been told to prepare for a "short and sharp" post-election operation | 08:45–09:34**

- US officials confirmed to the WSJ that US forces struck Iran's **Qeshm Island** on Monday, one of **a series of limited strikes carried out over recent weeks without public announcement**; the Pentagon referred questions to CENTCOM, which did not respond ([Jin10 news 234143](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/234143)).
- The island sits at the mouth of the Strait of Hormuz and hosts an underground missile facility, a naval base and a large desalination plant. The US line is that these strikes are "defensive," aimed at limiting Iran's ability to attack commercial shipping.
- The same report: the US has kept moving **missile-defence systems and munitions** into the region, CENTCOM commander Brad Cooper briefed Trump at the White House last week, and Gulf states have been told to prepare for a "short and sharp" operation.
- The other side of the tape: asked at 04:09 whether he would restart strikes after the election, Trump answered "no," and said on social media that oil is falling because "people think" there will be no war.
- **Price did not follow**: USOIL +0.09% on the hour, WTI December −0.13%, gold +0.03%. The invalidation conditions given last issue — a published list of sanctions relief, or independently measured recovery in Hormuz transit volumes — **have still not triggered**.

**② PBOC raises the FX deposit reserve ratio from 4% to 6%, effective 15 November | 08:41**

- The announcement cites "strengthening the management of foreign-exchange liquidity at financial institutions" and raises the ratio by 2 percentage points effective 2026-11-15 ([Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261103/80f848e7f7e547b2a7b72ef4c64c3262/c.html), carried at 08:45).
- Reuters, using official FX deposit data, estimates the move drains about **$24bn** from the banking system; MUFG's chief China financial-markets analyst Marco Sun reads it as "the PBOC responding to strong market-driven yuan appreciation." This is the **first hike since the September 2023 cut (6%→4%)**.
- The corroborating fact: today's fixing is **6.7150**, clearly weaker than Reuters' model estimate of roughly **6.66** and weaker than offshore **6.6550**; the official price deviates from the market by about **0.9%**, in the direction of the authorities leaning harder against appreciation.
- Impact: the direct liquidity effect is small (Reuters itself calls the signal bigger than the mechanics), but it confirms two things — appreciation pressure is real, and the PBOC chose a tool rather than verbal intervention.

## 3. Assessment

**Call one: the −7.4% in oil priced political statements, not restored supply. This hour supplied the most direct counter-evidence, and the market chose not to trade it.**

The direction is misaligned — the WSJ's strike, the munitions build-up and "Gulf states prepare for post-election action" all point to escalation, yet price barely moved. The reasonable explanation is **positioning, not fundamentals**: on voting day nobody wants to build a position against the "de-escalation" story and will push the call to after the result. What that means for assets: oil's downside is now well expressed and its upside risk is unpriced; energy equities are already absorbing the drop for crude (Hong Kong's three majors −4–5%, COSCO Shipping Energy −10%), and if action lands the worst hit will be shipping and refining rather than crude itself. **Invalidation: WTI December breaking decisively below today's low of 96.19 would say the market really is pricing supply restoration. Opposite trigger: a published list of concessions from Washington extends the decline below 90.** At this level I am neither short nor long; I wait for the election result.

**Call two: on AI, the question has shifted from "should we borrow" to "what does borrowing cost," while retail is still paying up.**

The evidence is two extremes in the same hour: Oracle priced $10bn of equity at $116, a 2.7% discount to Monday's close, with a Baa2/negative rating; more than ten onshore Nasdaq and chip ETFs were halted for premiums of 8%–41%. **Institutions are demanding a discount on the same assets retail is paying a 40% premium for** — the sharpest contrast of the issue, and a sign the marginal AI buyer is shifting from institutions to retail channels. The fragility is not in valuation but in quota and liquidity: once the premium rolls over, redemptions are one-way. The counter-evidence also holds: TSMC fell only 1.85% on 11-02 and Nvidia only 1.26%, no AI hardware company has cut orders, and the SOX −3.3% was mostly Marvell (−12.7%, its fixed exchange ratio shrinking with the share price) and Intel (−8.8%). **Confirmation: AMD's guidance at 05:00 tomorrow and the pricing spreads on the rest of this week's AI bond deals.**

**Call three: what fell in A-shares today was the weights, not risk appetite. The index levels say this is a second probe of a low range, not a style rotation inside a bull market.**

Three checkable facts. First, the damage is concentrated in banks and oil — the Shanghai Composite at −1.08% against the Shenzhen Component at +0.26%, and the two markets' weighting structures alone account for that 1.3-point gap. Second, position: ChiNext made a 52-week low today and rebounded nearly 2%, the Shenzhen Component's 52-week low was set yesterday, and the Shanghai Composite sits 0.22% above its own — all three indices are at the bottom of their ranges. Third, margin balances fell 12.7bn in a day while the PBOC ran an SFISF operation today (application quota above 100bn yuan) and three ministries cut the relending rate for buyback-and-increase programmes from 1.25% to 1% and made it a permanent facility — policy is leaning toward support, not tightening. Implication: do not treat today's Asian weakness as an extension of Monday's US selloff and short it; what is being priced is "AI financing cost" and "oil de-escalation," not "demand is broken." **One caveat: if the Shanghai Composite breaks 3,574.27 with banks still leading down, this is a liquidity problem rather than rotation, and "the Shenzhen Component turned green" is just stock-specific action.** One more note: Jinghua Micro's 20% limit-down is a company-specific CSRC probe (09:03), not a semiconductor sector signal.
