🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**🇮🇷 Iran Sends Mixed Signals — Hormuz Strait "Still Closed"**

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Baghaei released multiple statements this hour: while calling talks with Oman on Hormuz Strait management "making positive progress," he explicitly stated that "the situation at Hormuz has not changed, the strait remains closed." Additionally, Baghaei accused "some Gulf states of participating in the US war against Iran" and called Ukraine's stance on Iran "illegal," reserving the right to respond. This simultaneous diplomacy-and-toughness posture sharply contrasts with market optimism about de-escalation and is the core reason for oil's bounce off lows.

**🇩🇪 Germany IFO Crushes Expectations, European Economic Sentiment Improves**

Germany July IFO business climate came in at 86.6 vs 86 expected, 85.6 prior; expectations component at 86.7 vs 84.8 expected and 84.1 prior — a rare massive beat on the expectations sub-index. Current conditions at 86.5 slightly missed the 87.3 consensus, suggesting present activity remains weak, but forward-looking confidence has improved markedly. This may ease concerns over European recession risk.

**🇯🇵 Japan PM Takaichi Approval Slide Could Pressure Yen and JGBs**

Market strategists warn that PM Sanae Takaichi's declining approval ratings (though still above 50%) could push the government toward looser fiscal and tax policies, raising concerns over the yen and Japanese government bonds. Combined with downward revisions to May coincident/leading index final readings, Japan's fundamental signals are weakening.

**🏭 SK Hynix Q2 Expected to Hit Record High**

Meritz Securities analyst forecasts SK Hynix will report record pre-tax profit for April-June, driven by one-time investment gains and rising semiconductor prices, continuing to validate AI memory demand strength.

**🔥 Gaza Factory Fire Causes Ammonia Leak**

Per Al Jazeera citing Israeli media, two factories in the Sderot industrial zone near Gaza caught fire causing an ammonia leak. Regional tensions continue simmering without signs of major escalation.

Iran's "still closed" statement is the most important marginal development this hour, meaning the unwinding of oil's war premium will not be a straight line, with 82-85 likely becoming a new range.

Japanese political risk deserves attention — if Takaichi's approval continues sliding, it could trigger a fresh round of yen depreciation with ripple effects across global FX.
