## 4. Situation assessment

**On the election, I lean toward neither chasing the short nor buying the dip, because what is being priced is a probability, not a result.** Three reasons: counting is extremely early (11/35 Senate, under a fifth of the House), and Kalshi's 86–90% Senate odds imply **a 10–14% opposite scenario still alive**; the historical sample points to strength in the 12 months after midterms (CNBC: 11 for 11, average +13.7%, but only 11 observations — not usable as a probability); and what is really pressuring AI is financing, not politics — Meta raised **$15 billion** in dollar bonds overnight (Bloomberg), and funding costs and credit spreads are the binding constraint on this capex cycle. **Assets affected**: Nasdaq 100 and the SOX have the most elasticity, the Dow and value least, long-duration Treasuries benefit. **Confirmation / invalidation**: if the Senate ultimately stays Republican, the sweep discount has to unwind and tech has the most snap-back potential; if Nasdaq futures break today's low of **27,732.5** while credit spreads widen in tandem, the selling is coming from financing, the election is only the trigger, and the right response is to cut risk budget across the AI chain rather than wait for a bounce.

**Crude is short-term bearish, but this is an unwinding of the negotiation premium, not a loosening of supply.** Evidence: more than 35 Hormuz attacks in October (the war's busiest month) and five ships hit this month, and prices fell anyway; a 5.267 million-barrel API draw (vs +1.987 million expected) still could not push prices back above the settlement; meanwhile Gulf exports are recovering, near-term supply has outpaced demand for the first time since July, and Aramco says it will maximise output this quarter. **Assets affected**: energy equities pressured near term, airlines and transport get cost relief, lower inflation expectations are mildly supportive for long bonds. **Invalidation**: a clear breakdown in Qatari mediation, or an **actual loading/discharge disruption** (not another attack report), would rebuild the premium quickly. **Tonight's 23:30 EIA print is the testable point**: another large draw in line with API while prices still refuse to rise would confirm for a second time that the price is set by talks, not inventories.

**Gold is bid, but only as an inverse trade on the dollar and real rates, not as a new safe-haven trend.** Evidence: the dollar index −0.5%, with 2-year at 4.573% and 10-year at 4.917% falling together; gold probed **3,919.80** at 08:38 and rallied **$45** to **3,964.76** within 40 minutes before easing to 3,947.74. But **silver touched 50.198 and turned −0.30%**, so the precious complex is not moving as one, and SPDR holdings still fell 2.851 tonnes yesterday — the bounce has no ETF flow behind it. Gold also remains about 28% below its 2026 high (above $5,500 early in the year), so "bouncing" is relative to the 11-03 close, not to the pre-crash level. **Confirmation**: ETF holdings turning to net inflows together with falling real rates; if only the dollar weakens while the gold/silver ratio keeps widening, this is a dollar trade, not a haven trade. For A-shares and Hong Kong, I do not read domestic outperformance as a fundamental signal — a lower relending rate supports the valuation floor, but a three-month-low services PMI and a second month of price cuts say earnings are still deteriorating.
