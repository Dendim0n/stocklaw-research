🔥 **Overnight Key Developments**

**⚔️ Gulf of Oman Vessel Attacks Escalate Middle East Tensions**

An LNG carrier was struck by a drone in the Gulf of Oman, suffering an engine room fire and issuing multiple distress calls; crew are reported safe. Separately, an oil tanker reported being hit by an unidentified flying object on its port side, causing a fire. Maersk and Hapag-Lloyd subsequently announced plans to resume some Suez Canal services under their joint Gemini network. Moody's noted the Iran conflict further highlights Australia's position as a critical LNG supplier.

**📉 Samsung Posts Record Profit But Shares Tumble**

Samsung's Q2 earnings guidance shows operating profit surging over 1800% YoY, with a single quarter exceeding the prior three years combined. However, a "buy the rumor, sell the fact" reaction sent shares down 4.5% pre-market, dragging US memory chip stocks lower after hours.

**🏛️ Trump Intervenes in Retail Pricing**

Trump presided over the White House's first-ever joint NYSE-NASDAQ opening ceremony, publicly urging people to "go buy a Dell computer." He also announced multi-category price cuts at Walmart and called on other retailers to follow suit. With the midterm elections months away, elevated consumer prices have become the White House's biggest political liability.

**🏦 Fed's Waller: Less Forward Guidance May Be Better**

Governor Waller argued that telling markets the future rate path in advance could constrain the Fed's flexibility, stating that "forward guidance can be dispensed with entirely when needed." The remarks suggest the Fed may adopt a more conservative approach to policy communication.

**🇯🇵 Japan Wage Growth Slows, Household Spending Beats**

May real wages rose 1.4% YoY (fifth consecutive month of gains but decelerating), while nominal cash earnings grew 3.2% (below the 3.4% consensus). Household spending surged 3.7% MoM, far exceeding the 1.4% estimate.

**🇪🇺 Eurozone Investor Confidence Jumps**

July Sentix investor confidence index improved sharply to -3.1 (prior -13.4, consensus -10). German manufacturing orders rose 1.9% MoM (consensus +1.5%), and PPI came in at +5.9% YoY (consensus +5.7%).

**🇺🇸 US ISM Services PMI In Line**

June ISM non-manufacturing PMI printed at 54 (consensus 54, prior 54.5), while the services PMI final reading was 51.2. The employment sub-index rebounded to 51.2 (prior 47.9), returning to expansion territory. The Global Supply Chain Pressure Index eased to 1.25 (prior 1.77).

**🛢️ Saudi Arabia Launches Asia Oil Price War**

Saudi Aramco cut its official selling price (OSP) for Arab Light crude to Asia. JPMorgan and HSBC both stated that the current market pullback is a buying opportunity, favoring chips and Korean equities.

**🚀 SpaceX COO Donates 2 Million Shares to Trump**

SpaceX President and COO donated 2 million company shares worth approximately $320 million to Trump's political account, setting a super-donor record. xAI also rebranded its X account to SpaceXAI.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

Geopolitical risk premium is returning — the Gulf of Oman attacks directly threaten Strait of Hormuz shipping security, and with Maersk resuming Suez routes, oil supply-side uncertainty is rising.

Samsung's "record profit, stock drops" pattern is classic buy-the-rumor-sell-the-fact, suggesting memory chip sector optimism may be peaking in the near term.

Trump personally intervening in retail pricing underscores that inflation remains the core midterm pain point, and political pressure could further transmit to the Fed.

Waller's pushback on forward guidance deserves attention — it hints that the July FOMC may lean more on data dependency than pre-set paths, posing a correction risk to market-priced rate cut expectations.

Japan's slowing wage growth but stronger consumption keeps both yen direction and BOJ policy path uncertain.

Eurozone data continues to improve, resonating with US economic resilience. Global risk assets still have near-term support, but geopolitical and Middle East oil disruptions cannot be ignored.
