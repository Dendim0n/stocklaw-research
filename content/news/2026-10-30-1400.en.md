## 4. Situation assessment

**Judgment 1: this leg in Asian hardware is multiple compression — do not chase the decline, and do not treat a bounce as a reversal.**

Evidence: KOSPI −4.80%, Taiwan −2.49%, Nikkei −2.32%, STAR50 −3.76%, yet VIX closed yesterday at 17.20, a low level; US futures barely fell further this hour (NQ −0.25%, ES −0.02%), so the new selling is concentrated in the Asian cash session rather than a fresh US repricing. On the other side, Samsung Electro-Mechanics just booked MLCC volume through end-2027 and Amazon AWS grew 39.3%, a near five-year high (covered in the 12:00 edition) — demand has not broken; what is being discounted is the conversion rate from capex to returns.

Implications: **neither short the hardware complex nor buy the dip**; wait for two checkpoints — whether NQ holds 28,821.5 (today's low) at the 21:30 US open, and what NAND pricing and capex guidance Kioxia gives at its 17:45 call. Invalidation: if NQ breaks 28,821.5 and VIX rises above 20, reclassify from "multiple compression" to "risk event".

**Judgment 2: gold cut to neutral-bearish — not because haven demand vanished, but because the cost of holding it is too high.**

Evidence: the session low is still 3,987.40 and there was no new low this hour, but no bounce either — price sits 0.06% above the low. Meanwhile Treasuries rallied (at least −3bp across the curve in Asia, per the 10:13 report) and equities fell, yet gold attracted no haven bid. The drags are explicit: 10-year at 4.98%, 10-year TIPS real yield 2.67%, BofA's two more hikes, and CME's 85.7% for December. The COMEX premium to spot widened from 6.67 to 10.20, so futures did not lead the decline.

Implications: **do not hold gold as a haven long, but do not short it right at the 4,000 break either** — it has fallen two sessions and sits only 0.25% below 4,000, so the risk/reward on chasing is poor. Silver is weaker: −2.16% to 51.03, low 50.879, gold/silver ratio 78.19 (77.73 previously), and the physical evidence (London vaults at a near four-year high, ETPs down about 60m oz year-to-date, CME managed-money longs the lowest since February 2024) is firmer than for gold — the short case holds, but again do not chase. Invalidation: gold closing back above 4,000 with yields still falling → upgrade to neutral; silver recovering 51.5 with CME longs rebuilding → downgrade this call.

**Judgment 3: the JGB "hawkish hold, long-end rally" now has fundamental backing; the China bond divergence is the anomaly to watch this week.**

Evidence: Japan September housing starts −2.74% (expected +2.9%) and construction orders −11.1% line up with the 2-year −2.5bp and the 40-year falling below 4% — the market is not unafraid of hikes, it is marking the terminal rate lower. China's 30-year T-bond futures −0.73% and the ultra-long special bond yield +3bp went the other way, at the same time as limit-up waves in A-share liquor, dairy and CRO. **The two could be the same trade (money rotating from duration into consumer equities), but without northbound, ETF flow or institutional positioning data this is only a hypothesis.**

Implications: long duration in Treasuries and JGBs can stay; short-term risk in Chinese long bonds is higher and today is not the moment to add duration; the durability of the A-share consumer bid depends on volume tomorrow — today's liquor move looks more like crowding than a change in fundamentals. Invalidation: if Ueda gives a consecutive-hike path at 14:30, JGBs steepen and the first half of this paragraph fails; if the China 30-year recovers today's loss tomorrow, the anomaly downgrades to a one-day move.
