🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**1. ANZ slashes year-end gold forecast from $5,600 to $5,200**
ANZ cut its year-end gold price target to $5,200/oz from $5,600, reflecting the metal's sustained correction. With gold now below $4,200, institutional sentiment on the metal's medium-term outlook is turning notably cautious.

**2. US plans to withdraw one-third of NATO fighter jets**
The New York Times reports the US plans to pull back one-third of its fighter jets deployed for European defense under NATO. If executed, this would significantly weaken NATO air power at a time of heightened US-Iran tensions, signaling a reshaping of Europe's security architecture.

**3. SK Hynix fire brought under control, production normalizing**
SK Hynix confirmed the fire at its Cheongju fourth campus has been initially controlled and production equipment is operating normally. This was the second fire incident this month; despite official reassurances, back-to-back incidents keep the market alert to supply-side risks in memory chips.

**4. Physical gold surges despite price drop: Shenzhen jewelry at ¥1,060/g, queues in Beijing**
International gold's sustained retreat has pushed Shenzhen Shuibei jewelry prices to ¥1,060 per gram, yet Beijing investment gold counters are seeing long queues. Falling prices are actually activating physical buying — classic "buy the dip" behavior providing a floor for gold.

**5. BAAIC releases world's first general-purpose world foundation model "Physis"**
The Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence unveiled "Physis-v0.1" at the 2026 BAAIC Conference, claiming it as the world's first general-purpose world foundation model. AI breakthroughs continue at pace, with domestic LLM competition entering a new phase.

**6. ProShares launches 2x leveraged InnoLight ETF**
A US-listed 2x leveraged product tracking InnoLight (中际旭创) is being marketed by ProShares, currently in subscription phase with an expected listing in two months. InnoLight opened nearly 5% higher as overseas investor attention on China's optical module leader intensifies.
