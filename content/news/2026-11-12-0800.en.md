- **11-12 06:00-07:47**: after-hours losses widened from -2.6% to -9%. Jin10 separately reported the company is working with an outside firm to identify a successor and is considering an interim CEO — **market chatter, not company-confirmed**. ## 3. Judgements and counter-evidence

**A | Nvidia: the tape is pricing company-specific risk, not a demand bust.**
Call: **do not read -8% after hours as an AI top, and do not treat it as a buy point**; no directional preset for today.
Evidence (reproducible): the after-hours tape is **split** — AMD +4.37% and Broadcom +2.49% higher, TSMC -1.87% and Micron -1.74% modestly lower, and only Nvidia at -7.99%. If the market were repricing AI demand, suppliers would fall together and AMD and Broadcom would not be up. **That split supports a CEO-change discount; it does not support the end of the compute cycle.**
Counter-evidence and invalidation: if, after today's 22:30 open, Nvidia **closes below 156.20** (the after-hours low) and AMD's and Broadcom's after-hours gains are fully erased, the pricing has shifted from key-person to narrative damage and this call is void.
Unknowns that can resolve today: whether the board actually meets, what it decides, and whether an interim CEO is announced. None of these can be predicted, and no "it's already settled" claim currently has a company basis.

**B | Gold: the range floor is broken, and the pricing power sits with the denominator, not with haven demand.**
Call: **downgraded from neutral to neutral-bearish**, but not a short-chase.
Evidence: spot gold closed 3,987.93 on 11-11 (-1.08%) and traded 3,974.37 this morning, 1.3% below the 4,026.55 floor flagged yesterday; the dollar index is 102.92, the highest since April 2025; Jin10 attributes the move explicitly to "CPI above expectations for a third month plus hawkish Fed commentary". **The key observation**: safe-haven bids tied to the US-Israel-Iran situation were still present in the same window (Jin10 credits them with supporting the dollar), yet gold fell two days running — consistent with "gold is currently insensitive to geopolitics and sensitive to real rates". **That is an inference, not something flow data has proven.**
Counter-evidence: with Treasuries reopening today, if the 10-year falls back **below 4.85%** while gold reclaims 4,026, yesterday was a technical dollar spike and this call is cut.
Unverified: no "lowest since [month]" claim — daily history was not obtained this issue (Jin10 kline returns empty, yfinance rate-limited), so the only confirmed fact is the break of the 11-11 intraday low. There is no verified support below; 3,950 and 3,900 are round numbers only.

**C | Crude: a rollover spread is not a direction.**
Call: **neutral, no position.**
Evidence: the three quote bases are mutually inconsistent (see table notes) and `hf_CL`'s prior settlement jumped 0.57% in a day, consistent with a front-month switch. On fundamentals there is only incremental news, no incremental data: a Russian oil export hub was hit with loading impact **still being assessed**; Brazil has become a new target for Russian diesel (at least four cargoes, about 1.1m barrels, close to its import scale if fully booked); OPEC kept its 2027 demand forecast unchanged.
Counter-evidence: if the EIA weekly report (publication time not obtained this issue) shows a crude draw, the "supply is fine, geopolitical premium is already minimal" read has to change. **Without the official inventory print, any direction at 86 is a bet on a data source.**

**D | Yesterday's "AI trade re-ignites" and this morning's key-person shock do not cancel out.**
The 11-11 cash session was a risk-appetite recovery (Nasdaq 100 +2.28%, VIX 17.49); the 11-12 after-hours move is a single-company governance shock. **The earlier event does not disappear because of the later one, but the later one sets today's opening price.** That is why this issue refuses to merge them into one bullish-or-bearish conclusion.
