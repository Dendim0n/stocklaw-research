## 4. Assessment

**Judgment 1: this is an unwind of AI/semiconductor positions, not a macro rates event — the evidence is that nothing that should have moved did.**

Basis: while tech fell 2%-3.3%, the dollar index held at 101.72 (-0.06%), Japan's 30-year yield fell 3bp to 3.895% and the 10-year 2bp to 2.87%, and US Treasuries were unchanged from last night's 10-year 5.13% and 30-year 5.42%. A rates or liquidity shock would have moved the dollar and the long end first; neither moved. The ranking of losses (Hang Seng Tech -3.30% > ChiNext -2.12% > KOSPI -1.82% > CSI 300 -1.01% > Nikkei -0.91%; futures Nasdaq -0.86% vs Dow -0.23%) is a pure tech-beta structure, not a market-wide risk-off. The Bank of Korea naming "slower semiconductor profitability plus slower AI investment" is the hardest corroboration.

Implication: semiconductors, compute hardware, Hong Kong internet and Korean memory are tactically bearish; A-share policy beneficiaries (machine tools, humanoid robotics) rising against the tape shows this is **internal rotation**, not capital flight, so it should not be treated as a full retreat. The onshore chip LOF at a 40% premium is a forced-deleveraging amplifier whose risk is out of proportion to the underlying move.

Confirmation / invalidation: at tonight's 21:30 US cash open, if Nasdaq futures recover **30,000** (the 10-21 settle was 30,007) with the dollar unchanged, treat it as one-off repositioning; if the 10-year Treasury yield breaks **5.25%** and the dollar clears **102** while tech keeps falling, this judgment is void and it becomes a rates-driven de-rating.

**Judgment 2: gold's range is still valid, but the 4,106 line is losing its value.**

Basis: the intraday low of **4,107.22** is just **$1.2** from the 4,106 invalidation line set last edition; the intraday high of **4,145.93** is just **$0.07** below the 4,146 upper edge. More important is the driver structure: the dollar did not rise, JGB yields fell, the COMEX premium of $15.9 to spot is normal — yet gold fell 0.62%, silver -1.52% and palladium -2.00%. Precious metals falling together, with more industrial members falling more, looks more like **risk-budget contraction** (the same source as the Asian tech selling) than a repricing of the dollar or real rates.

Implication: gold stays **neutral-to-weak** in a **4,106-4,146** range; silver and palladium have higher beta and more short-term risk than gold. The gap must be stated: there is no GLD/SLV tonnage data today, so whether ETFs are the seller cannot be confirmed and last edition's outflow figure cannot be extrapolated.

Confirmation / invalidation: a London gold **daily close** below 4,100 (a round number, treated only as a psychological level) voids the range, and new support must be re-derived from the daily chart rather than preset here; if the European/US session recovers above **4,132**, the upper edge gets retested. Tactically: no chasing shorts, no bottom-fishing inside the range; wait for the daily close.

**Judgment 3: the bullish case for crude has upgraded from statements to physical evidence, but the price is not paying for an outage — a textbook bullish-but-do-not-chase setup.**

Basis: the exhaustion of Iran's floating storage is hard supply-side information (the buffer is at zero) and far more reliable than talk about strike plans; yet the price response was only a little over 1% and WTI failed to break its 98.40 intraday high. Counter-evidence is accumulating at the same time: Kuwait says exports are back to pre-war levels and container ships are returning to Suez. "Iran has less buffer" and "other producers are filling in while shipping normalises" are both true, which is why escalation has not bought a higher oil price.

Implication: energy **bullish**, but Brent 110 is a round psychological level, not a validated resistance, and chasing above it offers poor odds; the better trade-off is to wait for a pullback toward the December contract's prior settle of **108.82**, or to use "can it close above 110" as trend confirmation rather than buying the breakout.

Confirmation / invalidation: a Brent **daily close** above 110 for the first time with WTI closing above **98.40** (today's high) upgrades this to a trending bullish call; if later tanker/export data contradicts the floating-storage story, or Hormuz traffic materially recovers, the premium basis disappears.
