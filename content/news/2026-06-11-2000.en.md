🔥 **Key Headlines This Hour**

1. **US-Iran accelerate ceasefire talks amid frozen asset standoff**: Reuters, citing multiple sources, reports that the US and Iran are accelerating negotiations while exchanging airstrikes. The core dispute centers on the release mechanism for tens of billions in frozen funds — Iran demands a one-time unfreezing, while the US insists on phased releases.

2. **Iran's hardline posture**: Iran's defense ministry issued multiple statements — "Armed forces remain on high alert," "We will not start a war but will firmly defend our security and territory," "Any solution must include a ceasefire and cessation of hostilities." Al Arabiya reports Iran has responded to a letter delivered via Pakistan's interior minister.

3. **US intercepts third blockade-violating tanker**: US forces used Hellfire missiles to disable the "Jalveer" tanker's engine room in the Oman Gulf for allegedly transporting Iranian oil in violation of the blockade. This marks the third merchant vessel intercepted this week. Since the April blockade began, 9 ships have been disabled and 135 forced to reroute.

4. **Japan WF6 supply shutdown threatens chip supply chain**: Kanto Denka and Chuo Kaki informed major chip clients that their tungsten hexafluoride (WF6) inventory will only last until end of June, with permanent shutdown effective July 1. The two companies collectively hold a dominant share of global WF6 capacity, making this a potential new bottleneck for semiconductor manufacturing.

5. **Ukraine Q1 GDP down 0.6% YoY**, adding pressure on economic recovery.

🧭 **Outlook**

US-Iran talks on frozen assets signal a phased easing, but Iran's "high alert + no retreat" rhetoric reflects deep mutual distrust — a ceasefire deal remains elusive in the near term.

The US continues aggressive tanker interdictions, maintaining the Hormuz Strait blockade — the core geopolitical premium supporting oil prices. Yet WTI dropped over 3% today, suggesting demand-side fears are beginning to outweigh supply disruption anxiety in the market.

Gold breached 4,030 intraday before rebounding, holding the 4,024 support. However, consecutive declines with widening volatility signal intensifying bull-bear divergence. The long-term central bank gold accumulation narrative remains intact, but short-term technicals lean bearish — watch the 4,000 round-number defense.

Japan's WF6 supply disruption warrants continued attention in the semiconductor space. Without alternative supply sources, the impact may cascade into memory chip production from July onward.
