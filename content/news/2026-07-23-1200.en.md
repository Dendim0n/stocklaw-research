## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**US-Iran Conflict Escalates: B-1 Bombers Join the Fight, Iran Strikes CIA Facility**

The US has deployed B-1 long-range bombers from UK bases for missions against Iran — the most significant escalation since hostilities resumed. IRGC officials responded defiantly to Trump's "blow up the bridges" threat. Meanwhile, Reuters reports US intelligence is investigating whether Russia assisted Iran in the drone attack on a CIA facility, citing the strike's precision and Russian technical support for Iranian drones.

**WTI Crude Surges 2.2%, Analysts Say Brent Could Hit $100**

Driven by escalating Middle East tensions, WTI crude rose to $88.83 intraday. Phillip Nova analysts note Brent could reach $100/bbl if tensions persist. Goldman Sachs maintains its Q4 Brent forecast at $80 but expects prices to hold gains through July-August, supported by reduced Middle East output, summer travel demand, and slower SPR releases.

**Tesla Q2 Earnings: Revenue Beat but Profit Drops, Musk Hints at Merger**

Tesla posted Q2 revenue of $28.24B, beating estimates, but net profit fell 5% YoY. Capex surged to $5.8B, dragging free cash flow into negative territory. Musk hinted on the earnings call at a potential merger with an AI company — the market is calling it "burning cash to bet on AI."

**Benchmark Massively Upgrades AMD Target: $485 → $685**

Benchmark raised its AMD price target from $485 to $685, a 41% increase, reflecting sustained optimism in the AI chip sector.

**Paulson: Gold Bull Market Still in Early Stages**

Legendary hedge fund manager John Paulson says the gold bull market is far from over, citing ongoing central bank buying, accelerating private-sector allocation, and weakening global confidence in the US dollar. He is particularly bullish on gold mining stocks for their leverage.

**Trump "President API" Goes Live: $100K/Month**

Trump Media & Technology Group is packaging Trump's social media posts into a trading signal API, allowing quant funds to react within seconds. Institutions are already subscribing, sparking Wall Street debate over "nanosecond front-running."

**India Silver Imports Crash 96%**

A new licensing regime has brought Indian silver imports to a virtual halt, suppressing international demand while creating a domestic physical shortage. Analysts view the pullback as healthy for market structure, with long-term silver targets still at $70.
