## 2. Key incremental items

**1) China October trade: imports +41.6% far above expectations, IC exports $39.594bn, +137.1% y/y | 10:31-10:52, General Administration of Customs**

USD basis: exports **+35.2%** (33.5% expected, prior 36.3%), imports **+41.6%** (32.0% expected, prior 31.0%), trade surplus **$105.28bn** (expected $125.8bn, prior $133.53bn). CNY basis: October total trade **4.87trn yuan, +31.2%**, with exports 2.79trn +28.7% and imports 2.08trn +34.7%; trade balance 712.78bn yuan (prior 905.97bn); January-October cumulative 44.81trn yuan, +20%, exports 25.99trn, +17.6%.

Three structural details matter most: **IC exports of $39.594bn, +137.1% y/y**; **crude imports of 41.938m tonnes** (37.096m in September, **+13.0% m/m**) alongside refined-product exports of 5.18m tonnes (7.162m in September, **-27.7% m/m**); and broad weakness elsewhere — natural gas imports -7.8%, coal -9.1%, soybeans -38.2%, auto exports -6.3%, with rare-earth exports at 4,386.0 tonnes (4,269.8 in September).

**Implication**: the import beat combined with IC export value more than doubling pushes AI demand evidence from share prices into trade flows, an endorsement in dollar terms for compute chips, packaging and testing, PCB and optical modules. But IC figures are **value, not volume, and include price inflation** — they cannot be read as shipments doubling. The surplus of $105.28bn came in well below expectations as a result of the import surge: marginally negative for the yuan, marginally positive for domestic demand.

**Implication**: the public pricing anchor for AI assets is deferred, leaving less verifiable information ahead of the 18-20 November pricing window and downgrading industrial cornerstone backing from "settled" to "reported". This is not bad news for demand; it is rising uncertainty on valuation.

## 3. Assessment

**A | AI hardware: from "sentiment repair" to "data validation" — bullish, but do not chase before CPI.**

Three supports: China's October IC exports of $39.594bn, +137.1% y/y, with imports +41.6%, moving in the same direction, indicating both processing and re-exports expanded; last night's SOX +6.85% and AMD +13.26% were the same story in price terms; but A-shares **faded from the open** this hour — the Shanghai Composite narrowed from +0.58% at the open to +0.26% (3,670.33), Shenzhen Component from +1.51% to +1.04%, ChiNext from +2.44% to +1.90%, with only Hang Seng Tech strengthening against the trend (+0.78% to +1.26%). Price confirmation is weakening, and index futures (NQ -0.13%, ES +0.08%, YM +0.27%) show US equities with almost no direction before CPI.

**Trade-off**: do not chase, do not short. The real test is whether NVDA can stop falling after tonight's 22:30 open (Monday -4.27% to 178.67) — if trade data is this good and the leader keeps falling, what is being sold is valuation, not demand. **Confirmation**: SOX back above 10,033 (Monday's close) with NVDA reclaiming 180. **Invalidation**: a 0.3% core CPI month-over-month pushes discount rates higher again, and the hardware chain sells valuation first.

**B | Gold: bearish tilt maintained, but downside momentum has slowed — not a place to add shorts.**

Basis: -0.33% this hour, slightly faster than last hour's -0.06%, but the drop clustered after 10:30 and silver fell in step (-0.31%), which is real-rate pricing rather than panic selling; the price is -0.88% from the session high and still inside a 0.9% range of 4,110-4,147. Last night's US-Iran de-escalation should have pressured gold, yet gold rose 1.61% on the night equities rallied — the dominant variable is the dollar and real rates, not haven demand.

**Trade-off**: do not chase shorts near 4,110. **Confirmation/invalidation**: core CPI at or above 0.3% keeps the bearish tilt, with the next reference around the 6 November close (4,070.66 on our prior basis; the two sources differ by about $15, so this is directional only); core at or below 0.2% with gold back above 4,140 invalidates the bearish call. **Evidence gap**: TIPS real yields and a live dollar index were not obtainable, so we cannot separate "real rates rising" from "risk premium compressing" — two different things for gold.

**C | Crude: bearish but entering a phase of "diminishing bearish response"; Chinese imports are the first counter-evidence.**

Basis: WTI -0.19% this hour (86.086 to 85.925) and Brent -0.23%, a clear narrowing from last hour's -0.68%; in the same customs release, China's October crude imports rose 13.0% m/m while refined-product exports fell 27.7% m/m, a significant increase in net imports. That runs opposite to the supply-side bearish driver of "Trump in no hurry to restart strikes on Iran". **Implication**: a single month cannot distinguish strategic stockpiling, refinery maintenance timing and bargain buying, so it is not a demand inflection — but it is counter-evidence to the "oil can only fall" narrative.

**Trade-off**: do not chase shorts. WTI has held 85.7-86.9 for four hours, and the payoff here is worse than waiting for a bounce. **Confirmation/invalidation**: a close above 86.85 (the 11-09 settlement) invalidates the bearish call; a break of 85.68 (today's low) with further inventory builds in this week's EIA data opens the next leg. Today's 14:30 trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi are the only variable that could break the stalemate — note the direction: a deal limited to "no mutual attacks on energy facilities" is further bearish for oil, while a breakdown leaves room for war premium to return.
