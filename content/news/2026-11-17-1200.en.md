## Assessment

**A | Rates: hawkish pricing is spreading, but today's prices did not confirm it — do not treat 50bp as the base case.**
The evidence is two official datasets from independent sources: the Treasury's 11-16 curve at 10-year 5.12% and 2-year 4.83% (4.73% on 11-10), and bill auctions that moved +9.5bp at three months and +16bp at six months in a week; CME put the odds of a 25bp December hike at 95.7% (06:15 sampling). Yet in the same hour gold rose 0.40%, Nasdaq 100 futures 0.38%, and STAR 50 3.31%. **If the market were genuinely trading 50bp, those three should not be rising together.** Conclusion: the front end is repricing while risk assets have not accepted it, and one of the two must correct. This affects the dollar, gold and long-duration growth. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if the minutes or officials formally put 50bp on the table, today's rebound is directionally wrong; if officials talk only about 25bp, today's front-end move is an overshoot. **Do not**: treat the article's 50bp as a probability.

**B | Semiconductors: Asia is sending a "substitution" signal, not a "demand" signal.**
Evidence: STAR 50 +3.31% and SMIC +8.61% near its intraday high, with equipment and packaging moving in step; but the three optical-module names are still down 0.5–1.2%, Hygon is -1.20%, and US futures are up only 0.38%. That is a split inside one sector, not a whole-chain recovery. It affects SOX, memory, optical modules and equipment. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if guidance is decent and SOX reclaims 9,828.98, today was merely the unwinding of pre-event de-risking; if guidance misses and SOX breaks 9,587.11 again, even the equipment chain's standalone rally will not hold. **Trade-off**: do not chase equipment and materials names already at the limit; watch whether optical modules turn positive after the US open — that is the first evidence of demand pricing returning.

**C | Gold: short-term momentum has stalled; medium term it remains a tug-of-war between high real rates and unidentified buying.**
Evidence: with the 10-year at 5.12%, gold still rose 0.40% and touched 3,880, the opposite of a "falling real rates" explanation — **I could not identify today's driver and will not invent one**; it gained only 0.05% this hour and 3,880 was touched rather than held. Silver (COMEX +1.05%) is outperforming, which looks more like risk-leaning precious-metal buying. It affects gold miners, silver and real-rate-sensitive assets. **Confirmation/invalidation**: only a close above 3,880 argues for extension; a break of 3,843.56 voids the rebound. **Counter-evidence**: Goldman cut its target for the second time in a week (year-end 2026 at 4,150); institutions and the tape are still fighting.
