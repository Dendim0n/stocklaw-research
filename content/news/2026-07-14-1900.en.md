## 🔥 Key News This Hour

### ⚔️ Middle East Tensions Escalate Dramatically

**Iranian media reports US artillery strikes on Iranian soil.** On Tuesday afternoon, Iran's Abadan County and Mahshahr region came under US shelling, according to Iranian media. This marks a sharp escalation following Iran's bombing of a US base in Jordan and the US retaliatory 5-hour continuous airstrikes. Abadan is one of Iran's most critical refining hubs; Mahshahr is a key petrochemical port on the Persian Gulf.

**Trump claims US may "operate" the Strait of Hormuz.** Trump stated the US may take over management of the Strait of Hormuz in the future, adding that "if the US leads, the US will get paid." He also threatened a government shutdown in September if filibuster rules are not abolished.

**Iranian general signals military resolve.** Major General Saffavi publicly recalled that Supreme Leader Khamenei deemed 2,000 missiles insufficient back in 1997, insisting Iran needed tens of thousands for a months-long war — a saber-rattling message amid the current US-Iran military standoff.

**Strait of Hormuz transit nearly halted.** Asian refiners have turned to purchasing US crude as a hedge against Middle East supply disruption. Multiple industry executives confirmed spot procurement negotiations have restarted.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The US-Iran conflict has escalated from proxy warfare to direct military confrontation — Iran's report of US shelling on Abadan and Mahshahr marks a new phase in the conflict.

Abadan is one of Iran's most critical refining centers; any material damage would directly impair Iran's crude export capacity, adding further upside momentum to oil prices.

WTI's 3% single-day surge above $80, combined with near-total disruption of Strait of Hormuz transit and Asian refiners scrambling for US crude, indicates that supply-side risk premiums are being rapidly priced in.

Trump's claim that the US may "operate" the Strait of Hormuz is highly provocative, suggesting potential unilateral military control actions that would push geopolitical risk to new heights.

Gold holding above $4,020 reflects a confluence of safe-haven demand and inflation expectations — the short-term bullish structure remains intact.

JPMorgan's FICC revenue miss warrants attention — weaker-than-expected fixed income trading may signal growing market-making difficulty in a volatile rate environment; watch for Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley's FICC data ahead.

Overall Wall Street bank earnings are decent (Dimon cites record revenue, BofA wealth management beat), but the market was overly optimistic on FICC pricing — JPM's 2% pre-market decline reflects expectation recalibration.

An Iranian general openly discussing war preparations involving "tens of thousands of missiles" is a classic deterrence escalation signal — Tehran is conveying to Washington its readiness to fight if necessary.
