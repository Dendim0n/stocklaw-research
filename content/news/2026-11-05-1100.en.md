## 3. What is actually new

**1) A-shares spike and fade, Hong Kong holds - one "blue wave" logic, two funding structures | 10:02-11:03**
Jin10 described the Shanghai Composite at 10:24 as "back above 3,700"; the intraday high was 3,701.59 and at 11:02 it printed 3,684.46 (-0.05%). STAR50 was up as much as +5.03% (1,329.14) and now sits at +2.44% (1,296.41), giving back 2.6 percentage points in forty minutes; the Huaxia STAR50 ETF (588000) traded 10bn yuan in its first 54 minutes while up 4.63% (Jin10 10:28). Hang Seng Tech is +5.10%, only 0.76% below its high, with Tencent +6.32%, Alibaba +6.16% (10:47 timestamp), Meituan and NetEase up more than 8% (10:18). The driver is policy expectation, not earnings: Jin10 07:14 reported Democratic leaders seeking to repeal Trump's tariffs and end the war, and 06:59 said US-Iran talks should be clearer on Friday. **Impact**: A-share gains are amplified by leverage and retail sentiment while Hong Kong is being absorbed by allocation money; treating "Chinese assets rallying" as one trade means buying the most expensive part, the A-share theme complex.

**2) Meta raises about USD 25bn in two days; euro bond 6x oversubscribed, a European record | 10:29**
A EUR 9bn six-tranche deal (4 to 38 years) drew EUR 53bn of orders, nearly 6x coverage; with Tuesday's USD 15bn dollar deal that is roughly USD 25bn in two days (Jin10 citing Bloomberg, LSEG, WSJ). Meanwhile S&P put the outlook at negative on Oct 30: Meta's Q3 free cash flow was **-USD 2.1bn** versus +10.6bn a year earlier, costs rose 68% year on year to nearly USD 52bn, operating income fell 37% to nearly USD 13bn, and debt stood at USD 83.67bn on Sep 30; S&P projects a free operating cash flow gap of USD 22bn in 2026 widening to USD 71bn in 2027 (Jin10 10:29). **Impact**: This is the hardest evidence that AI capex has not stopped, and it also shows the funding mix shifting from internal cash to the bond market plus a potential equity raise (Reuters reports an arrangement of up to USD 97.5bn) - the test for the other side of this trade moves from "who is buying compute" to "who can service the debt".

**3) Oil grinds lower and the cost relief reaches downstream chemicals | 10:08-10:47**

## 4. Reading the tape

**A. Chinese assets: sentiment repair, not a re-rating; Hong Kong is the more credible signal.** The evidence is two price sets within the same hour - the Shanghai Composite fell 0.46% after touching 3,701.59 and STAR50 gave back 2.6 percentage points, while Hang Seng Tech sits 0.76% below its high; add a single STAR50 ETF trading 10bn yuan in 54 minutes and the A-share leg looks amplified by leverage and retail flow. The driver has no earnings behind it: repealing tariffs and ending the war are still "leaders' statements" and "clearer by Friday", with no text. **Impact**: constructive on Hong Kong internet and the export chain; neutral-to-bearish on the A-share limit-up complex in solar and semiconductor equipment, whose gains come from sentiment, not earnings revisions. **Invalidation**: if the Shanghai Composite closes back above today's high of 3,701.59 with STAR50 above 1,329.14, there was incremental afternoon money rather than distribution into the close; if Hang Seng Tech fades below +3% in the afternoon, Hong Kong too is just an extended gap-fill and regional reallocation does not hold.

**B. AI hardware: the discount is narrowing, but the driver is shifting from "permits" to "financing cost" - a slower, longer pressure.** Evidence: KOSPI recovered from a -5.23% low to -3.93% and NQ futures fell only -0.75% in the Asian session (ES -0.11%), so last night's panic selling did not continue; but Meta's financing structure shows the other side - USD 25bn of bonds in two days, a negative S&P outlook, and a projected USD 71bn cash flow gap in 2027. **Impact**: the hyperscalers remain relative winners (they can issue debt, they own existing campuses), while the pressure sits on second-tier compute and neoclouds that depend on new capacity and expensive financing. **Trade-off**: do not chase the short in semis already about 30% below their June high, and do not buy second-tier names merely because they have fallen; if you want exposure, favour names whose cash flow covers capex. **Invalidation**: if NQ breaks the Asian-session low of 28,180 and ES breaks 7,695.75 tonight, "within-supply-chain discount" escalates to systemic risk; if instead NQ reclaims 28,566.75 (prior settlement), this regulatory discount is over.

**C. Gold is still bid but momentum is decaying; oil's decline is compressing, but do not chase the bounce.** Evidence: gold is +1.73% on the day yet only +0.16% in this hour, while the two hours from 08:00 to 10:00 delivered +1.42% cumulatively - momentum is stepping down, and today's high of 4,139.30 is just USD 9 away. A 2.45 yuan/g onshore premium says the domestic market is hotter than the offshore one, usually a sign of short-term overheating. On oil, WTI fell -0.13% and Brent -0.70% this hour, with the decline compressing from -8% overnight to under 1%. **Impact**: do not chase gold above 4,130, wait for a pullback; do not take a directional bet in oil, wait for Friday's talks. **Invalidation**: if gold breaks 4,100 while SPDR holdings turn to net redemptions, this rebound is over; if WTI breaks today's low of 86.79 (Jin10; Sina prints 86.74) with no fresh supply news, the market is pricing the war premium to zero and the lower end of the range needs re-marking.
