🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**1. Trump Significantly Softens Tone on Fed**

Trump stated he doesn't blame Warsh for not cutting rates, shifting blame to the "politicized Board." This marks a notable shift from his prolonged attacks on Powell for not cutting rates. Markets interpret this as the White House potentially paving the way for Warsh's appointment, reducing direct pressure on Fed independence.

**2. NFP Data Tonight at 20:30 — Biggest Risk Event of the Week**

July NFP expected at 80K (prior 57K), unemployment rate expected at 4.2%. This week's initial jobless claims came in at 199K (below 202K expected), while continuing claims rose to 1.801M (above 1.79M expected), sending mixed labor market signals. ADP employment was only 44K (vs. 70K expected), a significant miss that increases uncertainty around the NFP print.

**3. BlackRock Warns: US Euro-Selling Yen Intervention Increases Long-Bond Risks**

BlackRock stated that the US decision to sell euros to support the yen without notifying European policymakers has heightened geopolitical risks and further weakened the appeal of long-term government bonds. This is a rare warning from the world's largest asset manager on the transparency of currency intervention.

**4. Goldman Sachs Warns of European Winter Energy Risk**

Goldman's co-head of global commodities research indicated that Europe faces dual risks to diesel and natural gas supply ahead of winter. TTF natural gas futures fell 7% this week to around €54/MWh, but Goldman maintains a cautious outlook.

**5. China's July Exports Beat Expectations**

China's July dollar-denominated exports rose 23.9% YoY (expected 22.1%), imports rose 27.5% (expected 27.7%), trade surplus reached $112.5B (expected $107B). Exports continued strong momentum, but YoY growth rates decelerated from June for both exports and imports.

Tonight's NFP is the biggest risk event of the week, and markets are repositioning aggressively.

Crude weakness aligns with global demand concerns, but watch tonight's rig count data. A significant NFP miss could further drag crude pricing on demand outlook.
