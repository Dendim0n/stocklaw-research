🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

Gold tumbled from 4,103 to 4,044 intraday before a modest bounce, down over 1%. On the surface this looks like a technical correction, but combined with the direct US-Iran clash in Isfahan, the market appears to be tentatively pricing in the assumption that "conflict won't escalate indefinitely." If that assumption is proven wrong, gold will rapidly reclaim lost ground.

The 2%+ drop in crude is even more telling. Tankers going dark through Hormuz is itself an extreme risk signal, yet oil is falling — suggesting the market is currently pricing demand concerns (global economic slowdown) over geopolitical premium. This pricing logic faces violent correction if the conflict escalates further.

The Isfahan strike marks a qualitative shift in this week's US-Iran confrontation. Prior Hormuz maneuvering stayed at the "deterrence" level; now there are actual casualties. Iran's Foreign Ministry rhetoric is hardline but leaves room for "diplomatic tools" — the key question is whether the US continues to escalate.

The UK/EU attribution of the Poland grid attack to Russia, with new sanctions, confirms all three sides (Russia, Ukraine, Europe) are preparing for a prolonged war of attrition. There is no near-term resolution.

US Q2 earnings window opens with upward estimate revisions and equal-weight outperformance — the market is rotating from "Magnificent Seven dominance" toward broader earnings support. If this trend persists, it favors value and small/mid-cap names.
