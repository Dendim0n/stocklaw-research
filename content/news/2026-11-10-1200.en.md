## 2. Key incremental items

**① Google expands TPU line to four chips: TPU 8i Max and 8tx, in service from 2027 | announced Monday (11-09), relayed by Jin10 at 11:33**

Two application-specific versions were added within the same eighth-generation family. **TPU 8i Max** packages two TPU 8i dies in one package, doubling compute performance, on-chip SRAM and HBM bandwidth versus 8i, aimed at the memory bottleneck in long-context inference. **TPU 8tx** pairs TPU 8t with an **AMD custom CPU** for hardware-level isolation in regulated industries such as finance and government, cutting latency by up to 50% versus 8t. Both ship in 2027. The commercial numbers matter more: external TPU customers are now **close to 100** (including Apple, Meta and Oracle); Oracle will resell TPU capacity to its cloud customers starting 2027; Anthropic holds an option, per regulatory filings, to buy up to **$150bn** of related chips in **2028–2031**; Bloomberg Intelligence expects TPU-related sales to grow from about **$12bn in 2026** to **over $135bn in 2028** (including supporting hardware and software, excluding cloud revenue).

**Impact**: demand is unchanged; what changes is who gets paid. Compute is moving from a single general-purpose GPU supplier to specialised, multi-cloud, multi-chip sourcing — that erodes NVDA's **pricing power** rather than its order book, and adds to the share narrative for AVGO (TPU co-development) and AMD (its custom CPU inside 8tx). The $12bn-to-$135bn curve is a sell-side forecast, not signed contracts, and cannot be read as revenue.

**② S&P 500 equal-weight has beaten cap-weight by the widest monthly margin since 2009 | 11:54 (article 234787, Jin10 relay)**

Three checkable quantitative points in the same piece: equal-weight outperformance over the past month is the **widest since 2009**; per the AJ Bell investment director quoted, information technology has gone from the top-performing sector in the first half to the **bottom**, with healthcare, consumer staples, real estate and materials taking over leadership; CME FedWatch now prices the **probability of a December hike above 83%** (the prior brief cited a Bloomberg figure near 80% — different platform, different sampling time, the two are not blended). The CPI consensus remains **+0.3% m/m headline, +0.2% core**, with year-on-year not published because last year's shutdown left no base period.

**Impact**: this is the price evidence for "switching cars, not leaving the market" — index-level risk falls while AI's relative return falls, and when both happen at once, either single narrative ("AI is collapsing" or "US equities are fine") gets the direction wrong. Whether the rotation persists is what tonight's CPI and the following Fed speakers will test.

**③ Asia faded from the open, A50 futures turned negative, Nasdaq futures weakened in tandem | 11:31–11:37**

All three A-share indices gapped up and faded (Shanghai +0.58%→+0.06%, Shenzhen Component +1.51%→+0.76%, ChiNext +2.44%→+1.49%); FTSE China A50 futures **flipped from up more than 1% to negative** (11:37). Index futures diverged at the midday close: IF −0.02%, IH −0.43%, IC +0.66%, IM +0.90% — large-cap weights sold, small-cap themes held. That is the classic "index flat, structure rotating" pattern. Over the same span Nasdaq futures fell from 27,978.9 (11:03) to 27,907.9 (12:04) while Dow futures rose 0.33%.

**Impact**: the overnight US discount-repair did not get confirmation in Asia. IH leading the decline while IM leads the gains says the selling is in weights and AI-linked names, not Chinese equities as a whole. For tonight's US open the implication is: **if the open is again dragged by the weights while equal-weight does not fall, the rotation call is confirmed**.

**④ Copper: Wall Street's longs halved, China's physical premium at a record | 11:41 (article 234784, Reuters' Andy Home)**

Two hard data sets pointing opposite ways. Financial side: CME copper futures managed-money net longs fell from **82,649 contracts** in mid-September (the largest since 2021) to **34,444** in the latest COT, with outright longs down from 96,824 to **58,475** — and that report does not yet include the worst of last weekend's selling; the CME premium to LME has narrowed from **over $700/t** in August to **$80/t**. Physical side: ShFE copper inventories have fallen to **33,848 tonnes** (a three-year low), the Yangshan copper premium rose to **$230/t on Monday** (a record high), and LME cash is at a **$195/t** backwardation to three-month; CME warehouses hold **over 60%** of global exchange copper stocks, or **85%** once LME-run US warehouses are included.

**Impact**: the financial side of the AI trade is leaving (longs halved) while the physical side is still tight (a record Chinese premium). Copper is one of the few parts of the AI capex story with a real delivery constraint, and its financial longs retreated earlier than equities did and are harder to explain away as sentiment. But the concentration of stocks in the US is an artefact of tariff arbitrage, not a global shortage — **a record Yangshan premium is not proof of a demand surge**.

## 3. Read on the situation

**A | AI hardware: downgraded from "rally" to "divergence" — relatively bearish the leaders, bullish broad indices, but do not take a directional bet before CPI.**

Four pieces of evidence: Google taking TPU to four chips and selling them externally turns "specialised compute" from a concept into a product line; equal-weight beating cap-weight by the most since 2009 is a direct read on market structure; IH leading the A-share decline while IM leads says weights are what is being sold; Nasdaq futures at −0.38% against Dow futures at +0.33% is more informative than either absolute move. On the other hand, last night's SOX +6.85% and AMD +13.26% were also real, so this is not a one-way collapse. **Trade-off**: do not chase the AI hardware bounce, and do not short the index; the change worth making is tilting from "beneficiaries of rising compute prices" toward "beneficiaries of falling compute costs", but that needs order or gross-margin evidence that this hour does not provide. **Confirmation**: at the 22:30 open, NVDA reclaims 180 and SOX back above 10,033 while equal-weight stops outperforming — then the divergence call is void. **Invalidation**: core CPI at 0.3% or above re-prices the discount rate higher, AI hardware de-rates first and equal-weight will not hold either.

**B | Gold: short-term bearish stands, but downside momentum is fading — do not add shorts here.**

Evidence: this hour's move is −0.07%, clearly smaller than the prior hour's −0.33%; the intraday low of 4,108.13 is only about $2 below the previous print; silver is −0.02% in tandem and the gold/silver ratio is essentially unchanged at 77.54 — this is real-rate pricing, not panic selling. The pressure sources remain: the weak 30-year JGB auction and USD/JPY at 157.96 mean both the long end and carry costs point to higher real rates. **Trade-off**: no new shorts and no bottom-fishing before CPI. **Confirmation / invalidation**: core m/m at 0.3% or above extends the bearish case, with only directional reference to the 4,070 area below (the two sources' closing prints differ by about $15, so no precise support is given); core at 0.2% or below with gold back above 4,140 voids the bearish call. **Evidence gap**: this machine still cannot obtain real-time TIPS real yields or the dollar index, so "real rates rising" cannot be separated from "risk premium contracting" — and the two have very different implications for how durable the move is.

**C | Crude: sixth hour of range-trading, a new low but no break — nothing to trade before 14:30.**

Evidence: WTI −0.07% and Brent −0.05% this hour, both within 0.1%; domestic SC crude still down more than 5% and LPG more than 6% at the midday close, but those are RMB-denominated contracts catching down to Monday's US session and are not synchronous with WTI, so they are not a new bearish input.
