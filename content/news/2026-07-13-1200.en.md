🔥 **Key Developments This Hour**

**🚨 Iran Revolutionary Guard Attacks US Military Base in Kuwait**
The IRGC has claimed responsibility for attacking a US military base in Kuwait. Combined with this morning's confirmation that US forces have begun a new round of strikes on Iran, the Middle East conflict has escalated dramatically. The Strait of Hormuz has been declared closed by Iran, sending crude oil supply risk sharply higher — WTI surged nearly 4% intraday.

**KOSPI Crashes 7%, Bank of Korea Forced to Intervene**
South Korea's KOSPI index briefly extended losses to 7%, breaking below the 7,000 mark to hit its lowest level since May 4. SK Hynix shares in Seoul plunged over 10%, despite its US ADR having surged 13% just last week. The Bank of Korea was forced into emergency market intervention.

**SK Hynix Q2 Outlook Downgraded**
Korea Investment & Securities forecasts SK Hynix Q2 revenue at 80.9 trillion KRW and operating profit at 60.4 trillion KRW, approximately 8% below market consensus. Separately, a South Korean court has barred two former Samsung NAND flash design employees from working at SK Hynix until April 2027.

**EU Imports Record Russian LNG in H1 2026**
According to the Financial Times, the EU imported a record volume of LNG from Russia's flagship project in H1 2026, absorbing nearly the facility's entire output just months before the EU ban on Russian gas imports takes effect.

**A-Shares Close Morning Session Sharply Lower**
Shanghai Composite -1.54%, Shenzhen Component -2.61%, ChiNext -2.38%. Defense electronics, CSSC-linked stocks, and commercial aerospace led the declines, while traditional Chinese medicine bucked the trend. In domestic futures, fuel oil surged over 4% while Shanghai silver fell nearly 3%.

**Ukraine Political Earthquake: Zelensky Fires Prime Minister**
President Zelensky announced the dismissal of the current prime minister and a sweeping cabinet reshuffle — the third major overhaul since the Russia-Ukraine conflict began. Ruling party officials called the move "completely unexpected."

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The Middle East situation has escalated from "conflict" to "war-level confrontation." Iran's attack on a US base in Kuwait means the battlefield has expanded beyond Iranian territory to US military installations across the Gulf.

The Strait of Hormuz closure is not a short-term event — roughly 20% of global oil transits through this chokepoint, providing a fundamental basis for further oil price surges.

Gold's decline despite the geopolitical crisis warrants serious attention — this typically signals a liquidity crunch where investors are forced to sell gold for cash, a classic extreme-risk-phase signal.

The KOSPI crash reflects multiple converging factors: deteriorating global risk sentiment + SK Hynix ADR premium mean-reversion + escalating Samsung talent wars. Bank of Korea intervention may only slow the decline, not reverse the trend.

A-shares are also under pressure but with relatively moderate losses. The defense sector's decline despite the Middle East conflict is notable — markets may view direct benefits to Chinese defense stocks as limited.

Warsh's Congressional testimony this week represents another potential risk catalyst — hawkish signals would further tighten global liquidity conditions.
