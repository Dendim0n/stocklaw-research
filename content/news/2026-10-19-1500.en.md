## 4. Situation assessment

**View 1: Oil has shifted from "risk-premium expansion" to "premium give-back", but this is a change in the marginal pricing evidence, not the end of the supply disruption; do not chase crude higher near term, and do not read today's -2.3% as a trend reversal either.**

Basis: the bearish items are verifiable — the US side's "Gulf flows back to 80% of pre-conflict", "97% of vessels passed safely", and no confirmable escalation over the weekend (FT quoting BNP Paribas). But the supporting structure is unchanged: the diesel end is tight (Huatai 07:48), G7 reserve releases were dismissed by ANZ as "too late and already priced", and Iran's foreign minister made three statements on 10-18 (the seven-day transit plan remains valid, the next move rests with the US) showing that restored transit is **still conditional**. Invalidation: if quantifiable new losses appear (attacks on regular fleets, larger damaged capacity at Russian refineries or export terminals), the premium will re-expand; conversely, "demand and rates outweigh supply risk" only holds if EIA inventories keep drawing sharply on 10-21 while prices fail to rise — that requires the diesel crack to weaken in tandem, which has not happened yet.

**View 2: Today's bounce is technical repair from "no bad news over the weekend"; the credit and return-on-investment doubts around AI are unresolved, so the trade here remains reducing exposure into strength rather than buying the dip.**

Basis (all checkable within the same hour): Nasdaq-100 futures +1.3% (independently +1.27%) appeared on the same day as BTIG's 14:04 "sell the bounce"; BTIG's historical base — after a weekly SOX drop of 10%+ the index rises the next week only 43% of the time with a median return of -1.7%; since 1 June, nine of the S&P 500's eleven sectors have fallen at least 9% from their own peaks while the index's maximum drawdown is just 5%, meaning **the index is masking deep intra-sector drawdowns**; add the 15:03 Barclays private-credit review, which shows the credit problem moving inward rather than outward. **Counter-evidence**: the 10-year Treasury yield fell 0.146pp last week to 5.097% (weekly-outlook basis), the largest weekly decline since mid-February, so haven demand is real; CITIC Securities argued on 10-18 that the long-end anchor has switched from inflation to the "growth/AI cycle". Confirmation / invalidation: if Nvidia's GTC on 10-20 to 10-22 provides quantifiable order and delivery figures, and this week's Meta and Tesla results give capital-expenditure guidance, the discount can close quickly and this view should be downgraded to "valuation correction"; conversely, if SOXX breaks $490 and CoreWeave-style credit prices deteriorate further, this escalates into a credit event.

**View 3: The divergence between China's headline and structural data means policy beneficiaries matter more than index direction — within A-shares, favour banks/dividends, the energy chain and robotics themes over ChiNext and STAR.**

Basis: Q3 GDP at 4.5% beat the 4.4% consensus and retail sales at +1.1% clearly beat 0.6%, yet fixed-asset investment at -7.2%, September power generation at -1.2% and refining throughput at -8.4% give concrete evidence of weak demand and weak production at the same time; the market's response was a higher Shanghai Composite, a -2.17% STAR 50 and RMB 117.9 billion less turnover, alongside record closes for ICBC and China Construction Bank and active robotics names. On policy, the same day brought the "technology-empowered commerce" opinions (10:14) and two export-control actions (15:00): the former helps smart consumer goods and AI applications, the latter is a compliance cost for equipment exporters. Invalidation: if the 10-20 LPR and subsequent October data show demand repairing (especially power generation, refining throughput and fixed-asset investment turning positive), or if STAR turnover expands again, the "rotation" narrative should revert to "sector rotation within a rising market"; and if today's 15:00 State Council briefing on technology-empowered commerce delivers materially better-than-expected measures, application-side valuations will be repriced.
