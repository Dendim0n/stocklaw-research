## 🔥 Key Overnight News

**Fed Hawkish Hold: Three Dissenters Vote for Hike, Warsh Downplays Forward Guidance**

The Fed held rates at 3.50%-3.75% for the fifth consecutive meeting. The vote was 9-3, with Logan (Dallas), Hammack (Cleveland), and Kashkari (Minneapolis) dissenting in favor of a 25bp hike — the first time since 2016 that three regional Fed presidents cast unified dissenting votes. Chair Warsh declined to call the decision any kind of "pause," emphasizing that markets had already done the tightening. CME data shows September hike probability dropped from 82% to 63%.

**Post-Market Earnings: Microsoft Shines, Meta Disappoints**

Microsoft Q4 revenue $90B beat estimates; Azure cloud revenue surpassed $100B for the first time; shares rose 8% after hours. Copilot paid users exceeded 30M. FY2027 guidance was positive with Azure growth expected to accelerate. Meta posted record revenue of $60.8B, but raised the lower end of its 2026 capex guidance to $130B, triggering cost concerns; shares fell 10% after hours. Qualcomm Q3 revenue $9.95B beat estimates but Apple-related revenue decline is accelerating; shares fell 4.6%. Arm Q1 revenue $1.29B beat but Q2 guidance was weak; shares fell 8%.

**Samsung Q2: Net Profit 71.3 Trillion Won Beats Estimates**

Samsung Electronics Q2 net profit of 71.3 trillion won surpassed the 68.5 trillion won consensus. Chip division operating profit was 89.2 trillion won. The company expects strong server chip demand in H2, but memory supply constraints persist and mobile/PC chip demand is slowing.

**Middle East Crisis Escalates: Trump Vows to Strike Iran**

Iran fired multiple missiles at US forces in the Middle East, all intercepted by Patriot systems. Trump declared the US will "teach Iran a lesson" and deliver a "forceful strike." The WSJ reported the Pentagon has drafted a two-week intensive airstrike plan against Iran, awaiting Trump's decision. A joint US-Saudi airstrike in Iraq killed four Iranian Revolutionary Guard members. Saudi Arabia is seeking to form an international coalition to protect Red Sea shipping.

**Russia Launches Massive Attack on Ukraine**

Kyiv was hit by ballistic missile strikes this morning with continuous explosions reported. Russian forces seized control of Svetly and Novasych in eastern Ukraine. A Ukrainian F-16 fighter jet crashed during a combat mission; the pilot ejected safely. Zelensky requested 300 Patriot interceptor missiles from Trump before winter.

**Bond Market Turmoil: 30Y Yield Breaks 5.20%**

The US 30-year Treasury yield hit 5.213%, the highest since July 2007. The 10-year yield rose to 4.661%. "Bond King" Gundlach warned that getting inflation back to 2% requires rate hikes. Apollo's chief economist Slok noted that Warsh's abandonment of forward guidance is creating "yo-yo" style bond market volatility.

## 🧭 Market Assessment

The Fed's hawkish hold with three dissenting votes signals deepening internal divisions, yet the September hike probability paradoxically fell from 82% to 63% — markets are processing Warsh's "let the market speak" approach with mixed signals.

The 30-year Treasury yield breaking 5.20% is a major signal — rising long-end rates indicate the market is pricing in a higher inflation baseline and fiscal deficit premium, not merely trading rate hike expectations.

The Middle East is the biggest tail risk right now — Iranian missile strikes plus Trump's hardline response plus a drafted US airstrike plan mean geopolitical premiums could continue to push gold and oil higher.

Microsoft's earnings validated strong AI cloud demand, but Meta's capex concerns show the market's patience for AI returns is thinning — same massive capex, Microsoft up 8% while Meta down 10%, a stark divergence.

Today is a super data day: European GDP releases + US Q2 GDP advance + core PCE + jobless claims — any directional surprise could trigger significant volatility.

Gold above $4,100, caught between safe-haven demand and cooling rate-cut expectations — the direction depends on whether geopolitical risk can continue to outweigh rising rate pressure.
