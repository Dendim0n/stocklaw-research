Berkshire Hathaway raised **JPY182.3bn** (about **US$1.14bn** at USDJPY 159.92) via a five-part yen bond with 3- to 30-year maturities; the 5-year priced at TONA mid-swap **+75bp** (3.007% coupon), 10-year **+90bp** (3.661%), 30-year **+105bp** (4.571%), versus 70/90/100bp for the same tenors in April ([12:43](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113124356953800), [09:58](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113095838723800)). Impact: a corporate borrower has now confirmed rising Japanese rates with its own cost of funds, which is harder evidence than one day of JGB volatility. That is a discount-rate headwind for global duration assets, and a curve-steepening benefit for Japanese banks and insurers.

**3) Goldman: silver speculative net length at the 2nd percentile of two years; ~$2.3bn sold in five weeks | 12:55**
Goldman's Robert Quinn: CFTC data show non-commercial accounts sold about **$2.3bn** of silver futures exposure between **10-06 and 11-03**, of which roughly **$2.6bn came from new shorts** and about **$0.3bn from new longs** - so a meaningful part of the decline came from fresh shorts rather than long liquidation; speculative net length fell to the 2nd percentile of the past two years, and total speculative positioning dropped from about $24bn at the January peak to about $8bn. Three tentative turning signals: the positioning reset, a **+7.1%** move in Goldman's China solar equity basket after the election, and fading demand for downside options protection. **The CFTC data end 11-03 and do not capture post-election flows** ([235138](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235138)).
Impact: this structure produces short-covering bids on any rally, which argues against a deep decline; the demand side points the other way - solar silver usage is expected to fall **21%** this year (Metals Focus, [09:24](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113092439184800)) - so it is not a case for a trend-long.

## 3. Read on the situation

**A | Asia tech: the afternoon acceleration shows the morning pullback was not a one-off gap repair; stay bearish on chasing.**
Evidence: after the reopen ChiNext was -2.39% (midday -1.47%) and STAR 50 -1.73%, while the **Shanghai Composite was only -0.15%**, a 2.24pp gap that continues the structural split (bank stocks hit record highs at midday, [11:41](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113114116168800)); Hong Kong PCB and optical names fell 5-10% while oil and petrochemicals rose 2-4%; at the same moment the Nikkei was **+0.82%** against KOSPI **-0.70%**, meaning selling is concentrated in memory and AI hardware (the Korean weightings) rather than across Asia. Impact: optical modules, PCB, AI server assembly and Korean/Taiwan memory are biased lower; energy, banks, dividend plays and film exhibition are relative destinations. Invalidation and confirmation: if ChiNext reclaims 2,500 by the 15:00 close (psychological level; prior close 2,509.43), this section is downgraded; the harder test remains **Nvidia's guidance on 11-17** - a cut would turn the pullback from sentiment into demand.

**B | Japanese rates: the funding-cost rise is now confirmed by corporate debt, a headwind for global duration assets, but on only one day of evidence.**
Evidence: Berkshire's 30-year yen spread at 105bp (100bp in April) and a 4.571% coupon, with the 10-year at 90bp and 5-year at 75bp - wider across the curve; JGBs at 2.875% for 10 years (12:16), 1.890% for 2 years (11:50) and 3.985% for 30 years (09:52); Prime Minister Takaichi said between 12:41 and 12:46 that she would not comment on FX, that Japan is in close contact with US financial authorities, and that fiscal and economic policy is not aimed at manipulating the currency, with USDJPY at 159.923 still below 160; BOJ Deputy Governor Himino said there are no plans to further review ETF sales ([12:39](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113123911309800)). Impact: higher carry costs weigh on expensive growth stocks and on gold's discount-rate channel; Japanese banks and insurers benefit from a steeper curve. Invalidation and confirmation: if Treasury yields and 2-year JGBs both rise again tonight, this is a global duration repricing; if Treasuries fall while JGBs rise alone, it is a domestic Japan story (fiscal and BOJ expectations) whose damage to global valuations is smaller than current pricing implies. What is missing: **the official 11-13 Treasury yield has not been published**, so the Treasury leg cannot be verified this issue and must be checked tonight.

**C | Gold and silver: tactically neutral-to-bearish on gold without chasing the short; silver's sell-side structure is already extreme.**
Evidence: gold -0.16% from 12:00 to 13:00 while silver (COMEX) fell -0.45% over the same hour; Goldman's data put silver speculative net length at the 2nd percentile of two years with $2.3bn sold over five weeks, mostly fresh shorts; domestic jewellery prices broke 1,000. The direction of this evidence is not "crash" but "leverage flush plus physical demand suppressed by high prices". Impact: near-term upside elasticity falls for gold, silver and gold miners; silver's crowded short base means a larger bounce is possible, but it needs solar demand to cooperate. Invalidation and confirmation: a reclaim of **3,987.93** (11-11 close, about +1.9% from here) voids this section; silver losing today's low of 49.87 (COMEX) would mean the flush is unfinished. Trade-off: neither chase the short nor bottom-fish here; wait for tonight's 21:30 PPI and see whether 3,900 breaks before choosing a direction.
