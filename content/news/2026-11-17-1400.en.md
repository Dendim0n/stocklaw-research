## Assessment

**A | Crude: geopolitics is escalating, price is denying it — do not chase longs.**
Evidence: four tankers mined, a tanker hit and burning, three carriers assembled, Iran declaring the southern route closed — that combination is heavier than anything in the past two weeks, yet WTI is still -0.88% versus settlement, Brent -0.60%, and two banks cut Q4 forecasts the same day. **Judgement**: the market is attributing today's price to supply and demand rather than war; a premium only gets paid against confirmed production or loading losses, and right now there are attack reports but no output data. Implication: energy equities lack a short-term catalyst, and shipping insurance and freight are the faster transmission channel. **Invalidation/confirmation**: Brent reclaiming 92.44 while WTI holds above 85.72 would mean the market is starting to pay for disruption; if the news stays at attacks with no fall in loadings, flat crude is the default.

**B | Asian AI hardware: this is de-risking before earnings, not a narrative collapse — but the short-term bias is lower.**
Evidence: STAR 50 handed back all 3.3 points gained in the morning, ChiNext made a fresh intraday low, the two optical-module leaders fell more than 5%, TAIEX closed -2.31% (Barclays had said forced retail selling drove Monday's Taiwan rout), and KOSPI fell -2.70%; yet the equipment chain is still up, SMIC A-shares are +6.08%, and US futures are flat — the selling is concentrated in "AI interconnect valuation", not semis as a whole. Implication: optical modules, CPO and AI servers are the short side; equipment and materials are relatively strong. **Confirmation/invalidation**: a STAR 50 close below yesterday's close at 15:00 means the afternoon rebound has fully failed; reclaiming +1% would mean it was only an intraday shakeout.

**C | Gold: 3,886 is a pulse high — turn neutral, do not chase.**
Evidence: the high printed at 13:34 and price fell back below 3,880 from 14:01, giving back 0.30% from the peak; the dollar index and the 10-year yield barely moved on the day, so this is not rate-driven; if it were geopolitical, crude should have firmed with it, and it did not; domestic Chinese jewellery prices have kept sliding to 1,153–1,158 yuan per gram, showing high prices are suppressing physical demand; institutional flows point the other way (Duquesne opened 1.05m Barrick shares in its 13F, a 9-30 snapshot). **Judgement**: this looks more like position adjustment than the start of a trend, and waiting for the close beats chasing. **Invalidation/confirmation**: a US close above 3,880 confirms the breakout; a break of today's low of **3,843.56** voids the rebound.
