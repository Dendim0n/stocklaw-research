🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**US airstrikes hit Iran's Bushehr province, conflict continues to escalate.** Iran's Nour News confirmed that parts of Bushehr province in southern Iran were hit by a new round of US strikes (09:48 CST). Bushehr is home to Iran's only operating nuclear power plant. Trump had previously warned of strikes on Iran's most fortified underground nuclear facility, "Kuh-e-Sabz" (Green Mountain). This marks the third consecutive night of US airstrikes on Iranian targets.

**Bank of Korea expected to raise rates this week, first time in over three years.** A foreign media survey shows economists expect the BOK to implement its first rate hike in more than three years on Thursday, with another hike before year-end. South Korea's June inflation rose to 3.2%, the fourth consecutive month above the 2% target (09:49 CST).

**US June CPI due tonight, July rate hike probability surges to 50%.** Fed Governor Waller issued a hawkish warning: if core CPI data continues to run hot this week, the FOMC needs to consider a near-term rate hike. The CPI release coincides with Warsh's congressional debut, making this a pivotal week for the Fed's policy path.

**Trump announces blockade of Iranian ports, effective 04:00 July 15 Beijing time.** The US will blockade Iranian ports starting 04:00 CST on July 15, while imposing a 20% "protection fee" on Strait of Hormuz shipping. Dubai has already planned a new port to bypass the Strait of Hormuz.

**China's State Council Information Office to release H1 trade data at 10:00.** Markets are watching China's June trade figures amid concerns over weakening external demand and further slowdown in export growth.

**Global Q2 smartphone shipments down 4% YoY.** Omdia reports the ongoing memory chip crisis is driving up component costs, continuing to pressure global smartphone shipments. Hong Kong large-model AI stocks fell in early trading, with Zhipu down 9.3%.

🧭 **Market Assessment**

The Iran situation is currently the most critical market driver. US airstrikes expanding from military targets to the Bushehr nuclear facility area represents a significant escalation in conflict intensity, with no signs of near-term de-escalation.

Crude oil's war premium still has room to run. If the Strait of Hormuz blockade is substantively enforced, global crude supply will face a major shortfall — $79 may not be the ceiling.

Gold is locked in intense tug-of-war around the $4,000 level. Geopolitical risk provides support, but a hotter-than-expected CPI would strengthen rate hike expectations, pressuring non-yielding assets. Tonight's CPI is the key near-term inflection point for gold.

A-shares and Hong Kong markets are squeezed by dual pressures — external geopolitical risk and internal economic data verification. Shanghai's 3900 level is a critical psychological support; whether it holds depends on today's trade data and subsequent policy expectations.

Fed policy expectations are rapidly tilting hawkish. Waller's warning combined with a cooperative CPI print could shift a July rate hike from tail risk to baseline scenario — a major variable for global risk asset pricing.
