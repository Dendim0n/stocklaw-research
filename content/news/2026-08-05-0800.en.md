🔥 **Key Overnight News**

**1. Hormuz Strait Reopening Gains Traction**
US Treasury Secretary Bessent indicated a US-Iran deal could be reached Wednesday to restore "free transit" through the Hormuz Strait. Iran is considering allowing European nations to clear mines from the strait, with European and Gulf states potentially co-funding the operation. However, Iran also demands "complete control" over all inbound vessels — a sticking point that could derail negotiations.

**2. SpaceX's First Earnings + Strategic Roadmap**
Musk announced at SpaceX's inaugural earnings call: Grok 4.6 launching next week, Grok 5 within this year; SpaceX AI services will run exclusively on NVIDIA systems, with plans for space-based data centers; revenue target of $1 trillion by 2030 (pulled forward one year); Starship expected to be human-rated by end of 2027; at least 1,000 Starlink V3 satellites to launch by Q2 next year.

**3. AMD Drops ~9% Post-Earnings**
AMD beat Q2 estimates but the revenue outlook disappointed some investors, sending shares down ~9% after-hours. CEO projected 2027 data center revenue to more than triple YoY. The market is no longer satisfied with mere beats from AI chip names — it demands accelerating guidance.

**4. Palantir Revenue Nearly Doubles**
Palantir Q2 revenue up 93% YoY, significantly raised full-year guidance. The "AI sovereignty" narrative is driving enterprise adoption from AI demand toward AI governance and deployment.

**5. S&P 500 Records New All-Time High**
US equities show healthy broadening from AI into traditional sectors. UBS is constructive, noting capital reallocation is underway with corporate earnings resilience supporting valuations.

**6. White House Excludes Open-Source Models from AI Testing Framework**
The White House has defined "covered frontier models" as closed models meeting specific capability thresholds, effectively excluding open-source models from advanced AI capability testing. Separately, the UK AI Safety Institute reported a safety incident involving Anthropic and OpenAI models.

**7. Trump Admin Has Refunded $100B in Tariffs**
Since the Supreme Court ruled presidential emergency tariff powers unconstitutional, Customs has processed approximately $100 billion in tariff refunds — a significant portion of total collected amounts.

**8. US Military Missile Stockpiles Running Low**
Five months of Iran conflict have depleted substantial precision-strike inventories: Patriots down 65%, Tomahawks nearly halved. Defense Secretary Hegseth pushed back on CNN's claim of 80% THAAD interceptor depletion.

**9. Bessent Warns on Yen Weakness**
US Treasury Secretary warned that extreme yen weakness is fueling Japanese inflation and threatening Asian financial stability. US Treasury used euro reserves to buy yen in intervention. Japan June labor cash earnings +3.4% YoY (vs. 3.5% expected). CME FedWatch prices 58.4% probability of a 25bp HIKE in September.

🧭 **Assessment**

Hormuz Strait diplomatic progress is the market's biggest variable right now. If Bessent's "today or tomorrow" timeline holds, oil prices face downside pressure and safe-haven gold could see a pullback.

But Iran's demand for "complete control" means negotiations could break down at any moment. The market is cautiously skeptical of a clean resolution.

AMD's post-earnings selloff signals tightening valuation anchors across the AI chip sector — beating estimates is no longer enough; the market demands accelerating forward guidance. This creates short-term headwinds for the entire AI hardware space.

SpaceX's public earnings debut and dense strategic announcements show Musk building an integrated "space + AI" ecosystem, with exclusive NVIDIA partnership further cementing NVIDIA's AI compute monopoly.

The White House's exclusion of open-source models from AI testing frameworks effectively creates a regulatory moat for top closed-model providers (OpenAI, Anthropic), disadvantaging the open-source community.

Fed September hike probability rising to 58.4%, combined with yen intervention and BoJ policy constraints, signals tightening global liquidity conditions worth monitoring.

US military missile stockpile depletion could trigger defense supplemental budgets — a potential catalyst for defense stocks.
