🧭 **Market Assessment**

Tonight's Global Central Bank Forum is the focal point. Worshe's first international appearance since taking office is critical — markets need to gauge his true stance on inflation and the rate path, though explicit guidance is unlikely.

Gold continues to weaken technically, approaching the death cross threshold. However, the OMFIF survey of 74 central banks projects gold reaching $5,000–6,000 within 12 months — the medium-term bull case remains intact, and the current pullback is largely technical clearing.

Crude remains under pressure post-Hormuz reopening. Goldman Sachs warns global crude surplus could exceed 3 million barrels per day, and even strategic reserve purchases won't fully offset new supply. Oil looks bearish medium-term.

The afternoon pullback in A-share chip stocks is notable. Individual stock dispersion is at decade highs — the S&P 500 looks calm on the surface, but implied correlation has hit rock bottom, meaning risk repricing could happen in an instant.
