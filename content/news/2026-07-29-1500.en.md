Whether "hawkish words, dovish hands" can restore central bank credibility remains an open question.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The AI selloff has evolved from a tech valuation correction into a leverage liquidation crisis — hedge fund margin calls could trigger cascading forced selling, keeping near-term risk appetite under heavy pressure.

The Middle East situation is undergoing real escalation — Houthi attacks combined with US-Saudi military operations in Iraq are rapidly pricing risk premium into crude. With WTI above $80, a push toward the $82–85 range is plausible if geopolitical conditions continue to deteriorate.

Tonight's Fed decision is the week's biggest uncertainty event — Warsh's first rate call will define market policy expectations for months to come. Whether he hikes or holds, volatility will spike significantly.

Asian markets are showing rare divergence: Korea plunging 6%, Japan modestly lower, while Australia and Singapore rally — capital is rotating away from AI-heavy markets (Korean semiconductors) toward resource and commodity-linked economies.

The yen's 165 level and the BOJ decision form a dual-game setup — if the BOJ disappoints on Friday, USD/JPY could accelerate upward, putting further pressure on Asian currencies.
