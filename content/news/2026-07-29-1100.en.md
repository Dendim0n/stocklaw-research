## 🔥 Key Developments This Hour

**🛢️ Middle East Powder Keg: US-Saudi Coalition Strikes Iraq**

Al Jazeera reports that Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) confirmed US and Saudi forces conducted joint airstrikes on its positions in Nineveh province, killing 8 members and wounding several others. The PMF condemned the strikes as an "extremely dangerous escalation." Hours earlier, Iran fired ballistic missiles at US military bases in the Middle East (all intercepted), and Houthi forces attacked another Saudi-flagged oil tanker. The regional proxy war has entered a new spiral of escalation.

**🇰🇷 South Korea KOSPI Crashes 6%, Government Scrambles**

South Korea's KOSPI index plunged 6% intraday. Finance Minister Koo Yun-cheol stated the government is "internally studying market stabilization measures." Prime Minister Han Seong-sook simultaneously ordered a crackdown on hoarding and market disruption, citing supply concerns from the escalating Middle East conflict. Geopolitical risk is transmitting to Asian markets through supply chain anxiety.

**🇺🇸 Fed Decision Day: Warsh Becomes the Wild Card**

Multiple analyses suggest Warsh will likely "talk hawkish but act dovish" (hold rates while maintaining hawkish rhetoric), constrained by the triple bind of inflation, employment, and financial stability.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The Middle East situation is undergoing a qualitative shift — from a bilateral Iran-Israel / Iran-US confrontation to a multi-front proxy conflict. The US-Saudi coalition directly striking Iran-aligned forces in Iraq means the conflict radius has expanded from the Strait of Hormuz and the Red Sea to Mesopotamia.

This is no longer a "potential risk" to oil supply but an "unfolding reality." WTI's nearly 4% surge today reflects this cognitive recalibration.

South Korea's 6% KOSPI crash is a textbook signal of Middle East risk contagion — an export-dependent economy heavily reliant on energy imports and global supply chains bears the brunt of geopolitical escalation. The government's emergency stabilization rhetoric confirms the severity.

Tonight's Fed decision will be made against the backdrop of Middle Eastern turmoil. Warsh faces a classic dilemma: geopolitically driven oil spikes may push inflation expectations higher (arguing for a hike), but violent financial market turbulence demands central bank restraint (arguing for a hold). The "hawkish talk, dovish act" probability is indeed higher — hold rates but signal a hawkish bias.

China's A-share V-shaped rebound today shows resilience, but caution is warranted for tomorrow's open given further external risk escalation. Singapore's STI hitting a record high amid the chaos is notable — it may reflect safe-haven capital rotating from Northeast Asia toward Southeast Asia.
