## 4. Read on the situation

**Call one: gold's decline is driven by the dollar and positioning, not by vanishing inflation-hedge demand — which is why it can fall, but also why chasing it lower before FOMC is a poor trade.**
The evidence is cross-sectional rather than a single print: gold −0.44%, silver −2.06%, platinum −1.97%, palladium −2.17%, while copper is only −0.11% — the damage is concentrated in one complex. The gold/silver ratio at 75.7, with silver falling 4.7 times as much as gold, is classic deleveraging, not a haven rotation. Meanwhile French INSEE shows household inflation expectations rising and **US September core PCE lands 10-29 20:30** (expected 3.10%, prior 3.00%): inflation expectations are going up while gold goes down, which can only be explained by higher nominal rates and a firmer dollar (DXY 102.12). Implication: precious metals are biased lower short-term and longs should wait for FOMC (10-29 02:00, upper bound expected unchanged at 4.00%); silver carries higher beta, so it also bounces harder. Invalidation: if gold closes above 4,000 for two consecutive sessions after the 10-29 decision, or DXY falls back below the 10-26 close of 101.96 and stays there the next day, this call is downgraded; if tonight's core capital goods orders come in well below expectations, falling real yields would show up in gold first.

**Call two: crude's strength is physical supply, not a news pulse; but at the 86th percentile of its range, chasing it is a bad payoff — the right move is to wait for inventories and transit data.**
Evidence: this hour both USOIL and NYMEX December printed session highs with **no** new supply headline. Hormuz being "fully closed" is Iran's unilateral claim (14:59) against the US position of free navigation; neither side has published transit volumes. The Najran alert was lifted after eight minutes with no confirmed attack — noise level. On position, CL's three-month range is 75.22–105.83 and 102.18 sits near the **86th percentile**, just 3.6 dollars below the top. Implication: energy producers and tanker names are biased higher, cost-side users such as airlines and chemicals lower; options rather than spot are the better expression here, because a decisive break of 105.83 (a close above, not an intraday touch) would switch the narrative to a prolonged strait closure. Invalidation: a large build in tonight's API (expected +522k barrels, prior −4.931m) and tomorrow's EIA, or official confirmation that transit has resumed, would bleed the premium out quickly.

**Call three: the A-share conclusion does not need to change, but the size gap in the support bid has to be stated plainly.**
Evidence: RMB 47.114bn of main-fund outflow against a RMB 5bn buyback cap across 13 STAR50 companies is roughly 9:1; RMB 37.083bn of small-order net buying says the absorber is retail, not institutions, and a RMB 147.8bn drop in turnover says the absorption is getting shallower. The prior edition's "missing bids rather than capitulation" therefore holds, and tomorrow's test is unchanged: STAR50 needs to reclaim the 10-26 close of 1,315.50 with turnover at least 1.3 times today's to count as repaired; a break of today's low of 1,249.76 and of CSI 500's 6,511.37 would confirm a second leg. Implication: the relative strength of dividends, large financials and utilities comes from passive concentration of flows, not earnings upgrades, so it is a defensive position and not a growth thesis. Hong Kong weakening in parallel (Hang Seng Tech −1.78%, Xiaomi −4.98%) shows this is a China-asset risk-appetite problem, not a single-market one. Counter-evidence: STAR50 ETFs took in nearly RMB 24bn over the past week (as of 10-26), which is real absorption; a high-volume long lower shadow tomorrow would be an early sign that the panic supply has cleared.
