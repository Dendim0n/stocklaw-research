🔥 **Key Developments This Hour**

**🛢️ US-Iran: Strikes Paused, Diplomacy Steps In**

US Central Command has not announced new strikes against Iran for two consecutive days (Friday and Saturday). A Pentagon source told CNN that Iran-related operations are currently "paused," following 13 consecutive nights of US strikes. The White House pushed back against a New York Times report claiming Trump shelved plans for a major escalation, insisting the President retains all options.

On the diplomatic front, Iran and Oman held multiple rounds of deputy foreign minister-level talks in Tehran, discussing common principles and operational mechanisms to ensure safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's Foreign Ministry described the negotiations as "productive," with technical and political consultations ongoing. Iran's parliamentary National Security Committee is simultaneously reviewing a draft bill on Strait of Hormuz management.

Iran's military spokesman struck a defiant tone, claiming the US has "failed to achieve its objectives" in the Strait of Hormuz and in weakening Iran, warning that "the geographic scope of the war will expand further" if the US continues hostilities.

**🔗 Iraq-Syria Oil Pipeline Agreement**

Iraq's cabinet authorized the Basra Oil Company to sign a pipeline construction agreement with Syria's Oil Ministry, building a route connecting Iraqi oil fields to global markets via the Mediterranean. This represents a strategic move to diversify Iraq's export corridors.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The US-Iran conflict has officially entered a "fight and talk" phase — the US pauses its 13-night bombing campaign while Iran-Oman negotiations on the Strait of Hormuz show progress. Both sides demonstrate willingness to manage escalation.

WTI crude dropped over 2% from the 93 level to the 90 handle, reflecting market pricing of marginal de-escalation. However, caution is warranted: Iran's military rhetoric and parliamentary legislative moves signal hardliners continue to apply pressure, and the situation could reverse at any moment.

Navigation conditions in the Strait of Hormuz remain unchanged for now, but formal passage of Iran's parliamentary bill would introduce a new dimension of legal uncertainty.

Gold remains in high consolidation above $4,000. Geopolitical risk premium is retreating in the short term, but the upcoming super central bank week — with the FOMC decision as the headline event — will be the key variable determining gold's next directional move.

The Iraq-Syria pipeline agreement is a medium-to-long-term supply-side positive, offering Iraq an export route that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz. However, construction timelines are long, limiting near-term impact on oil prices.
