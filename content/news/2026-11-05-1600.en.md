## 3. Key incremental items

## 4. Assessment

**A. Pricing power has shifted from "Asian panic" back to US rates — and this is the most fragile link.** Judgement: today's real upstream is the long end and the "peak tightening" expectation, not the Middle East or Korea. The evidence is four same-day co-movements: gold +1.65%, A-share precious metals and Shanghai silver leading, Hang Seng +3.64%, and the prior US session's broad rally with the Dow +3.03% and Nasdaq +1.44%; the 10-year Bund fell to 3.252% in parallel. But tightening is not over: the RBA delivered its fifth hike of 2026 this week, lifting the cash rate to 4.85%, an 18-year high (Jin10 15:01, citing the deputy governor). **Impact**: long-duration growth, gold and Hong Kong are most rate-sensitive and are tactically favoured. **Invalidation**: if the Bank of England hikes 25bp at 20:00 tonight (consensus 4.00%) and signals another move in February — Goldman, ANZ and ABN AMRO all expect a 4.25% terminal rate — the "peak tightening" narrative gets pushed back and the long end and gold fall together. Reading one hike as the end of a cycle is today's easiest mistake.

**B. The divergence between Korea and Chinese assets is real and should not be expressed with one position.** Judgement: Korea is a valuation event in AI hardware (heavily concentrated in memory), while Chinese assets are a rates- and policy-driven re-rating. Evidence: KOSPI closed near its low (just 0.88% above it), while on the same day Hon Hai was +45%, UMC +18% and Maersk raised guidance — three positive demand readings; the Hang Seng closed at 24,791, only 0.34% below its high, and more than 4,800 A-shares advanced. **Trade-off**: do not chase Korea's decline (the drop is large but the close shows no stabilisation), and do not chase Hong Kong at the high (already +3.64% today; Jin10 flashes also show southbound net selling widening from RMB 13.0bn at 15:04 to RMB 18.0bn at 15:40 — a single-platform figure not cross-checked against HKEX data, and if accurate it means this rally lacks mainland backing). **Invalidation**: if KOSPI gaps down tomorrow but recovers above 5,500, today was a mispricing; if the Hang Seng breaks today's low of 24,253, this repair loses credibility.

**C. Gold added no momentum this hour; crude's bounce looks like range-filling.** Judgement: gold's +1.65% day gain came almost entirely in the early Asian session, with 15:00→16:00 at −0.11% and $4,126.88 only 0.07% above the previous brief. Evidence: the day high of 4,146.79 has not been retested; silver at 52.586 sits 1.66% below its high of 53.476; COMEX gold carries a $13.05 (0.32%) premium to spot, within normal carry and showing no squeeze; gold remains about 26% below its January record near $5,600. Crude: WTI rose 1.21% this hour back to roughly the 88.12 prior settlement, still −0.14% on the day and 1.47% above the 86.63 low — a short-covering repair with no new supply event behind it. **Trade-off**: gold shifts from "chase" to "wait"; 4,100 is only a psychological level, with the day's low of 4,057.89 set in the morning and the afternoon low only 4,116.95, so it is not a buy signal here. No directional bet on crude. **Invalidation**: if gold loses 4,100 and slides toward the 4,057.89 low, the "rate-cut trade" pricing weakens; if crude clears today's high of 88.37–88.43 (Jin10/Sina basis), the "war premium at zero" pricing needs revision.
