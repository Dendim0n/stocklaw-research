## 3. Key increments

**① [Gap-fill · 10-16 10:02–11:14] MSCI proposes moving Indonesia from emerging to "standalone market"; Jakarta Composite closes down 11.39% — the period's largest risk event**

- MSCI launched a consultation on Thursday, saying Indonesia's governance and trading-mechanism problems persist and it is no longer suited to emerging-market status, while a frontier classification would also be insufficient — hence "standalone" (no Asian precedent; the category currently holds Argentina, Lebanon and Palestine). The consultation runs to **2027-04-30**, with a final decision expected by **2027-06-30**.
- Prices: the Jakarta Composite opened down 8.0% at 5,393.92 and triggered a 30-minute halt; after reopening it fell as far as **5,193.22 (-11.4%)**, the lowest since early November 2020, and closed at 5,195.81 (-11.39%, independently verified). Year to date it is down **38%**, among the world's worst major markets; foreign investors have net sold **$4.9bn** of Indonesian equities this year; the rupiah fell 0.5% to **18,035** per dollar and is about 8% weaker this year, Asia's worst currency; the 10-year Indonesian government bond yield was broadly unchanged.
- Additions: a 16:07 flash (after the window) showed the iShares MSCI Indonesia ETF down **11.2%** pre-market; Indonesia's regulator said between 15:00 and 15:06 that it had penalised three companies including Dian Swastatika Sentosa and Barito Renewables, but investors are still waiting for further action; FTSE Russell reaffirmed **emerging-market** status for Indonesia earlier this month, so the two index providers disagree.
- Impact: if the reclassification proceeds, passive funds tracking MSCI EM indices may be forced to sell Indonesian equities. Risk premia for low-governance EM assets go up; Indonesian domestic liquidity takes additional pressure.

**③ [Gap-fill · 10-15 22:33] IEA agrees to accelerate release of its remaining ~100mn barrels — the most direct driver of today's oil decline**

- Per Bloomberg as relayed: IEA members will accelerate release of the **~100mn barrels** left from the March plan. That collective action began in March with 400mn barrels planned, of which **330mn have been used**; EU officials noted that the up to 100mn barrels the G7 agreed in early October is "merely the unfulfilled part of the March commitment, **not additional supply**"; the US is informally lobbying for **at least another 100mn barrels**. IEA members hold about 1.1bn barrels of public emergency reserves (including over 200mn barrels of diesel), and the pending portion includes **more than 20mn barrels of diesel**.
- The same IEA monthly report: 2026 global oil **demand is expected to fall 2.6mn b/d**, with Q4 down 2.5mn b/d year on year; September global supply rose 2.5mn b/d month on month to 102.3mn b/d; observed inventories fell 95mn barrels in August and are down **507mn barrels since February**.
- Counter-evidence in prices: EIA data released on 10-16 at 00:00 showed crude stocks **-8.557mn barrels** (previous -3.186mn, the largest draw since February) and distillates -2.527mn barrels, with production down to 13.679mn b/d — yet crude did not rally. **Data note**: one Jin10 flash at 00:12 described the same release as crude inventories "rising more than expected", contradicting its own 00:00 print of -8.557mn barrels; this report does not use that headline.

**④ [This hour · 15:50, 15:56] MOFCOM and MOST propose revising the technology export list: 40 items across AI, biomedicine and solar PV, with immediate interim registration**

- **AI: 23 items** (large models, agents, compute clusters, information processing); **biomedicine: 5 items** (cell and gene therapy, small nucleic acid drugs, pathogenic microorganisms); **solar PV: 12 items** (polysilicon manufacturing, wafer manufacturing, cell manufacturing, plus the key core equipment that carries those technologies). Technologies already in the public domain through open source or published papers are excluded, and outbound licensing that does not transfer ownership qualifies for automatic licensing.
- Timing: **from the date of the announcement**, exporters must register on MOFCOM's unified platform before exporting the listed technologies, including exports already begun but not completed.
- At 16:04–16:05 (after sampling) MOFCOM's Q&A added that the AI scope is "precisely limited": **overseas localised deployment falls within the managed scope** (deployment on the firm's own overseas cloud and other controllable cases may qualify for automatic licensing), while **API calls, app/web interaction, SaaS applications and on-device lightweight deployment do not** fall under large-model technology export management.
- Impact: a new compliance cost for AI businesses that deploy locally overseas and for PV equipment exporters; relatively bullish for the domestic-substitution and self-sufficient-compute narrative. This is a **draft for public comment**; the final list and effective date are not yet settled.

**⑤ [This hour · 15:40] NDRC and NEA order accelerated restart and output ramp-up at Shanxi coal mines**

- The meeting required Shanxi to draw up "one mine, one plan" restart schedules, streamline restart verification, push temporarily idled mines back into production quickly and to full capacity, and raise output back to normal levels; it also called for daily monitoring, higher fulfilment rates on medium- and long-term thermal coal contracts, and a list of emergency supply mines. A separate NDRC meeting in Hami on 15:19 stressed energy supply security for this winter and next spring.
- Impact: a clear policy signal that **supply is being pushed toward expansion** — bearish thermal coal prices, supportive for fuel costs at coal-fired power and coal-chemical producers. Actual output and restart progress are not yet in the data, so nothing is quantified.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Call one: this combination of "stocks down, bonds up, dollar down, gold up" is buying growth and credit sustainability, not an inflation blow-off. The trade-off is: do not chase richly valued growth equities, prefer long duration and gold on a relative basis, and treat tonight's import prices and industrial production as the first hard constraint.**

Evidence: PPI at 6.3% y/y and +1.3% m/m, retail sales +0.8% — both independently verified with FRED; yet the 10-year Treasury yield fell from 5.19% to 5.089%, the dollar index fell to 101.81, and gold rebounded from $4,107.9 on 10-14 to around $4,200. Under an inflation trade, the long end and the dollar would be rising together. The credit side points the same way: per Jin10 item 232634 (10-16 09:26), even with hyperscaler tenants, prices on a number of AI data-centre bonds have fallen below par; and at 15:54 Jefferies warned that US capex plus AI infrastructure spending will reach 2.5%–3% of GDP over the next three years, larger than any infrastructure build-out in US history, and that a slowdown would hit both the companies involved and the wider economy. Falsification: if tonight's **20:30 US September import prices (forecast +8.1% y/y) and 21:15 industrial production (forecast +0.4%)** also beat, the long end can turn bear-steepener and the "buy duration as a hedge" trade gets repriced first, hitting growth equities and gold together. On gold itself: at $4,184 it is still about 21% below the January peak, a repair rather than a breakout.

**Call two: crude is being priced by policy on the supply side and by demand forecasts, not by the geopolitical headlines. Neutral to bearish near term, with volatility larger than direction; the "22nd sanctions package" or Lavrov's Iran trip is not a buy signal.**

Evidence: platform USOIL is -0.494% on the platform day, while NYMEX benchmarks show CL=F -2.31% and BZ=F -3.08%; at the same time bullish prints — EIA crude stocks down 8.557mn barrels and production down to 13.679mn b/d — produced no rally. Policy is spending its ammunition early: the IEA accelerating the remaining ~100mn barrels, the US pushing for at least another 100mn, and the IEA's own monthly report cutting 2026 demand by 2.6mn b/d. The genuinely tight link is diesel — over 20mn barrels of the pending reserve release is diesel, US diesel prices are surging, and Rubio is going out of his way to visit Reliance's 1.4mn b/d refinery — so the variable to watch is the diesel crack, not the absolute Brent price. Confirmation and counter-case: only a quantified production loss (duration of Hormuz transit disruption, damaged Russian refining capacity and recovery timeline) would upgrade this to a supply-interruption call; conversely, if the demand forecast is falsified and macro improves, low inventories would amplify upside — the main counter-case for the bearish view.

**Call three: Indonesia's -11.4% is an index-rule plus governance event, not evidence of a broad exit from Asian assets. Raise the risk premium on low-governance emerging markets; do not chase the Asian AI hardware names that have already rebounded.**

Evidence: today the KOSPI is +2.05%, the Nikkei +1.39%, the Hang Seng +0.52% and the Shanghai Composite +0.24%, with the memory and optical-communications names that were hit hardest yesterday recovering in A-shares and Hong Kong (Jin10 flashes 09:23–09:54). That contrasts with the 10-15 panic of "two Korean circuit breakers" and suggests yesterday's decline was mostly a position and valuation flush. Indonesia's magnitude is a different matter: down 38% this year, $4.9bn of net foreign selling year to date, the rupiah at 18,035, an MSCI consultation running into 2027, and FTSE Russell taking the opposite view. Affected assets: passive funds tracking MSCI EM may be forced to cut Indonesia, and Indonesian dollar bonds and the rupiah see higher volatility; the A-share policy news this hour (technology export controls, coal supply) is structural and does not change the index direction. Falsification: if the selling spreads from Indonesia into northbound flows and Korean/Taiwanese markets (for example consecutive MSCI EM outflows), the nature of the event upgrades to a regional liquidity event; and the 10-19 State Council Information Office briefing on "technology-driven commerce" may clarify the export-control list, requiring item ④ to be reassessed.
