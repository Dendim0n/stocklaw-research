## 1. The hour's main thread

**The previous brief argued the rally was risk-premium compression, not an earnings upgrade. This hour split that judgement in two: the broad indices held (Dow futures +0.1%), while one supply chain - AI hardware - sold off.**

The new pricing logic is a **regulatory discount**, not a demand downgrade. After the Democrats took both chambers, the results of 41 local data-centre ballot measures from Nov 3 were confirmed one by one (Ballotpedia: 46 of 47 decided favour restrictions), and Texas elected a governor who campaigned to "pause new data-centre construction pending stricter safeguards". Korea, home of the memory duopoly, took the hit first: KOSPI fell as much as 4.7%. Implications: bearish AI hardware (memory, second-tier compute, names dependent on new capacity); bullish Chinese internet and AI applications; bullish gold (weaker dollar plus hedging demand).

## 3. Material increments

**1) Korean memory majors crater; KOSPI triggers a sell-side sidecar | 09:16-09:22**
KOSPI opened -1.82% (5,580.09), fell to 5,418.15 (-4.67%) by 10:16 KST, with KOSPI 200 futures down 5.21% at 843.96; the exchange suspended programme selling for five minutes at 10:16:19 (the 56th time this year). Samsung Electronics -6.65%, SK Hynix -8.88% to -9.03% (its lowest since Oct 26), Samsung Electro-Mechanics -11.39%, KOSDAQ -4.57%. Foreigners were net sellers of KRW 930bn, while institutions and retail bought KRW 24bn and KRW 846.5bn respectively (Asia Business Daily, 10:13 KST). **Impact**: memory is the most upstream cash market in the AI capex chain; it selling first means the market is pricing "new data centres getting blocked", not "compute demand disappearing" - the Nikkei at only -0.34% and Dow futures still +0.1% confirm this is not systemic risk.

**2) The primary statistics on data-centre ballots are now out, firmer than last night's reports | confirmed Nov 4, traded this hour**
Ballotpedia: at least 50 local data-centre measures across 13 states in 2026, of which 41 were on the Nov 3 ballot; as of Nov 4, 47 had been decided and **46 favoured restricting development** (approving limits, or rejecting measures that would have supported development). The Washington Post counts at least 38 jurisdictions passing bans or restrictions; the Guardian counts at least 40 jurisdictions in 10 states, with 14 municipalities in Ohio alone. Texas is more pointed: Republican Governor Greg Abbott lost to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who advocated pausing new data-centre construction until stronger safeguards exist; Musk spent USD 474m personally (USD 224m of it in Texas) and won nothing (Jin10 09:58, citing CNBC's analysis of campaign-finance records). **Impact**: this explains why the first-day reading of the blue wave (uncertainty removed, tech broadly up) reversed within 12 hours - what is restricted is siting, grid connection and permitting, not demand. The beneficiaries of higher costs are players with existing campuses and grid allocations; the losers are second-tier names with a high share of new capacity.

## 4. Assessment

**A. AI hardware: this is a regulatory discount, not a demand falsification. The discount has only started, but day one is too early to call an industry top.**
The evidence is three independent prices: memory majors -7% to -9% in a day (the most upstream spot market), Nasdaq futures -1.3% against Dow futures +0.1% (internal to the chain, not systemic), and SOX already -3.13% on Nov 4 and -29.83% from its June high (valuation fell first). The counter-evidence is equally firm: the 46 of 47 ballot measures constrain siting and grid connection; hyperscaler capex has not turned - Microsoft completed a USD 25bn ten-tranche bond on Nov 4, Meta issued EUR 9bn, Google plans USD 15bn for a Malaysian campus (all in the previous brief). **Trade-off**: do not chase SOX short after -30%, and do not buy second-tier compute because "it has fallen a lot"; what actually benefits is assets with existing campuses, grid allocations and the balance sheet to sit through permitting cycles. This is a structural issue, not a directional one. **Invalidation**: if within two weeks a hyperscaler formally cuts its 2027 capex guidance (not merely delays a single project), "regulatory friction" escalates to "demand downgrade" and AI hardware exposure should be cut across the board.

**B. Gold: constructive, but do not chase above 4,100; this is a dollar-and-hedge move, not a real-rate move.**
Evidence: a break of 4,100 to a two-week high and +1.42% this hour, with the dollar -0.53%, 10-year yields -5.8bp and ETF holdings +2.851 tonnes, while the implied real rate at 2.64% barely moved - if this were a rate story, the real rate would be falling visibly. Counter-evidence: an 81.5% probability of a 25bp December hike (CME, relayed by Jin10 at 06:04) makes the high real rate a genuine opportunity cost; gold is still **22.5%** below its January high of 5,318.4 and silver **53.9%** below its January high, so the precious complex has not returned to a bull structure. **Trade-off**: existing longs can be held; new positions should wait for a pullback rather than chase. Tonight's 21:30 unit labour cost print is the first stress test (prior 1.2%, consensus 0.6%); a beat strengthens the hike narrative and raises the odds of a gold pullback. **Confirmation**: gold holding above 4,100 with three consecutive days of SPDR inflows would mark a trend rather than a bounce.

**C. Crude: de-escalation pricing has run ahead of the facts, domestic catch-up is done, and this is not a level to chase short.**
Evidence: offshore crude fell only 0.8%-0.9% this hour, decelerating from -8% overnight; domestic SC -10% and LU -12% are catch-up, not new information. The contrary evidence is intact: the Revolutionary Guard says it downed another US drone, Korea is extending its anti-piracy mission to near Hormuz with additional naval assets (09:40, Yonhap), a new round of US sanctions on Iran landed on Nov 4, and Trump said explicitly that he decides when the war ends. **Trade-off**: no directional bet, only conditions - if Friday's US-Iran talks produce no substantive text, a partial retracement is the base case; if loading is actually disrupted (observable via Hormuz transit volumes and the first cargo after Iranian loadings went to zero), the entire drop gets retraced and possibly overshot. **Invalidation**: if WTI breaks 85 and Brent 92 with no new supply-side news, the market is pricing "war premium to zero" rather than "de-escalation", and the lower end of the range needs re-examination.
