## 4. Assessment

**Judgement 1: The AI trade has changed from "unwinding in one direction" to "highly divergent." What is rising today is Korean memory's price elasticity, not confirmation of a global semiconductor upturn. Chasing this Asian spike here is unattractive.**

Evidence: while KOSPI ran +4.39%, the Nikkei — up 1.43% — was 0.63% below its intraday high, the ASX200 turned negative at -0.21%, the Shanghai Composite went from +0.14% to -0.09%, and the Hang Seng gave back its entire +1.14% open to +0.54%; no market broadened its gain as KOSPI accelerated. Korea's own drivers are "stock improvement": working-day-adjusted export growth of 65.9% is below September's 89.8%, and $61.6bn is below September's $71.4bn. What actually supports the move is Nomura's GDP upgrade (QoQ 0.1%→1.6%) and the low-base elasticity of memory.

Assets affected: Korean equities and the memory chain (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, SanDisk) have the most elasticity; for A-share semis this is sentiment spillover rather than fundamental transmission; for the US, the first up-day after SMH's nine-session losing streak needs confirmation tonight. **Confirmation**: the SOX and SMH close higher tonight on volume above the trailing 20-day average before this repair can be called anything more than Korean. **Invalidation**: if the Korean close at 14:30 gives back more than half of the +4% gain, this was short covering rather than allocation money.

**Judgement 2: Today's 1.3% in oil is pricing "the risk is still there," not "supply is being cut." Kpler's flow data caps most of the upside; upstream cash flow improves but this is not a trend to add into.**

Evidence: Middle East flows of 24.0 mb/d already exceed the pre-war 23.3 mb/d, Hormuz itself is back to 82% of pre-war, and 40% of non-Iranian crude already bypasses the strait versus 17% before the war. If supply were genuinely being cut, flows and prices would deteriorate together; they are moving in opposite directions. Prices are being held up by freight, war-risk insurance and crew bonuses (some owners are paying 2–3x monthly wages and up to $25,000 per voyage) — this is **cost push, not volume shortage**. Tuesday's large triple draw in API data went unpriced that day, which makes today's catch-up the more likely short-term trigger.

Assets affected: upstream oil and gas (cash-flow beneficiary) > oil services > airlines and chemicals (cost side remains under pressure). **Caps**: the IEA executive director has explicitly said more emergency reserves will be released if needed, and Witkoff meets Putin in St Petersburg on Wednesday to discuss an energy ceasefire. **Confirmation**: the 22:30 EIA report confirms a draw with Cushing turning down from +936k, and Hormuz flows fall below 82% of pre-war. **Invalidation**: substantive progress in the talks (lifting the Russian diesel sanctions or an energy ceasefire) or a specific IEA release volume — either one would erase today's ~$1 recovery first.

**Judgement 3: Gold holding 4,100 with 10-year yields at 5.12% is resilience, not strength; silver lagging gold says this is not a liquidity-driven precious-metals move.**

Evidence: gold at 4,119.18 (-0.14%), intraday low 4,106.19 and high 4,131.72, a range of only 0.62% for the whole window; silver -0.60%, down as much as 1.00% to 57.41 at 09:08, with the gold/silver ratio at **71.46**. Over the same period the 10-year Treasury yields 5.12% and the 2-year 4.79% (Tuesday's close, no update in this window) — real rates are not making room for gold. Silver has the larger industrial share, and its underperformance says the pricing comes from hedging and central-bank demand rather than expectations of easier liquidity.

Assets affected: gold miners' cash flow remains healthy at this quarter's average gold price, but there is no catalyst for multiple expansion; silver-linked names will be more volatile than gold, and gold's bull logic should not be used to trade silver. **Confirmation**: the gold/silver ratio breaking below 70 with gold above 4,131.72 (today's high) would mark a broad precious-metals turn. **Invalidation**: 10-year yields pushing through 5.20% while gold breaks 4,100 would mean the safe-haven premium is being eroded by rates.
