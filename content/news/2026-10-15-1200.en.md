## 1. The hour's main thread

The most important change this hour was Korea's market going from "falling" to **mechanically halted**: KOSPI and KOSDAQ both reached -8% and triggered first-tier circuit breakers in turn (the KOSPI halt was the tenth this year), the Nikkei fell 5.18%, Taiwan's weighted index 3.56%, mainland shares were broadly lower into the midday break and the Hang Seng lost 1.48% — while, in the **same hour**, long-dated government bond yields and gold rose and the dollar index fell 0.56%. The cross-asset combination is "risk-off", not a pivot to easing. The resulting stance: memory and compute hardware biased lower short term, long-duration bonds and gold relatively favoured, crude oil neutral (geopolitical risk is accumulating but price is not acknowledging it).

Relative to the previous issue, the real change is not the size of the decline — the Asian tech selloff was already under way last hour — but that **the credit market has begun pricing the funding cost of AI infrastructure**: CoreWeave unsecured bonds fell below 80 cents on the dollar with yields above 15%, and five-year CDS now imply a default probability above 50%. This is the first price-level credit alarm in this AI trade, and it is a different kind of event from a falling share price.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Call one: Korea's selloff is a positioning-and-valuation washout in the memory chain, not yet a reversal in aggregate AI capex — so the trade-off is "don't chase the short, don't catch the first bounce", with TSMC's 14:00 results and guidance today as the first hard constraint.**

Evidence: the decline is heavily concentrated in memory — KOSPI -8.02% and KOSDAQ -8.02% with a circuit breaker, against only -1.48% for the Hang Seng and -1.98% for the Shanghai Composite; the worst hit are leveraged long-memory products (2x long SK Hynix -23%). Meanwhile industry data point the other way: Counterpoint Research ranks YMTC third globally by NAND shipments in Q2 2026 (ahead of Kioxia and Micron), and Anthropic reported Q3 revenue of $16.8bn, up roughly 45% quarter on quarter, with an 85% adjusted gross margin and positive free cash flow. Prices are falling while cash flow and orders are not deteriorating in step — the signature of valuation and positioning, not fundamentals.

Impact and invalidation: memory and compute hardware are biased lower short term, but a trend decline is not yet confirmed; the relatively favoured side is whoever is not carrying AI capex — Apple rose about 0.5% on 10-14, **closing above a $5tn market capitalisation for the first time** and the only Magnificent Seven gainer, and the equal-weighted Magnificent Seven ETF has now outperformed the semiconductor ETF for eight straight sessions (Jefferies trading-desk estimate). If TSMC's guidance weakens or capex turns conservative at 14:00, the character changes from a valuation event to a fundamentals event, and the memory chain should be reclassified from "oversold" to "marked down" rather than averaged down.

**Call two: the real macro change this hour is "equities down, bonds up, dollar down" — what is being bought is growth and funding sustainability, not easing expectations; so long duration and gold stay relatively favoured, without chasing a gold breakout or shorting long bonds.**

Evidence: the 10-year Treasury yield broke below 5.10% (a three-week low) with the 5-year down 10bp, the 10-year JGB down 7bp, Australian 3-year yields down as much as 12bp and New Zealand 10-year yields down 4bp, while the dollar index fell 0.56% and equities fell. A genuine easing trade would show the front end and the dollar weakening together with growth stocks stabilising first, not value and gold being bought together. Three central banks contradicted each other at the same moment (the BOJ hawkish, the BoE denying the start of a series, the RBNZ "completely open"), so "synchronised global easing" does not hold.

Impact and invalidation: relatively positive for long duration and precious metals; negative for the front end and for highly leveraged assets sensitive to refinancing (see item ③). If tonight's **20:30 US September PPI (expected 5.3% y/y, +0.5% m/m; core 4.4%) and retail sales (expected +0.3%, core +0.5%) both beat**, the long end would turn bear-steepener, "buy duration as a haven" would be repriced first, and growth stocks and gold would come under pressure at the same time.

**Call three: crude's marginal price-setter sits on the rates and demand side while geopolitical risk accumulates unpriced — don't chase longs short term, and don't treat "escalation" as a buy signal.**

Evidence: platform USOIL and UKOIL fell 1.27% and 1.75% on the platform day, with the NYMEX basis pointing the same way; ANZ attributes that day's copper and aluminium declines to Fed tightening expectations, indicating the common factor for commodities was rates rather than supply. Yet geopolitics delivered three escalation signals at once (two ships sunk in Hormuz in 24 hours, Ukraine resuming strikes on Russian refineries, CNN reporting a new US strike plan on Iran), which should be oil-positive, and price did not follow. That is supply risk going unpriced, not a supply interruption occurring: without updated quantification of lost output and recovery time, it can only be logged as an upside source of risk premium. Countervailing variables also exist: Trump said explicitly there will be no attack on Iran before the midterms, and Washington had previously discussed easing "shadow fleet" sanctions and allowing Russian diesel in.

Impact and invalidation: crude is neutral short term, with the upside in volatility rather than direction. The decisive test is **the official EIA crude/Cushing/SPR inventory at 00:00 the next day (crude expected +1.90m barrels, prior -3.186m) and the diesel crack spread**; if geopolitics keeps escalating while oil refuses to rise, then rates and demand expectations are outweighing supply risk and this line is temporarily void.
