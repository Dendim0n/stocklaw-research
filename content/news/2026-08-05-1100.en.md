## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**1. Hormuz Strait Deal Imminent — Announcement Expected Wednesday**

According to Axios, the US, Iran, and Oman are close to reaching a temporary agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The US plans to announce the deal as early as Wednesday local time. The preliminary agreement would meet Iran's main demands regarding the strait. This marks a major turning point in Middle East geopolitics — upgrading from last hour's "positive progress" to "deal about to land." The news directly pressured oil prices, with SC crude down 6% and WTI falling to $73.9.

**2. US Restricts Tungsten Scrap and Battery Recycling Material Exports**

The US will ban exports of tungsten scrap and recycled battery materials later this month. This comes less than a week after President Trump authorized restrictions on critical materials. Tungsten is essential for cemented carbide and semiconductor manufacturing, and this ban will further tighten critical mineral supply chains.

## 🧭 Situation Assessment

If the Hormuz Strait deal lands on Wednesday, it will be a major geopolitical turning point — the geopolitical premium is rapidly exiting oil and shipping markets, with SC crude down 6% and container shipping down 8% as markets price in the outcome.

However, beware of "buy the rumor, sell the news" risk — the agreement is only a temporary arrangement, and Iran's leverage and execution commitment remain unknown.

The coexistence of a precious metals surge and crude oil plunge indicates the market is not simply risk-on or risk-off, but repricing supply chain security — the US restriction on tungsten and battery material exports is a new catalyst.

Critical mineral controls are expanding from import to export side, as the US builds a bidirectional supply chain barrier, posing medium-to-long-term pressure on China's high-end manufacturing and semiconductor material chain.

AMEC's evaluation by Samsung and SK Hynix is a milestone for Chinese semiconductor equipment going global — if it materializes, it could partially offset the impact of US equipment bans.

Bessent's direct intervention in the yen market breaks the tradition of US Treasury Secretaries not directly participating in forex operations, signaling that dollar system dynamics are entering a new phase.
