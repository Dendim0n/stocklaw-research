## 3. Assessment

**A. US equities and AI hardware: bearish short term, but this is "tech-only selling plus a governance discount", not a demand peak.**
Three facts hold simultaneously: Taiwan's October exports (+58.9%) and imports (+75.1%) show orders still accelerating, the opposite of the "AI demand has peaked" story; the decline is concentrated in tech — NQ −0.91%, ES −0.11%, YM +0.39% — and a genuine risk-appetite shift would not stratify the three futures like this; and both the board statement and the company line point to operations being unaffected with the CEO staying. **Impact**: the segments most tied to Nvidia (HBM, CoWoS, Taiwanese foundries, optical modules) see wider swings tonight, with semis more volatile than the S&P; the direction for Asian AI hardware tomorrow depends on tonight's US close, not today's Asian gains. **Trade-off**: do not chase semis short on futures prices before the open — event discounts usually repair faster than fundamentals change — but equally do not buy the dip before the investigation is clear. **Confirmation/invalidation**: if Nvidia closes within −2% and NQ reclaims 26,985 (last edition's sample), treat it as a completed event discount; if the investigation produces specific allegations of leaked secrets or affected supply, downgrade fundamentals rather than waiting for sentiment to recover. **Medium-term variable**: if the SoftBank report is confirmed, the financing side of AI capex becomes the real problem, and the repricing will not be limited to Nvidia.

**B. Gold: cut from mildly bullish to neutral, on three divergences this hour.**
First, gold fell 0.51% while silver was flat — if this were a change in haven demand, higher-beta silver should have fallen more. Second, the dollar was weaker over the same span (EUR/USD +0.32%) and gold got no currency support. Third, the domestic premium compressed to 0.21 yuan/g, meaning Asian physical buyers were not absorbing supply near 4,100. Together these point to gold's own longs de-risking rather than a macro variable flipping. **Impact**: gold miners and silver carry more beta than the metal, and miners often fall first when gold stalls; for tomorrow's CPI, gold's current level leaves no premium for an inflation surprise. **Trade-off**: do not buy above 4,100, and do not chase shorts above 4,070 — the US$30 in between is a noise band with no information. **Invalidation/confirmation**: a break below **4,070.7** (today's base and near Friday's close) fully erases today's +0.46% and voids the repair thesis; turning bullish again requires gold reclaiming 4,110 while silver holds and the dollar weakens. **Evidence gap**: VIX and intraday real yields were not obtainable here, so there is no way to distinguish a compressing risk premium from a rising cost of carry.

**C. Crude: bearish, but do not chase shorts at this level; wait for the range to resolve.**
The evidence structure is clear: supply is deteriorating (a Leningrad refinery hit, Iranian exports near a standstill, the Kurdistan gas field offline), yet price chops in a 96.3–97.1 band with the day still −1.40% — the war premium has been stripped out and liquidation (US$6.6bn of Brent exposure sold in a week, US$4.9bn of it long liquidation, CTA short-term momentum negative for the first time since August) still sets the price. **Impact**: upstream energy and oil-services earnings expectations stay under pressure; refiners, airlines and chemicals get cost relief; no effect on tomorrow's October CPI energy component, since the fall is in November prices. **Trade-off**: the move is already 17%+ (from about 118 on 30 October), and shorts are new rather than covering, so the risk/reward is worse than waiting for a bounce toward 98. **Confirmation/invalidation**: two consecutive Brent closes above **98.32** (Friday's settle) void the bearish call; downside confirmation needs consecutive closes below **96.33** plus further builds in this week's EIA inventories; if tonight's Trump–Qatar PM meeting produces a timetable for the "two-day plan", the first leg down may already be done.
