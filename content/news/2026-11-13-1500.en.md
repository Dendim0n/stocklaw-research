## 2. Key incremental items

**① Hormuz: Iran says an LPG carrier struck a mine; crude prints its intraday high at 14:45 | 14:28 → 14:45**
Iran's IRGC Navy said a tanker **carrying LPG** struck a mine and exploded in the Strait of Hormuz ([14:28](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113142838657800)). Attribution matters: this is a **one-sided statement by the attacking party's military**. A similar claim about "two tankers hit mines and caught fire" was already made at 02:32 today (see this day's 08:00 edition); what is new here is the **vessel type - an LPG carrier** - with no owner, insurer or third-party confirmation. Prices: Brent rose from 95.978 at 14:00 to **96.74** by 14:45 (day high; Jin10 platform 96.77), WTI from 88.616 to **89.09**; Shanghai crude主力 rose over 3%, fuel oil and low-sulphur fuel oil each +4% ([14:45](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113144557956800), [14:10](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113141027213800)).
Implication: Brent outperforming WTI, with the spread widening from 7.36 to 7.61, fits a "transit risk" signature rather than "lost barrels" - damage to an LPG carrier does not cut crude output, it raises voyage insurance and rerouting costs.

**② Counter-evidence: Asian refiners have resumed Middle East crude purchases | 14:45, article ID 235156**
Saudi Arabia, the UAE and other producers are gradually restoring exports, and Asian refiners are re-adjusting sourcing; the supply gap previously filled by US crude is being replaced by Middle East barrels ([ID 235156](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235156)). This points the opposite way to ①, and is consistent with last night's verified **EIA crude build of 4.657mn barrels** (consensus -1.109mn) and US output at a record 14.013mn b/d.
Implication: supply is normalising. The price is paying for "might be interrupted", not "already short". When both hold, the room for a geopolitical premium is set by the degree of event confirmation, not by inventories.

**③ "Bets on a 50bp Fed hike are rising" is a misleading headline: the 50bp is Waller's verbal possibility, the base case is still 25bp per move | 14:13, 14:28**
A Jin10 promotional piece in this window was headlined "bets on a 50bp Fed hike keep building" ([14:28](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113142838063800)). The verifiable framing is: **Fed Governor Waller hinted a 50bp hike is possible**, while SEB expects most officials to prefer "every other meeting, 25bp" - i.e. **+25bp in December and another +25bp in March** - and expects the 10-year yield to hover near 5% over coming weeks ([14:13](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113141322910800)). CME FedWatch prices **three cumulative 25bp hikes by next March**, taking the target range from 3.75-4.00% to 4.50-4.75% ([ID 235145](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235145)).
Implication: reading "50bp" as a single hike doubles the assumed duration shock. The correct read is a longer hawkish path, not a jump in per-move size. Direction (negative for high-multiple growth and gold) is unchanged, but the magnitude must be scaled down.

**⑤ The real reason behind SoftBank's $3.7bn charge: small gas turbines fell out of favour, 13GW of reservations cancelled | 14:32**
The FT reported that SoftBank's $3.7bn power-equipment charge stems from Masayoshi Son's bet on **smaller gas turbines**, as the industry shifted to larger, more powerful units, leaving that equipment unmarketable ([14:32](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113143250741800)). Attribution: **the $3.7bn charge itself was disclosed with earnings on 11-10** (alongside a ¥1.12trn quarterly net loss); today's increment is the cause. Per the WSJ citing people familiar with the orders, SoftBank had reserved more than **13 gigawatts** of gas turbines (mostly from GE Vernova), then cancelled the reservations it could not use.
Implication: this is the first hard evidence in the "AI power bottleneck" narrative of **over-ordering followed by write-down** - scrambling for equipment and getting the specification wrong. It is an order-quality issue for power equipment makers, and a reminder for data-centre operators that power-cost assumptions may be too high.

## 3. Situation assessment

**A | Crude: tactically bullish, but do not chase - this rally is transit risk, not lost barrels.**
Evidence: Brent +0.71% clearly beat WTI +0.15%, and the same-source spread widened from 7.36 to 7.61, concentrating the premium in waterborne crude. But four counter-points exist simultaneously: EIA data for the week to 11-06 showed crude **+4.657mn bbl**, gasoline +1.799mn, distillates +4.071mn, with output at 14.013mn b/d - a record for a third straight week; Asian refiners have resumed Middle East purchases; and Goldman Sachs' 2027 Brent base-case average is **$88**, more than $8 below spot. Affected: front-month crude, product cracks, tankers and war-risk insurance are tilted higher; airlines and chemicals face higher input costs. Invalidation and confirmation: if Saudi Arabia or a third party confirms actual loading disruption, "transit risk" upgrades to "supply risk" and this section is void; if WTI loses today's low of **88.04** (Sina `hf_CL` basis) and Brent breaks **95.06**, the premium is drained. Trade-off: do not open new longs here; existing length is better trimmed above 96.7-96.8 than added to.

**B | Discount rate: the path is turning more hawkish, but 50bp is a tail, not the base - do not chase duration-sensitive assets.**
Evidence: Waller floated 50bp verbally, while SEB and Goldman both use +25bp in December and another +25bp in March as the base case, and FedWatch already prices three cumulative hikes by March; the 10-year at 5.10% and 2-year at 4.86% are 11-12 official values (11-13 not yet published). The yen is the cleanest corroboration: even with BOJ hike expectations building (Sumitomo Mitsui's CEO personally expects two hikes to 1.75% before next summer), USD/JPY still touched **160.18**, showing that **the rate differential, not the BOJ, is in charge** - 160 is the zone where the Ministry of Finance has repeatedly jawboned before, and touching it is not a breakout; it had already retraced to 159.82 at sampling. Affected: Nasdaq, high-multiple software, unprofitable growth and gold are tilted lower; banks and insurers are relatively favoured. Invalidation and confirmation: US October PPI at 21:30 tonight, core MoM expected **0.3%** (prior 0.7%) - at or below 0.3%, this leg looks like pre-data front-running; at or above 0.5%, 5.10% is a starting point, not an end point. Trade-off: with Nasdaq futures narrowing from -0.44% to -0.11%, this bounce is better used to trim than to add.

**C | Precious metals and A-shares: gold is an oversold repair, not a reversal; A-shares are selling positioning, not demand.**
Evidence: gold bottomed at **3,889.96** this hour (14:47) and recovered to 3,902.49, up +0.65% on the day, retracing only a quarter of yesterday's -2.76%; silver +0.74% outran gold, a sign of leveraged-position repair. **Gold and oil rose in the same direction** (oil peaked at 14:45, gold bottomed at 14:47, then both rose), indicating a single pricing factor (geopolitics) rather than money rotating between the two - without positioning or flow data, one cannot write "haven bids are coming in". On A-shares: ChiNext closed only 9 points above its day low, CPO leaders fell 8-11%, but banks made new highs and Taiwan/Japan closed higher - a rotation into defence, not an exit. Affected: precious metals have room for a short-term repair but the trend has not turned; A-share AI hardware and property stay under pressure, banks relatively favoured. Invalidation and confirmation: if gold reclaims **3,923.05** (today's high; the 13:17 rebound to 3,918.95 also failed), the bounce upgrades to a reversal; if ChiNext's decline narrows inside 1% tomorrow and HS Tech stops falling, today was one-day deleveraging. Trade-off: neither short nor long gold, wait for PPI; do not bottom-fish A-share AI hardware, wait for Nvidia's 11-17 guidance.
