## 3. Key increments

**① [10:26, 10:35; 11:03] AI credit pricing is moving up: SoftBank CDS at 432bp, a four-year high, and BNP Paribas cuts its CoreWeave target by 30%**

- Jin10 citing institutional data: SoftBank's five-year CDS quote rose to **432bp** on Tuesday, the highest since early November 2022; the spread had broken through 400bp the prior week and has since widened more than 30bp further (10:26:12). The same item attributes it to concerns over AI financing and a potential large acquisition.
- Independent corroboration (different dates, same direction): S&P Global Market Intelligence data on 10-15 had SoftBank at **424bp** and Oracle five-year at a **record 288bp**; LSEG data had CoreWeave past **1,000bp**, with its 2032 notes around **77 cents** (FinancialMarkets / Securitas citations).
- 11:03:08 Jin10: **BNP Paribas cut its CoreWeave target from $169 to $117** (-30.8%). Note: CRWV closed at **61.12** (-0.44%), so the revised target is still about 1.9x the current price — this is a cut to expectations, not a call for the stock to fall to spot.
- Implication: the marginal cost of funding AI capex is rising, and the discount lands first on the thinnest balance sheets (neoclouds, lease-then-build campuses), not on the companies with the fewest orders.

**② [10:44, article ID 232918] Nscale and Anthropic discuss a second ~$45bn contract — but the money for the first one is not secured**

- Bloomberg citing people familiar: Nscale is negotiating with Anthropic over the **second building** at its West Virginia Monarch campus (460MW, Nvidia Vera Rubin), potentially similar in size to the **~$45bn** first building signed in August; if signed, Anthropic's purchase commitment to Nscale roughly doubles.
- The counter-clause (FT): **as of mid-September Nscale had not obtained binding financing commitments** for the first contract, and Anthropic may exit if financing is not secured by the agreed deadline; some state-level water permits and air-emission permits for the gas generation at Monarch are still outstanding.
- Scale: Nscale posted **$141m revenue and a $1bn net loss** in the first half; IPO valuation references are about $30bn (Bloomberg) and $35bn (FT).
- Implication: the orders are real, but construction and financing risk sit with the supplier — exactly what the credit market is pricing today. Separately, at 10:47:55 Jin10 reported (single source, "according to reports", unconfirmed by either side) that Nvidia won a CPU chip order from AI startup Modal, which had used only Intel and AMD — directionally a marginal shift in x86 server share.

- The core demand is a profit-sharing scheme at 15% of operating income; the company has offered a NT$1m bonus per employee.
- Implication: a one-day strike has limited output impact, but at a moment when HBM/DRAM is running full it is supply-noise; the real variable is the Taichung mediation covering 12,000 workers, not this one day in Taoyuan.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Judgement one: credit is the main battlefield of this AI correction, and the Asian equity bounce is not evidence that risk has cleared. Bearish neoclouds and leveraged compute infrastructure, relatively bullish on hyperscalers that self-fund.**

Evidence: SoftBank at 432bp, Oracle's record 288bp reading, CoreWeave past 1,000bp with its 2032 notes near 77 cents are three readings of one thing; BNP Paribas cut its CRWV target by 30.8% the same morning; and Nscale's first $45bn contract still lacks binding financing, with Anthropic retaining an exit right. Along this chain, the steeper a company's order growth, the more it depends on a market that is getting more expensive. **Trade-off**: do not chase the neocloud bounce; treat today's Asian and US rebounds as a window to switch exposure. If you want AI exposure, prefer names whose capex is covered by operating cash flow over names with the steepest orders — cash quality before growth story. **Confirmation / invalidation**: confirmation would be CRWV and ORCL underperforming the Nasdaq after the 21:30 open, or wider new-issue spreads on investment-grade AI debt this week; invalidation would be CRWV reclaiming its 10-19 open near 65.83 with CoreWeave paper back above 85 cents. **What is missing**: real-time CDS series and bond-by-bond prints are unavailable; only media-reported readings with different dates.

**Judgement two: the Asian equity move is a technical repair, not a return of risk appetite — Korea is still falling, Hong Kong is still weak, and A-share gains are small-cap themes.**

Evidence: KOSPI merely narrowed from -4.76% to -2.51%, and the -2.39% print at 11:00 was already weakening again; CSI 300 -0.30% diverges from ChiNext +1.02%; Hang Seng -0.84%; the Taiwan dollar at 32.588, the lowest since April 2025, is not confirming the equity bounce; foreign and institutional investors sold ~1.39 trillion won yesterday. **Trade-off**: today's Asian bounce is not worth chasing and suits trimming or switching; A-share humanoid-robot and CRO themes lack earnings validation, so participation needs explicit stop discipline. **Confirmation / invalidation**: if KOSPI closes above 5,700 at 14:30 with the Taiwan dollar back inside 32.4, the repair upgrades; if it breaks today's 5,493 low, this is confirmed as global deleveraging rather than sector rotation.

**Judgement three: gold's rebound is a technical repair under rate pressure, and silver's relative strength shows haven demand has not disappeared; crude's front-month support comes from longer transit, not a demand surge.**

Evidence: platform gold at 4,138.60 is up 0.56% from its 4,115.60 low (set in the previous hour) but still -0.05% on the day and has not reclaimed the COMEX prior settlement of 4,160.10; silver +0.49% and COMEX silver +0.45% clearly outpace gold; the Shanghai gold morning fix at 889.95 yuan/g is only 1.22 yuan above the international conversion, so there is no domestic physical premium — this decline is not caused by absent Asian physical buying. On crude, December WTI is +0.32% with 1.27bn barrels in transit near a record and only 90m barrels floating. **Trade-off**: neither short nor long gold here; let the 21:30 US session set direction. If you want the same macro expression, silver has more beta and deeper drawdowns, so size it smaller. Crude is not a chase at 98–99; wait for the 10-21 02:30 roll to complete before talking trend. **Confirmation / invalidation**: gold's invalidation is a reclaim of COMEX 4,160.10; crude's bullish upgrade condition is December above 99.00 with the 04:30 API drawing again (prior -11.123m barrels). Gold down and crude up describe relative performance only — **there is no flow data proving money moved from gold into crude**.
