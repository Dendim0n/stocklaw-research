🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**1. Hormuz Strait Blockade Enters 5th Month — US Navy Intercepts 20 More Vessels**
The US military has cumulatively intercepted 55 vessels heading to Iran, with 20 in the past week alone. ADNOC Gas CEO stated the company is closely monitoring the strait situation and studying alternative routes. Data shows the strait closure has caused Iraqi oil exports to plummet 75%.

**2. Trump Comments on Fed Rate Path**
Trump said whether Fed Chair Wash avoids rate hikes before midterms "depends partly on him, but the committee decides." White House economic adviser Hassett noted that excluding government employees and World Cup factors, job growth was ~100K, emphasizing focus on the unemployment rate.

**3. Yen Worst G10 Currency in August — Japanese Companies Call for Stability**
Yen gains from joint US-Japan intervention have faded, making it the worst-performing G10 currency in August. Japan's energy- and import-dependent economy is pressured by yen weakness, with companies collectively calling for exchange rate stability.

**4. US Private Credit Fund Defaults Rising**
Risk signals are accumulating in top US private credit funds, with exposure concentrated in healthcare and energy-sensitive sectors. The market is particularly focused on traditional software companies impacted by AI disruption, warning that a storm could detonate during an economic slowdown.

**5. Rhine River Flow Hits Record Low**
The Dutch water authority reported the Rhine River's average flow at the Lobith station dropped to 614 m³/s — the lowest on record. The situation is expected to persist in the near term, placing continued pressure on European inland shipping and industrial logistics.

🧭 **Situation Assessment**

The Hormuz Strait blockade continues to escalate, with US interception frequency clearly accelerating (20 vessels last week vs. 35 cumulative previously). Yet oil prices fell rather than rose, suggesting the market may have fully priced in the geopolitical risk premium.

Trump's remarks on the Fed were relatively restrained — no direct pressure, but emphasizing "the committee decides" implies acknowledgment of policy independence. Hassett's interpretation of employment data (~100K excluding one-off factors) leans dovish, suggesting labor market cooling.

The yen's trajectory warrants caution — rapid weakening after joint intervention signals insufficient market confidence in intervention efforts. Continued depreciation could trigger expectations of another round of intervention.

The historic low Rhine River flow is a hidden risk for the European economy, potentially exacerbating transport costs and supply chain pressures for European industrial goods.
