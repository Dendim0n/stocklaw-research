## Assessment

**A | A-shares: the index is propped up, the internals are breaking — +0.62% is not a recovery signal.**
Evidence: STAR 50 was held up by equipment and foundry (SMIC +6.59%, Naura +4.09%) while the three AI-interconnect names fell 3.9%–5.1%; ChiNext closed 2.17% below its high; main-force outflow of RMB 26.6bn and turnover down RMB 26.8bn to RMB 1.458tn. **Judgement**: this is intra-sector rebalancing — sell growth, buy equipment — not a revival of risk appetite. The "STAR 50 turns negative" condition did not trigger, but neither did it retake +1% (the other side of the 14:00 brief's test), leaving it between the two thresholds: that tells us about index weights (semiconductors), not about sentiment toward AI interconnect. Implication: bearish optical modules, CPO and AI servers; relatively strong semiconductor equipment and materials. **Invalidation / confirmation**: if optical modules stop falling and STAR 50 holds above 1,221 tomorrow, the rebalancing is near its end; if optical modules keep leading declines and equipment starts to crack, this shifts from "structural divergence" to "broad de-risking".

**B | Crude: no premium for the third time — pricing power sits with supply/demand and the dollar, not the battlefield.**
Evidence: the third claim against US facilities in Bahrain in five days, yet WTI rose only +0.22% this hour and Brent +0.03%; the full-day WTI range was 84.680–85.560 and it remains -0.74% versus the 11-16 settlement, while DXY at 103.59 sits near an 18-month high. In the BofA research cited by Jin10, crude and energy equities are among the few assets with a mild positive correlation to the dollar, but that is a long-run relationship and does not explain a single hour. **Judgement**: the market files these attacks under "known risk"; a premium requires verifiable shipping or production losses, and with Hormuz already closed the marginal information content of similar headlines is declining. Implication: energy equities lack catalysts; shipping insurance and freight are the faster channel. **Confirmation / invalidation**: Brent reclaiming 92.44 and WTI above 85.72 = the market is starting to pay for supply disruption; a break below 84.68 = the focus returns to demand and the dollar.

**C | Gold and the dollar: spike-and-fade plus a strong dollar — tactically bearish, but not a collapse call.**
Evidence: gold peaked at 3,886.68 at 13:34 and faded, -0.19% this hour and -0.28% over two hours, now 3,870.50 — 0.42% below the day high and 0.70% above the day low; DXY at 103.5852 has essentially recovered yesterday's decline, with EUR/USD at 1.0993 near its 52-week low. Institutions and physical demand diverge: Morgan Stanley sees 5,250 by end-2027 (+35%), while Chinese jewellery prices have fallen below RMB 1,000/g and the Shanghai gold midday price carried only a RMB 1.7/g premium to international gold (Jin10 15:28). **Judgement**: the near term is driven by the dollar and real rates; geopolitics no longer supplies incremental buying. The medium-term bull case stands but lacks fresh flow evidence. Implication: gold miners and silver (silver -0.17% this hour, still +1.21% on the day). **Confirmation / invalidation**: a US close above 3,880 = the fade is over and the breakout restarts; a break of today's low **3,843.56** = the rebound is void.
