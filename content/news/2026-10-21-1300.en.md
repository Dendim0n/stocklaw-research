## 3. What actually changed

**① [12:28 / 12:18 / 12:05; 12:40 article 233033] Crude sheds its war premium while diesel sits at a record**

- Crude: FT reported at 12:28 that Netanyahu instructed the military this month to draw up a new large-scale strike package, originally slated for the weekend of 10/10, targeting power plants and nuclear sites, and that **the operation was called off because Trump opposed restarting the conflict before the midterms**. White House deputy spokesperson Kelly said "there will be no US attack on Iran before the midterms" and described the bid to blockade Iranian ports and push Gulf oil out through the strait as "working." Rubio said at 12:18 that envoy **Witkoff is en route to St Petersburg** (one of the invalidation conditions we set for crude last issue); Vance said at 12:05 that Ukraine's strikes on Russian refining are raising US energy costs and should stop.
- Diesel: AAA put Tuesday's US retail diesel average at about **$6.32/gallon**, a record high. Energy Secretary Wright said the administration is discussing "reopening as many refineries as possible" but conceded not all can restart; API CEO Sommers said refinery utilisation is already at **95–100%** of capacity, with some operators deferring maintenance, and that new capacity takes months to years. Washington is working with **India** to lift its refining output and consulting **Europe**, has temporarily waived part of the Russia diesel sanctions this month (Wright conceded those barrels are more likely to land in New England), and its push for Europe to release 50m barrels of diesel fell short; the IEA says it stands ready to coordinate further releases (it plans to accelerate the release of more than 20m barrels of diesel).
- Takeaway: the headline is hawkish, the content is near-term de-escalation — **the geopolitical premium is being compressed, not accumulated**; and with refinery runs already maxed, diesel cracks and refining margins are a higher-conviction exposure than outright crude. <https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233033>

**② [12:15 / 12:29] CATL Q3 unit profit hits a record-low Rmb82/kWh; Nomura 632→408, UBS 820→730**

- UBS: Q3 net profit **Rmb21.2bn** (+15% y/y, -6% q/q), about 10% below buy-side expectations; volumes above **260GWh** (storage ~25%); unit profit down to **Rmb82/kWh**, a record low, attributed to product mix, more low-value-added projects, the lithium-battery consumption tax from September 1, and the export VAT rebate cut from 9% to 6%. 2026–28 net profit forecasts cut 11–14%; target HK$820→730, Buy maintained.

- Takeaway: both brokers point the same way — **more volume, lower price**. This is a structural step-down in margin, not a one-quarter wobble. Bearish the battery chain, a cost tailwind for automakers.

**③ [12:30] BOJ Deputy Governor Himino: upside inflation risk, real rates still low — yet the long end rallied**

- Seven consecutive remarks: underlying inflation is "fairly close" to 2%; medium- to long-term expectations are rising; **upside risks to prices are high**; **delaying policy adjustment could bring inflation risk and damage the economy**; real rates remain low and bank lending is growing fast; AI demand is lifting capex and exports.
- Market: 30-year JGB yield **3.920%, down 2.0bp**; USD/JPY 158.06 (-0.04%). Takaichi said at 12:45 that anti-inflation measures are "not fully working yet," and the government is considering winter electricity and gas subsidies (Kyodo, 12:35).
- Takeaway: hawkish language with a long-end rally means the market is buying duration rather than trading faster tightening — **the yen has stopped responding to hike rhetoric**, an anomaly worth recording this hour.

**④ [12:13] Four dead at TSMC's Kaohsiung fab; similar steel work halted island-wide**

- The company confirmed four workers died during steel-structure work (local media say they fell while welding steel beams), has suspended construction at the Kaohsiung site, **halted similar steel-structure work at all its Taiwan fabs**, and is cooperating with the investigation. It is the worst accident at the site since construction began.
- Takeaway: the deaths were already reported in the 11:00 issue; **what is new is the formal statement and the island-wide scope of the stoppage**. No direct impact on current output (this is build-out phase), but a prolonged probe would affect the pace of future fab construction — another data point for the "AI capacity ceiling sits in civil works and grid connections" thread.

## 4. Reading the tape

**Call one: policy is actively deflating crude's war premium — don't chase it. Refining cracks point the other way, and the two should not be bet in the same direction.**

Evidence: WTI -0.46% and Brent -0.44% on the hour, and the trigger was not supply data but three policy events pointing the same way — the White House ruling out an attack before the election, Witkoff departing for St Petersburg, and Vance demanding Ukraine stop hitting Russian refineries. On the same day the physical side was officially acknowledged as tight: retail diesel at a record $6.32, refinery runs at 95–100%, "reopening refineries" facing hard constraints. Outright crude carries a geopolitical option; refined product carries a capacity constraint. The two pricing factors diverged this hour.

Implications: short-term risk/reward in upstream oil and crude ETFs deteriorates; refining, diesel cracks and product tankers are relatively favoured; the cost squeeze on airlines and chemicals is unresolved. **Confirmation**: tonight's EIA shows refinery utilisation holding above 93.5% with distillate stocks drawing further. **Invalidation**: utilisation falls materially or distillate stocks build unexpectedly, or the Witkoff–Putin talks produce real progress (Russian diesel sanctions actually lifted) — in which case the retracement accelerates and the supply-side support for refining margins weakens with it.

**Call two: "the impulse faded" is the wrong summary for Asia — A-shares are weakening while Korea is repairing, and the two have decoupled.**

Evidence: last issue's "KOSPI's impulse is not a trend" was partly overturned this hour — KOSPI went from 5,695.05 (+2.56%) back to **5,710.25 (+2.84%)**, while A-shares kept sliding into the afternoon (Shanghai -0.46% near its low, CSI 300 -0.81%, ChiNext -1.25%) and the Hang Seng sat at 23,765.50, 15 points off its low. The split is compositional: Korea is driven by memory and AI hardware (Montage's Hong Kong line +8% is corroborating evidence), whereas the A-share drag is the battery chain (CATL down 10% in both Hong Kong and Shanghai) and new energy — an **industry event, not a liquidity event**.

Implications: China and Hong Kong equities soft near term, especially batteries, lithium and storage integration; Korea's memory complex is more resilient than we credited last issue but still sits at +2.84%, 1.83% below its high — poor odds. **Confirmation**: Korea holds above +2.5% at the 14:30 close and Shanghai stays above 3,700 at 15:00, and "Korea strong, China weak" stands. **Invalidation**: Korea gives back to within +1.5% late, or Shanghai reclaims today's 3,740 open in the afternoon.

**Call three: gold's range is structural — central-bank buying floors it, real rates cap it, and chasing a breakout inside the range is a low-probability trade.**

Evidence: gold was -0.08% on the hour and -0.25% on the day, ranging inside a $25.5 band between 4,106 and 4,132 despite a heavy risk-news hour, showing no safe-haven response. Goldman's October 19 Precious Metals Comment (relayed by Jin10 at 12:30) supplies the framework: official-sector buying was about **79 tonnes** in August, a seasonally adjusted three-month average of roughly **85 t/month** — about five times the pre-2022 baseline — with China the largest identifiable buyer; but the report is explicit that central-bank demand "is more likely to change the structure of demand and how gold behaves in a selloff than to guarantee sustained short-term upside." One detail worth noting: in August, foreign central banks' gold held at the New York Fed fell about 40 tonnes while custody holdings at the Bank of England rose about 46 tonnes.

Implications: gold neutral near term; range trading beats directional bets, and the valuation ceiling on long-duration bonds is still capped by the December hike consensus. **Confirmation**: the 10-year yield falls meaningfully below 5.12% while gold breaks 4,131.72, which would show private money taking over pricing. **Invalidation**: the dollar and yields rise together and gold breaks 4,106.19 — then central-bank buying only slows the decline rather than stopping it.
