## 4. Read on the situation

**Judgment 1: today's move is sector rotation plus short covering — short-term bullish, but only into the close, and not a level to chase at intraday highs.**

The structure and the magnitude are both clear: the Shanghai index went from -2.29% back to +0.01%, ChiNext from -3.21% to +2.53% (+5.93% off its low), STAR50 from -3.72% to +1.23%, all three indices were green by 13:25, and the key 3,617.60 level has been reclaimed. But the internals run the other way — banks fell across the board (BoC -4%) while brokers, fintech, humanoid robotics, semiconductors and HK AI applications (MiniMax +12%, Zhipu +7%) led. This is not "more money," it is "money in different places." Heavy ETF turnover (three broad-based ETFs past RMB 10bn, ChiNext ETF at 14.2bn) and nearly 2,300 advancers are evidence of absorption, and the RMB 1.53trn combined turnover at 14:06 shows no volume decay.

The counter-evidence is just as solid: the window **produced no new policy facts** — the three items at 13:04 (Industrial Securities' Wang Han), 13:29 (Huachuang's Zhang Yu) and 13:56 (Everbright's Wang Kai) are all broker commentaries, and the last of them cites a speech by CSRC chairman Wu Qing "in recent days" at the 2026 Financial Street Forum, which did not occur inside this window and is old news re-interpreted. The bounce lacks a verifiable new catalyst; at 14:06 the Shanghai index was just 0.34 points above the 3,658.03 prior close, i.e. flat, with the entire gain coming from position covering within ninety minutes.

On assets: small-cap growth and HK tech have the most upside elasticity and the most fragility; heavyweight banks are the seller in this rotation, and "banks falling" should not be read as a failure of support — it looks more like the unwinding of a crowded trade. The trade-off is **not to chase the first push after 14:00 and to wait for the 15:00 close**: trading above a level intraday is not the same as closing above it.

Confirmation and invalidation: a 15:00 close above **3,658.03** with turnover sustained → the reversal holds and the deleveraging is close to done; a close back below **3,617.60** → one-day wonder, and the framing reverts to "leveraged deleveraging incomplete."

**Judgment 2: oil's return above 100 came after three "more supply" headlines — that is a rebuild of geopolitical expectation, not a physical shortage. Neither chase the upside nor short it.**

Evidence: CL 100.372 (13:15) → 100.898 (14:06), +0.52%, 0.07% below the 10:05 high of 100.970; Brent 113.984 → 114.504, 0.19% below its 114.720 high; over the same span spot gold went 4,006.38 → 4,017.16 (+0.27%) and silver 53.738 → 54.148 (+0.76%). All three moved the same way, which says this hour was a broad rebuild of geopolitical premium rather than oil-specific strength. More important are the 13:58 industry items: Indian imports from the Middle East restored to pre-war levels, the Russia-US diesel deal about to take effect, Saudi expanding its east-west pipeline, and Nord Stream restart called "inevitable" — **three of four add supply**, yet the price rose. That only holds together when the market is pricing expectations rather than inventories. The 13:59 feature was merely a repost of the 12:51 FT story, with no new facts.

A second piece of counter-evidence comes from agriculture: wheat has fallen eight weeks running, the longest streak since 1997, as the Black Sea premium fades on peace-talk expectations. Energy adding premium while agriculture removes it does not add up to re-accelerating inflation, so this oil move cannot be used to argue for a steeper Fed path.

On assets: upstream already carries the premium and the risk/reward on chasing is poor; downstream (airlines, chemicals) cost pressure is unresolved, and yesterday's FT line that "tickets won't get cheaper even if oil falls" is airlines defending margins, not strong demand; tanker upside comes from route disruption and freight, independent of crude direction. The stance is **hold existing positions, add on neither side**.

Confirmation and invalidation: CL above **100.970** with freight rising in tandem → the premium is re-expanding and the "expectation rebuild, not new event" call is void; below **98.840** → the second leg of premium give-back is confirmed. A verifiable production or loading loss → upgrade from "premium" to "supply gap," and the logic inverts.

**Judgment 3: the pressure on long-end yields is shifting from the US to Europe while the US turns marginally more dovish — and the two forces cancel out in the dollar, which is why FX has confirmed nothing.**

Evidence: French 10-year at 5.039% (+4bp) and 5-year CDS through 100bp, layered on weekend troop deployment; UK September retail -0.4% m/m, +0.8% y/y (prior revised to 2.7%) and core -0.7% m/m are hard evidence of a weakening consumer. On the US side, Jin10's 13:50 roundup said "traders no longer fully priced in a Fed hike before year-end" — **no pricing venue or specific meeting was named and no probability was obtained, so it is used for direction only, never quantitatively** — but the meaning is clear: if that direction holds, it is the first-hand signal of a marginal dovish turn. On Japan, September core inflation exceeded 2% for the first time in nine months (11:20), the largest union federation is seeking a 6% wage hike for 2027, and corporate loan demand is strong (13:50), reinforcing December hike expectations.

Yet the result: DXY at 101.6788, almost exactly the 101.6807 seen at 13:15 and only 0.02% from 101.6592 at 12:00; EURUSD 1.1211 and USDJPY 156.886 are both within 0.1%. **Bonds and equities are telling two different stories and FX refuses to price either** — that is the divergence worth watching most right now.

On assets: French government bonds and European banks are exposed, and the euro lacks upward momentum; the yen has support but is already expensive (today's high 157.118) and carry-trade risk is rising. The trade-off is **not to be long European duration, and not to short the yen at 156.9** — both directions are already at the crowded end.

Confirmation and invalidation: weekend protests smaller than last week's benchmark (3,000 shops looted, 2,700 arrests) with the OAT back below 5% → French sovereign-risk pricing de-escalates; DXY above 101.72 (today's high 101.7198) with USDJPY through 157.12 → the "FX confirms nothing" state ends and risk assets must be repriced for a stronger dollar.
