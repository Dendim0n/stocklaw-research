**1) Middle East physical crude loosens for the first time since July; Dubai spread flips to contango | reported 13:44 (facts are from early this week)**

- Dubai crude's front-month intermonth spread turned to **contango**, the first time since July; traders put the **November contract about $2/bbl below December**. This is the pricing basis for nearly all crude exported from the region ([article 234173](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/234173), citing Bloomberg, Kpler, Vortexa and S&P Global Energy).
- October exports from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iraq and Kuwait rose about **10%** to **15.53 mb/d**, essentially back to February's pre-war level. Saudi **7.0 mb/d (+13% m/m)** and Yanbu port 3.07 mb/d (+35%); Iraq 3.35 mb/d (+9.5%), UAE 3.74 mb/d (+50 kb/d), Kuwait 1.44 mb/d (about a third higher).

## 4. Judgement

**Judgment 1: The physical market has already produced evidence of a supply turn. This crude rally is a geopolitical-premium refill, not a trend reversal - do not chase it, and do not short here either.**

The evidence cuts both ways. On the physical side: Dubai's spread is in contango for the first time since July with November about $2 under December; the four Gulf exporters are back to pre-war volumes with Saudi up 13% m/m; and Iran's two months of zero loadings still did not stop total exports from rising - which means the increase came from the other four, not from Iran's absence. Aramco itself concedes volumes fell in Q3. On the geopolitical side: the refinery hit by 124 drones and Iran's comment about a wider war are the only attributable events behind this hour's rise, but a refinery strike damages refining capacity rather than crude output, and the scale was not disclosed. **Implication**: neutral on crude - medium-term bearish because supply is normalising, short-term bullish only on tail risk, the two roughly cancelling near $96. Chasing longs is poor odds; shorting requires waiting for the geopolitical premium to bleed out first. **Invalidation**: if Dubai's spread returns to backwardation, or Brent's spread to WTI widens rapidly from 8.352, the market is re-pricing Middle East risk and the medium-term bearish view should be withdrawn.

**Judgment 2: The A-share move has gone from "hardware rotation" to "spreading into the broad index", so last hour's neutral call gets cut; but it is not yet a broad selloff.**

The evidence is four levels widening together: STAR 50 -3.51% to -4.99%, ChiNext -1.74% to -2.94%, CSI 300 -0.52% to -0.98%, Shanghai Composite -1.12% to -1.54%, with CSI 300 at 3,977.43 just **0.20%** above today's low of 3,969.55. TAIEX closed **-3.59%** and both IC and IM front-month futures fell 2%, which says selling has reached index derivatives rather than a handful of AI names. The "Hong Kong hedge" that supported last hour's bullish tilt is also fading: Hang Seng Tech narrowed from +0.78% to +0.13% and the Hang Seng from +0.46% to +0.23%. Counter-evidence remains: Hong Kong consumer names rallied in the afternoon (Haidilao +5%), A-share hog names were strong, and CSI 300 is still the best of the major indices. **Implication**: A-share broad index **neutral-bearish**, AI hardware **bearish**, Hong Kong **neutral**. Buying into the AI-hardware slide while it is still spreading is not justified, but the broad index is not a trend short either. **Confirmation**: if CSI 300 closes below 3,969.55 at 15:00 with the Hang Seng turning negative, the "rotation" call is void and it becomes a broad selloff; if CSI 300 recovers above 4,000 into the close and STAR 50 finishes better than -4%, it was intraday panic and the broad index can go back to neutral.

**Judgment 3: Gold is retesting the 30 October low. What is missing is a turn in real rates, not haven demand; neither buy the dip nor chase the short here.**

Three points. First, position: 3,918.72 is only **1.08%** above the 30 October swing low of 3,876.89, and today already printed a new low of 3,916.03 - a low-level retest, not a pullback from a high. Second, silver has underperformed gold for a third straight hour with the ratio at 79.69 (77.96 at Monday's close) - the more industrial metal is falling harder, so the pricing is happening on the demand side, not the monetary side. Third, risk assets are falling alongside, not instead: Nasdaq-100 is **10.36%** below its 6 October high of 31,224.69 on my own calculation from Tencent's US daily series, a technical correction, and gold is falling at the same time - so what is being sold is not the "risk appetite" label. The 13:55 round-up from the HKMA summit laid out the split: the new additions were Bain's Connaughton calling it "a bubble that may eventually burst", Franklin Templeton's Johnson warning that platforms are shifting from asset-light to capital-heavy, and Oaktree's Marks calling AI "an overly crowded trade"; PIMCO's Roman ($10trn over five years, ultimately beyond what the market can absorb) and the bullishness of Goldman's Solomon and Morgan Stanley's Pick ($10-15trn of capex) were already reported and are carried forward only ([article 234174](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/234174)). **Implication**: precious metals **neutral-bearish**; the thing to wait for is a turn in the 10-year TIPS real yield, not softer central-bank language. Silver's industrial beta makes it a poor haven substitute in this regime. **Invalidation**: if gold holds 3,876.89 and reclaims Monday's close of 3,946.47 (a 0.70% move), the retest has succeeded and the bearish call is withdrawn; below 3,876.89 the only reference is 3,800, a psychological round number, not a technical support.
