## 🔥 Key News This Hour

**🛢️ Middle East: Iran Initiated the Ceasefire Proposal**

According to the Jerusalem Post, it was Iran — not mediators — that first proposed the 10-day ceasefire currently being pushed by negotiators. This reverses the prior market narrative that mediators were driving the initiative. Iran's move may signal battlefield pressure or a tactical pause.

**🚀 Jordan Shoots Down 5 Iranian Drones, Strait Remains Paralyzed**

Jordan's armed forces confirmed intercepting five Iranian drones and pledged continued airspace monitoring. Ship tracking data shows zero vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz in the past 24 hours — the most severe disruption since the conflict began.

**💰 Goldman: Brent Could Hit $120 If Strait Stays Blocked**

Goldman Sachs warns that if the Hormuz disruption persists, Brent crude will surge above $120/bbl in Q4. Commonwealth Bank of Australia assigns a 55% probability that the war continues at current intensity for at least two more months.

**🇺🇸 War Costs Spiraling, Defense Secretary Heads to Congress**

As U.S. strikes on Iran enter day 10, the Trump administration's $87.6 billion supplemental war funding request faces intense congressional scrutiny. Defense Secretary Hegseth will testify before the Senate.

**📈 Korea KOSPI Rebounds 3.6%, Leveraged Selling Eases**

After plunging 28% from its all-time high, the KOSPI bounced 3.6% on Tuesday as forced liquidation of leveraged positions subsides. Citi maintains its 10,000-point target.

**🏭 China Railway Wins 56 Billion Yuan in New Contracts**

China Railway Group announced major project wins across railways, highways, municipal works, and overseas projects totaling approximately 56 billion yuan (~5.14% of 2025 revenue).

**💾 German DDR5 Memory Prices Hit New Record, +7% MoM in July**

Retail DDR5 memory prices continue climbing, with the German price index rising 7% MoM in July to a new all-time high, reflecting sustained AI-driven demand on hardware supply chains.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

Gold breaking $4,070 is a concentrated release of safe-haven demand — Iran volunteering a ceasefire actually signals mounting battlefield pressure.

Zero vessels through the Strait of Hormuz in 24 hours is the most dangerous signal this week; tanker attacks have escalated from isolated incidents to systematic blockade.

Goldman's $120 oil warning is not alarmist — if the strait disruption persists another week, the second wave of inflation shifts from expectation to reality.

Korea's 3.6% bounce suggests the forced-liquidation phase may be ending, but recovering from a 28% drawdown takes time — caution on dip-buying.

Silver's 4.6% surge outpacing gold reflects both safe-haven and industrial demand — war drives energy and defense metal pricing.
