## 4. Read on the situation

**Judgement one: this A-share decline is a liquidity problem, not a valuation problem; a rapid drop on shrinking volume means "nobody is bidding", not "selling is done", and this is not a tape to buy on price alone.**

Evidence: STAR50 −4.81% to 1,252.28, down 37.7% from its 7-14 close; CSI500 −3.00% to 6,511.42, down 21.3% from 8,275.94 — both the lowest closes since 13 July. The SSE Composite settled at its intraday low of 3,648.30 and broke its 10-22 close, while volume ran at 0.94x the prior seven-session average and STAR50 volume fell 10.6% — **the decline widened without volume expanding**. At the same time KOSPI rose 3.09% (SK Hynix +5%), the Nikkei rose 1.73% and Nasdaq futures fell only 0.46%, so there is no evidence the external environment deteriorated; A50 constituents closed with "most in the red but banks bucking the trend" (Jin10 chart, 15:03), which also supports a structural withdrawal of funds rather than a broad risk event. Implications: dividend, large financials and utilities keep the relative edge; STAR board and high-beta themes have no case for adding. Large caps are not an "opportunity" — their relative strength comes from passive concentration of flows, not from earnings upgrades. Counter-evidence and invalidation: nearly RMB 24bn of weekly inflows into STAR50 ETFs (as of 10-26) is real demand; if STAR50 reclaims its 10-26 close of 1,315.50 tomorrow on volume at least 1.3x today's, this call is downgraded. If CSI500 breaks today's low of 6,511.37 and STAR50 breaks 1,249.76, a second leg is confirmed and there is no support level within three months to cite.

**Judgement two: oil up and gold weak at the same time does not prove "money moved from gold into crude" — the evidence supports two independent stories: oil being pushed higher near the top of its range by geopolitics and a firm product complex, and gold being underweighted.**

Evidence: USOIL 101.191 (+0.63%, day high 101.342), +1.01% over the hour, but on the CL basis it had already gone from 100.94 at 14:07 to 101.74 at 14:44, with only about another $0.4 added after the flash. Gold went 4,005.60 → 4,005.45 (about 0.00%) over the hour, with a spot low of 3,997.27; COMEX December minute data put its intraday low of 4,009.87 at **14:48** — **no bids for gold after the 14:45 headline**, the opposite of a haven bid. Silver −2.06% with a new intraday low of 52.663 and a gold/silver ratio of 75.8 shows precious metals being underweighted as a group, not rotated into. Position: CL=F's three-month range is 75.22–105.83 and the current price sits near the **87th percentile**; gold has closed below 4,000 on only one day in six months (3,992.1 on 7-16) and is **15.2%** below its 5-8 high of 4,730.7. Implications: chasing crude long here is poor odds, and an unconfirmed supply headline is noise — the real answer is tomorrow's API inventory. Gold's 4,000 is a psychological level, not technical support, and has become a churn zone, so there is no basis for directional adds. Invalidation: crude needs to clear 105.83 (the three-month high) for a new pricing regime; two consecutive gold closes below 4,000 would extend the drawdown from May, but **SPDR Gold Trust holdings were not obtained this run, so we cannot tell whether ETF redemptions or futures selling dominate**.

**Judgement three: AI's financing structure keeps hardening — vendors are underwriting their customers' equity raises, which changes the judgement on earnings quality, not the judgement on orders.**

Evidence is terms, not narrative: at 13:53 Nvidia confirmed a $3bn investment in Meta's equity offering (company statement) and same-window expansion of Vera Rubin, Vera CPU, Spectrum-X and BlueField-4 deployments; Meta's round is $25bn of common stock at $625/share with a $95bn total cap (reported last hour), and of the three vendors that had signalled up to $5bn of non-binding intent, Nvidia alone has now committed $3bn. The two ends must be read separately: for NVDA it locks in demand and ties the customer's balance sheet into its own shareholder base; for META it enlarges the denominator and deepens dependence on a single supplier. Implications: valuation for the mega-cap platforms should migrate from "EPS growth" toward "free cash flow after dilution and interest", while vendors (packaging, memory, equipment, power) genuinely gain order visibility. Confirmation and invalidation: tonight's SK Hynix results (after the US close) and this week's platform capex guidance test both ends; if NVDA and META both fall after the US open, or META keeps sliding on heavy volume below the $625 pricing level, the market has begun discounting circular financing; if both rise, the market is still pricing orders, and this item's bearish weight on the buyers is downgraded.
