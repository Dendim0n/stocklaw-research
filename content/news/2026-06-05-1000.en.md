🔥 **Key Headlines This Hour**

1. **Japan sells record foreign securities to defend yen** — Japan offloaded $75.6 billion in foreign securities in May, an all-time record, revealing the "ammunition" behind its yen support efforts. The yen touched 160 for a third consecutive session. PM Takaichi said she hopes to build an economy resilient to FX volatility but denied policy was aimed at direct intervention.

2. **Norway oil strike crisis averted** — The Norwegian Safe union reached a wage agreement, cancelling a threatened strike that had raised concerns about North Sea crude supply disruption.

3. **Iran's Kharg Island resumes crude loading** — Maritime analytics firm Windward satellite imagery shows Iran's key oil export hub Kharg Island has resumed loading operations, with a VLCC moored at the western terminal. This eases concerns about Iranian crude export disruption.

4. **Korean stocks crash, circuit breakers triggered** — Kospi fell over 6% in early trading, led by SK Hynix. The index had gained 105% YTD but was critically dependent on just two companies — the "two-stock dependency" is now backfiring.

5. **Fed's Daly: prepared for both directions** — SF Fed President Daly said the Fed should not offer clear rate guidance given uncertainty, and is prepared for both rate hikes and cuts.

6. **Blackstone limits redemptions on $79B credit fund** — Blackstone's flagship private credit fund has restricted investor redemptions for the first time, following Swiss asset managers who also limited redemptions, hitting US private equity stocks. PIMCO CIO previously warned of credit market risks.

🧭 **Market Assessment**

Japan deploying record-scale foreign asset sales to defend the 160 line underscores how stretched the defense is — the market keeps probing the government's willingness and capacity to intervene.

Blackstone's redemption gate is a concrete manifestation of liquidity stress in private credit; if it spreads, it could be an early warning signal for broader systemic risk.

Norway strike resolution is a short-term positive for crude supply stability, but Iran's Kharg Island resumption adds to supply expectations — netting out as a neutral for oil.

Gold's retreat from 4,481 to 4,448 signals near-term technical weakness; watch the 4,440 support zone.
