## 3. Views and counter-evidence

**A | Semis: the invalidation condition from the last brief is only half triggered - SOX has broken down, but the "guidance cut" leg is decided tonight.**
**Evidence**: SOX closed 9,610.65 (-5.28%), **2.22% below** the 11-13 low of 9,828.98, and just 0.25% above its session low (on 11-13 it rebounded 3.23% off the low); the 10-year yield **fell** 3bp the same day, so this is not a discount-rate move. Three verifiable drivers: Nvidia governance (01:39), the US tightening remote access to advanced AI chips (Jin10 summary, original not obtained), and the 13F showing institutions leaving data centres and power (05:45). **View**: this is pre-event de-risking, not proof that the capex cycle has been invalidated. SOX is -34.42% from its 52-week high but still **+56.02%** above its 52-week low; a single -5.28% day cannot establish an industry trend. **Counter-evidence**: in the 13F aggregate, semis were net bought (2,951 adding vs 2,584 trimming); Microsoft -1.12%, Meta -2.38% and the Dow -1.41% fell far less - if demand had cracked, cloud and advertising would not have held up. **Invalidation (revised this issue)**: if Nvidia's Q4 guidance comes in below consensus tonight **and** SOX breaks 9,587.11 tomorrow, this switches from "valuation/governance discount" to "capex-cycle pricing" and the whole chain (memory, optics, neoclouds) needs re-rating; if guidance holds and SOX reclaims 9,828.98, today was just pre-event de-risking.

**B | Nvidia: the reinstatement option is void, so the governance discount moves from tail risk to base case.**
**Evidence**: the board is not considering a return, is prioritising the investigation into whether trade secrets were involved, and has hired Wachtell Lipton (01:57); of the 180,200 signatures, 8,800 claim to be current employees and 94,000 shareholders, but **the site says identities are unverified**; Bessent wants an external CEO; Trump "wouldn't mind" him back, but that carries no weight with the board; BI notes the listed-company situation is not analogous to OpenAI (04:16). NVDA closed 156.14 (-4.12%), **-35.84%** from its 52-week high. **View**: the market had assigned option value to a return; the board has now voided it. Two pricing variables remain: **the investigation's conclusion** (which sets legal liability and management survival) and **tonight's guidance**. **Counter-evidence**: Tiger Global added 7.2% to NVDA in Q3, to 12m shares; Altman says demand is strong and the industry is fine; market cap is still **$3.76trn**. Institutional opinion is genuinely split, not uniformly bearish. **Do not do**: use a single quarter's revenue to prove either a boom or a bust; the 13F is a 9-30 snapshot and says nothing about today's flows.

**C | Today's drop was not rates-driven - the denominator framework needs revising, but do not jump to "recession".**

## 5. Data limitations

1. **Run date vs data date**: this runs on 11-17 (Tuesday). All US equity data are the **11-16 (Monday) close**, commodities are **11-16 settlements plus 11-17 08:02 real-time**, Treasuries are the **11-16 official Treasury curve**, and A-shares/Hong Kong are the **11-16 close**. What actually happened inside the 00:00-08:00 window is **the second half of the US session and the after-hours**; the 22:30 Beijing open was outside it.
2. **Coverage gap**: there was **no brief** between 11-14 08:00 and this one - the cron execution table has no 08:00 entry at all on 11-16 (several other jobs failed with `RuntimeError: Request timed out` between 10:00 and 17:01 that day). Gap-period events are dated in the backfill section and are not mixed into today's news.
3. **Flash coverage**: `flash` caps a single call at 300 items; the pull covered **11-16 22:40 to 11-17 07:47**, with **251 items** inside the 00:00-08:00 window. Anything before 22:40 or after 07:47 is missing, so some pre-open moves and squawks from the first 90 minutes are not included.
4. **Three oil definitions, not merged**: official settlement -3.21% / Jin10 platform -4.44% / Sina electronic session vs settlement -0.50%. The differences are base and timestamp, not two markets; Jin10 USOIL does not state a contract month and is quoted only as a platform series. Brent `hf_OIL`'s prior-settlement field is stuck at 95.840 (11-13), so its percentage was not used.
5. **Single-source items**: VIX only from Longbridge `.VIX.US` (Tencent `usVIX` is stale, timestamped 2026-09-15, and Sina `gb_$vix` returned empty); SOX only from Sina `gb_$sox` (Longbridge `.SOX.US` returned an empty array). Neither has a second source; levels may be slightly off, though direction and magnitude match Jin10's closing flashes.
6. **Hike odds from one platform**: CME only (06:15 flash), for the December meeting. It is **not the same source as the LSEG 91% cited on 11-14 and cannot be subtracted from it**. The meeting date follows Jin10 (12-08/09) and was not checked against the Fed website.
7. **13F timing**: all figures are a **09-30-2026 snapshot filed on 11-16**. Reuters' "138 liquidations / 119 new positions" is a **count of filers**, not net dollar selling, and cannot prove November flows; the text does not extrapolate today's buying or selling from it.
8. **Not obtained**: the full text of Hammack's and Warsh's remarks (Jin10 paraphrase); Barkin's 07:10 remarks (no output in the window at all); the text and scope of the US remote-access chip rule; Nvidia's Q4 consensus; the seasonal adjustment notes behind the NY Fed manufacturing print; any hike pricing beyond CME (e.g. SOFR futures).
9. **Unattributable items**: Alphabet -4.07% had **no company-specific negative** in the window (only product/partnership news: Ask Admob, Nano Banana 2.1, a Japan cybersecurity tie-up), so no cause is asserted; Micron -7.62% and SK Hynix -7.29% likewise had no company-specific supply/demand news and are treated as sector moves only.
10. **No single-indicator conclusions**: this issue does not use VIX +18.7%, SOX -5.28% or any single headline to declare a crash, a bottom, or institutional buying/selling; every view carries explicit counter-evidence and an invalidation condition.
