🔥 **Key Overnight News**

**⚔️ US-Iran Ceasefire on the Brink — The Biggest Story of the Night**

Less than 20 days into the fragile ceasefire, it is unraveling fast. The US launched a new round of airstrikes on southern Iran, with the strike scale expanded fivefold compared to prior operations. Washington also revoked the general license permitting Iranian oil sales, directly targeting merchant vessel attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. Simultaneously, Iran formally asserted to the UN that portions of the Strait's waters fall within its territorial sea. The US-led naval coalition has raised the strait transit threat level to "severe."

This marks the effective collapse of the late-June ceasefire. With roughly one-fifth of global oil trade passing through the Strait of Hormuz daily, the escalation poses a direct supply-side risk to crude markets.

**🏦 Fed's Williams: Policy Is "Just Right"**

NY Fed President Williams expressed slightly increased optimism on the near-term inflation outlook, emphasizing that current monetary policy is well-positioned and the Fed will continue to rely on data for guidance. Against the backdrop of new Chair Warsh's preference for letting markets "self-navigate," Williams' dovish tone offered some reassurance.

**🇪🇺 Trump Stirs Tensions Ahead of NATO Summit**

With the NATO summit approaching, Trump revived calls to take over Greenland and issued a sweeping threat to withdraw US troops from Europe, further laying bare frictions with allies.

**📉 Nasdaq Volatility Hits 20+ Year High**

Extreme position concentration, leveraged ETF amplification, and SpaceX's inclusion in the Nasdaq have pushed volatility to levels not seen in over two decades. AI trade crowding is flashing warning signals, and with institutional "firepower" stretched thin, the mega-cap turmoil may not be over.

**🥇 PBOC Adds Gold for 20th Consecutive Month**

China's central bank continued its gold purchasing streak, providing a structural floor for bullion prices.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The US-Iran conflict is now the dominant tail risk — the speed of the ceasefire breakdown has surpassed market expectations, and if Strait of Hormuz transit threats continue to escalate, crude could see a sharp risk-premium spike.

However, Williams' dovish remarks and the ISM non-manufacturing PMI holding steady at 54 (in line with expectations) suggest the US economic fundamentals have not yet deteriorated, making it unlikely the Fed will shift its policy path due to geopolitical events in the near term.

Nasdaq volatility surging alongside AI crowding signals continued deleveraging pressure on tech — watch for liquidity-siphoning effects spilling into other sectors.

Gold remains caught in a tug-of-war between geopolitical safe-haven demand and dollar resilience — the 4090-4110 range-bound pattern is intact, with central bank buying providing a floor but conditions for a renewed unilateral bull run still not in place.

Crude's geopolitical premium near 72 remains moderate, but should the Hormuz situation deteriorate further, oil could rapidly test the 75-78 range.
