🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**WTI Crude Plunges 7.7% — Kazakhstan CPC Terminal Shutdown Triggers Chain Reaction**

Kazakhstan's Energy Ministry confirmed daily oil output has been halved following the CPC terminal closure. Yet oil prices fell nearly 8% instead of rising — the market interprets this as crude being stranded and unable to reach global markets, creating regional oversupply, compounded by risk-aversion ahead of this week's Fed decision. Polish PM Tusk signaled potential measures to further lower fuel prices.

**US-China AI Dispute Escalates — Commerce Ministry Fires Back at Sanctions Threat**

China's Commerce Ministry spokesperson stated China firmly opposes the US politicizing tech and trade issues, and pointed out that "many American AI companies have distilled Chinese models in their R&D and training." The State Council Information Office will hold a press conference on July 28 at 3 PM regarding China's stance on the so-called "overcapacity" issue.

Fed Chair Warsh has refused to telegraph the policy direction ahead of this week's rate decision (Thursday 2:00 AM Beijing time), keeping markets on edge. Bank of America warns that the August-October window is the "weakest" for US stocks, with gold and the dollar poised for opportunities.

🧭 **Situation Assessment**

WTI crude crashing 7.7% while gold breaks above 4,100 simultaneously — this is classic "recession trade" signaling, with markets dumping risk assets en masse ahead of the Fed decision and flooding into safe havens.

The Kazakhstan CPC terminal shutdown should theoretically push oil prices higher, but the counter-directional crash reveals that current market logic is not supply-driven but demand-and-sentiment-driven. "Bullish news, bearish reaction" is itself a powerful bearish signal.

This is Super Central Bank Week — the Fed on Wednesday (Thursday Beijing time), the BOE on Thursday, and the BOJ on Friday all in succession. Warsh's rejection of forward guidance means the market can only "open the blind box" at the moment of the decision, carrying an extremely high uncertainty premium.

The US-China AI dispute is escalating from the technical level to the diplomatic level, with the Commerce Ministry unusually firing back directly at US accusations. This is no longer simple tariff jousting — it is a battle for technological hegemony with implications far beyond trade itself.

On China's A-share market, Dongfang Precision's H1 net profit surge of 867% deserves attention, but the sustainability of the profit source (whether it includes one-off gains) requires verification.
