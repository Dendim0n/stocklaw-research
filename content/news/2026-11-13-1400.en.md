## 3. Read on the situation

**A | Duration: the global repricing is confirmed; negative for high-multiple growth and precious metals — this is the position assumption most worth changing this issue.**
Evidence: Treasuries 2Y +13bp and 10Y +12bp over two sessions, JGB 10Y +8.5bp, **front end faster than the long end**; Berkshire's yen bonds priced at 75/90/105bp over swaps for 5/10/30 years, all above April's 70/90/100bp (listed in the previous issue). Falsification and confirmation: US October PPI at 21:30 tonight, core MoM expected 0.3% (prior 0.7%) — at or below 0.3%, this move looks like front-running ahead of data and duration pressure can ease; at or above 0.5%, 5.10% is a starting point, not an endpoint. Gap: 11-13 official yields only arrive after the US close, so this issue can only use 11-12.

**B | Chinese AI hardware: the divergence from Taiwan says this is a valuation and positioning problem, so neither chase the decline nor buy the dip.**
Evidence: ChiNext −3.65% and Hang Seng Tech −2.96% moved opposite to the TAIEX +1.35% and Nikkei +0.45% on the same day; A-share money rotated into banks (several at record highs) and pork, a defensive shift rather than a full risk-off; on 11-12 in the US, NVDA −4.34%, AMD +5.86% and AVGO +5.54% with the SOX down only 0.54% — again rotation within the sector, not exit. There is no cross-border flow or southbound data for this hour, so **it cannot be asserted that foreign money is leaving**. Impact: A/H-listed optical modules, PCB, components and memory are hit hardest; Taiwan and the SOX chain are relatively resilient. Falsification: if tomorrow A-share losses narrow inside 1% and Hang Seng Tech stabilises, it was one-day de-grossing; Nvidia's 11-17 guidance is what would confirm a demand-side problem.

**C | Crude and precious metals: crude leans bullish short-term but do not chase; gold leans bearish but do not short into it.**
Evidence: Westpac's chief economist said the RBA may already be done cutting ([13:04](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113130409491800)), and SMFG's CEO personally expects the BOJ to hike twice more to 1.75% by next summer ([13:01](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113130153631800), **a banker's personal view, not policy guidance**) — both point to the end of the easing cycle, which caps commodity demand and supports the dollar; crude's daily picture is still WTI slightly down and Brent slightly up, so the hourly strength has no daily confirmation. Impact: energy equities have relative strength but no hard driver; gold's short-term momentum turned down after failing twice at 3,923, and silver is weaker. Falsification: if WTI clears today's high of 88.92 with an officially confirmed supply disruption, crude upgrades to a trend; if gold reclaims 3,923, this section is void. Trade-off: do not chase either side here; wait for PPI to set direction.
