**③ WGC Q3: central banks buy 321t, fastest since end-2024; Indian demand -29% | 14:11-14:24**

- Central banks **bought a net 321 tonnes** in Q3, the fastest pace since end-2024; year-to-date purchases total **675 tonnes**, slightly above the same period last year. Among reporting countries, **China added 63t and Poland 41t**, with **Russia (-19t)** the only major seller. WGC strategist John Reade said weaker prices in the quarter may have prompted some central banks to buy more (14:11:52).
- The other side of demand: Indian Q3 gold demand **fell 29% year on year to 162.5 tonnes**, with jewellery -20% and investment -40%; full-year demand is seen near **650 tonnes**, the lowest since 2020 and about -15% y/y (14:24:38). After India more than doubled import duties to 15%, Q3 unofficial imports rose from 3 tonnes to **50 tonnes**, more than 15x y/y (14:19:11, citing Metals Focus).
- **Impact**: official demand is accelerating while price-sensitive demand is collapsing - both are true. That explains the split of "gold falling while central banks keep buying", but it also means **central-bank bids underpin a long-term floor, not the short-term direction**. The 321t is a quarterly first estimate and these numbers are routinely revised (Q1 this year was revised down substantially); no Q4 inference is drawn from it here.

**④ Supply splits: oil loosening, gas tightening | 14:09-14:37**

- Per sources, **Kazakhstan's October crude and condensate output rose 8% m/m to 2.08 mb/d**, and **Kuwait raised October output to 2.10 mb/d**, the highest since the Iran war began. Vitol's CEO said oil demand needs a year to recover to 2025 levels (14:09:03 energy digest).
- Gas runs the other way: the IEA sees global gas demand **down about 1.5% (nearly 65bcm)** this year, but LNG supply **down 3% (nearly 20bcm)**, the largest annual decline on record, with European storage entering the heating season at the **lowest since 2013**; Goldman lifted its Q1 2027 European gas forecast from **EUR 44 to 74/MWh** (14:24:18, 14:28:14, 14:30:30, 14:32:22, 14:09:03).
- **Impact**: for crude, both output and export capacity are rising, the opposite of the $96-98 quote - it supports "the geopolitical premium will eventually bleed out" without fixing a timing. For gas it is the reverse: pressure on European power and data-centre energy costs is structural, supportive of gas and power assets and negative for European energy-intensive manufacturing.

**⑤ PBOC lifts the FX deposit reserve ratio to 6%, effective 15 November | 14:26:58**

Minsheng Bank's Wen Bin attributed the recent RMB strength to large trade surpluses and corporate conversion demand, said the move helps break one-way appreciation expectations, and estimated it would freeze roughly **$20-25bn** of FX liquidity at banks.
- **Impact**: this is a counter-cyclical FX tool, not a rate tool. For A-shares and Hong Kong it means **a weaker FX tailwind but a broken one-way appreciation trade** - neutral overall, mildly supportive for exporters. The $20-25bn figure is a bank estimate, not official.

## 4. Judgments

**Judgment 1: The A-share "broadening selloff" was bought back before the close, but the character is absorption, not a turn - broad index back to neutral, AI hardware remains the only bearish leg.**

Three pieces of evidence. First, the shape of the reversal: CSI 300 rose **+0.79%** in the final hour and closed above its open, the session low of 3,969.55 sits exactly at the level flagged in the prior brief and held, and SSE 50 turned green and closed at its session high - that is the footprint of support-style absorption, not a broad counter-attack (the Shanghai Composite still closed below its 3,624.58 open). Second, volume: CNY 1.70trn combined, a fifth straight session above 1.5trn, four broad-based ETFs above CNY 10bn for the first time since the September 2024 rally, and CNY 15bn of southbound net buying - the scale of absorption is real. Third, the counter-evidence: STAR 50 -3.81% and ChiNext -2.73%, with losses concentrated in growth and AI hardware; Alibaba went from +0.75% at 13:00 to -0.30%, so the Hong Kong platform hedge is also diverging. **Implication**: A-share broad index **neutral**, AI hardware **bearish**, Hong Kong **neutral**; the worst response to a late-session ramp is to read it as a trend reversal and chase STAR 50. **Confirmation**: if tomorrow STAR 50 reclaims today's 1,184.53 open and CSI 300 holds 3,969.55, the selling has run its course; if CSI 300 loses 3,969.55, the prior brief's "rotation, not a broad selloff" call is void and the broad-selloff assumption returns.

**Judgment 2: This crude move is geopolitical pricing, not supply-demand pricing - don't chase it, but don't short it either, because Hormuz is still shut and price is more sensitive to headlines than to output.**

Two sets of opposing facts. Geopolitical: a senior Iranian official explicitly said the US and Iran are "far" from a deal and that reopening Hormuz requires meeting Tehran's conditions first; Jin10's WTI quote touched 97.11 within 4-6 minutes of those reports, with WTI December and Brent rising to 98.14 and 106.74. Supply: Aramco says it can raise output quickly and is studying an East-West pipeline expansion, Kazakhstan's October output rose 8% m/m, Kuwait reached 2.10 mb/d (the highest since the war began), and yesterday brought the Dubai spread flipping to contango plus the four Gulf exporters back to pre-war shipment levels. **Implication**: crude **neutral** - only tail risk is bullish short term, the bearish supply evidence is harder, and the two offset near $98; the odds on chasing longs are worse here than they were at $96. **Invalidation and confirmation**: if WTI December loses today's 96.12 low without new US-Iran escalation, treat it as the premium starting to bleed; if the Dubai spread returns to backwardation (needs the next round of Middle East physical quotes, no update this hour), withdraw the medium-term bearish call. Note also that the USOIL-to-WTI-December spread widened from 0.961 to 1.114, and with Jin10 not labelling the contract month, **that widening cannot be used as evidence of spot tightness**.

**Judgment 3: Gold's short-term pricing is no longer about real yields; the prior brief's "wait for real yields to turn" condition is shown by the data to be insufficient. Don't chase this decline, but don't catch it either.**

Evidence: the 10-year TIPS real yield was **2.62%** on 11-02, already **33bp** below the **2.95%** peak on 10-05, with the nominal 10-year at 4.91% (3bp below 4.94% on 10-30), and gold still printing new lows (today's 3,911.50) - the rates side cannot explain the past month, only the medium term: real yields rose from **1.72%** in February to 2.95% in October (+123bp), matching gold's **26.45%** drawdown from its 5,318.40 high. The demand split is clean: central banks bought 321t in Q3 while price-sensitive buyers retreat (Indian jewellery -20%, investment -40%, full-year 650t the lowest in six years), which says the marginal seller is Western capital and liquidity demand, not official reserves. **Implication**: precious metals **neutral-bearish** with lower conviction; spot at 3,911.70 is only 0.90% above the 30 October low, and the remaining downside depends on the assumption that the liquidity squeeze continues - an assumption that reversed in the A-share market today - so chasing the short is unattractive; going long has no basis either until price reclaims Monday's 3,946.47 close (+0.88%). **What is missing**: there is no intraday dollar-index basis this hour (Sina DINIW basis fields are inconsistent; 101.75 versus the 11-02 close of 101.72 is broadly flat), and no gold ETF flow data, so the chain "energy importers buy dollars -> dollar strength -> gold sold for liquidity" cannot be verified this hour and is kept only as a hypothesis.
