## 3. Key increments

**① RBA hikes 25bp to 4.85% on a 6–3 vote, and writes AI investment into its inflation attribution | 11:30**

- The decision matched expectations (prior 4.60%) and is the **highest since November 2008** and the **fifth hike this year**; **six members voted to hike, three to hold** ([Jin10 234171](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/234171)).
- Forecasts were raised sharply: headline CPI **peaks at 4.5% this quarter**, with both headline and trimmed mean **above 3% until end-2027** (about three quarters later than the August forecast), returning to the 2.5% midpoint only by mid-2028; 2026Q4 GDP +1.6%, unemployment 4.7%.
- Two lines in the statement matter: **"AI-related investment is pushing up the prices of technology goods quickly"**, and **"the global inflation outlook was also revised up, partly due to the AI boom."** This is policy-document wording, not market interpretation.
- Price reaction: AUD/USD dropped nearly 30 pips to **0.6978**; the ASX 200 reversed to **+0.2%** at 8,877.60. Bullock's press conference is at 12:30.
- **Read-through**: naming AI capex in a policy statement's inflation attribution is a rare, direct formulation among G10 central banks. If others adopt it, the implication is higher-for-longer and no decline in real rates — and gold has no cash flow, so its carrying cost *is* the real rate. Note the timing: metals were already selling off between 11:00 and 11:30, **before the decision**, so the rate call cannot be blamed for the move.

**② Marvell's $115bn all-stock purchase of Lumentum: terms disclosed, both stocks sold off | article 11:17, US reaction 11-02**

- The deal has an **enterprise value of about $115bn including Lumentum's debt** and an equity value of about **$102.54bn**; the exchange ratio is **4.5 Marvell shares per Lumentum share**, equal to **$1,132 per share, a 23.9% premium** to the October 28 unaffected close; Marvell holders will own **67%** of the combined company and Lumentum holders **33%**; closing is expected in **H1 2028** ([Jin10 234159](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/234159)).
- The hardest demand-side fact: Lumentum's CEO says **some products are "completely sold out" through early 2029**, with customers starting to lock **2030** orders; Nvidia invested **$2bn in each company** earlier this year. Lumentum did over $1bn of revenue last quarter with $461.1m of adjusted profit.
- Prices: Monday Lumentum **−18.6%** to 684.40, Marvell **−12.7%** to 171.41 (its worst three-day stretch since October 2000). At current prices the ratio is worth 4.5 × 171.41 = **$771.35**, **12.7% above** Lumentum's 684.40 — under an all-stock structure the consideration floats with the acquirer, and the target's discount to the exchange value says the market is pricing closing risk, not that the premium is too small.
- **Read-through**: optical interconnect scarcity is the least contested fact in this AI cycle, but the buyer is paying with 33% of its equity. Constructive on the shortage itself, negative on companies expanding through stock-funded M&A.

## 4. Situation assessment

**Judgment 1: the nature of this leg down in precious metals is a repricing of an asset with no cash flow against "AI inflation," not a retreat of haven demand.**

Three sets of facts hold at once. First, the RBA today put AI investment into its inflation attribution and extended above-3% inflation to end-2027; the 10-year real rate (T10YIE 2.29%, nominal 4.91% on 11-02) is back at **2.62%**, up 2bp on the day. Second, haven demand is rising and gold is not the beneficiary: VIX closed 11-02 at **21.15 (+8.91%)**, up **36%** over 20 sessions, while gold fell **5.3%** over the same 20 sessions. Third, gold is about **26%** below its January 29 high (COMEX continuous contract 5,318.40) and set a one-year low on October 30 (futures 3,898.90, spot roughly 3,876.89) — today's 3,925.91 is a retest 1.26% above that low, not a pullback from a peak. **Implication**: neutral-to-bearish on gold; this is not a "it has fallen enough" entry. Silver's heavier industrial mix and its 3.5x underperformance this hour say the pricing is coming from the demand side, not the monetary side. **Invalidation**: holding the October 30 low of 3,876.89, reclaiming Monday's close of 3,946.47 and silver back above $50 would say the deleveraging is over; the opposite confirmation is a break of today's low of 3,924.41 with the gold/silver ratio still climbing.

**Judgment 2: the Marvell deal exposes a worsening payment structure for AI capex, while the optical-scarcity fact still stands.**

All-stock, Lumentum holders taking only 33% of the combined company, closing not until H1 2028 — all three terms point the same way: the buyer has no cash and can only pay in equity. The market's verdict is blunt: acquirer **−12.7%**, target **−18.6%**, both down together, meaning investors neither think the price is good for the buyer nor expect the deal to be consummated at the quoted value (the exchange ratio now implies $771.35, 12.7% above the target's price, i.e. the target still trades at a discount to consideration). **Implication**: Lumentum being sold out into early 2029 with customers locking 2030 orders is the most concrete supply-demand evidence in this AI cycle — constructive on optical components. The acquirer paying 33% dilution is the net valuation loser near term. **Confirmation**: whether Marvell can stop falling before the shareholder vote, and whether the deal gets repriced; continued tandem declines would undercut the "vertical integration" narrative for AI infrastructure.

**Judgment 3: Asia's divergence says this is not broad risk-off but rotation out of AI hardware; today's decline should not be traded as a haven trade.**

Taiwan weighted −3%, A-share STAR 50 −2.54%, and copper and tin falling (Jin10 at 11:05 attributed this to a darker demand outlook for data-centre construction and circuit-board soldering; tin is down 10% this quarter, the worst of the LME's six metals) all point one way. On the other side, the CSI 300 is only −0.08%, more than 3,300 A-shares are higher, Hang Seng Tech is +1.08% and Tencent +1.73%. **Implication**: neutral on broad A-share indices, bearish on compute hardware, constructive on Hong Kong platforms. Gold falling in this environment is not contradictory — what is being sold is "expensive" and "no cash flow," not the label "risk asset." **Invalidation**: if the CSI 300 turns negative in the afternoon, advancers drop below 2,000 and Hong Kong gives back its gains, this is broad selling rather than rotation and the index resilience needs re-rating.
