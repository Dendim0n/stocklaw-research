## 5. Judgements and counter-evidence

**Judgement 1: this leg of crude is being priced off physical product shortages, not pure geopolitical sentiment — but marginal sensitivity is falling and high prices are already destroying demand at the margin.**

Evidence: ① Chinese night-session chemicals went **limit-up** — styrene, methanol, PX, benzene and propylene up more than 5%, fuel oil up more than 5% intraday and over 7% on the week ([23:00 night-session close](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261023230052664800)); ② the December Brent-WTI spread of **$13.45 = 13.02% of WTI** is itself a read on seaborne physical tightness, since WTI does not transit Hormuz while Brent is a waterborne physical benchmark; ③ Everbright Futures (relayed by Jin10, [article 233287](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233287)) notes fuel oil has no pipeline or ship-to-ship substitute, uses smaller vessels and carries higher insurance, so a strait disruption hits it far harder than crude. **All three point to physical supply, not expectations.**
Counter-evidence (must be stated alongside): ① Bessent says Gulf exports already exceed pre-conflict levels; ② Trump says Hormuz flows are above pre-war and that Iran is fabricating the attacks; ③ Germany's EBV is **selling** diesel and crude from reserves; ④ US crude exports to Asia have reportedly **halved** with export premia collapsing (article 233232) — high prices are already pricing out marginal buyers; ⑤ **after the mine report at 03:32, WTI moved only from the 103.27 settlement to 103.49 by 04:59 (+0.22%)** — a heavy supply shock drew a minimal response.
Implication: no chasing upstream (sensitivity to bullish headlines is decaying); refining and airline cost lines are deteriorating; the long case in refined products and fuel oil is firmer than in crude itself.
Invalidation / confirmation: December WTI above **103.92** (this window's Sina high) with Brent through **116.72** → premium expands; WTI below **98.84** → the second leg unwinds. **Direction will not be set by new statements, only by hard loading or production data — neither of which was obtained.**

**Judgement 2: Friday's split tape is the "oil → inflation expectations → rates" chain, not the AI narrative; the two legs are trading separately.**

Evidence: NDX **-0.004%** versus Dow **-1.23%**; two energy gainers (CVX +2.88%, XOM +2.79%) against broad consumer/industrial/financial weakness (AXP -3.68%, KO -2.76%, NKE -2.66%, CAT -2.42%, HD -2.41%, PG -2.12%); simultaneously the 10-year **+4bp back to 5.00%**, 1-year inflation expectations at **4.7%** and PMI output prices at a four-year fastest. Thursday's victims — Microsoft (+0.66%), Micron (+2.46%), SK Hynix (+4.05%) — rose on Friday.
Counter-evidence: Western Digital -2.97% (post-earnings), SanDisk -1.82% and Alphabet -1.24% show the AI complex was not uniformly up; and a 4bp move in the 10-year cannot explain a 1.23% Dow decline, so **the consumer and industrial selling also embeds a demand view** (Michigan sentiment 48.0 near record lows, durables buying conditions at a record low).
Implication: Friday should not be read as "risk appetite restored" (NDX flat is not up), nor as "AI's second crash" (NDX did not fall). **What is actually being priced is "oil lifts costs, costs lift rates, rates cap consumption and multiples".**
