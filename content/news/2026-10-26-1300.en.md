## 4. Situation assessment

**Crude: the ceiling on the geopolitical premium has now been written down, while the downside is held up by physical tightness — chase neither side inside 100–102.** The evidence is three facts from the same source: after the week's hardest escalation language ("large-scale", 96 hours) landed, oil moved **-0.07%** on the hour; the report itself pushes the order window past the midterms; and Hormuz remains a mix of blockade, escorted transits and mine clearance that an official calls unsustainable. Impact: upstream neutral; the relative strength in refining and tankers (PetroChina +4%, COSCO Shipping Energy +6%) comes from products and freight rather than crude direction. Invalidation and confirmation: a verifiable halt in loadings or a mine incident would gap oil back above 103 and void this view; if the U.S. session holds oil above 100.05 while energy equities keep outperforming, the physical-tightness logic carries on.

**Gold: neutral-to-bullish, but this is an allocation hour, not a haven hour.** The shape is the evidence: gold +0.52%, oil -2.44%, NQ +0.36%, the Nikkei cutting its loss — all four point to risk appetite intact and carry falling. Real yields falling 16bp from 10-19 to 10-22 provide medium-term support, but the data is stale and today's 0.5% has no verifiable catalyst, so it **cannot be attributed to the 96-hour story** — if that were the driver, oil could not be flat. Impact: gold itself bullish, miners not to be chased (Zijin -5% on the same day as a target cut is a crowding signal). Invalidation: gold back below 3,978 (the session low) with real yields rising would mark today's bid as short-term positioning; heavy volume rejection near 4,034 (the 10-24 high) would define the top of the range.

**Asian equities and AI hardware: what is being sold is valuation, not orders — but Singapore has just supplied the first counter-datapoint.** Singapore's September output fell from 15.4% to 3.4% y/y and -3.9% m/m, an official-statistics signal of slower electronics; at the same time Eoptolink and Changchuan went out of their way to describe full order books. The two are not contradictory — orders and output are separated by a lag — but it means "orders are strong" can no longer stand alone as a bullish argument. Impact: do not chase an AI hardware bounce; wait for tonight's SOX. If SOX falls markedly more than the Nasdaq, this is global deleveraging and the local rotation call should be withdrawn. Confirmation: SOX outperforming the Nasdaq plus a rebound in Singapore's October data would make the slowdown single-month noise.
