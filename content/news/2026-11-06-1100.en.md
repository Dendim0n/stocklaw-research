## 3. What's actually new

**① Korea: from circuit breaker to −4%, and the stabilisation tool is real | 10:45–10:57**
Deputy PM and Finance Minister Lee Hyung-il chaired an "emergency market conditions review meeting" with all four top economic and financial officials present (per Yonhap via Jin10, 10:57). The core measure: **for three months starting Nov 9, brokers are temporarily exempt from maintaining the 140% credit-financing collateral ratio**, to prevent forced selling of margin-bought shares from deepening the slide; further measures are ready if volatility worsens. **Why it matters**: this severs the mechanical loop of "decline → forced liquidation → further decline". It **reduces forced selling; it does not create buying** — so it supports the floor, not the ceiling.

**② Saudi OSP at a six-year low: physical crude is loosening while futures pay a war premium | 10:28**
Saudi Aramco cut its December Arab Light OSP to Asia by **$2/bbl to a $6.50 discount** to the Oman/Dubai average, the lowest since May 2020 (Northwest Europe and Mediterranean each cut $1; US unchanged). The same report carried three physical-market datapoints: **Middle East spot premiums have fallen back to pre-war levels**, exports are **still recovering** despite more tanker attacks in Hormuz, and weaker Asian refining margins are curbing buying. HSBC raised its Saudi Q4 output forecast from 6.3 to **8.3 mb/d** and its 2027 forecast from 8.8 to 9.8 mb/d. **Why it matters**: futures are paying a geopolitical premium while physical cargoes are getting cheaper — the most fragile seam in the oil price right now. (Note: an 02:58 flash had cited a $7 discount; the 10:28 item citing the pricing file puts it at $6.50, which is what I use.)

**③ Trump says he will "soon decide" on striking Iran again — but the third carrier isn't there yet | 11:06 (Axios via Jin10)**
In his first dedicated meeting with senior national-security advisers since the midterms, Trump laid out three paths: renewed large-scale strikes, continued economic pressure, or a deal with major concessions. Qatar is pushing a **two-day phased draft**: on day one the US lifts its naval blockade and unfreezes some Iranian funds in exchange for Iran inviting IAEA inspectors into the bombed nuclear sites; on day two **the Strait of Hormuz reopens**, after which nuclear talks resume. The counter-evidence is equally concrete: the Pentagon says thousands of air and naval personnel are ready for a "short but intense" operation targeting energy infrastructure and nuclear sites — but the **USS Theodore Roosevelt strike group begins a Singapore port call on Friday and is not expected to enter CENTCOM's area of responsibility until mid-November**, so the military option cannot be executed immediately. **Price reaction**: WTI barely moved after the 11:06 headline (91.41 at 11:00 → 91.39 at 11:10). **Why it matters**: this is the single largest downward variable for the war premium, but the market is clearly pricing it as a "draft", not a fact.

**④ Asia repairs intraday and Nasdaq futures hit +1.25%, but Dow futures fall −0.60% | 10:05–11:10**
At 10:05 Nasdaq futures extended gains to +1%, from more than −1% earlier, with overnight moves of **Micron +5.5%, SanDisk +6%, AMD +5%, Nvidia +2.4%**. Onshore, **STAR 50 went from −5% at the open to green by 10:50**, and several semiconductor-equipment ETFs flipped from −6% to positive (10:38); Hong Kong tech recovered with Meituan +3% and Tencent's loss narrowing inside 1% (10:27). **But Dow futures are −0.60% and S&P futures only +0.31%** — this is a **short-covering rebound in tech, not a broad return of risk appetite**: money rotated from defensives into AI, the total didn't grow.

## 4. Judgement

**A. Asia's repair is "selling pressure fading", not "risk cleared" — don't chase AI hardware here.**
Evidence: KOSPI recovered from −8.78% to −4.11% (+5.11% off the low), STAR 50 and semiconductor-equipment ETFs turned green, Nasdaq futures are +1.25%, and overnight semis rallied — four independent observations pointing the same way, indicating the marginal US selling pressure has decayed since 05:00. But three counterpoints are just as hard: Dow futures are **−0.60%** rather than joining the rally, the Shanghai Composite is still −1.07% (from −2.16%), Hong Kong has only returned to roughly flat (−0.3% to −0.4%), and combined Shanghai/Shenzhen turnover has now topped 1 trillion yuan for a **352nd consecutive session** — turnover proves churn, not new money. Korea's measure cuts off forced selling; it does not inject bids. **Conclusion**: this is short covering plus policy jawboning working together; durability depends on the real absorption at tonight's 22:30 US open. **Confirmation**: Nasdaq futures hold +1% and semis close higher on volume after the open. **Invalidation**: KOSPI closes back below 5,000, or Nasdaq futures give back to flat before the 21:30 payrolls print.

**B. Oil: the war premium is biased lower, but neither chase the rally nor short volatility.**
The case rests on three hard physical datapoints (a six-year-low Saudi OSP, regional spot premiums back to pre-war levels, HSBC raising Saudi output) plus the "Hormuz reopens" clause in Qatar's draft — all pointing to supply recovering — while futures at Brent 99.60 and WTI 91.39 are still paying for a cargo disruption that has not happened, and the 11:06 de-escalation headline produced almost no price response. **The risk to that view is real**: the Pentagon's "short, intense" plan is drafted and includes **energy infrastructure** as a target, and the third carrier arrives in mid-November, reopening the military window. **Conclusion**: directionally short the premium, but it is a slow variable — don't use a tactical short to bet on the timing of a single post. **Invalidation**: verifiable declines in Hormuz loadings, or Iran publicly rejecting the draft.

**C. Gold found no haven bid on the same night VIX printed its fourth-highest close in a year — that matters more than the price.**
Evidence: VIX closed at 28.18 on Nov 5 (+69.66%, 98.8th percentile of 260 sessions), yet spot gold rose only 0.72% that day and is down another 0.74% today. The path matters more: **−0.88% between 09:00 and 10:00, then essentially flat (+0.07%) after 10:00**, which says the selling came from real rates and positioning, not panic. **Conclusion**: gold is trading off real rates, not the fear index; 4,056 (today's low) is a short-term reference, but without haven demand the bounce lacks force. **Do not treat 4,060 as a "haven confirmation level"**, and do not treat it as the start of a trend either. **Confirmation**: a payrolls print well below 65k with December hike odds falling further should lift gold back above 4,090 (the Nov 5 close). **Invalidation**: payrolls above 65k with average hourly earnings at 0.3% m/m or more likely sends gold toward 4,000.
