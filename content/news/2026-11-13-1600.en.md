## 2. Key increments

**① Nvidia: WSJ exposes the Huang-Kress rift, and the core issue is the pace of AI financing guarantees | 15:51, article ID 235185**
The Wall Street Journal reported Friday that in August Huang Renxun sharply criticised then-CFO Colette Kress at an internal meeting, calling her a CFO who only reported data; the dispute was over how fast to extend **financing guarantees** to cash-strapped data-centre developers and cloud providers. The trades were pushed by his son-in-law Nico Caprez, promoted to vice president in January, alongside Raj Mirpuri ([ID 235185](https://xnews.jin10.com/details/235185)). Keep the numbers separate: on 26 July WSJ reported Nvidia was **considering** up to **$250bn** of financing support, and the stock fell about 5% the next session; Kress then reworked the OpenAI Ohio project (originally slated to deploy about **$300bn** of Nvidia chips), and a scaled-down agreement was signed on 17 August with a **guarantee obligation cap of $105bn**; Reuters, citing the latest financial report, puts related commitments at about **$164.5bn** — commitments and contingent obligations, not cash spent. Kress became interim CEO on Thursday; new chairman Harvey Jones says the board is evaluating both internal and external CEO candidates, with an **18 November** employee meeting.
Impact: this is not a single-company governance event but a pricing problem for the credit chain of vendor financing → cloud expansion → chip purchases.

**② Europe is joining the hawkish camp: German 2Y +16.5bp on the week, French and Spanish inflation elevated, Germany's 2027 budget adding €210.4bn of borrowing | 15:06 / 15:53 / 15:54 / 16:00**
German two-year yields are at **3.14%**, +1.5bp on the day and up **16.5bp** on the week, the largest weekly gain in two months; Jin10 attributes it to "US inflation data coming in above expectations + markets raising ECB hike bets + equity rebound eroding safe-haven demand" ([15:53](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113155302694800)). French October final harmonised CPI was **3.7%** (revised down 0.1pp from the prelim), accelerating from **3.4%** in September and the highest since December 2023 ([15:54](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113155402329800)); French October CPI came in at 3.4% y/y and 0.4% m/m, both in line ([15:46](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113154609697800)). At 16:00 Spain's October final CPI and harmonised CPI both printed **4.9%** (m/m 0.6% / 0.5%), in line with expectations and the prior, still among the highest in the euro area ([16:00](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113160002361800)). The same day Germany's budget committee finalised the 2027 budget: **€561bn** of total spending and **€210.4bn** of new borrowing — the second highest since WWII, with €123bn of core borrowing and €62bn of investment ([15:06](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113150614943800)). At 16:00 the Czech central bank minutes showed members unanimously agreeing policy must stay relatively tight and that "a modest hike to limit second-round effects may be desirable", though they differed on timing ([16:00](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113160022562800)).
Impact: inflation stuck high + fiscal expansion + heavier supply together push European duration up in step with the US 10Y at 5.10% — the discount-rate assumption for global high-multiple assets needs another notch higher.

**③ Two opposing forces in crude: Qatar discounting to Asia while Hormuz traffic stays depressed | 15:22 / 15:54**
Pricing documents show Qatar set its **December** official selling price for marine crude at a **$10/bbl discount** to the Oman/Dubai benchmark, with land crude at a $9.5 discount ([15:22](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113152215062800)). That is direct evidence of a producer cutting prices to defend Asian share, consistent with last night's EIA build of 4.657m barrels and with Asian refiners resuming Middle East purchases. The counterpoint: a Jin10 futures column says **211 vessels** are anchored and Hormuz traffic is depressed ([15:54](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113155401922800)), but that is a promotional column with no statistical date or methodology — a lead, not data.
Impact: physical cargoes are being discounted while the waterway is congested. That combination means "ample supply, tight logistics", which supports high volatility, not a one-way trend.

**④ China's 15th Five-Year Plan energy-storage implementation plan lands: capacity pricing, storage at computing hubs, curbs on blind battery expansion | 15:11–15:18**
The NDRC and National Energy Administration issued the "15th Five-Year Plan" new-type energy storage implementation plan. Key points: establish a **capacity price mechanism for grid-side independent storage**, and after spot markets run continuously, orderly build a reliable capacity compensation mechanism; build storage stations at the **eight national computing hubs** (Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, Yangtze River Delta, Greater Bay Area, Chengdu-Chongqing, Guizhou, Inner Mongolia, Gansu, Ningxia) and explore cluster-shared storage backup to improve power reliability for computing facilities; implement lithium-battery industry controls and **guard against blind expansion** in storage cells ([15:14](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113151453076800), [15:11](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113151157722800), [15:12](https://flash.jin10.com/detail/20261113151233577800)).
Impact: capacity pricing backstops revenue for storage installations, bullish for system integrators and power equipment; "guard against blind expansion" is a supply constraint on cells, bearish for tier-2/3 battery makers — the same policy cuts in opposite directions at the two ends of the chain.
