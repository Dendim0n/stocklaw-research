## 🔥 Key Developments This Hour

### ⚔️ US-Iran Military Conflict in Full Escalation

**Third round of US strikes.** US Central Command confirmed completing the third round of strikes on Iran this week on July 11, deploying land-based and carrier-based fighter jets, drones, and naval vessels with precision munitions against approximately 140 Iranian military targets, including missile and drone bases, naval capabilities, ammunition storage facilities, communication networks, and coastal surveillance sites. Cumulative strikes now exceed 300 targets.

**Iran retaliates across multiple nations.** Iran's Revolutionary Guard launched missile and drone attacks on Bahrain (home to US Navy Fifth Fleet), UAE, Qatar, and Kuwait. A fire broke out at US naval facilities in Bahrain; explosions heard in Doha, Qatar; UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, and Kuwait activated air defense interceptions. Iran claims to have destroyed the command and control center and MQ-9 drone hangars at Prince Hassan Air Base in Jordan.

**Iran's hardline stance.** Iran's military warned that the consequences of US actions and their damage to regional security will be borne by both the United States and Israel. Iran also claimed US provocation near the southern Strait of Hormuz was forcefully halted by its navy.

## 🧭 Strategic Assessment

The US-Iran conflict has rapidly escalated from diplomatic maneuvering to direct military engagement, with intensity climbing sharply within 72 hours — from the first round of strikes to a third, and from unilateral strikes to large-scale Iranian retaliation.

Iran's choice to strike US military facilities across multiple Gulf states rather than Israel proper is highly calculated: it demonstrates retaliatory capability while avoiding full activation of US-Israel mutual defense treaty triggers.

Qatar's nationwide security threat level elevation is particularly notable — as the key mediator between the US and Iran, this signals a serious risk of diplomatic channel disruption.

Gold settling at $4,119 in extreme record territory ahead of the weekend sets up Monday's Asian open as the ultimate stress test for geopolitical pricing — continued Iranian strikes over the weekend could challenge $4,200; any ceasefire signal could trigger a rapid $100-150 pullback.

Crude oil shows a severe disconnect from geopolitical intensity — WTI at just $71 suggests demand-side concerns are overwhelming supply disruption risk, but this pricing would evaporate instantly if Strait of Hormuz transit is disrupted. US Central Command's confirmation that commercial shipping continues through the strait is the critical indicator to watch.

The core flashpoint remains the "memorandum terms" dispute, with Qatari representatives having flown to Iran for mediation. Weekend diplomatic developments will determine Monday's global market open direction.
