🔥 **Key News This Hour**

**🛢️ Oil Plunges 3.6%, Global Energy Buffer Near Depletion**

WTI crude crashed over 3.5% during late Asian/early European hours, falling from above $93 to $89. According to Jin10 reports, US refineries are running at near-maximum capacity with almost no idle backup, while the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) continues to be drawn down. Combined pressure from the Strait of Hormuz, Red Sea shipping routes, and Russian energy infrastructure is pushing the global energy market into its most vulnerable state of buffering capacity.

**🏦 BOJ Expected to Hold Rates at 1% Next Week**

Nikkei reports that the Bank of Japan plans to keep its key interest rate unchanged at 1% at the July 30-31 policy meeting. A majority of the nine-member board is expected to vote in favor. However, a separate Jin10 analysis notes that the BOJ's rate hike logic is quietly shifting — from earlier concerns about Middle East-driven oil price spikes to a focus on whether businesses are persistently passing on production costs.

**🇪🇺 ECB's Simkus: Oil at $100 Would Impact Inflation**

ECB Governing Council member Simkus made multiple statements: inflation risks are tilted to the upside, oil at $100/bbl would impact inflation, but "there is no value in rushing decisions now," noting that the past six weeks have shown increased environmental uncertainty.

**🇺🇸 Trump Ties US-Saudi Nuclear Deal to Israel-Saudi Normalization**

Trump has set a new precondition, binding the US-Saudi nuclear cooperation agreement to Israel-Saudi normalization, adding uncertainty to what was already a closely watched nuclear deal.

🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The 3.6% single-day oil crash is today's most important signal — but lower oil prices may actually ease inflation pressure, providing a marginally dovish variable for next week's FOMC.

The ECB explicitly flagged concerns about oil and upside inflation risks but chose to wait and see, indicating that policymakers remain highly uncertain about the current landscape.

The BOJ is expected to hold steady, but the rate hike logic shifting from external shocks to endogenous inflation means the window for hikes will reopen once corporate price pass-through broadens.

The escalation of Russia-Ukraine conflict on ports and energy infrastructure, combined with Strait of Hormuz tensions, is exposing the fragility of global energy supply chains — oil prices retain upside pulse risk in the near term.

Intel's earnings beat is a positive catalyst for the semiconductor sector; watch for spillover effects on competitors like AMD and NVDA and the broader supply chain.

Trump tying the US-Saudi nuclear deal to Israel-Saudi normalization adds complexity to the Middle East geopolitical chessboard, increasing near-term uncertainty for Saudi nuclear technology access.
