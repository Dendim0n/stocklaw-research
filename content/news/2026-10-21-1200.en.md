## 3. Incremental news

**① [11:49, item 233021] Anthropic's draft prospectus: revenue 14.8x year-ago, $80.7bn H1 net loss, over $600bn of compute commitments**

- Operations: H1 revenue **$16.3bn** (versus $1.1bn a year earlier and $4.6bn for all of 2025); compute and infrastructure spend **close to $9bn** (versus $2.3bn a year earlier and $7.3bn for all of 2025) — half a year burned more than the whole prior year.
- Loss: H1 net loss of **$80.7bn** (versus $13bn a year earlier), of which **about $72bn is an accounting charge from fair-value changes on convertible notes and other financing instruments**, not cash out the door. This must be read separately from operating losses.
- Commitments: roughly **$450bn** of cloud contracts over the next 7–10 years, mostly non-cancellable and partly payable on committed volumes rather than actual usage; disclosed so far are Google Cloud $111bn, AWS $110bn, Microsoft $31bn, Nscale plus Lambda up to $86bn and more than $160bn of Broadcom-related Google chip leases, for a total **above $600bn**; another ~$150bn is under negotiation, which could take the potential total to **$750bn**.
- Concentration: two customers account for about a fifth of revenue (roughly $3.26bn). Potential valuation: $2trn.
- Nature of the data: a draft reviewed by the *Wall Street Journal* and relayed by Jin10; Anthropic **declined to comment**; this is not a public SEC filing and cannot be cross-verified.
- Implication: order visibility for NVDA/AVGO and the cloud providers (bullish); a stress test for AI application valuations and private marks (bearish) — it turns "the AI bubble" from a P/E debate into a question of non-cancellable cash commitments. <https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233021>

**② [11:20, item 233025] Reuters poll: all 100 economists expect a December hike**

- Survey of 10/16–20: 94 economists expect the 10/27–28 meeting to hold at **3.75–4.00%**; a December hike went from "fewer than half" in last month's poll to **all 100 respondents**. The Fed already hiked 25bp in September.
- Terminal rate and count: nearly 60% see a terminal rate of **4.00–4.25%**; **38 of 92** expect at least two more hikes (about one in five in the September poll). These are different questions and are not combined into a single hike count.
- Inflation: the median PCE forecast stays more than 100bp above the 2% target for several quarters and does not return to target until at least **2028**; Q3 GDP is expected at 3.2%, above potential.
- Corroboration in the same direction: former BoJ executive Eiji Maeda said at 11:52 that the next hike comes in December, then again in March or April; Goldman Sachs said at 10:11 that it expects the RBA to hike 25bp to 4.85% in November.
- Implication: three central banks tightening in the same direction is a systemic headwind for long-duration assets. Yet gold is flat and Nasdaq futures are +0.80% — there is no visible sign in prices that this is being priced. <https://xnews.jin10.com/details/233025>

**③ [11:07 / 11:08] NextDC cuts guidance on a grid delay, -44% intraday; China's September power use +1.2% but data-centre use +35.4%**

- NextDC (Australian data-centre operator): grid upgrades for the M3 project at Footscray in Melbourne **slipped to 2027**, and planning approval was pushed past next month's state election; profit guidance was cut by **more than a third** with revenue guidance lowered too; the stock fell as much as **44% to A$5.15** at midday, taking its market value to A$4.3bn (it traded above A$16 in early June).
- China's National Energy Administration: September electricity use **899.7bn kWh, +1.2% y/y** (August +1.7%); residential use **-6.0%** (a typhoon cut cooling demand across central, eastern and southern China); tertiary industry +4.9%, with **internet data services at 9.8bn kWh, +35.4%**, and EV charging at 16.8bn kWh, +57.0%. January–September cumulative 8,073.4bn kWh, +3.9%, with internet data services at 79.3bn kWh, **+41.6%**.
- Implication: the AI power growth is real, but September's absolute volume is only **1.09%** of the total and contributed roughly 0.3pp to the year-on-year rate — it cannot hold up the aggregate, and the aggregate slowdown came from weather, not from compute peaking. The real constraint is interconnection and permitting — NextDC is the first listed company to quantify it into guidance. Bullish for grid equipment and interconnection work, bearish for single-campus data-centre operators.

## 4. Assessment

**Call one: AI hardware is bid tonight, but this is event-driven multiple repair, not an earnings upgrade — a gap-up is not worth chasing.**

Evidence: Nasdaq futures +0.80%, having touched 30,010; in the same hour Asian cash gave the opposite sample — KOSPI -1.07% and -2.09% off its high, the Hang Seng closing the morning near its low, and ChiNext's -1.07% driven mainly by CATL alone (-10.6%, turnover above RMB 20bn) while more than 2,800 stocks in the whole market rose and CSI 1000 futures gained 1.28%. The same AI narrative is a bid in US futures and an offer in Asian cash, which says the marginal buyer is responding to an event rather than a trend. Last issue's boundary still holds: Supermicro's 8-K settles "volume" but not "return", and it is unaudited.

Assets affected: server ODMs, GPUs and memory are a short-term long; neoclouds and data-centre operators dependent on interconnection are the short side. **Confirmation**: SMCI holds above its pre-8-K close of $32.47 after the gap (i.e. the +15.65% is not given back) and SMH volume exceeds its prior 20-day average. **Invalidation**: SMCI gives back the entire gain, or NVDA/AVGO fail to follow — then it is a single-stock move, not a sector repair.

**Call two: a December hike is consensus; "at least two more" is the unpriced tail.**

Evidence: the Reuters poll has 100/100 expecting a December hike and nearly 60% seeing a 4.00–4.25% terminal rate, yet 38 of 92 expect at least two more hikes — those two numbers imply rate paths one hike apart. Add the same-direction expectations for the BoJ in December and the RBA in November, and this is the macro variable most worth guarding against this hour, rather than the single 25bp already in the price. Long-end yields hit multi-decade highs on 10-07 and have since retraced almost 25bp, which shows a bond market repeatedly revising rather than moving one way.

Impact: a valuation ceiling for long-duration growth stocks; a real-rate headwind for gold that is being offset by geopolitical premium, hence the flat tape above 4,100; a firmer dollar. The trade-off is **no chasing a gold breakout here and no adding long-duration bond exposure**, with equity exposure tilting toward assets that return cash early.

**Invalidation**: the 10/27–28 statement drops "prudent" language, Warsh provides an explicit path, or December hike odds fall back from 100%.

**Call three: the physical bottlenecks are all midstream — refining, grids, interconnection — not upstream resources.**

Evidence: Morgan Stanley's 3.4m bpd of refinery maintenance (2.5x normal), Goldman's sevenfold rise in shipping costs, NextDC cutting guidance on a grid delay, and China's data-centre electricity use growing 35% off a base that is only 1.09% of total demand. Crude itself looks loose: on last issue's Kpler read, Hormuz flows had recovered to 82% of pre-war levels and Middle East volumes exceeded pre-war. All four point the same way — the constraint sits in processing and connection capacity, and that kind of bottleneck holds pricing power longer than the resource itself.

Impact: refining margins, grid equipment and interconnection work are the most direct beneficiaries; the absolute crude price and pure upstream resource exposure offer a worsening risk/reward. **Invalidation**: Hormuz fully normalises and refinery maintenance ends early (the products narrative collapses), or large data-centre projects interconnect on schedule (the grid bottleneck is disproved).
