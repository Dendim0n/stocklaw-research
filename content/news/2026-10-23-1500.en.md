## 4. Assessment

**Judgement 1: the A-share reversal is confirmed, but its nature is a passive-flow and short-covering repair, not the start of a new trend; do not chase next week's open.**

The evidence is in the closing prints: Shanghai 3,690.48 versus a prior close of 3,658.03 (+0.89%), closing 0.02% below the intraday high; ChiNext from a -3.21% low to +4.16%, STAR50 from -3.72% to +3.04%, SSE50 at its intraday high. Three indices closing near session highs means sellers were exhausted in the final session. ETF turnover of RMB 200bn (RMB 124.2bn broad-based) and combined turnover of 1.96trn are quantified absorption.

But the counter-evidence is just as clear: there was no new policy fact all day - the 14:24 GF Securities note from Guo Lei on "four positive changes", the 13:04 Industrial Securities note and the 13:56 Everbright note are all broker commentary. A 7.79% ChiNext amplitude from -3.21% to +4.16% is the shape of stop-losses being absorbed, not of new money arriving. Main-force flows bought memory and optical modules but sold banks, utilities and gold miners - that is rebalancing, not broad accumulation.

For assets: small-cap growth and HK tech carry the most beta and the most fragility, while large caps (SSE50) closed at the high and should not be read as a failed defence. The trade-off is to **hold existing growth exposure, not add near 3,690, and wait to see how next week's opening gap is handled**; if Monday gaps up more than 1%, wait for the gap to fill before adding.

Confirmation and invalidation: holding **3,690** on Monday with turnover above RMB 1.5trn means the repair continues; a break below **3,617.60** makes today a one-day wonder and reverts the read to "leverage unwind incomplete".

**Judgement 2: tonight's US risk is positioning, not news - the Goldman PB data show cross-strategy deleveraging, not a TMT-only de-rating; Asia's semis provide counter-evidence, so neither chase the short nor catch the first bounce.**

The evidence: all three equity strategies down more than 1% on the same day for the first time since Covid - fundamental long/short -1.4%, TMT -2.7%, systematic -1.3%, pod shops -1.9%. That combination comes from risk-budget contraction, not company quality. The pod-shop drawdown is the dangerous one because its standard response is to cut gross leverage.

The counter-evidence is also hard: SK Hynix +4.41%, KOSPI higher, SEAJ September equipment orders **+48.2%**, STAR50 +3.04% with ChangXin Memory leading main-force inflows. US futures at NQ +0.20% (versus prior settle), ES -0.02%, YM -0.31% describe a tape that cannot fall but will not rally.

For assets: semis and AI hardware have short-term repair potential, but uncleared leverage (the prior brief's Kioxia correction) caps the elasticity; defensives and utilities may have a relative edge tonight. The trade-off is to **neither short semis nor chase the first bounce**, and to judge whether deleveraging is over by price rather than by sentiment.

Confirmation and invalidation: NQ holding today's low of **29,022.75** and ES holding **7,685** means the deleveraging is an impulse and repairable; NQ breaking 29,022.75 with TMT still being sold means pods are still cutting and Asia's stabilisation does not count; if the YM/NQ divergence widens beyond 1%, money is rotating from tech into defensives rather than de-risking broadly.

**Judgement 3: crude has stopped reacting to escalation rhetoric and only reacts to supply facts; gold held above 4,000 while the dollar printed a new intraday high, which means gold's bid is not coming from a weaker dollar.**

The evidence: three escalation headlines inside the window (the Iranian ultimatum, the Arab League condemnation of attacks on Saudi airport and Shaybah field, the West Bank attack) and December WTI still fell -0.08%, further from its high, while a 40,000-tonne Russian diesel cargo headed for New York. **Bad news no longer lifts the price while supply additions accumulate** - that asymmetry carries more information than any single headline. Crude is still +1.35% on the day, so the premium has not disappeared; it has simply stopped being pushed higher by new headlines.

The gold evidence: after an intraday low of 3,986.56 (below 4,000), it recovered to 4,014.50 (-0.27%) while DXY printed a new intraday high of 101.7505 and EURUSD a new low of 1.12055. Gold did not follow the dollar lower, so there is non-dollar-driven buying near 4,000. Central-bank and ETF flow data were not obtained, so this is inference only - no claim about the source of funds.

For assets: do not chase crude longs (premium is embedded, supply additions are accumulating), and do not chase shorts either (diesel and jet fuel remain tight, Hormuz LNG flows are still impaired); gold is neutral-to-long but only on pullbacks, not above 4,030; the euro lacks upside momentum and the yen has support but is high above 157, raising carry-trade risk.

Confirmation and invalidation: CL above **100.980** with Brent through **114.720** means the premium is re-expanding and the asymmetry read is void; CL below **98.840** confirms the second leg of premium give-back; gold below **3,986.56** invalidates the 4,000 support and requires re-assessing the nature of the bid.
