🧭 **Strategic Assessment**

The overnight market's core tension is clear: sharply escalating Middle East geopolitical risk combined with a new US tariff wave is weighing heavily on risk assets.

The S&P 500 dropped over 1.2% and the Dow fell more than 500 points — the market is simultaneously pricing both "war escalation" and "trade friction expansion" tail risks.

Iran's formal rejection of the ceasefire proposal marks a critical turning point. The window for negotiations that markets had been hoping for is now effectively closed, and Trump's "larger attack" rhetoric signals continued escalation.

While crude oil pulled back slightly overnight, Brent holding above $100 puts real pressure on global inflation expectations. The ECB's sudden pivot and the surge in Fed rate hike probability are direct evidence.

Gold hovering near 4,050 reflects the market's conflicting signals — geopolitical safe-haven demand supports prices, but rising rate hike expectations cap upside. The near-term direction depends on which factor gets priced in first.

Japan's GPIF repatriation is a stealth long-term risk. While not immediate, rebalancing a $1.8 trillion portfolio is enough to shift supply-demand dynamics in US Treasuries and equities.

Two things to watch today: further military escalation in the Middle East, and whether US PMI data confirms an economic slowdown (stagflation narratives would further hit risk appetite).
